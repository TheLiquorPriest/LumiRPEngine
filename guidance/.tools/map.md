description: How the tools for a place's map of squares are called - map_lay, map_edit, map_place, map_view, map_measure and map_door. It says how to call them, not when. Delete this file and an agent that holds one of them is given no such guide.
---
A square is `[column, row]`, counted from 1 at the top left, with north up. Name a place, a person or a thing as the world does, or by its id. map_lay draws a place's map once: `rows`, top first, one symbol to a square (`#` wall, `.` floor, `+` door, a space nothing, and the symbols of the `legend` you give), or, with no rows, a walled room of `width` by `height` with `doors` as squares of its wall; `over` and `square` lay it over one square of a coarser map, and `feet` is how wide a square is. map_edit changes part of a map without redrawing it: `strokes`, each a `symbol` put on `squares`, in a `rect` between two corners or along a `line`, and a `legend` to add or change symbols (null takes one out). map_place takes `pieces`, one object for each person or thing: `who` and one of `square`, `near` (beside someone), `zone`, `on` (lying on a thing, sharing its square) or `lift` (off the map); `walk` true has it walk there, and the answer gives the way and the time it took; `size` and `blocks` (way, view, both or none) give a footprint. map_door takes the door's `door` square and its `state`: open, closed or locked. map_view shows the map as text, around a `centre` (a square, or someone) when it is too large to show whole. map_measure reads and changes nothing: from a spot, `to` gives the distance, whether the two see each other and the way between, `within` who is in range, and `area` (burst, cone or line) with its `size`, and `toward` for a cone or a line, the squares it covers.

map_place records each position as you give it and reports what does not add up (a square in a wall, no way there); a square that is not on the map is refused, and the answer says what the map has. Each write also takes `source`, and `precision` and `standing` where you know them.
