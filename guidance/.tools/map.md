description: How the tools for a place's map of squares are called - map_lay, map_edit, map_place, map_view, map_measure and map_door. It says how to call them, not when. Delete this file and an agent that holds one of them is given no such guide.
---
A square is `[column, row]`, counted from 1 at the top left, with north up. Name a place, a person or a thing as the world does, or by its id.

map_lay lays a map for a place: `rows`, top first, one symbol to a square (`#` wall, `.` floor, `+` door, a space nothing, and the symbols of the `legend` you give), or, with no rows, a walled room of `width` by `height` with `doors` as squares of its wall; `feet` is how wide a square is. Where the place has a map already, the one you lay goes over it in this chat only: the answer says so, the world's own map is untouched, and a swipe takes it back. A map takes at most 64 rows of 64 squares; a larger one is answered with the most it takes, and the way is to lay it in parts, each room its own place over its square of a coarser map. `over` and `square` lay it over one square of a coarser map: where that map draws the place, its square opens onto the room grid, and a place the square does not hold is not laid for: the grid is laid for the place the square holds, and the answer says so. A symbol no legend names reads as floor and is reported, and the map stands as assumed.

map_edit changes part of a map without redrawing it: `strokes`, each a `symbol` put on `squares`, in a `rect` between two corners or along a `line`, and a `legend` to add or change symbols (null takes one out). A symbol no legend names is read as floor and reported, so name it in the legend; the squares drawn with it are recorded as assumed.

map_place takes `pieces`, one object for each person or thing: `who` and one of `square`, `near` (beside someone), `zone`, `on` (lying on a thing, sharing its square) or `lift` (off the map); `walk` true has it walk there, and the answer gives the way and the time it took; `size` and `blocks` (way, view, both or none) give a footprint. A piece may take its own `source`, `precision` and `standing`, over the call's: one call can record a position from the prose as established and another, by plain sense, as assumed. Each position is recorded as you give it, and only a square that does not exist is refused: one off the map, or at a place with no map, where the answer names map_lay, what is kept there by zone or words, and the near places that have a map. A `near` with no free square, or a full `zone`, puts the piece on the nearest square all the same, reports who it stands over, and records that square as assumed. A zone the map lacks, or someone who stands on no square, stays as words where they are, with a report. Someone at another place is brought here first, and that is reported.

map_door takes the door's `door` square and its `state`: open, closed or locked. map_view shows the map as text, around a `centre` (a square, or someone) when it is too large to show whole; at a place with no map it answers that, with what is kept there by zone or words and the near places that have a map. map_measure reads and changes nothing: from a spot, `to` gives the distance, whether the two see each other and the way between, `within` who is in range, and `area` (burst, cone or line) with its `size`, and `toward` for a cone or a line, the squares it covers. Someone who stands on no square is told as such, with where they are kept, and the rest of the call is measured.

Each write also takes `source`, and `precision` and `standing` where you know them.
