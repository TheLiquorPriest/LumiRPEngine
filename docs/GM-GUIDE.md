# LumiRPEngine GM guide

This guide is for whoever runs the game: you, playing your own world as its GM, or a friend running one for you. The player's side of LumiRPEngine shows only what the point-of-view character knows or can see. The GM side sees everything, secrets included, and can change anything in a running chat. Here is how to use it without breaking the story: what the player sees and why, the GM view and its edits, fixes and Undo, the rules for checks, realism and time in one chat, moving a chat to a new revision of its world, reading the Context inspector, and running a depth chat.

Every button is described in the [user guide](USER-GUIDE.md); this guide is about using them well. Authors who build the secrets in the first place will want [RECIPES.md](RECIPES.md) and the [reference](REFERENCE.md).

## Who sees what

Everything in a world has a visibility, and every rule about it fails closed: when in doubt, the player doesn't see it.

| Visibility | Who sees it |
|---|---|
| `public` (the default) | Everyone |
| `known_by` | Whoever knows it: a `knows` link to it, or a match for its **Known by** selector (the members of a group, people of a kind or a tier, a tag, a classification) |
| `secret` | The GM only |
| `discoverable` (places only) | Hidden until the POV has been there, has a `knows` link to it, or matches its **Known by**; found for good after that |

People, places, tracks and single fields of a kind each take one. A value the engine doesn't understand hides the thing, and a **Known by** that names nothing the engine reads grants nobody; lint flags both.

**The player layer.** Scene, Cast, the palette, the map, the travel list, chips and the Journal show only what the POV knows or can see. Cast lists the people you have met and the people where you stand; someone who isn't here shows only where and when you last saw them, never where they are now or what they plan. The map draws the places you know; in **Known** display a place you have only heard of is a dashed ring, and in **Fog** what you haven't seen lies under fog. Another person's money stays out of sight unless you hold the account.

**The narrator** is told the same facts the player may see, plus cues. A secret's truth reaches the prompt only once the secret is revealed, and the engine drops anything the model proposes that would carry an unrevealed truth. When the model suggests that you learned something, it may only be a public fact, or a place you could hear of; secrets move only by their own conditions. The **Stance** section tells it how the people with the POV behave: whether they know you and their regard in words, what they are about, and the cues the author wrote for their secrets, while those stay hidden. A cue that would carry an unrevealed secret, or name someone or something the POV doesn't know, is left out, and nobody the POV can't see gets a line.

**Secrets in a world** live in two places: a track's stages, each with an optional `truth` that stays GM-only until the stage is revealed, and secret lore entries, which have a **gate** (a condition that opens them), an **audience** (the helper steps that may read them before then; none by default) and their **holders** (the people whose own secret it is: only their minds read it). Lint refuses a public lore entry that repeats a track's truth, and warns when a helper that writes prose, such as the brief or the outline, is in a secret's audience, because what it writes can reach the narrator. How a secret shows before then is the **cue**: a track stage's cue, or a person's own **Cues** in Build, each standing for one secret (a lore entry, a track or a story flag). Write behaviour, never the secret ("evasive about the cellar"); lint refuses a cue that holds its secret's words and warns about one that names what the player may not know.

**Before a session,** run **Check world** in Build: its transcript is the author's view of each day, secrets included, and its notes flag any secret that leaked into a section.

## The GM view

Open **GM** mode: it opens straight onto the truth, with no spoiler gate. Every GM page is yours to read; nothing is hidden from the player, and surprise is your choice not to look. Looking never changes the chat. Play's suggestion and outline chips that carry a secret stay collapsed until tapped, and your own agent's MCP tools still need `spoilers: true`.

- **Entities:** every person, thing, vehicle, group and account, including the ones spawned in this chat and the ones removed, with badges for **You**, **Removed**, **Spawned**, **Agent** and anything that isn't public. A person opens their character sheet and a thing its thing page (see [GM → Things](#gm--things)); anything else opens a form: its fields as its kind defines them, its designed sheet, where it is, its relations, its simulation detail (tier, whether it lives off-screen, and each domain's level), and its classification and tags.
- **Places:** every place with its visibility. With the Organizations pack, a place opens whose it is (12 Birch Lane is Marcus Delgado's), who may enter it and now, its hours, its keys and who carries one, and the latest entries without leave; **Change entry** sets who may enter (Owner only, Members only, Staff only, Customers, Anyone, or as the world has it) and its keys (item ids or item types, between commas) for this chat, one edit with its **Undo**. An organization's own places also show under **GM → Orgs → Property**.
- **Tracks:** every quest, secret, puzzle and agenda, with all its stages and every truth.
- **Orgs:** every organization, the party first, with its members all named, its powers, delegations, History and fields (see [GM → Orgs](#gm--orgs)).
- **Money:** each person's money at their own Money level: their band, accounts, bills and latest transactions. An organization's money is on its page under **Orgs**.
- **Edits:** the corrections in force on this version of the story, each on the latest reply with its **Undo**, and every change that didn't apply after a reply, with the exact reason.

**While a turn runs**, the GM view is *Held* by default: it shows the chat as it was before the turn until you tap **Refresh**. **Settings → Speed → GM page while a turn runs** set to *Live* follows every change instead. Each tab reads only its own parts, and a part that hasn't changed since the last read isn't built or sent again. The entity list is slim: a person's or a thing's page reads its full entry when you open it.

## GM edits

Anything the GM view shows, you can change: a field (set it, or add to a number), a relation and its fields, where something is (at a place, inside a container, or out of it), money on an account (**Add** and **Take**), a track's stage, someone's tier, whether they live off-screen and their levels, their classifications and tags, and you can **Spawn** a new entity or **Remove from the world** an existing one.

- **Checked, all or nothing.** Every save is checked as the change it makes: a value outside its range, a choice that isn't one of its values, a relation that breaks its kinds or how many it allows, is refused with the reason, and nothing in that save applies. **Add** to a number is the one exception: it keeps the result inside the field's range instead of refusing. Values are taken as typed, never as formulas.
- **Locked fields** (🔒, the ones the AI never sees or writes) are writable here and nowhere else.
- **Money stays balanced.** Adding or taking money posts against an outside **Correction** account, so the books still balance and the Money panel shows it as a correction.
- **Loans read as owed.** On a loan, a card or a line, GM → Money reads **Pay down** and **Add to what's owed** for **Take** and **Add**: corrections, never income or spending. In Build, a loan's opening reads **Amount owed**, typed positive (it is stored as what the holder owes). Problems offers a Fix for a loan opened in credit (as written it owes nothing and closes as paid off at its first bill), a job at a rate of nothing (**Make it unpaid**) and an organization with no account (**Open … checking**).
- **A track's stage plays as the story would.** Moving a track on, to a stage that follows from where it stands, is that stage's real entry: the stage it leaves grants its rewards (not when the new stage is how it fails), and the new stage applies its effects, grants its own rewards when it is an end and records its reveal. Stages it skips play no part. Moving a track back, to a stage it has passed or can't reach, only sets the stage, so nothing is granted, spawned or revealed twice. Either way it is one edit with its **Undo**, and the new stage's own ways on wait for the next turn. Your move wins over the story: if the story moves the track meanwhile (a Re-run or a re-read of that reply, or the reply your save waited for), the move is made again from where the story left it. A track the story already took to that stage stays as it is, so nothing is paid twice; anywhere else, it moves on to your stage from there.
- **Remove, don't delete.** **Remove from the world** takes someone out of every scene, list, process and prompt, with whatever they hold, and drops their relations: no rule, schedule or zone picks them out, they don't count in a group's regard, and a removed group's schedules stop moving its members. **Restore** brings them back where they were. Undoing the removal brings the relations back too. Your own character can't be removed.
- **Claimed things.** A thing the player said they had that the engine made real is marked **Claimed** in **World**, and while there are any, **Claimed by the player** lists them at the top with who holds each or where it lies. Open one and **Remove from the world** to take back a claim you don't want in the story.
- **Life and death.** An entity's **Life** card shows its life state, who knows it, and sets it: pick a state and save, and anyone with them learns it then; **Tell someone…** adds people who learn it too (you included). Nobody else hears until they walk in on it or are told, so the player sees only what their character knows. A dead or dying person stays where they lie, unlike **Remove from the world**.
- **Classify for this chat.** An entity's **Classification** card sets its value of each of the world's classifications, hidden ones included, and its tags, for a person spawned in play as much as for one the world defines. A classification or value the world doesn't have is refused. What you set holds in this chat over the world's definition, so rules, schedules and **Known by** selectors match it from then on, and an Upgrade keeps it by default (below). A classification players can't see stays out of their Journal, and tags never reach Play.
- **The world's definitions stay in Build.** Kinds and the world's places are edited in Build and reach the chat through an Upgrade (below).

Each save is one edit: a notice in the GM view says what it did, in a sentence that names who, what the setting means and the value in words ("Marcus Delgado now sleeps at Alder Automotive (Lakeshore County › Port Alder)", "3 minds are now pinned at Near fidelity"), with **Undo**, and a **GM edit** chip on the latest reply has the same **Undo**. The chip says only what the player's character could know: the change when they see it, else whom it was about if they know them ("About Sam Reyes"), never where someone went or what was set, and otherwise that it happened behind the scenes. A save made while a reply is streaming waits for the reply and lands right after it. A save made while the engine is running a turn answers at once with "Queued — lands after the turn", and lands with its **Undo** once the turn is over.

## GM → Things

**GM → Things** is where the chat's belongings are kept straight. The engine keeps four facts about a thing apart: **where it is** (one place or one holder), **who has it** (whoever carries it), **whose it is** (its owner, or, when it has none, whoever's it plausibly is: a business trading where it lies, the household living there; shown as "the household's (presumed)") and **who may use it** (its owner, those they hand it to or lend it to, and the world's household, housemates, partners, staff and customers rules). Most mistakes in a story's belongings are one of those taken for another, so keep them apart when you fix one.

- **Here** shows what is where the player character is: **In the room** (furniture, built-in things and vehicles, with what they hold), **Lying here**, and **On the people here**, each with whose it is and why its holder has it. Start here when the narrator gets the room wrong.
- **By place** and **By owner** are the same trees as Build's, for the chat as it stands.
- **A thing's page** makes each change one GM edit with its **Undo**, its notice saying in a sentence what it did. **Hand to…** asks **Holding**, **Gift** or **Loan**, or offers **Return it to** its owner: pick **Holding** for a thing someone only has for now, so its owner stays its owner; **Make it theirs…** is for a real change of owner. **Kept at…** sets where it goes back to, and **Put it away** sends it there now. **History** shows each change in this chat, by the story, you, a fix or an agent, and why.
- **Wear** puts a thing with a slot on whoever has it (**Wear…** asks whom when nobody does), and **Take off** takes off what they wear; it stays with them.
- **Select** (beside the search) makes Here, By place, By owner and search results choose: a row's tap picks it. The bar under the list changes every thing picked as one GM edit with one Undo: **Move to…**, **Put in…**, **Put away** (each to where it rests; those already there, or kept nowhere, stay), **Make it theirs…**, **Size…** and **Remove…**. It also has:
  - **Wear** or **Take off**: **Take off** once every thing picked is worn; otherwise **Wear** puts on what takes a slot, each on whoever has it.
  - **Hand to…**: **Holding**, **Gift** or **Loan** to anyone, a loan lasting a day, three days or a week by the HUD's clock. What they hold already is left out.
  - **Let use…**: whom, and until a day, three days, a week or no end. With an end it is a loan; with none, it is assigned. A thing they own is left out.
  - **Rename…**, for one thing.

  Each change is one item op a thing. One edit holds at most 64 changes, so pick fewer when the bar says so. **Done** leaves Select.
- **+ Add** (beside Search and Select) opens Add's sheet: the name; the kind and what it is (a catalog type of that kind, **Something else** with a size, or a vehicle class with make, model and year); how many; and where (any place, person or holder, starting where the player character is). It is made at once as one GM edit ("The GM put it there") with its **Undo**. **Vehicle** shows once the chat's world has vehicle classes, its years counted from the year the chat's clock is in.
- **A person's page** (World → a character), from Gear 3, has **Outfits & loadouts** under Things: each saved set with its pieces (a piece gone from the world is named missing) and **On now**, with **Change into**, **Forget** and **Save what they wear as…** (**Outfit** or **Loadout**), each one GM edit with its **Undo**.
- **A place** may be made a property: **Add a property** in GM → Money takes an **At** place picker, listing the places that aren't properties yet. On a place's sheet, **Make this a property** is a GM edit that follows the chat's claims policy.
- **Moves keep the size rules.** **Move to…** and **Put in…** never put a fridge in someone's hands (it lands where they stand) or a thing into something smaller; a built-in thing moves only because you, the GM, say so.

**Tidy up** is the audit: things too big to carry on someone, things in two places at once, owners a handover changed in an older chat, likely duplicates, things made in play with no size, things **worn but lying elsewhere** (its fix takes it off them) and Gear or Place lines **dropped by the final review** (with the things they name, and no fix), each said in a sentence. **Fix** is one edit with its **Undo**; **Leave it** puts a finding aside for this chat only (it changes nothing in the story); **Ask the Curator** hands the finding to the GM assistant, whose proposal you then Apply or not. The fixes with a single answer, a thing too big to carry on someone and a thing worn but lying elsewhere, are made **every turn**, before the story goes on, as **Things tidied** with its own Undo (see the user guide's Old chats): a thing whose tidy the player undid is left while it stays where it is, and a finding you left is never tidied. An older chat's fridges and TVs, bought by an older build onto the buyer, go home on its next turn under one such chip; the rest waits here. Whether that fix is made follows the world's **Tidy impossible holds every turn** (Build → Packs → gear → Tidy up), unless **GM → This chat → Things** gives the chat its own setting: switch on **This chat has its own setting**, then **Tidy impossible holds every turn in this chat**. Like the chat's knowledge choices, it is kept with the chat, not its story, and applies from the next turn.

**Keep in the world** copies the things and places the chat made in play into the world's draft, under their own ids, so the world you commit has them and an Upgrade keeps the chat's copies. Do it in Build's spirit: keep what belongs to the world (the back booth everyone now sits in), not what belongs to one story.

**The Curator.** On a chat played by agents, the Curator keeps things and places true to each reply, with the same item and place changes these controls make. Its changes are staged with the turn and land after the read's, and a change of owner asks first, as the reply's own do. When it gets one wrong, fix it here; your edit wins over the reply it follows, as every GM edit does.

## GM → Orgs

**GM → Orgs** is where who belongs where, and who may do what, are kept straight. Each organization's page there is the one the player sees, with every member named and the GM's controls, each save one edit with its **Undo**. Your edits are the world's, not anyone's act: they ask for no power, so you can make a position nobody there could, and the History line names no one.

- **Ask before you change.** **Who may…** answers a person and a power with the chain it rests on: an owner's role, a position and how it is held, a delegation and its granter's own basis, or a member's default. When the player is refused ("Over your limit of $500.00 a payment for Alder Automotive", "Danny Okafor isn't below Shop foreman at Alder Automotive"), read the chain first: most refusals are the rules working.
- **A title is words; a position is power.** A member's title ("Butler", "Reeve of Brindlemere") is what the story calls them; a position, on **Rules**, carries powers, seats, limits and whom it reports to. Give a title when the story only names someone, and a position when they should be able to act. A title of their own shows over their position's.
- **Delegate for a while, appoint for good.** A delegation lets one person act with some of its granter's powers, within limits, and ends by itself on its date when it has one; a position stays until someone is taken out of it. Lady Honoria hiring and paying the Thornes' staff for Sir Alaric is a delegation; their Master is a position.
- **Employers are anyone.** A job's employer may be a business, a household, a group, a trust, the party or a person: the Thornes employ their butler, and the party pays a hired hand from its purse. Payroll pays from the employer's payroll account, else a company's operating account, else its deposit account.
- **Entry never vetoes.** A place's entry greys the player's **Go** and is told to the narrator, but a move the reply tells still happens, recorded as an entry without leave on the place's **Property** card. To keep someone out, change who may enter, give someone else a key, or have the story lock the door.
- **The party is tiers.** Its core and companions come along on every move (a companion stays for their own shift, appointment or plan); hired hands and allies come only when taken. Set someone's tier on their character sheet, or for many at once in **World**'s bulk change.
- **Control passes down.** An organization that holds another runs it: a controlling stake (more than its decisions' share of the votes), a managing role on a trust, a seat it holds there or a delegation to it passes its people's powers on down, at most eight organizations deep. **Who may…** shows the chain: the basis at the top, then each hold ("… controls … (100% of its votes): its powers pass down"). A subsidiary with its own seated officers keeps their positions' powers, while its parent's people still see its money and change its officers; and whoever controls never pays themselves or their household from what they control.
- **Trusts seat officers too.** A trust's officers and directors are people, or a company seated as its entity director, under **People** (**Add an officer or director**). With **Trustees decide together** in its terms, a trustee's payments, transfers and payouts wait for another trustee's approval in **Decisions**. The player settles a trust or starts a nonprofit from Play's **Start something…**; you do it with the org op `found` (`type` `trust` or `nonprofit`; a nonprofit's founder holds its Director seat), and `admit`, `appoint` and `title` take an organization as `who`.
- **The Chart** is Play's, with every control: who reports to whom, who holds each seat and which are vacant, then who owns it up to the top and what it holds. Its edits are GM edits: positions through the position editor (`orgs.position`), appointments through the membership link or the job.
- **Projects** (on a company, a group or a trust) is Play's board, each control your edit with its Undo, and **Set a step back** adds days or a cost with a reason. The effects behind it: `economy.project_start {org, template | project | steps, name?, account?, payee?}`, `economy.project_pause`, `economy.project_resume` and `economy.project_drop {project}`, and `economy.project_note {project, step?, days?, cents?, text, source: "gm"}`. Your note may set a step back up to 365 days and add any amount; the story's, at most 30 days and the world's cap on narrated prices. Nothing marks a step done but its time.
- **Revenue streams**, under **Money**, list what earns for the organization, each with about what it makes a month, and **Add a stream** starts one from a template: Walk-in customers, One product, A catalog, Oil royalty, Gas royalty, Water royalty, Fees or Custom. Its form shows only what its type needs (price, units and buyers; a catalog's items; a royalty's production, price, share, deductions, severance tax and how many months after production it pays; or a formula), then how it swings, when it pays and whether **The agents may adjust it**, with its first twelve months beside it. Each save is one GM edit: a new stream is spawned, a change is set, and **Remove** takes it out of the world. A stream the agents may adjust takes the GM agent's `gm_revenue` (a factor of 0.25 to 4, or an amount, for 1 to 31 days, at most four at once).
- **Lending as an organization** is the org op `lend {org, to, cents, apr?, months?, from?}`: its note, the borrower's loan and the cash in one change, repaid monthly by autopay.
- **Taxes** adds what applies to it: **Tax on kept earnings** (its earnings and profits at the year's start; last year's personal holding company or accumulated earnings tax, with what it kept, the credit, the passive share and the top five holders' share; consent dividends; paid or due 15 April; or "This world doesn't tax kept earnings"), **Carried forward** (a net operating loss, a capital loss, gifts over the cap), **Foundation payout** (the payout so far, what it granted this year, what is still to grant for last year by 31 December, last year's excise and 30% tax due 15 May, and the donor limit) and **S corporation** (its shareholders, at most 100, and why one can't hold it).

**History** shows the organization's own record (who was admitted, appointed or paid for it, and by whom), then this chat's edits in force that name it: look there when the story and the page disagree.

**In Build.** Build → Packs → economy → **Books** sets depreciation for the world (**None: costs are expensed**, the default, or **Straight-line, with cost depletion**), each organization's **Depreciation** field overrides it, and a property's **Capital cost**, **Useful life (months)**, **In service from**, **Salvage value** and **Reserves (units)** drive it. With straight-line on for the organization, a project's capitalized steps post as capital spending and the asset it makes records its capital cost, life and in-service day; with none, they're expensed and it records no capital cost. **Taxes** there gains **Tax on kept earnings** (off by default). A property set to **mineral** has no insurance or upkeep; give it a **valuation** (present value, cap rate or multiple) and link its royalty streams to it, and its value follows what they will pay. A project is an entity of kind **Project** (its `org`, `steps`, `status` planned or active, and `start`), and lint checks its organization, its steps, what each comes after and any loop among them, what it capitalizes, what it unlocks, and its template.

## GM → Live

Each agent turn shows **This turn's plan** once its pre phase knows it: for each role, **Runs**, **Kept**, **Off**, **Auto** or **None**, with why. The Minds row says **Kept** when every mind due was kept, and **Runs** with how many were kept when only some were. The plan is in the turn's transcript too. Under it, **Where the last turn's time went** lists the turn's parts with their times, the slowest marked, with a way to the setting that decides it.

## Fixes and Undo

A **✎ fix** is the player's quick correction of a value, from the status pill: the place, the weather (from Weather 2), the cash and the scene type. It is checked like a GM edit, refused rather than clamped, and saved the same way, with a **Corrected** chip and **Undo**.

- **Undo covers the latest reply.** Each fix and GM edit on it has its own Undo, and so do automatic changes the engine read from the reply. For anything earlier, change it again with a new edit, or use Keep or Discard after editing an earlier message.
- **Your corrections win over the story.** A fix or GM edit keeps what you asked for, and is made again when the reply it waited for lands, when a reply before it is read again and when a swipe carries it, so it still says what you said.
  - **Swipes carry corrections.** Re-narrate makes every fix and GM edit on the turn again after the same outcome, and Re-run after the new roll: cash you set is that amount whatever the new roll spent. One that can't be made at all (the thing it changed was only in the old reply) is dropped with a **Not carried** note in the Journal; one you undid stays undone. A swipe that finishes while the chat is paused (an earlier message edited and not yet kept or discarded) carries none of them, and each gets that note.
  - **A correction made while a reply streams** waits for the reply, then lands over whatever the reply changed; a part the reply already did is passed over, and one the reply did whole still stays, so a later read, swipe or Undo that takes back what the reply did makes it again. The same correction sent twice is one: one Undo takes it back. Only one the reply made impossible (a box put into a crate the reply put inside that box) gets a **didn't apply after the reply** chip, until dismissed, and the **Edits** tab keeps the exact reason.
  - **A later read or Undo never undoes it.** Read again later (a **Re-read edit**, a **Retry read**), a reply written before your correction comes before it, and the correction is made again after that read. Undo an automatic change your correction came after, and the correction is made again in its place, with its own chip and Undo. Replies written after it carry the story on from it.
- **Switching swipes and branches.** A correction belongs to the version of the story it was made on. Swipe away and it stops counting; swipe back and it counts again.

## Checks in this chat

How a check is decided (when the dice roll, what the player's described approach is worth, who settles it, and when a failed try may be tried again) is a policy. The world sets one in **Build → World → Ruleset → How checks are decided**, a player sets their own for new chats, and a running chat can change its own in **Settings → Checks → This chat**: pick **Narrative-first**, **Balanced**, **Crunchy** or **Custom**, and tap **Apply to this chat**.

- The change applies from the next turn and is recorded like a realism change, so it follows swipes and branches, and earlier turns keep the rules they were played under.
- **Narrative-first** rolls only risky checks and lets the story settle them, with the exact odds as guidance (when no judgement of the player's approach is available, the dice settle them instead); **Balanced** rolls every check except sure things and hopeless ones, counts the player's approach as a small bonus and flags close calls; **Crunchy** rolls everything and lets the dice settle it. **Custom** sets each value.
- **Marked checks.** With **When checks roll: only where marked**, a check rolls only for an action marked **Its check always rolls**, at a place marked **Checks roll here** (and the places inside it), or while a quest marked **Checks roll while it's in progress** is under way. Mark the dangerous moments in Build and let the rest of the story flow.
- **What the table sees.** The palette shows the verdict before anyone commits (**No roll needed**, **Too hard to try**, **Retry needs a change**, **The story decides (odds 35%)**), and the **Roll** chip says what was rolled, or "No roll:" and why, with how the approach was rated and any close call. Other people's checks always roll, under narrative authority too.
- **Inferred checks.** Below the rules, the same page sets how a typed attempt no action covers is read as a check of the world's sheet: off, offered on a chip or rolled automatically, for which attempts, who rolls, with which skills and difficulty bands, how much the narrator is told and whether others' numbers show. Change it, tap **Review the change**, then **Apply to this chat**; it applies from the next turn like the rest. The world's default is beside the rules in Build, and each value is in the user guide's [Inferred checks](USER-GUIDE.md#inferred-checks).

The full table of presets is in the user guide's [How checks are decided](USER-GUIDE.md#how-checks-are-decided).

## Realism and time

Both are per chat once the chat has started, and both are recorded with the story.

- **Realism:** **Settings → Realism → This chat** changes the running chat for **Everyone**, or for one band: Leads, Supporting or Background. Before you apply, the migration preview names what stops, what freezes and what will be filled in when it is first shown, and any rule of the world that depends on something that would stop updating. A world's ruleset can set floors (stat modifiers need Sheets 3; the grid system keeps Space at 3), and the sliders never go below them. For a single person, set their levels in the GM view's **Simulation** section.
- **Player claims:** below the sliders, **Player claims** sets what this chat makes of a thing the player says they have that the world doesn't: **Make it real**, **Ask first** or **Hold me to the world**, and whether Make it real asks first above the world's claim cap. It applies at once, from the next turn. See the [user guide](USER-GUIDE.md#player-claims).
- **Time:** **Play → Calendar** changes this chat's ticks per scene type, time scale, Timelapse interrupts, Speed through and Montage. **Pause clock** stops time for the current scene, for a conversation that shouldn't cost the afternoon.
- Changes apply from the next turn; earlier turns keep the levels and times they were played at.

## What the narrator knows

By default the narrator knows what everyone in the scene knows, with each fact the player's character doesn't know marked, and a secret the player hasn't found goes in marked as a secret. The player sets the default in **Settings → Narrator**, a world can set its own in **Build → World**, and **GM → This chat → Knowledge** sets one chat's: under **What the narrator knows**, switch on **This chat has its own choice**, then **Everyone in the scene** or **Your character's only**, secrets **Marked** or **Left out**; under **Knowledge tracking**, **This chat has its own setting**. It is kept with the chat, not its story: no turn already played changes, there is no Undo, a Branch keeps it, and it applies from the next turn. In **GM → This chat → Sections**, a section's **Whose knowledge** is **The narrator's knowledge** unless you pick a scope for it.

## Upgrading a chat

A chat stays on the world revision it started on. When you commit a newer revision of the world in Build, **GM → This chat → Pins and upgrade** offers **Upgrade to the latest revision**, and shows what it brings into this chat, entity by entity, before anything changes. (Importing a world makes a new world, so it never upgrades an existing chat.) The world's other definitions, such as places, rules and actions, come with the revision without a pick. A newer LumiRPEngine's data tables come the same way: Pins and upgrade marks the chat's tables that have newer shipped versions, and **Open Data updates** takes you to **Build → World**, where **Apply** and a commit make the revision the Upgrade brings in.

- **New** people and things are listed, and so are ones the revision no longer has: they stay in the chat unless you pick **Remove**.
- **Each change** to the rest (a field, a name, a tier, classes, a relation, an account's opening balance) shows the chat's value, the revision's value and a pick. **Unchanged in play** (nothing in the chat touched it) starts on **Take**. **Changed in play** (a turn, a fix or a GM edit touched it, even if it later changed back) starts on **Keep**, so an Upgrade never overwrites what happened in the story unless you say so.
- **Take all unchanged** resets the untouched ones to Take, and **Review changed in play** lists only the touched ones. **Upgrade with these picks** applies it.
- A quest sitting at a stage the revision removed goes back to its first stage, with a Journal note.
- **A company made a trust, or a trust a company** (Build → Organizations → Basics → **Change what it is**, which converts either way and previews it) comes as one change, **Change what it is**: from its kind and owners to its new ones, the fields it drops and whom it seats. Take it or keep it whole; nothing of it lands apart.

The Upgrade takes effect from the next turn and is recorded like any other change, carrying only your picks, so swipes, branches and Re-narrate treat it alike. Detail the chat has already shown stays as it was; new tables apply to what hasn't been shown yet.

## Reading the inspector

**Play → Context** shows what the engine put into the prompt for each generation. Step through earlier ones with **Older** and **Newer**; the last ten are kept per chat, and survive a restart.

- **Sections** (Outcome, Scene, People and the rest) each show their size in your main model's tokens and a badge. **Delivered** means the test prompt build found the section in the prompt. **Not delivered** means it didn't: check where the world's book and your preset place it. **Unchecked** is normal for Outcome, Montage and Outline, which only a real turn fills. A section too big for what was left of the section budget loses whole lines from its end until it fits, and its row says how many ("3 lines left out: over its caps or the section budget"). **Clipped** means not even its first line fitted, so it was left out of that turn.
- **The budget bar** is the section budget spent. Sections are kept in rank order and Outcome is never left out, so a trimmed or clipped section is a hint to raise **Settings → Model use → Engine budget (tokens)**, or to give the section a lower rank in the world's sections. Stance comes last by default, so it is the first to go on a small budget. Its size counts its people lines only: its cues ride in whatever budget is left and aren't counted, since they change with secrets the player hasn't found.
- **Activation** reads **Activation confirmed** once Lumiverse reports every section the engine switched on as activated, and **Not confirmed** until then or when one wasn't.
- **Warnings** say when the world's book wasn't attached to the chat or some of its section entries were missing (both are put right for the next turn), when your preset has a setting that causes trouble, and when a damaged journal was set aside, with where the old file is.

Your own World Books work as they do anywhere in Lumiverse, so the inspector doesn't list them.

## Running a depth chat

On a [depth](DEPTH.md) chat the author runs each scene, characters with minds play as their own agents, and the GM keeps the truth. You steer it differently from a classic chat:

- **A character's agent never sees the truth,** the author's notebook or another mind. To shape a character, edit its core in **GM → Minds** (who it is at root), edit another file (a directive the agent keeps and never rewrites), or interview it. Its `now.md` is how it is this minute and stays its own to change.
- **The author steers through the world,** as a DM does with players: the scene, what happens and what reaches a character. An arc lives in the author's notebook, never in a mind. Edit a notebook file in **GM → Depth → Notebook** to give the author a directive: the file is pinned, and the author may comment on it but never rewrites it.
- **Edit a reply to change what happened.** The author reads your edit and amends or strikes the beats it changes; a cosmetic edit amends nothing. Everyone who lived a replaced beat catches up on the new one and keeps or rewrites each file of their mind that drew on the old one. A file that ends up with no verdict is flagged **Out of mind**: it stays readable, and your own edit brings it back.
- **Beliefs beside the truth.** **GM → Minds → Beliefs** shows each character's beliefs linked to the facts they concern, true, false or partly, with the GM's reason. A beat the player character perceived that reveals a secret opens that secret for him, as if he had learned it; a link the GM takes back closes it again.
- **Off screen.** **GM → Depth → Drafts** lists what the author simulated off screen. A draft isn't canon until it settles; **Settle now**, **Drop** and **Re-draft** are yours. **GM → Depth → Treatments** shows what got which effort and what it cost, with the Ask me questions waiting on you.
- **Read a turn.** **GM → Depth → Turn** shows every model call a reply made, where everyone was, each beat with who decided it and who perceived it, and each passage with the GM's check. Start there when a reply reads wrong: a passage that departs from its beats has a chip, and Re-narrate renders the same beats again.
- **The bootstrap's spot checks** are the one place your marks change canon in bulk: a beat marked wrong is amended before the next chunk. Mark honestly and briefly; a run of wrong marks pauses the run and offers a redo from that chapter.
