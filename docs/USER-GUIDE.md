## How RPE works

You chat in Lumiverse as you always do. A chat whose connection is "RPE" is played by RPE's agents, and RPE keeps a record of the story and of the world beside the chat, turn by turn.

**The agents.** A turn is run by a team, each member an agent with its own instructions:

- **The Author** leads every turn. It is the story's author: it decides what happens, brings in the others when the turn needs them, and writes the reply.
- **The game master (GM)** keeps the game side: it rules, rolls, stages the consequences and keeps the map. The Author asks it when something is contested, costly or risky, or when real time passes.
- **The characters.** A principal character can be run as an agent of its own, in its own mind and voice, one run per character.
- **The narrator** writes the prose from the Author's brief, when the Author hands it over. As shipped, the Author writes the reply itself, and hands it over when this chat's notes on writing ask for a separate voice, or when the turn's working has grown large.
- **The keeper, the historian, the indexer and the mapper** do work that can wait: bookkeeping after the reply, reading a stretch of the chat's past, indexing your lore, and mapping the preset's prompt. The Author starts them as jobs.

How each agent behaves is written in plain files you can read and change, not fixed in code (see [Files, agents, rules and skills](#files-agents-rules-and-skills)).

**A turn.**

1. **You act.** You send a message, swipe, regenerate or continue a reply, or press something in the play views that plays at once.
2. **Lumiverse builds the prompt** from your preset, character, lore and history, as it does for any chat. RPE's extension leaves the history beyond your window out of it: the latest 40 messages stay (**History window**, `history.window`).
3. **The Author is briefed.** It starts from that whole prompt and a briefing: what kind of turn this is, what changed since its last turn (a reply you edited, turns you deleted, a stretch played without RPE), what you asked for beside your words, the world state, who is on stage, a map of the lore, the story so far beyond the window and the story brief.
4. **Intent first.** The Author weighs what you want before it decides anything, and writes a short intent record. Then it decides how to run the turn, and works as an agent: it looks things up with its tools, asks the GM and the characters, and changes the world only through the engine's operations. The engine keeps the numbers: the clock, prices and balances, rolls. Nothing cuts the Author's work short unless you set a limit, and it thinks at its highest effort unless you set another.
5. **The reply** reaches the chat as Lumiverse's own reply, streamed.
6. **Right after the reply**, in the same run, the Author settles the turn against the reply as it went out: who moved where, what changed hands, the story's beats, threads and scene, what each character on stage perceived, and its own record of the turn. What can wait runs as a job.

**Two lanes.** Every change RPE records is marked with its lane. The **story** lane holds beats, threads, arcs, the scene, perceptions and what characters remember and feel. The **engine** lane holds time, places and positions, what people wear, carry and own, money, stats and conditions, quests' stages, rolls and rulings. When the two disagree, the story is what happened, and the record is brought in line with it.

**The world's rules advise.** The engine applies what an agent records and reports anything that does not add up: a sum that does not balance, a thing in two places, a requirement not met. The report goes to the agent, stays listed as unsettled, and shows on the turn's page. How binding the rules are is written in `SIMULATION.md`, which you can change. RPE refuses only what it cannot use: a name that is nothing it knows, or input it cannot read.

**The record follows the chat.** Each change belongs to the turn that made it, so a swipe, an edit, a deleted message or a branch takes RPE's record with the story (see [Swipes, edits, deletes and branches](#swipes-edits-deletes-and-branches)).

**A world is optional.** A world is a setting RPE keeps in its own database: places, people, belongings, money, rules and a calendar ([Worlds in RPE](world.md)). Attach one to a chat and RPE settles what rules should settle. A chat with no world plays on a blank world, which the agents fill as the story goes.

## Playing in a chat

### Switching a chat to RPE

In Lumiverse, open a chat and choose the connection "RPE" for it, which `rpe up` made ([Install and run](install.md#rpes-connection-in-lumiverse)). Write a message: RPE plays the turn. A chat on any other connection is not RPE's, and RPE leaves it alone.

What you type is read by the Author, with the rest of the prompt. Write as you would in any chat: an action, speech, a stretch of time, a question, an out-of-character note. The Author decides what it means and what happens. Nothing reads your words for a list of commands.

A request Lumiverse makes on RPE's connection that is not a reply in a chat (a title or a summary of its own) is passed on to a model connection of RPE's: the default one, or the one named by **Connection for requests with no tag** (`turn.passConnection`).

### The composer: Depth and Swipe

RPE adds two controls to the row of tools beside Lumiverse's message box. Each offers the choices its file lists, so they change when you change the file.

- **Depth** asks how deep the Author goes on your next message. **None** ("The Author chooses") is the default. The others come from the `depths:` list in `TURN.md`. As shipped: **direct** (no look-up, ruling or invention), **assisted** (a look-up, a ruling, a roll or one character's answer, then write) and **full** (several minds, a contest, or a stretch of time). The choice waits for your next message, and the button shows it until that turn takes it.
- **Swipe** asks how the next swipe or regenerate is played. The choices come from the `modes:` list in `SWIPE.md`. As shipped: **another-telling** (the same outcomes, told differently) and **afresh** (the turn run again from the start, with new rolls). You can add a line under "What was wrong with it? (optional)". **Set for the next swipe** keeps it for your next swipe, and **Clear** takes it away. A new message does not take it: it waits for a swipe.

Feedback you type in Lumiverse's own regenerate dialog reaches the Author too. Where RPE can tell it apart, the turn's page shows it under **What you asked for**.

### Under each reply

Under each reply RPE played, a line says:

- the world's date and time when the turn ended, and where your character was, where the chat plays a world;
- **Story** and **Engine**, each with how many changes the turn made in that lane; tap one for the short list;
- **Turn**, which opens the turn's page (see [The Turns page](#the-turns-page)).

A reply RPE did not play says **Made without RPE.** A reply carried over from the previous build says which engine played it.

A reply whose turn's record was not finished (its run failed or was stopped after the reply had begun to go out) says why. Where it can be settled, **Settle now** has the Author finish the record of that reply now; nothing is written to your chat.

An empty reply, which Lumiverse keeps when a turn ended before a word of its reply went out, says so: "Swipe it or delete it in Lumiverse." The button beside it opens the setting **Take away an empty reply a failed or stopped turn left** (`lumiverse.reply.removeEmpty`), which is off as shipped ([what it does](troubleshooting.md#a-turn-ends-without-a-reply)).

**Questions under a reply.** Where you set a lane's writes to wait for your word (see [Who may write what](#who-may-write-what)), each write that waits shows under its turn's reply with **Go ahead** and **No**.

**While the Author is still working** after the reply went out, a marker under the newest reply shows the run's newest progress line, or "The Author is still working.", with **Live**.

**Where the window ends.** A thin line stands under the last message the prompt leaves out, and says how many messages lie above the window and how many of those the story record does not cover yet: "40 messages above the window, 12 not covered by the story yet." Where that message is not loaded, the line shows at the top of the chat.

RPE's notices show in the chat's toolbar, so you see them with the drawer shut.

### The HUD

While the chat plays a world, RPE shows the world's status over the chat.

- **On a wider screen**, a small pill floats over the chat. Drag it where you like; it remembers its place, the phone's and the desktop's apart. It shows the time (with a pause sign while the clock is paused, and the time scale when it is not 1×), your place, the weather and your cash, each when the world has it, and ✎ where a value can be fixed. On a screen narrower than 760 px it shows the time and **Show place, weather and cash**. A pulse shows while a turn runs, and **Paused** while a turn is held on the Live page.
- **On a phone**, a status line sits above the message box instead: the time, your place and your cash, the same pulse and **Paused**, and **Act**, which opens the actions sheet.

Tap either for the **World** details: the time and the part of the day, your place, the scene type and how long the last turn took, the weather and your cash, each with its ✎ where it can be fixed; **This turn**, with its calls, seconds, tokens and lore lookups; **Pause clock** or **Resume clock**; **Map**, where the world has a map; and **Actions**.

While the chat plays a world, the composer's action bar has **World actions…** (the actions sheet), **Timelapse…** (the sheet at its Timelapse tab), **Pause clock** or **Resume clock**, and **World map**.

#### Fixing a value

✎ beside a value corrects it: the time, your place, the scene type, the weather or your cash.

- **Time** sets the date and the time of day, in the world's calendar and as the clock reads where you are. A later time moves the clock on; what the time in between brought is the Author's to decide, at the next turn. An earlier time puts the clock back.
- **Place** moves your character to a place you know: search it by any word of its name, or browse from the top level down.
- **Scene type** lists the scene types you may know of; **Place default** goes back to the type of wherever you are.
- **Weather**, where the world simulates the sky, picks one of the climate's states for where you are.
- **Cash**, from Money level 2, is typed in the world's own money.

A fix is your own change, made at once and kept on the chat's newest turn, with **Undo**. The Author is told of it at the next turn. A fix the world's rules cannot take (a value outside the field's range) is answered with why, and nothing changes.

### The actions sheet

**World actions…**, **Act**, and **Actions** in the World details open the actions sheet over the chat. It lists what your character can do here and now, by tab: **Actions**, **Buy**, **Pay**, **Transfer**, **Lend**, **Drive**, **Timelapse** and, in a world with combat, **Combat**, each where it has something. An action shows its targets, its minutes and its price, and an action with a check shows its odds. One that is not possible here and now stays listed, with why. **Go to** asks who comes along. **Timelapse** offers lengths of time, times to go until, and what may stop it.

What you pick does not play by itself. It waits for your next message, and the Author is told what you pressed and on what, and plays it as what you want, with your words for how. Pick the same thing again to take it back. See [What a press does](#what-a-press-does).

**World map** opens the map over the chat (see [The map](#the-map)).

### RPE's pages

RPE's pages are in a tab of Lumiverse's drawer called **RPE**, for the chat Lumiverse has open. Its bar holds **Live**, **Turns**, **World** and **Play**, and **More** holds the rest. The same pages are in RPE's web app, where the **Chats** page lists the chats RPE plays, the latest played first, and you pick one ([the web app](install.md#the-web-app-and-its-key)).

- For one chat: **Live**, **Turns**, **Sessions**, **World**, **Cast**, **Prompt**, **Story**, **Places**, **Quests**, **Lore** and **Play**.
- For everything: **Jobs**, **Agents**, **Rules**, **Build**, **Files**, **Connections**, **Settings**, **Runtime**, **Storage** and **Migration**.

At the top, a line says how the page reaches RPE: **Direct**, or **Through Lumiverse** (by way of the extension). Where the extension does not hold RPE's registration or lacks a permission, a banner says so, with **Check again**. Where RPE refuses something, the page shows it in RPE's own words.

**The spoiler gate.** What the agents planned and did can give away what comes next. So the intent record, the agents' work and much of what RPE keeps (secrets, the Author's plans, every character's mind) stay behind a notice, **Spoilers are hidden**, until you press **Show them for this chat**. **Hide them** shuts it again. The gate opens for one chat at a time, and shuts when you leave the chat or reload the page.

### Live

**Live** shows what runs in the chat as it runs: every run under way, a turn and the jobs that began beside it. Each shows what it is, how it stands and what it has spent, with **Stop**, and what went to the chat as it streams. Behind the spoiler gate: the intent record, and the agents as a tree, each with its model calls, its tool calls and their results, its `todo` list and the rules that fired.

- **Pause** holds a turn before its next model call (the call in flight finishes), and **Resume** lets it go on.
- **Say something to the Author** sends a line the Author hears at its next call.
- With nothing running, Live shows the last runs as they ended. With no chat open, it shows your jobs of no chat.

## The Play page

**Play** holds the play views of the previous build, running on RPE: the scene, your character, money, gear and vehicles, the people you know, organizations, quests and the map, each as the chat's world has them. It is for a chat that plays a world.

A chat with no world shows **Not a world chat** and the worlds Build holds, each with **Attach**; **Open Build** goes to Build. Attach gives the chat that world, and it plays the world's newest revision from then on. A chat plays one world: attaching another is refused with the world and revision it plays.

A bar of six places runs along the page: **Now**, **Me**, **People**, **Orgs**, **Places** and **Story**. A place that holds more than one part shows them as tabs. A place shows only while it has something to show: a place whose packs the world does not use is left out. What each shows follows the world's [realism](#realism) levels: a domain that is off shows nothing, and one at a low level shows less. A dot on a place or a tab means something there wants you. Details open as sheets over the page (a person, a place, a thing, an account, a quest), each with a back button; Escape goes back one level.

Everything here is shown as your character knows it. Someone your character cannot know of is never listed, and a place stays hidden until your character has been there or heard of it. A secret stays out of every place, sheet and Journal line until the story reveals it.

On this page, **Act**, **Timelapse…** and **Combat actions** say "The actions sheet (Act, Actions, Timelapse) does not open in this build of RPE", and the map's **Full screen** says Places shows it here. In the chat, the actions sheet and the map open over the chat (see [The HUD](#the-hud)).

### What a press does

A press in the play views (here, in the HUD, in the actions sheet or on the map in the chat) does one of three things.

- **It is done at once.** Every money and organization control (paying, moving money, cards, standing payments, credit, investing, property, invoices, staff, members, structure, votes, the plan), your Belongings' controls, and each ✎ fix. It is your own change, kept on the chat's newest turn. A notice says what was done, with **Undo**. No message is sent, no reply is written and no time passes.
- **It waits for your next message.** Everything else: going somewhere, taking or giving a thing, buying, driving, a Timelapse, an action of the world's own. RPE keeps it with your next message, and the Author is told what you pressed and on what, and plays it as what you want, with your words for how. Press the same thing again to take it back. Nothing on the page shows it while it waits; once the turn has run, its page lists it under **What you asked for**.
- **It reads.** Opening a sheet, a tab or a place changes nothing.

**Telling the Author.** A control that is done at once has **Tell** and **Don't tell**: whether the next turn's Author is told you did it. When you choose neither, its group's setting decides: **Tell the Author: Payments to others**, **Credit**, **Property**, **Business**, **Staff**, **Members**, **Structure** and **Hand-overs and outfits** are on as shipped; **Your own accounts**, **Investing**, **Governance** and **Things** are off (`taps.tell.<group>`). **Payments told from** (`taps.tell.paymentsFrom`) sets the amount from which a payment to someone else is told.

**When the world's rules say no.** The world's rules advise. Where a press does not add up by them (a requirement not met, someone who is not there), it is done all the same, and the notice says "Done, though the world's rules say: …". It is refused only where it names someone, somewhere or something your character does not know ("Rena isn't anyone you know"), where it would change nothing ("That changes nothing."), or where the world cannot do it at all, and then the notice says why and nothing changes. The words are in `.prompts/play-answers.md`, yours to change.

**Undo** takes back everything the change did. Where something has changed part of it since, Undo takes back the rest and says which part stays and why ("… stays as it is, because something has changed it since"). It undoes nothing once the turn it was kept with is no longer part of the chat's story (a swipe or a rewind took that turn away).

**While a turn runs**, Now says **A turn is running**: "A change you make now applies at once, and the running turn goes on from where it began", with **Watch it work**, which opens Live. While a turn is held on Live, Now says **Paused**, with **Open Live**.

### Now

**Now** is where Play opens. From the top: the banners above; the **scene card** (the time and the part of the day, **Clock paused** while it is, your place, the scene type with how long the last turn took, the weather and your cash, each with ✎ where it can be fixed); **Needs you**, the questions waiting under replies, each opening its reply in the chat; the **Encounter** while a fight is under way (the round and whose turn it is, the order with HP and conditions, and what you have left; see [Combat and encounters](#combat-and-encounters)); **Here**, the people with you, each opening their sheet; **glances**, short cards from the world's packs (what is coming up, the quest in hand, your vehicles, money alerts, things lent and overdue, your conditions, your organizations), each with **Open**; and **Act**, **Timelapse** and **Map**.

### Me

**Me** has a tab for each part of your own life, each where the world has it:

| Tab | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **You** | Your name, where you are and what you are doing, your tier and your conditions; then your character sheet: stats, skills and derived values, as numbers or in words by the level. A pool such as HP is a bar that turns red at a quarter or less; a level shows its XP. |
| **Needs** | From Cast life 4: hunger, fatigue, social and fun, each a gauge from 0 (met) to 100 (urgent), in words once it is pressing ("hungry"), with the point it is pressing from and what meets it ("Met by eating"). |
| **Money** | Your own money: see [Your money](#your-money). |
| **Garage** | A card for each vehicle: whose it is, its class, fuel, condition, where it is and its home base, its odometer or hours, cargo, specs, who has it on loan and who may use it, and service due. **Where is it?**, **Home base…**, **Owner & use…**, and **Specs…** (your own lines, "Seats: 5") are done at once; **Drive…** waits for your next message. **+ Add a vehicle** follows the world's claims policy. |
| **Belongings** | Everything you have, wear, keep at home and lend: see [Belongings](#belongings). |

#### Belongings

- **At the top:** **Search your things** (inside bags too), **Select**, and **+ Add**, which the world's claims policy hides under **Hold me to the world**.
- **Filter chips:** **All**, **Wearing** (Gear 3+), **Carried**, **Arsenal** (weapons, armor and ammunition), **Home**, **Here** and **Lent**.
- **The outfit bar** (Gear 3+) names the saved outfit you wear now. Tap it for **Outfits & loadouts**: each saved set with its pieces, **Change into**, **Forget**, and **Save what I'm wearing as…** an **Outfit** or a **Loadout**. An outfit dresses you; a loadout is what you carry ready and the arms and armor you wear.
- **Sections:** **Wearing**, **Carried** (bags open in place, with their load), **Here**, **Home storage**, **Elsewhere**, **Lent and borrowed**, and **Vehicles (n)**, which opens the Garage.
- **Each row** has one main verb (**Take off**, **Ready** or **Stow**, **Wear**, **Put in**, **Take**, **Take back** or **Give back**), and **⋯** holds the rest: **Move to…**, **Put away**, **Drop**, **Owner & use…**, **Rename** and **Size**.
- **Every change here is done at once**, with **Undo** in the notice. **Select** lets you pick rows and do one thing to them all, as one change with one Undo.
- **Move to…** offers the places you know, yourself, the people here and every holder you can use, with **Where it's kept**, **Recent**, **On you**, **Here** and **Home** first. A holder too small or too full is greyed, with why.
- **Owner & use…** shows whose a thing is (**Mine**, **Household** or **Nobody's**) and who may use it, each with **Stop**, and offers **Let someone use it…**.
- **+ Add** asks the name, the kind (**Item**, **Clothing**, **Container**, **Kit**, **Weapon**, **Armor**, **Ammo** or **Vehicle**), what it is, how many and where. Under **Make it real** it is made at once; keys, weapons and documents ask first, and so does anything priced over the world's claim cap while that is on. Under **Ask first** it always asks.

Tap a thing for its sheet: where it is and what holds it, whose it is and why you have it, where it is kept, its slot, quantity and condition, whom it is lent to, a kit's pieces and a container's load. **In the story** (**Fetch**, **Put away**, **Give back**, **Hand to…**) waits for your next message; **Change it** (**Move to…**, **Owner & use…**, **Rename**, **Size**) is done at once. A holder of yours opens as its container page, with **Put in from…**, **Add here…** and **Empty into…**.

### Your money

**Me → Money** is your own money. What it shows follows your Money level: at 1 a wealth band; from 2 your cash and accounts; from 3 cards, standing payments, income and bills; at 4 the full books (terms, statements, pending lines and holds, what your net worth is made of, investment lots, your paycheck's taxes and your credit report).

**Money detail.** Below the top Money level the money hub keeps the books simple, and at the top level it shows them all. Under any preset, a world can turn single money features on or off in **Build → Packs → economy** (**Money features** → **Feature switches**). Switched on, a feature runs from the lowest level it can, and the hub shows its own detail there; switched off, it runs at no level, and its detail is gone from the hub at the top level too.

A row of segments sits under Me's tabs, each where you have something in it:

- **Summary**: your net worth and wealth band, cash on hand, your first alerts (**All alerts** opens the rest), what is coming up in the next seven days, and the last 30 days' cash flow.
- **Accounts** (from Money 2): **Move money** (**Transfer**, **Pay a card or loan**, **Cash out**, **Cash in**, and from Money 3 **Lend money**), every account you hold or share, the cards you hold on others' accounts, **Activity** with its filters, and **How you pay**. An account's page has its balance, limit, what is due, its interest (from Money 3), its owners, cards and lines, with **Pay**, **Transfer**, **Autopay** and, for a card, **Change** (**Freeze**, **Unfreeze**, **Report lost**, **Replace**). **Income and spending** opens your statements for a period.
- **In & out** (from Money 3): **Income** (each job, its pay, payslip and deductions, **Pay goes to**, **Quit**), **Standing payments** (**Active**, **Paused**, **Ended**, and **New standing payment or one-off**, with **Edit**, **Pause**, **Resume** and **Delete** on each), **Invoices to pay**, your **Household**'s share, and **Autopay**.
- **Assets**: what your net worth is made of (full books), **Investments** (each holding with **Performance**, **Buy more**, **Sell**, **The market** and **Buy something new**, from Money 4), **Property you own** and **For sale** (**Let it**, **End the lease**, **Sell**, **Buy** with a mortgage), your stakes in organizations, and your vehicles.
- **Credit** (Money 4): your score from 300 to 850 and the credit you could apply for, each opening its **Apply** page.

Each page opens over the page with a back button. A control's page has you fill it in, then press its one button, which says what it does (**Pay** *amount*, **Start the lease**); while something is missing, the button waits and the page says why. Every one is done at once (see [What a press does](#what-a-press-does)), but buying something in a shop, which waits for your next message. A business's or your household's money is on its page in [Orgs](#orgs).

### People

**People** lists **Here** and then **People you know**: each with their role, what they are doing or when and where you last saw them, how they feel about you, and any life state but alive. **Filter and group** groups and filters them by the classifications the world lets players see, by group and by tier. **Your groups** lists the organizations you belong to.

A person's sheet shows where they are, or **Last seen** (from Cast life 2); their **Next** commitment; **Today**, a 24-hour timeline of where their routine and commitments put them, with **Why this plan?**; **Their usual day**, where each block comes from as far as you may know (a private block reads **Busy**); **How they see you**; **What they're after**, their public agendas; their **Organizations**; and **Known as**. Their day is what they would usually do. What the story establishes comes first, and nobody moves by their routine until an agent records it (see [Time, needs and routines](#time-needs-and-routines)).

**Where are they?** corrects where someone is, as ✎ on your own place does: search a place by any word of its name or browse from the top, among the places you may know. It is done at once, with Undo.

### Orgs

**Orgs** is every organization your character belongs to or knows of: your household, the company you work for or own, a group, a trust, and your party. Members, positions, titles, powers and delegation show at every Money level; an organization's money, payroll, ownership and taxes show at its Money level, as yours do. Every control on its pages is done at once, with **Tell** and **Don't tell** and **Undo** (see [What a press does](#what-a-press-does)).

**The home**: **Your party** (who is in it, here and away, and its **Purse**); the organizations you belong to, as a tree (**Households**, **Companies**, **Groups**, **Trusts**), each row with the one figure you may see and your title there, nested by who holds what, four levels deep with **Show from here**; **Known organizations**; and **Start something…**: **Start a company**, **Settle a trust** and **Start a nonprofit** (from Money 3), **Start a group** and **Name your party**.

**An organization's page** has its name, a badge for what it is ("Company · LLC", "Household", "Party") and your title there. **⋯** holds **Rename** where you may govern it, **Leave** where you belong by a link of your own, and **Open in GM**, which opens it on the World page behind the spoiler gate. Its tabs, those it has and you may see:

| Tab | What it shows |

**Powers.** What anyone may do for an organization is one of its 21 powers, in seven families: **Members**, **Staff**, **Money**, **Things**, **Property**, **Ownership** and **Governance**. A power rests on an owner's role, a position, a delegation, what the organization's type gives a member, or control through an organization that holds this one. A position or a delegation may carry limits (so much a payment, so much a month). An act over your limit can be put to those above you for approval (**Ask for approval to pay**, in **Pay as…**), and waits under **Decisions**.

**Acting for an organization.** Where you may spend for an organization, **Pay** and **Buy** in the actions sheet offer **Paying as**, and its **Money** tab has **Pay as…**.

**The party.** Your party is the people who travel with you, each in a tier: **Core** and **Companion** come along wherever you go (a companion unless their own commitment is due); **Hired** and **Ally** come only when you take them. Its page lists them by tier. As its leader you have **Add someone you know**, **Change a tier**, **Name the party**, **Start a purse** (from Money 2), **Share out** and **Hire a hand** (from Money 3).

### Story

**Story** has four tabs.

- **Quests**: quests, puzzles, secrets and agendas, sorted into **Active**, **Done** and **Failed**, each with its stage and objectives. A quest's sheet shows where it stands, **Stages gone by** (each stage the quest has been moved past, with whether it was done or failed and why, as the Author gave it), its objectives, what you have learned, and its hints, which stay closed until you open them one at a time (**Show a hint**, then **Show another hint**); opening one is done at once, with Undo. Only what you know is shown. To answer a puzzle, say it in the chat. The quests as the Author keeps them, secrets and all, are on the [Quests page](#places-and-quests).
- **What you know**: the facts your character holds, newest first, while the chat tracks who knows what (**Track who knows what**, `secrets.tracked`, on as shipped): each with how you came by it (**Told by** someone, **You saw it**, **From the start**, **You worked it out**, **A guess**) and since when, and badges for a suspicion, an open question or what you make of it.
- **Journal**: what each turn recorded that your character may know, newest first, and what took no effect and why, filtered by **All**, **Notes & montages** or **Not applied**. It reads the newest 100 turns at a time (**Turns the Journal reads at a time**, `taps.journalPage`). **Save to lore…** keeps the latest reply, or a part of the chat you select or paste, as a lore entry in the world's draft in Build.
- **Calendar**: the date and the clock, today's holidays and what is coming up, with **Pause clock** or **Resume clock**.

### The map

The map is **Places**. Its bar holds the **breadcrumbs**, **List of places** and **Full screen**. Drag to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom, or use **Zoom in**, **Zoom out** and **Fit map**. The map shows one place at a time, with the places inside it and the routes between them, and opens where you are; the breadcrumbs lead out a level at a time, up to **World**.

Tap a place for its sheet: whether you are here, have been or have only heard of it, and **Getting there**, each way with its mode, time, vehicle and cost, or why there is none. Choosing a way waits for your next message. Where you are, the sheet adds **Things here**, and your own things there have Belongings' controls. **Parked here** lists vehicles, with **Drive…** and **Park here**; **Property here** has **Buy…** and **Live there**; **Make this a property** follows the world's claims policy.

**Known** and **Fog** choose how the map shows what your character knows, for this chat: **Known** draws a place you have only heard of as a dashed ring; **Fog** dims what you have not been to and covers what you have not seen. Your choice is the chat's setting **How the map panel shows the map** (`map.display`).

Where the world uses the grid system, you stand on a square: tap a square of your grid and its card offers **Walk here** with the time it takes, around the walls and through the doors, or says "No way there from here."; the walk waits for your next message. Walls and doors are drawn as thin lines; a place you know may carry a mark (**Home**, **Yours**, **For sale**), named in the legend under **The map shows**.

In the chat, **World map** and **Map** open the same map over the chat.

## Time, needs and routines

A chat has one clock. With a world, it runs on the world's calendar, and the HUD, the scene card and **Story → Calendar** show it. A chat with no world has a clock with no date until the story sets one: nobody chose its start, so no date is shown, and times are given as how long from now.

**Time passes when an agent passes it.** The Author, or the GM it asks, passes the time the story took: a conversation is minutes, a meal an hour, a night's sleep a night. In a fight on a room grid, time moves in rounds of a few seconds. Nothing moves the clock by itself between turns, and a turn takes no set time of its own.

**What time brings due.** When time passes, the engine lists what fell due in that span near the scene: where people's routines now put them, who arrives, a need that crossed its point, an effect that ended, something the Author scheduled, a payment, a change in the weather or in prices. It applies none of it. Whoever runs the turn decides each item (it happened as scheduled, differently, or not at all) and records what happened. An item nobody decides did not happen: someone listed at school at 8:00 is still at home. For a long skip, the Author asks ahead what will come due, passes the time a stretch at a time, says what the people off stage did meanwhile, and records totals for the routine remainder (meals, wages, rent). All of this is written in the shipped skill `time` (`.skills/time/SKILL.md`), which you can change like any file.

**Your controls.**

- **Pause clock** and **Resume clock** (in the World details, the actions sheet, the composer's action bar and **Story → Calendar**) pause the clock for the scene. The pause is a mark, not a lock: an agent that moves the clock while it is paused is told so, and the move stands.
- **✎ on the time** sets the clock (see [Fixing a value](#fixing-a-value)). Moving it on decides nothing about what the time in between brought: that is the Author's, at the next turn.
- **Timelapse**, in the actions sheet in the chat: **For** (lengths, with **Custom**), **Until** (the next morning or another part of the day, your next shift, the end of a trip, a date), **Follow my routine** for your own character, and **Interrupts for this run**. What you pick goes with your next message: the Author is told the length or the target and your choices, and plays the stretch of time.

### Needs

From Cast life 4, people get hungry, tired, lonely and bored: hunger, fatigue, social and fun, each from 0 (met) to 100 (urgent). They rise with the clock. They come down only when the engine records what met them, one of the world's actions: **eat** for hunger, **sleep** for fatigue, **chat_with** for social, **rest** for fun. Passing the minutes of a meal does not meet hunger. As shipped, the `time` skill tells the agents to record a meal, a night's sleep or a rest with its action, so that the need comes down.

**Me → Needs** shows your own. A need that crosses its point is listed among what falls due. A world sets each need in **Build → Needs**: how fast it grows (**Grows by … points an hour, out of 100**), the point it is pressing from (the field reads **Told to the narrator from …**), and its alert point (**An alert from …**), with an **On** or **Off** for each and **Use the defaults**. Anyone can have a **bed** (the person's **Bed** field in Build): **sleep** takes them to it.

### Routines and schedules

A **routine** is what someone can be relied on to do on an ordinary day: opening the shop at seven, a walk by the lake on a fair weekend. A **schedule** is a commitment with set hours, such as a shift or school. A world's routines and schedules are written in Build (see [Routines in Build](#routines-in-build)).

They are defaults, never a script. They show what someone would usually do: on a person's sheet in **People** (**Today**, **Their usual day**), and among what falls due when time passes. Nobody moves by their routine until an agent records the move. What the story established always comes first.

An agent can change one character's routine in a chat (the tool `gm_routine_edit`), as a change of that turn, without touching the world.

## Realism

Realism is how closely a world keeps each part of life. It is set per **domain**, each at a level from 0 to 4: **Off**, **Narrative**, **Simple**, **Detailed** or **Simulation**. The domains are **Money**, **Gear**, **Vehicles**, **Cast life**, **Relationships**, **Space & travel**, **Calendar & time**, **Weather** and **Sheets & checks**.

A higher level keeps a domain more exactly, and the play views show and list more of it: Money 1 is a wealth band, Money 4 the full books; Space 2 is places and routes, Space 3 adds grids and positions. A level never decides whether a change is applied: every write is applied, and every report made, at every level. The world state the Author is given each turn opens with the chat's realism, level by level.

**Presets.** A world's realism starts from a preset:

| Preset | Money | Gear | Vehicles | Cast life | Relationships | Space | Calendar | Weather | Sheets | Band caps (Leads / Supporting / Background) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Story** | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 1 | none |
| **Grounded** | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 2 | 2 | none / none / 1 |
| **Simulation** | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 4 / 3 / 2 |

Moving any level yourself makes it **Custom**.

**Tiers and bands.** Not everyone is kept as closely as your own character. Every character has a **tier**, one of the world's own (**Build → Tiers**), and each tier belongs to a **band**: **Leads**, **Supporting** or **Background**. A band can cap each domain, and a character is kept at the lower of the domain's level and their band's cap. Your own character is never capped.

**Where it is set.** A world sets its realism in **Build → Realism defaults**, and a chat takes its realism from its world as it starts. This build has no control that changes one running chat's levels.

**Things you say you have.** The world's claims policy (**Build → Realism defaults**, **Player claims**, once **This world sets what new chats make of them** is on) decides what the play views offer when you add something: **Make it real** adds it at once, **Ask first** asks before it is added, and **Hold me to the world** hides **+ Add**. **Ask first above the world's claim cap** makes something priced over the cap ask first under Make it real. In what you type, a thing you say you have is the Author's to weigh, with `SIMULATION.md` and the lore.

## How checks are decided

A **check** is the dice an action of the world rolls: a stat or a skill against a difficulty, with outcomes such as success and failure (see [Checks, pools, progression and sheets](#checks-pools-progression-and-sheets)). In RPE the game master rules by the world's actions and checks. They are what it rules with, not gates in code.

- **Who rolls.** As shipped, the GM rolls when an outcome is uncertain and it matters, so that chance decides it, and says what was rolled and why (`SIMULATION.md`, "Dice"). The Author asks the GM when something is contested, costly or risky.
- **How.** The GM carries out one of the world's actions with the operation `act`, naming the outcome it ruled, or leaving it out: then an action of one outcome takes it, and an action with a check has its check rolled by the world's rules. The tool `roll` rolls dice or one of the world's checks for someone, records the roll with what it was for, and gives its id, which a later change can cite as its source. `checks_odds` reads an action's odds and changes nothing.
- **Requirements advise.** An action whose requirement does not hold (you would need something you do not have) is carried out all the same, and the answer says which requirement did not hold. What to do about it is written in `SIMULATION.md`: as shipped, apply it and let the report stand, so that it can be put right later. To make the rules bind, change that paragraph.

**The world's rules for checks.** A world can set how its checks are decided in **Build → Ruleset**, under **How checks are decided**: when a check rolls (every check, only risky ones, only where marked, or never), whether sure things pass and hopeless tries fail without a roll, who settles a check (the roll, the roll with close calls, or the story), and when a failed check may be retried. Its presets are **Narrative-first**, **Balanced** and **Crunchy**; **Custom** sets each value. These rules apply wherever a check is rolled by the world's rules: an `act` with no outcome, and the odds the actions sheet shows.

**What you see.** The actions sheet shows each action's odds before you pick it, and what the world's rules will make of it. After the turn, the turn's page lists every roll and ruling in its engine lane, behind the spoiler gate.

## Swipes, edits, deletes and branches

A **turn** is one reply attempt. Every change RPE records belongs to the turn that made it, and RPE's record of the chat follows the messages Lumiverse shows. At the start of every turn, RPE compares the chat as Lumiverse has it (which messages, which swipe shows, what each says) with its own record, and tells the Author what it found.

### Swipes and regenerates

A swipe or a regenerate is another attempt at the same turn: a new turn beside the earlier one, which starts from the record as it was before the earlier attempt. The Author reads `SWIPE.md` with `TURN.md`. As shipped, it keeps what was decided (the outcomes, the rolls, what the characters did) and tells it differently, and does not repeat the earlier attempt's opening, structure or key images. The earlier attempts are in the turn's folder, under `previous-attempts/`, each with its reply, its intent record and anything you typed about why.

- The composer's **Swipe** control asks for one way: **another-telling** or **afresh** (the turn run again from the start, with new rolls), with a line of what was wrong (see [The composer](#the-composer-depth-and-swipe)).
- What you asked for with the turn you swipe (its depth, a press in the play views) is asked again of the new attempt.
- What you record belongs to the attempt you keep. Show another swipe on the newest reply, and that attempt's record is the chat's, its own changes intact.
- Flip an earlier reply to another of its swipes, with later replies still after it, and the later turns are kept and their changes applied again on top of the swipe now showing. A change that no longer applies there is listed, never dropped silently: the next briefing tells the Author which stretch to look over, and the world state lists what no longer applies.

### Edits

- **A reply you edit** in Lumiverse: the turn is marked **edited**, and at the next turn the Author is told the text before and after. The text that stands is what happened, and the Author settles both lanes against it. **Settle now** has it do that at once (below).
- **A message of yours you edit**, with turns after it: the later turns are marked "an earlier message edited", and the next briefing names the stretch after it, for the Author to look over.
- **A reply Lumiverse saved differently** from what RPE released is marked **saved otherwise**.

How much the briefing gives of edited replies is **Edited replies in the briefing** (`briefing.editedTokens`): every one in full, as shipped.

### Deletes

Delete a message and its turns leave the chat's story: everything they recorded goes with them (the world's changes, perceptions, memories, beats, the Author's records and notes), and the record is as it was before them. The next turn's briefing names the turns that are gone, and the Author reads `DELETE.md`: it does not refer to what was deleted, checks the scene, and takes your new message on its own terms.

A deleted turn stays on the Turns page, marked **off the path**, as does an attempt you swiped away from.

### Continue and impersonate

**Continue** extends the same turn: the Author adds to what it recorded and does not start over. The Turns page shows the turn as continued, and **Where a continue went on from**. **Impersonate** has the Author write as your character, briefly, in their voice, and change nothing in the record.

### Branches

A branch in Lumiverse is a new chat whose turns point back into the chat it was branched from, so the history they share is shared, not copied. Turns played in the branch are its own. Until the branch has a turn of its own, what would write to its newest turn (a strike on the Cast page, Settle now) is refused, because it would go into the other chat's history too.

### A stretch played without RPE

Replies written while the chat was on another connection, after RPE's first turn in it, are a stretch played without RPE: the Author is told the range, and as shipped it records what matters of it, in passing or as a job. The line under each of those replies says **Made without RPE.**

### The Turns page

**Turns** lists every turn RPE has played in the chat, newest first: what it was (**New reply**, **Swipe**, **Regenerate**, **Continue**, **Impersonation**, **Settle run**), when, how long it took, its calls and tokens, its marks, and how it ended (**Reply released**, **No reply**, **Stopped**, **Stopped at a limit**, **Failed**). A turn the migration carried is marked **carried**, and opens to what the old engine recorded.

A turn's own page has its sections, those behind the spoiler gate shown once you open it:

- **Marks on this turn**, **Timings**, **Tokens**, **Its runs** and **What went to the chat**;
- **What you asked for**: your depth, your swipe's way and feedback, what you pressed in the play views;
- **What the Author was given** (gated): the prompt by part, its lore, the turn's files, and what each agent's first call was sent;
- **The prompt's front, and what changed in it**, and **A reply asked for whole**, where one was;
- **Intent record**, **The Author's record of the turn**, **Tokens by agent** and **The run, step by step** (gated);
- **Changes by lane**, and **The changes, lane by lane** (gated), each with who made it, how sure it is and where it came from;
- **The review card** (gated), for writes that waited for your word (see [Who may write what](#who-may-write-what));
- **Who perceived the turn**;
- **Quests it touched**, **Threads it left open**, **What is still open** and **Unsettled**, what did not add up (gated);
- **Gap checks, consents and what it was told** (gated): each gap check and what came back, each consent, each rule that fired.

A turn whose reply ended empty says **Its empty reply is still in your chat**, with the setting that would take such replies away.

### Settle now

**Settle now**, on the Turns page, on the page of the chat's newest turn, and under a reply whose record was not finished, runs the Author on the chat's newest turn against the chat as it reads now. Use it after you edit a reply, or when a turn's run stopped before it recorded everything. It writes no reply and adds nothing to your chat; its run shows on the turn's page as a **Settle run**. It waits while a turn of the chat runs ("Settle it once that turn is over"), and is refused where the chat has no turn of RPE's, or where its newest turn is shared with the chat it was branched from.

## The world's record, and who may see what

RPE keeps four records for a chat: the world's state, the places and quests, each character's record, and the story record. The Author reads them at every turn, and the agents write them through their tools as the turn's changes, so a swipe or a delete takes them back with the turn. You can read each on its page and correct it, as your own change, which the Author is told of at the next turn.

What the records hold can give away what comes next: secrets, the Author's plans, what a character knows that yours does not. So most of each page stays behind the [spoiler gate](#rpes-pages) until you open it for the chat. What your own character knows is on the [Play page](#the-play-page), which shows only that.

### World

**World** shows the chat's world as one tree, at the chat's newest turn or as it stood at any turn: the world, its places, the people and things in them, their groups. Open any level to any depth (**Fewer levels**, **More levels**, **Up a level**, **The whole world**). Each value says where it comes from (its own, inherited from a level above, or kept by a game system) and which change last wrote it; facts recorded on the same key stand beside it and never overwrite it. The whole page is behind the spoiler gate.

- **History** reads a value's changes turn by turn.
- **Change** and **Remove** change a value as your own edit, with **How exact** and **Tell the Author at the next turn**, and **Undo**. **Add a value or fact** adds one to a level.
- **Systems** shows what each game system keeps at a level (money, gear, a person's sheet), as the system keeps it.
- **What the time brought due** shows, where it concerns, what a turn's time listed.
- After you flip an earlier reply to another swipe, the page lists the later changes that no longer apply.
- The things browser stands below the tree: who has what and where, a thing's page with its history, and **Tidy up**, which finds holds that cannot be (a sofa on someone, a coat worn and lying elsewhere) and offers the fix for each, or **Leave it**. Nothing is fixed by itself. Whether each turn's world state lists such holds is **Tidy-up findings in the world state** (`things.tidy`), which follows the world's own switch until you set it. **Keep in the world** copies what the chat made in play into the world's draft in Build.

### Places and quests

**Places** lists every place of the chat, the world's own and those made in play, searchable a page at a time. A place opened shows its record, now or as it stood at a turn, in nine parts: **What defines it** (the lore entries bound to it), **Its base** (which you can write over with **Write it as your base**), **What the story changed there since**, **Its features**, who and what is here, its ways, who may enter and its doors, its history and who knows of it. Its grid, where it has one, shows everyone and everything on it: pick a person or a thing, then tap a square, to move them there at once, with Undo. A grid the chat made has **Keep in the world**. The records are read only while the gate is open.

**Quests** shows every quest, secret, puzzle and agenda as the Author keeps it, behind the gate: what it is, its stages with what moves each on, who and where, its stakes and rewards, how much of it you are shown, and its history. Outside the gate, **What you see in your journal** shows each quest as far as it lets you see: the whole quest, only its name and the stages named, or nothing.

### Cast

**Cast** lists every character the chat knows, with its casting: **principal**, **supporting** or **extra**. A principal is run as an agent of its own when the turn needs it. Opened, behind the gate, a character's whole record:

- **Base**: who they are, drawn from their sources (the card, lore entries), with **Growth** laid over it as the story changes them; each source marked current, changed, not drawn from yet, or gone.
- **Mind**: what is on their mind now, by kind.
- **Memories**, by strength: a memory fades over story days unless it matters or is recalled, and a faint one shows as its gist. As shipped, when a principal sleeps or days pass for it, the Author has its agent dream, which settles what it lived into long-term memory. The settings under **Characters and their memories** set how fast (`cast.fadeDays`, `cast.topOfMind` and the rest).
- **Relationships** (trust, warmth, respect, fear), **Knowledge**, and **History** with its beats.
- **Perceptions**: what reached each character of each moment it was on stage for. Every principal and supporting character on stage gets one; extras do not. A character's memories and beliefs are built from them, by its own agent. As shipped, the rule `perceptions` says who gets one and what goes in it, and the rules `perceptions-owed` and `perceptions-missing` remind the Author of one that is missing.
- **Game side**: the world's person it is; **Away**: while a principal is off stage, where it is and what it means to do; and the mind files the migration carried from the previous build.

You can strike an entry and take the strike back; write a perception for a turn the character was on stage in and has none of; set or pin its casting ("it stands over the Author's casting until you take the pin away"); redraw its base; bind a source; and name the world's person it is. Each is your own change, and the page says what the cast did with it. Whether your own character has a mind of its own is **Your character has a mind** (`cast.playerMind`, off as shipped).

### Story

**Story** shows the story record as the Author keeps it, behind the gate: the **Timeline** (chapters, scenes and beats in the chat's order, each beat beside the message it stands for); **What the record covers** of the chat, with every stretch it does not cover or has beats for and no summary of; **The story so far, as the Author is given it** for what lies beyond the window; and the parts of the story model: **Premise**, **Threads**, **Arcs**, **You, as the Author reads you**, **Canon**, **Retcons**, **The Author's records**, the **Horizon** and the **Futures**. You can strike or correct any item, as your own change.

The story record stands in for the history the window leaves out: the Author keeps it ahead of the window, so that what leaves the prompt is still known. The briefing's story so far takes a share of the Author's window (**The story so far: share of the window**, `story.soFar.share`, at least `story.soFar.minTokens`). **Hold uncovered messages** (`history.hold.on`, off as shipped) keeps a message in the prompt until the record covers it. Above the record, the Story page has the chat's **Bootstrap** (see [Jobs and sessions](#jobs-and-sessions)).

### Secrets

A secret is something kept from some characters, or known only to some. You mark it in a lore entry's own text:

```
{{// Secret: The lighthouse keeper is the smuggler.}}
{{// Secret (known to Ada Lorne): The key is under the third step.}}
{{// Secret (kept from you): Tobin has already sold the boat.}}
```

Lumiverse strips every mark from a prompt by itself. In a chat that plays a world, RPE puts each one back into the prompt, marked as a secret with who holds it, unless what the prompt is cut to leaves it out: **Narrator's knowledge** (`secrets.narrator.knowledge`: `scene`, everyone in the scene, or `pov`, your character only) and **Narrator's secrets** (`secrets.narrator.secrets`: `marked`, or `omit`, left out). A secret your character has come to hold goes in as theirs. When RPE cannot read the chat's state, every secret stays out.

A character holds a secret once the story gives it to them (told, found out, shown) and an agent records it with `perceive`. **Track who knows what** (`secrets.tracked`) and **How sure a holding must be** (`secrets.threshold`) decide when a holding counts. Build has tools to mark secrets in your books and to move an older build's secrecy into marks (see [Secrecy in your World Info](#secrecy-in-your-world-info)).

### Who may write what

Each lane's writes go ahead, wait for your word, or are left out, by the **Write permission** settings: **the story lane** (`permissions.story`), **the engine lane** (`permissions.engine`), and **tools that reach outside RPE** (`permissions.external`: the web, a command, an MCP server). Each is `auto` (go ahead) as shipped; `confirm` makes each write wait for your word; `never` leaves it out.

A write that waits shows under its turn's reply with **Go ahead** and **No**, and on the turn's page under **The review card**: each change by lane with **Undo it**, and **Questions asked on it** with **Let it go ahead** and **Do not**.

## Lore

RPE uses your lore; it does not replace it. Lore comes from two places: **your books** in Lumiverse (every book the chat reads, or the ones **Books the lore reads**, `lore.books`, names), and **the world's own lore**, the entries the world's document holds, which Build writes and **Save to lore…** adds to. An id says which an entry is (`e-112` a book entry, `w-31` a world entry). Lumiverse still puts your activated entries into the prompt as it always does.

**The lore map.** At the start of every turn, the Author's briefing maps the lore: each entry activated in the prompt, with what it is and what it defines; entries the story has since moved past, with the note; the world's own entries whose keys match the recent chat; and, up to 12 (**Lore map, bound entries**, `lore.index.bound`), entries bound to who and what is on the horizon that did not activate. The agents read any entry in full, and search every book the chat reads and the world's lore, when they need more.

**The index.** The **Lore** page shows your books and the world's lore as RPE understands them: the index, a page at a time and searchable, each entry with its book, its kind, a line saying what it is, what it defines and the records it is bound to. An entry no indexing run has read yet shows its title and keys. The lines come from an indexing job: **Index now** starts one, on the connection the indexer's definition names (its `model:` is `default`, your default connection, unless you name another); the Author starts one when its briefing says entries are not indexed; and an entry whose lines were written for an earlier revision is marked **out of date**, with **Index again**. You can set an entry's kind, its line and its related entries yourself; each is yours from then on, over any run's, with a way back to the run's.

**Where the story moved past an entry.** When a turn leaves an entry behind (the inn it describes has burned), the Author writes an **overlay**: a note tied to that turn, which the lore map gives from then on. The entry itself is not touched. The Lore page lists each overlay on the chat's path with its turn.

**Proposals.** RPE never writes your books by itself. When something the story made belongs in your books, an agent drafts the entry or the edit and offers it (the skill `lore-proposals`). **Proposals waiting on you** shows each one with the passage as it stands beside what would replace it: **Approve**, **Approve with my edits** (its book, title, keys and text are yours to change first) or **Reject**. **What RPE wrote to your lore** lists every write it made, newest first, each with **Put it back**, after a question that says what that does. A write checks that the entry is as it was read, and keeps a copy of what it was.

**This chat's projection book.** Off as shipped. Switched on (**Projection book**, `lore.projection`, for this chat), RPE keeps a book of the chat's own, attached to it, that carries what the story changed and made into Lumiverse's own activation: notes on your entries, the people it brought in, the canon it set down. It is rewritten after scenes and when the chat's path changes, and a branch gets its own. **Apply now** makes or rewrites it at once, or takes it away when it is off. Your own books are never touched.

## Model use

LumiRPEngine does most of its work without the AI. When it does ask a model, it uses small, focused calls, each with its own job. **Model use** presets set which steps may call a model, when, and how many calls a phase may spend. **Settings → Model use** shows them as four cards, **Lean**, **Standard**, **Full** and **Custom**, each saying in plain words what the preset does and then its limits; tap a card to use that preset, and the one in use is marked **In use**.

| Preset | Before the reply | After the reply | Model steps beyond the narrator: most in one turn | Budgets (calls before / after) | Lore lookups (before / after) | Engine budget (sections) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Lean** | Reads every message you type | Reads the reply | 2 (reading your text, reading the reply) | 1 / 1 | none | 2,000 tokens |
| **Standard** (the default) | Reads every message you type; judges your approach when the rules for checks need it; asks about NPC choices on close calls and invitations | Reads the reply; NPC choices off screen on close calls | 4 (reading your text, judging your approach or an NPC choice with you, an NPC choice elsewhere, reading the reply) | 2 / 2 | 4 / 3 | 8,000 tokens |
| **Full** | Reads every message you type, judges your approach when needed, decides NPC choices for everyone present, writes a brief and a prose outline | Reads the reply, decides off-screen NPC choices | 8 (adds the brief and the prose outline, and has room for judging your approach, an NPC choice with you and a puzzle answer in one turn; an answer has no approach to judge, so a turn runs at most 7) | 8 / 4 | 6 / 4 | 12,000 tokens |
| **Custom** | Any step, any trigger, any connection and model | Same | Depends on the steps you enable; the call meter shows it live | Yours | Yours | Yours |

The number is the most steps a turn can run: each step takes one call from its phase's budget when it starts, so a phase never runs more steps than its budget allows. A step can cost more calls than that: each lore lookup is one more (from its phase's lookup budget), and a JSON repair, a Decision call falling back to the helper, or `interpret` asking again after a dropped connection adds one that no budget is charged for. Most turns cost less, because a step runs only when its trigger fires. The call meter counts every engine model call, not the narrator, so it shows each turn's real count. **Lean** is the floor. It keeps the cost down and leaves NPC choices to the engine's own rules rather than a model. Standard is where a model starts deciding close calls.

### The steps

| Step | When it runs | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `interpret` (Interpret typed input) | Before the reply | Reads every typed message as the actions it takes, in order (up to five play in the turn), including a request to let time pass, from what your character can do now, the scene and the messages before (see [Typed text](#typed-text)); what the turn doesn't play is a chip for your next message. |
| `npc.choose` (NPC choices) | Before the reply for people who are with you; after the reply for people elsewhere | Picks what an NPC does on a close call or invitation, from a list the engine enumerates (always including "do nothing"). |
| `puzzle.answer` (Puzzle answers) | Before the reply | Judges a free-text puzzle answer, only for puzzles whose author opted in; the answer is a probability against a threshold. It runs unless you turn it off, on every answer while its Signals are empty. |
| `judge` (Judge your approach) | Before the reply | Rates how well you describe your approach to an action's check, with one line on why, and when the story decides gives each outcome's odds. It runs only when the world's rules for checks need it; see [How checks are decided](#how-checks-are-decided). Off in Lean. |
| `brief` (Scene brief), `outline` (Prose outline) | Before the reply, once the engine has planned the turn | Write a short briefing or a prose outline for the narrator. The engine always writes a deterministic outline from its own beats; the brief and the prose outline stack on top of it in the Outline section. |
| `extract` (Extract changes) | After the reply | Reads the reply for changes. See [Extraction modes](#extraction-modes). |

| `choices` (Suggested choices) | After the reply | Offers next-step options as **Suggested choices** chips. |
| `mind` (Character minds) | Before the reply, and at time-skip beats | One call per character, as that character: their monologue, depiction, intent and reactions. Runs on its own cadence; see [Character minds](#character-minds). |
| `memory` (Character memory) | After the reply | Per character who heard your message, at the levels you pick: the notes and commitments they take from what the reply said, and the plans it states. |
| `skip` (Time-skip beats) | Before a time skip, once you turn it on (it is off in every preset) | Proposes further moments in the skip worth stopping at, beside the ones the engine finds. |
| `consolidate` (Memory consolidation) | After the reply | Folds a character's faded and surplus notes into their summary. |
| `knowledge` (Who knows what) | After the reply, once its reading is in, while the chat tracks who knows what (minds on or off) | One call for the scene, or one per character (Settings → Minds → Knowledge): who learned what by seeing or being told it, what someone worked out and how sure, and what someone now doubts. The engine checks it: no one sees what they weren't there for, and whoever tells something must know it. |

A step runs when one of its **signals** fires (in **Settings → Steps and connections**, under each step's **Advanced: signals and lore**). `always` fires every turn; `never` turns the step off and wins over the rest. The others:

| Signal | Fires when |
|---|---|
| `typed_input` | The turn's text was typed rather than chosen. A step saved on the retired `classifier` signal runs on this. |
| `reply` | A reply exists (after the reply). |
| `scene_change`, `slot_crossed`, `day_start` | You changed place; the turn crossed a part of the day; it crossed midnight. |
| `beat` | The plan has outline beats. |
| `agents_present`, `present`, `offscreen`, `tie_zone` | Agents are with you; a close call is open for someone with you; for someone elsewhere; for the people this phase decides for. |
| `invitation`, `timelapse`, `conflict` | An invitation was made; the turn was a Timelapse; your action failed, was declined or rolled a failing band. |
| `every_n:k` | Every *k*th assistant turn. |

Prose steps (`brief`, `outline`, `choices`) are shown only what your character could see: the facts they get are the player-visible ones, and any line that would give away an unrevealed truth or a secret lore entry's text is dropped before the call. A question that carries a secret never shares a call with prose.

### The helper model and connections

Every model call the engine makes for itself (reading your actions and the reply, NPC choices, judging, the brief and outline, minds and their memory, time-skip beats, lore lookups) runs on the **helper model**, set in **Settings → Helper model**, the first page under **Getting started**:

- **Connection**: any of your Lumiverse connections. **None** runs each call on the connection Lumiverse marks as your default, with its own model and settings.
- **Model**: for a connection you picked, its model list (or type a name); empty uses the connection's own model.
- **Reasoning effort**: sent with each call as Lumiverse's own reasoning setting, which Lumiverse turns into the provider's field (DeepSeek reads Low to High as High, and Max and Extra high as Max). **Connection's own**, the default, leaves the connection's reasoning setting as it is, and **Off** turns reasoning off.
- **Max tokens**: 8,192 by default, with a warning below 4,096. Reasoning models think before they answer, and the thinking counts toward the limit, so a smaller one can leave the answer empty.
- **Temperature**, **Top P**, **Top K**, **Frequency penalty** and **Presence penalty**: each is **the connection's own** (its preset's value) until you switch it off and set a number. Lumiverse passes each one you set to the provider, which uses those it supports.

Picking a preset never changes the helper model. The Decisions API takes no effort, max tokens or sampling parameters, so steps on it run with their connection's own settings.

Each step can set its own, in **Settings → Steps and connections**, which opens with a **Helper model** row that leads to the page above. Each step is a card that shows what it runs on: *Helper*, the connection, the model, the effort, the max tokens and any parameter set; or *Decision* and its connection; or **Off**. Open it for a line on what the step does, **Enabled**, a **Provider** (*Decision* or *Helper*), a **Connection** and, for a connection you picked, a **Model**; then for a helper step **Reasoning effort**, **Max tokens** and, under **Sampling parameters**, each parameter. Each starts at **Use helper default**, and the card shows the helper model's value beside it; whatever the step sets wins over the helper model's, one by one. The exceptions are **Character memory**, **Memory consolidation** and **Time-skip beats** (which also plans a time skip), whose **Reasoning effort** starts at **Off**: their answers are short notes on what happened or is due, and a reasoning model could spend the whole of its max tokens thinking about one and answer nothing, leaving the reply's reading to wait half a minute and fail. Set any effort on their cards, **Use helper default** included, and they reason again. Every step but the minds steps has **Recent chat**: **Reads recent chat**, and while it's on how many of the messages before this turn's it reads (**Messages**) and about how many tokens of them (**Tokens**), each a number or **no limit**, the newest first, stopping at whichever limit comes first; the step gets them, oldest first, as a "Recent chat" block after its own input. *Interpret typed input* reads 8 messages and 3,000 tokens unless you change it; every other step reads none until you switch it on. Under **Advanced: signals and lore** are its **Signals** and lore. Nothing is cut or simplified because a model is small; each default is a tap to change. Picking **Lean**, **Standard** or **Full** in **Settings → Model use** replaces every step's settings with the preset's, connections and models included, so pick the preset first; changing a step afterwards makes it **Custom** and keeps the rest.

Each card also has a **Prompt**: your own wording for the step, which wins over the world's step template for it (Build → Step templates), and that over the built-in. A line under its name says what it does to the step, as a world template does: for reading your typed input and reading the reply it is added before the built-in instructions, which it can't change, so its box starts empty (**None added**); for the brief, the outline, suggested choices, NPC choices, puzzle answers and judging your approach it replaces the built-in instructions or question, and its box starts from that text. Below it, folded and read-only, is the built-in: the text a prompt replaces, or, for the two that add to it, what the built-in instructions hold, since they're made fresh each turn from the scene. The answer format and the leak checks never change. **Save** keeps it, **Revert** drops your edits, and **Reset to the default** goes back to the world's template or the built-in. A world's `effects` templates still run beside your prompt. The minds steps' prompts are the same ones as in **Settings → Minds**; *Time-skip beats* has two, its beats' and **The skip plan**'s, the call that plans how the people the model plans spend a time skip. Picking a **Model use** preset keeps every prompt of yours. Beside each prompt, **Placeholders** lists the `{{vars}}` that step is given, when it runs, and what each holds; Build's step templates show the same list, and name each purpose and output in words. A new step template isn't in the draft until **Create**: leaving its page before then asks first.

Each step's card also says which roles of an agent chat run where it does ("On agent chats, Intent (reading your message) runs where this step does."), and which of those roles set a connection of their own. **Settings → Helper model** says the same of the roles whose tasks run on it: those whose step and role set no connection.

By default, the helper model handles `interpret`, `extract`, the prose steps and the minds, and your **Decision connection** (Lumiverse's Decisions API) handles `npc.choose`. The Decisions API takes no reasoning effort or max tokens, so a Decision step runs with its connection's own settings. If a decision call fails, the step falls back to the helper model, and then to the engine alone. A failed call is never blindly retried, and neither a repair of malformed JSON nor a decision falling back to the helper is charged against a budget. The model and connection you pick for a step are the ones its call uses.

**Preview before playing**, at the top of **Settings → Steps and connections**, lets you look at a turn before it plays. Both switches start off.

- **Show the action read from your message before it plays.** When the engine reads an action or a time skip from what you typed, the turn waits, with nothing generated, and your message gets a card saying what **Play** would play, with **Play**, **Edit** and **Cancel**. For actions it names the first one and what it acts on, and how many follow ("Play Take: letter, then 2 more?"); below, one line each, it quotes your own words for it ("“I take the envelope from the clerk”"), then each action after it with your words ("Then Go to: Bluebird Coffee — “I head to the cafe”"), the time your message lets pass after them, anything it left out and why, and any words it didn't read as an action. People and places appear by name only when your character knows of them; something you typed that the world has no name for, such as a thing you say you have ("letter") or a code ("red_door"), appears as you typed it. The card never names someone or something your character doesn't know of that you didn't name yourself: when the reading puts such a name into what you said (an answer to "whoever was in the shop last night" read as a name), that part is left off, while your own words always show as you typed them. For a time skip it says how long and from when to when ("Play Timelapse: 1 h, 10:29 PM to 11:29 PM?"), what fills it in your words ("Filled with: “I sort the stock”"), and what Play does: the clock runs on in one go, you stay where the story has you through a stated length (or follow your routine to a part of the day or a commitment), and the reply tells it. **Edit** opens the Actions sheet at that action with what was read already picked; pick something else and press its **Play** button to play that instead. It offers only what the sheet always offers your character, so nobody and nowhere your character doesn't know of. Cash you hand over plays as **Pay**, and an edit of it still plays as your hand-over would: to someone who isn't there it plays as roleplay, and a smaller amount adds only what your cash lacks of it, while a larger one adds nothing you weren't asked about. For a time skip, **Edit** opens the Timelapse sheet at the skip as read, with Follow my routine as read; choose another length or end and press its **Play Timelapse** button. **Play** plays it as read. **Cancel** drops the read and leaves the turn unplayed, so you can edit your message and send it again; pressing Regenerate instead reads your message afresh, rather than bringing back the card you cancelled. An action you tap in the palette or on a chip plays at once.
- **Show the turn's outline before the reply is written.** Once the engine has planned the turn, it waits with the Outline the narrator would get: the brief, the engine's beats and the outline step's, one per line. **Play** writes the reply from it. **Edit** lets you change, add or remove lines, and the reply is told your outline. **Cancel** leaves the turn unplayed. Playing it keeps the turn exactly as you saw it planned, dice included: no mind, NPC choice, brief or outline is asked again. That holds for that reply only: a swipe after it plans the turn again and shows you its new outline. A line with something your character hasn't learned yet is a spoiler: it stays in the outline as it was, marked as a secret for the narrator, and the card lists it under the others, folded (*A line with a spoiler: tap to show*) until you tap it open; **Edit** never offers it. For a new reply after a reply (**Send** with nothing typed), the card is under the last reply, and **Play** or **Edit** keeps the outline and asks you to press **Send** with nothing typed: only your Send can start that reply, and it writes it from the outline you let through, never rolling the reply before it again.

### Budgets, deadlines and fallbacks

Budgets are a **number of calls for each phase**: **Before the reply** (reading your text, the NPC choices for people with you, puzzle answers, judging your approach, the brief and the prose outline, and the minds beside them) and **After the reply** (reading the reply, off-screen NPC choices, suggested choices). You set them in **Settings → Model use** under **Advanced: budgets and the engine budget**, each phase with **Calls** and **Lore lookups**, beside **Engine budget (tokens)** for the sections; how long each phase may take is in [Settings → Timeouts](#timeouts). Calls, lore lookups and the token budgets have no upper limit. Changing a budget makes the preset **Custom**. Each step takes one call from its phase's budget before it starts; when a budget is spent, or its time is up, each remaining step uses its fallback (**Calls** 0 means no model at all in that phase): number-only NPC choices, the deterministic outline, a check rolled as if unjudged (one the story would decide goes to the dice), or plain roleplay plus reading the reply. Steps run by priority, and independent ones run in parallel. Lumiverse stops a Decision call at 30 seconds, its own limit, and the step then uses the helper. Simulation itself is never cut off by a clock; it is bounded by counts (days, agents, events).

Settings saved before these defaults rose keep every value you changed; a value still at an old default (a step's 2,000 max tokens, which now follows the helper model; the old lore tokens, lookup counts and section budgets) reads as the new one. Likewise, Character memory, Memory consolidation and Time-skip beats saved on **Use helper default**, while the helper model's effort is **Connection's own**, now start at **Off**; an effort you chose for them, or for the helper model, stays. The engine budget rose again when the sections began telling everything whole (every lore entry about the place, every thing lying there, every field of a vehicle): an engine budget still at Standard's old 4,000 or Full's old 8,000 reads as 8,000 or 12,000.

### Timeouts

**Settings → Timeouts** (under *Getting started*) holds every wait the engine keeps, each with what it limits, what happens when it runs out, its default and its value in seconds. None has a ceiling, and **No limit** lifts one wherever that can't break anything. Picking a Model use preset never changes them.

- **Turns**: **Engine run** (5 min: everything before the reply is generated; when it runs out the run stops, no reply is generated, and the chat says which step ran out, with a Retry), **Lore lookups before the reply** (2 min), **Final review** (5 min: the main agent's review of the narrator's prompt on a chat on agents; when it runs out its edits are dropped and the narrator gets the prompt as the engine made it, or the turn stops, as the review's own setting says), **Reading the reply** (5 min: what changed, people elsewhere, choices, memory), **Lore lookups after the reply** (2 min), **Generation guard** (2 min: Lumiverse's wait for the engine's check, and for its writing of the turn's sections, as a generation starts; Lumiverse caps it at 2 min) and **Time skip plan** (the helper call that plans a time skip for those **Planned by the model**; when it runs out nobody is planned, so they keep to their sleep and meals, the GM's routine page says why, and the skip runs).
- **This tab**: **Engine requests** (5 min: how long the tab waits for each answer) and **Preset check** (1 min: how long Caveats waits for its test prompt before saying it's still running).
- **Jobs and background work**: **Minds run on demand** (10 min), **Import and Refine calls** (10 min each; the lore index, map and routine proposal calls in Build take it too), **Token counts** (30 s; 0 always uses the engine's estimate), **Background lane start-up** and **Background lane heartbeat** (1 min each; Lumiverse caps both at 2 min, and No limit on the heartbeat asks it to check none), and **MCP server reconnect** (1 min: how long **Reconnect in Lumiverse** waits for a server Lumiverse runs to come back with the new tools before its line tells you to reconnect it by hand).

**The runtime's waits**, under the engine's own, says where the harness's are: the waits of the harness and its runtime (how long a page waits for the runtime, **Runtime wait**; the link's; Lumiverse's proxy road, **Proxy road wait**; the agents' calls, turns and jobs) are LumiAH's settings, on its own Timeouts page in the drawer's **Harness** tab → **System** → **Settings** → **Timeouts**, each with its default and its field. A timeout of the harness, the runtime's own included, names the wait that ran out by that place and its group there, as the line quotes one: "Runtime wait (LumiRPEngine's Harness tab → System → Settings → Timeouts → Waiting for the runtime)". (The Harness tab shows while a chat the harness plays is open; the previous build's harness engine has the rest of its runtime: its connections, its updates and the permission `cors_proxy`.)

The page ends with **Lumiverse's own limits**, which no setting changes. When a timeout runs out, [Diagnostics](#diagnostics) records it with the setting's name and how to raise it, and a request the tab gave up on says so in its error. Settings saved before Timeouts existed keep the wall clocks you set; one still at an old preset's default rises to the new default. The command line and the MCP server take `--timeout <seconds|none>` (or `LRE_TIMEOUT`), 300 seconds by default.

### Lore for the helpers

The engine's own helpers read the world's lore ([World lore](#world-lore)) to do their jobs: `interpret` resolves names and aliases, `extract` recognises who and what the reply is about, `npc.choose` weighs a person's past and motives, `judge` weighs your described approach against what the world says, and the brief, the outline and puzzle answers read what bears on the scene. The narrator reads the lore about where your character is in the **Place** section; the rest of the world's public lore reaches it only through what the brief and the prose outline write, since they may read it to plan the scene.

- **Prefetch.** Before a step asks its model, the engine searches the lore with the step's own inputs: your message or the reply, the people and places in the scene, the quests you can see, or the people and options of an NPC choice. What it finds goes into that step's prompt, cut to fit the step's lore tokens. A prefetch costs no call.
- **Lookups.** A step can also ask for more. On most connections it calls the lore tools (search, read one entry, list a topic); when a call with them fails for good (not a timeout or a busy provider), the step asks once more in a plain form instead (`{"need": [...]}`), and only a connection that said it takes no tools is remembered, to ask in that form from then on. Each time the model asks, the engine answers and the step is asked again: one more call, a **lore lookup**. Steps on the Decision connection can't use tools, so before deciding they get one lookup on the helper that asks what lore the decision needs.
- **Per step**, in **Settings → Steps and connections**, under each step's **Advanced: signals and lore**: **Lore** (*Off*, *Prefetch*, *Look up* or *Both*), **Lore tokens**, and **Lookups by** (*Auto*, *Tools* or *Ask form*). A step without its own setting takes its preset's.
- **Per phase**, in **Settings → Model use** under **Advanced: budgets and the engine budget**: **Lore lookups**; in **Settings → Timeouts**, how long after the phase starts one may begin. Each lookup takes one from its phase's lookup budget, apart from the phase's calls, and the steps that run first get them first. A lookup starts only while a third of the phase's time is left, so a step still has time to answer. When none is left, the step answers with what its prefetch found. **Lean** makes no lookups.
- **Pinned lore.** In **Build**, a character's page and a place's page (a place drawn on a map too) have **Pinned lore**, and **Pinned lore** in Build's **World** list has a section for each step, with what holds for every step's pins said once at its top. **Pin world lore** picks one of the world's entries, and **Pin from World Info** picks one of your World Info books (the ones attached to the open chat first) and then **The whole book** or one of its entries, even one switched off in its book. A whole book is read as it is each time a call reads it: its switched-on entries, in the book's order, so entries added to it later (the chapters another extension writes, say) are read without pinning them again. What is pinned to a character is read first by their mind every time they think; what is pinned to **Character minds** is read by every character's mind, after their own pins, and what is pinned to **Time-skip beats** by every mind at a time skip's beat too, after those; what is pinned to any other step is read first by every call of that step, whatever its **Lore** setting; what is pinned to a place is read first by every helper and mind there, or in a place inside it. **Character memory**, **Memory consolidation** and **Time-skip beats**' own call read only what is pinned to them, and so does the time skip's plan for those whose skip the model plans, which reads what is pinned to **Time-skip beats**. For a helper, pinned text comes first within the step's **Lore tokens**; for a mind, the other minds steps and the skip's plan, within **Lore pinned to them and to where they are** in **Settings → Minds → What a mind reads** (no limit by default). When it doesn't all fit it is cut, and the **Context** panel says so and which limit to raise. Each pin chooses what a cut keeps, under its name: **When it doesn't fit, keep the start** (the default), or **keep the end** for an entry written oldest first, such as a running timeline, a log or a journal, whose newest part matters most; a whole book's entries each keep the part its pin keeps. Pins are part of the world: commit, then **Upgrade** the chat, to use a new pin in it; a pinned book's text is always read as it is now. A pinned entry is as secret as it is everywhere else. A pinned World Info entry is read without its secret lines, and one that is only a secret line reaches only the minds of the people it is known to. A secret one, the world's, a World Info entry an earlier version made secret with its fields, one a secret entry of the world was indexed from or one with the same text as any of these, still reaches only its audience and its holders' minds until its gate holds (both gates, when it is secret in its book and in the world), and the **Context** panel doesn't name it before then; one a Known-by entry of the world was indexed from reads only for those who know what it links; a pinned entry the AI wrote waits for your review like any other. **Check** warns of a pin to an entry that is gone, twice over, or that nobody can read, and opens the page it is on.
- **Secrets.** A secret entry reaches only the helpers its audience names, until its gate holds; the brief, the prose outline, suggested choices and extraction never get it, so Build doesn't offer them as its readers (one an entry named before is ignored). A secret line in your World Info reaches no helper at all, only the minds of the people it is known to. An entry marked **Known by** is read only once your character knows every person and place it links. Nothing a helper writes for you may carry a secret's text: a chip, a Journal line, a section, an action read from your words or the reason given for a judged approach that repeats an unrevealed truth, or a sentence or clause of a secret entry, word for word is dropped, unless the words were yours. The check looks only for clauses of at least four words and 16 characters, so a shorter phrase or a paraphrase isn't caught, which is why a secret never goes to a step that writes for you. The helpers are told only who your character can see there, and a reason for a judged approach that names someone, a quest or a place your character can't see, or a secret entry by its title, another name it goes by or a keyword it is found by, is dropped unless your message named it too, since dropping your own words would tell you a name you typed is hidden; the rating stands. A brief, a prose outline line or a suggested choice that names one of them is left out unless your message, or what the step was told (who is there, what happened, what you have and know; for a choice, the reply too), named it, so what you typed reads the same, on the outline gate and in the **Context** panel's counts as much as to the narrator, whether or not someone hidden goes by it. Everyday words aren't names there: a hidden quest's step titles (*Whispers*), a secret entry's keywords, and anything your character saw that is now used up or gone (the coffee you drank) never leave a line out. Names count word for word: *Vex is watching* names a hidden Vex Morrow, while *dangerous* doesn't name a hidden Dan. A plural counts too (*the Okafors* names a hidden Ada Okafor), and so do the accents people leave out (*Zoe* names a hidden Zoë, *Soren* a hidden Søren). Someone whose name is a single letter, such as *Q*, counts as that capital letter standing alone, except *A* and *I*, which are ordinary words. Anything else, a place, a thing, a quest or an entry's title, counts only by all its words in order, so a secret *Under the Chapel* isn't named by *under* alone. A name in another script counts wherever it appears, even inside a word, which catches a language written without spaces (*李雷在监视你*) and a name with an ending added (*Вексом* for *Векс*).

### The call meter

The call meter counts each turn's engine model calls, seconds and tokens, and its lore lookups when there were any. **This turn** in the World sheet and the pill's details shows the latest turn. **Settings → Model use** has a **Call meter** section with rows for **This turn**, **Average** and **95th pct**, and columns for **Calls**, **Tokens**, **Lookups** and **Time**; **GM → This chat → Context** ends with the same meter. If a preset costs more than you want, this is where you see it. The meter is saved for you, so a restart keeps its numbers.

### Engine tokens versus your context

The engine's sections are a fixed overhead in every prompt, up to the engine budget. Nothing checks that budget against your connection's context size, so keep it well inside it: if the fixed part of the prompt is larger than the context, Lumiverse drops the chat history, including your latest message.

### Diagnostics

While a turn runs, the pill, the status line and the top of the drawer say what the engine is doing and for how long. While the engine runs (or reads a reply), the drawer's line has **Cancel**: it stops it at once. A cancelled turn isn't a failure. Nothing is generated, no red flag appears, and Regenerate or Send plays the turn again. A cancelled reading leaves the reply unread: nothing it found changes the world, and the reply gets **Retry read**. While the line only waits for the reply, or for you to press Send, there is nothing to cancel, so it has no Cancel. If a step fails, they turn red ("Failed: Planning"); the drawer's banner has **Diagnostics** and **Dismiss**. The characters' memories are updated beside the reading of the reply as a step of their own: a memory cut off at its max tokens shows as "Failed: Updating the characters' memories", while the reply's reading, which went through, stays fine.

On a chat that plays on agents (GM → This chat → **Engine**), the drawer's line opens into one row per role at work while the turn runs: its name as **Settings → Agents** gives it (**Main agent**, **Intent**, **Minds**…) and the tokens all its agents have sent and written this turn, with **Cancel** on each row while the turn can be stopped. A role that works for several people at once (the minds, a choice for each person, the memory) still has one row, so the line never tells how many people it is thinking for, someone off-screen or out of your character's sight among them. A row says nothing of what its agents look up or call, since that could name someone your character hasn't met; Diagnostics holds the rest, behind its curtain. Cancel on any row stops the whole turn, every agent of it, as the line's Cancel does.

**Settings → Diagnostics** (also under About) shows what the engine did in this chat. It can name someone your character hasn't met, so it opens behind **Show diagnostics**. Then:

- **Errors** lists what failed, newest first, with the reason and what to change: for example, an answer cut off at its max tokens tells you to raise **Max tokens** for that step in **Settings → Steps and connections**.
- Each **turn** lists its steps in order (planning, the plan, the outline, filling the prompt, reading the reply, the characters' memories, memory) with their time and outcome, and under each the model calls it made: the connection and model, tokens in and out, why the answer ended, and the time. Filling the prompt lists each section, the preset outlet it goes to, and whether it got text.
- An **agent's call** is a row of its own (the GM agent's, outside any turn): the agent's role, the model on its connection, tokens in (with how many came from the provider's cache and, when the provider says, how many it wrote to the cache) and out (with how many were reasoning), the time, and how it ended (ok, failed, cancelled or refused) with the error and the tools it called. A count the provider didn't report is the engine's estimate, marked **~**; what was written to the cache is never guessed, so a call that wrote none (**0 written**) tells apart from a connection that doesn't say (no **written** at all), and a prefix that was written but not read on the next call shows as such. **Show text** opens what the agent was sent and answered, in a scrolling block; once its retention (**Settings → Agents → Retention**, 30 days by default) has passed, the row stays but its text is gone, and it says so.
- **Slow** marks a view or an edit that took over a second.
- **Where the last turn's time went** lists the turn's parts in order: waiting for the chat, setup, summarizing earlier turns, each main-agent stage's sends, the plan, the narrator's brief, the final review, the reply, the reply's read and memory. Each has its time and cause. Each agent call sits under its part, with the tools it was offered (how many, and about how many tokens) and its cached share. The slowest part is marked, with a button to the setting that decides it ("Summarizing earlier turns: 7 min 46 s → Summarize ahead of time"). The same timeline is on **Settings → Speed** and under the turn's plan in **GM → Live**.
- Each agent call also records the tools it was offered and about how many tokens they took.
- **Show** filters to **Problems**, **Model calls** (agents' calls included) or **Slow**; **Copy** puts the shown records on the clipboard as text, **Download** saves them all as JSON for a bug report, and **Clear** forgets them.

The engine keeps the newest records of each chat, so they survive a restart, and writes each as a line to your `diagnostics.log` (an agent's call without its text). **Records per chat**, on the page even before you open the curtain, sets how many: 400 by default, with **Default** to go back. Agents' calls fill them faster than the engine's steps; lowering it drops the oldest.

### Retention

The agents keep what they write once, in your LumiRPEngine storage, shared by all your chats: each turn's part of their session (with the summaries compaction writes and the spans they replace) and its transcript, the workspace's files, each chat's learned files, your global files (~/), and what each of their calls was sent and answered. A reply's record, a chat's learned files, your global workspace and Diagnostics point at these texts, so a swipe, a Re-run or a Branch copies nothing. **Settings → Agents → Retention** says which turns keep their transcripts, and how long a text stays once nothing points at it; each setting has **Default** to go back.

**Turn transcripts** are what GM → Workspace lists under transcripts/: everything each turn's agents did, as GM → Live showed it. By default every turn keeps its own, however many and however old; set either of these to let older ones go:

- **Turn transcripts** (No limit): how many of a chat's latest turns keep their transcript. Turn **No limit** off and it keeps the latest 50, which you can change; after each turn, the transcript of the turn that falls past it leaves the list, and no agent reads it any more. Each chat counts its own turns, a Branch too, so a Branch keeps the transcripts of its own latest turns even when they are older in the chat it came from.
- **Turn transcript age** (No limit): how many days a turn's transcript is kept after it was written (30 when you turn **No limit** off). One older leaves the list the same way. A transcript written before this setting came has no date to count from, so only **Turn transcripts** above lets it go.

A transcript that leaves the list is still stored until a sweep finds nothing points at it any more and **Unused texts** has passed (below), so raising a limit again soon brings it back; one a sweep has removed by then lists but reads as gone. The stored texts:

- **Agent call texts** (30 days): how long Diagnostics keeps what each agent call was sent and answered, after the call. The call's row stays as long as **Records per chat** keeps it; only its text goes. **No limit** keeps each text as long as its row, and 0 keeps none.
- **Unused texts** (7 days): how long a text nothing points at any more is kept before a sweep removes it: one only a deleted reply, swipe or chat used, a turn's transcript past the limits above, or a call's text past its limit. It counts from the first sweep that finds it unused, so the sweep that first finds a text unused never removes it, however long ago it stopped being used; with 0, the sweep after that one does.
- **Sweep** (every 24 hours): how often a sweep looks. It runs while you use LumiRPEngine and never while a turn runs, and one under way stops removing when a turn starts. **Never** removes nothing.

A sweep never removes a text anything still points at: a record on any swipe of any reply in any of your world chats (a Branch included, whether or not LumiRPEngine has opened it; a turn's transcript only while the limits above keep it in that chat), the session parts a compaction's summary or archived span reads, any chat's learned files, your global files, the call texts Diagnostics keeps, and what a turn under way, a change waiting for a turn to end or a chat's journal holds that isn't on its messages yet. While your global files, a chat's messages, or the engine data on one of them, can't be read, a sweep removes nothing and says why in the log. It reads your chats without keeping them in memory, so the chat you're playing isn't read again whole after it. So deleting a reply, a swipe or a chat frees only what nothing else points at, once **Unused texts** has passed.

### Settings → Agents

**Settings → Agents**, the first page of its own group (**Agents**, **Roles**, **Context**, **Main agent**, **Assistant**, **Prompts**, **Retention**), holds what LumiRPEngine's agents run under: the GM assistant, and the turns of a chat whose engine is **Agents** (GM → This chat → **Engine**). A chat on the steps engine reads none of it. The page opens with **A turn at a glance**: each stage of a turn in the order it plays (1 Intent; 3 Scene, minds and decisions; 5 Beats; 6 Final review; 7 Read; 8 Memory, or *Memory, left to the next turn* while memory runs before the next turn), the roles that work in it, where each runs (the connection and model its tasks actually go to, and where that comes from: *its own*, *from Steps*, *from Helper model*, or Lumiverse's default connection) and how, when that isn't as an agent with tools (*one call, no tools*; *in a time skip*); roles alike share a line, a stage with nothing to run says so, and each role's name opens its page. A badge says which pipeline runs the turn. Then **Every agent call**: **Streaming** and **Thinking in tool loops**. Then a row for each of its sections, each a page of its own under its name, saying what it holds now and how many of its settings are changed: **Pipeline**, **Review card**, **System instructions**, **Budgets**, **Compaction**, **Recording**, **Throttles**, **Caching** and **Acceptance runs**. Throughout these pages a setting that differs from its default says **Changed**, and only then shows its default and **Default** to go back to it; a limit is folded to its name and value until you open it; Back, or Escape, goes up one level, and focus returns to the row you opened.

While a main agent decides the turn, **A turn at a glance** also shows how each stage runs: **The main agent decides** or **Runs on its own** (Main agent → Stages, below). A role whose **When it runs** is **Off** has no row there.

- **Thinking in agent tool loops** (off): while it's off, every agent call that offers tools goes with reasoning off, whatever the agent's effort, because Lumiverse doesn't yet pass back what a reasoning model thought before a tool call, which it must be sent again. Calls without tools keep their effort. Switch it on once Lumiverse passes it back. While it's on, a call with an effort of its own (not *Connection's own*) also asks for the model's thinking summarized, so its text comes back: some models leave it out unless asked.
- **Streaming** (on): **Stream every agent call's answer** has each agent call take its answer as Lumiverse streams it, as the model writes it, rather than all at once at the end, which a gateway or proxy between the model and Lumiverse may cut off on a long call. The answer reads the same either way (its text, tool calls, reasoning and usage), **Cancel** stops a call mid-stream, and every time limit (a call's, an agent's, the turn's, the final review's) still counts the whole call, however long it streams. Off, each call waits for its answer whole.
- **Pipeline**: **How a turn is run**, *A fixed pipeline* (the default: each role runs in the steps' own order, working its step's part, with the steps engine's gates and cards) or *A main agent decides* (one main agent per chat decides which roles to run and what to tell them, in the order the engine keeps, and keeps a session from turn to turn); **When memory runs**, *After the reply* (the default, as the steps engine does) or *Before the next turn* (the reading of the reply ends sooner, and what the minds remember belongs to the next turn, which remembers it once, even after waiting at a gate, on either engine: switching the chat to the steps engine loses none; a Re-read edit or Retry read of that reply remembers in its own reading instead); and **The Curator keeps the world's things and places** (on): after the reply, beside the read, the [Curator](#settings--agents) makes the world agree with it (the things in it and who has them, the places it makes, the people who walk on and a purchase's goods), and the read leaves those to it; off, the read reads them itself, as the steps engine's read does.
- **Skip it on quiet turns**, the Curator's (on its page under **When it runs**, with **Changed** and **Default**): *Off*, *Shadow* (the default) or *On*. The check reads only the reply and your message. The Curator runs when they mention a thing in the scene, a name the world doesn't have, a hand-over, a placement, a purchase or a price, something worn or used, or the turn's own Buy. Under *Shadow*, Diagnostics records what the check would have decided, and the Curator always runs. Under *On*, a quiet turn skips the Curator, and the read's held changes land instead.
- **Smart persistence**, under **Pipeline** (off by default, for every agent chat): while a scene is unchanged since the last turn, its quiet Live minds and the scene's lore and notes are kept instead of asked again. They are recorded as this turn's, and the narrator is told "Unchanged since the last turn" (the text is Prompts → Final output → Kept unchanged). A scene is unchanged when, since the last turn, none of these changed: the place; who is here and where; the time of day; the world's revision and the live lore; the things here and on everyone present; how they regard each other; and no GM fix or marker came. **Turns kept in a row** (3) and **World minutes kept** (30) bound it; while it is off, a limit you changed still shows, with its **Default**. It never applies to a Re-run, a swipe, a Continue or a timelapse, to a mind's first thoughts, or to a mind the GM asked to refresh or one you or the reply name; and the intent, knowledge, read, Curator, memory, skip and world steps are never kept.
- Smart persistence survives a restart: the last scene is kept beside the chat's settings, and it holds only for the reply it was written for.
- **Review card**: where a turn's changes that ask first wait: *On the review card after the reply* (the default: the turn goes on, and the narrator isn't told of a change until you apply it) or *On the review card before the reply* (the turn waits after its plan, as at the outline gate, until you answer). See the review card under [Under each reply](#under-each-reply).
- **Compaction**: **Compact the session past** (32,000 tokens) and **Recent turns kept whole** (3): once the main agent's session passes the size, everything but its latest turns becomes one summary, which GM → Workspace can roll back; and **The main agent's summarize tool** (on), which lets it compact its own session sooner.
  - **Summarize** (under **Main agent → Session**): *Ahead of time* (the default) or *Before the turn*. With *Ahead of time*, once a turn's session is settled and the next turn will compact, the summary the next turn needs (the oldest kept turns folded into the summary) is made in the background. The next turn adopts it ("Where the last turn's time went" shows compaction as *ready*), or waits for it if it is still running (the activity line reads "Summarizing earlier turns…"), or on a miss makes it then. A swipe, Re-run or Re-narrate of that turn finds the same summary. Nothing survives a restart: the next turn then makes it while you wait, once.
  - The summary's **connection** and **model** (the main agent's unless you pick one), its **effort** (the main agent's by default, or any agent effort) and **Summary length** (16,000 tokens).
  - A summary cut off at its length, or that ends mid-sentence, never lands. The session goes on whole, Diagnostics warns ("Raise Summary length, or lower the compaction's effort"), and the next turn tries again. A Re-narrate makes no summary of its own: it adopts one that is made or under way, or else goes on whole.
  - **The session keeps**: *Receipts* (the default) or *Everything whole*, with **Receipt length** (600 characters). With receipts, once a turn is over its session keeps the final review's message as each part's id, header, line count and hash, plus the review's edit list; each tool answer longer than 600 characters as its first 600 characters, with its length, lines and hash; and the review's own tool calls as counts. The turn's own later stages still read everything whole.
  - The main agent's **past_turn** tool lists a past turn's messages, reads one whole a page at a time, or searches one turn (or, with no turn named, the latest 40) for words. Every message is kept whole beside its receipt. Turns are named by the reply's number, as the digest numbers replies.
- **Recording**: **Narrator-facing output, each** (2,000 characters): how long each line an agent turn records for the narrator may be (an outcome, montage or outline line, the brief, a scene note); a longer one is cut to it, ending in "…", and the cut is noted in your `diagnostics.log`, not on the Diagnostics page.
- **System instructions** (8,000 tokens): how much of each agent's prompt its system files may take together: **AGENTS.md** and its own **roles/<role>.md**, your global workspace's (~/) and this chat's, written in GM → Workspace (see [Workspace](#workspace)). They follow the agent's system prompt, the global ones first, so this chat's have the last word; past it, the file that no longer fits is cut at a line, the rest are left out, and the agent is told which. None ships, so until you write one they cost nothing.
- **Budgets** is a page of its own: *Each agent*, *Each turn, every agent together*, *Loops and large results*, *The GM's edits*, *Kept in memory*, each a group with its Changed count; a role, the main agent or the assistant may set any of the first group as its own, on its page. Each limit says what it limits and what happens when it's reached: for **each agent**, the prompt and the answer of one call (the answer at 8,192 tokens), the time of one call, the tool calls one answer may make, its prompt and answer tokens in all, its rounds of tool calls (10), its time, its **revisions** (2: the messages it may be sent after its first answer) and how many of its tool calls run at once (4); for **each turn**, every agent together: prompt tokens, answer tokens, rounds and time, each none by default (a long chat's turn sends far more than a short one's, so each agent's own limits bound it; set one only where you want a limit for the whole turn). A call never asks for more answer tokens than the agent or the turn has left, and one that its prompt would take past a budget isn't sent: the agent stops with what it has, and its record says which limit stopped it.
- **Fresh runs per request** (1), among each agent's budgets: how many times the main agent may run a role afresh on a request it already answered; the fresh run drops what the last one staged, and its revisions start again. It applies to turn roles only (not the assistant, the main agent or the final review).
- **Loop detector** (3): the same tool call with the same arguments three times in a row stops the agent before it's run. The final review's rewrites and take-outs count only while the prompt hasn't changed since the last one: they name a line by its number as it reads now, so the same number after an edit is the next line. **Engine answers shown whole** (8,000 characters): how much of one answer of the engine's own tools an agent is shown whole. On a Harness chat it bounds the world tools' answers: a longer value comes as an outline to read further into, and a list of names or issues is cut to half of it, saying how many more (LumiAH's own **Tool results clipped past** is LumiAH's setting, under **Harness** → **System** → **Settings**). On the agents engine a longer result reaches the model clipped, and the rest waits in the agent's scratch, which it reads with its built-in **scratch_read** tool. A file saved at the old default of 4,000 reads as 8,000; a number you chose stays.
- **GM edits per turn** (10), among the budgets: how many of the GM's own edits (a spawn, a removal, any field, a stage, the clock) a turn's agents may stage together, over the whole turn: those made before the plan, after it and after the reply all count; the ones past it are refused, and the agent that asked is told so. Only a role whose tier grants the GM's edits (**Roles**) makes any.
- **Kept in memory**, among the budgets, each with its **Default**: **Agent turns under way** (64): past it, starting another lets the oldest go, a turn that went stale; **Turns waiting at a gate** (256), across your chats: a turn that waits on you (the action or the outline shown before playing, a question asked first, the review card) keeps everything its agents had done, so answering it asks none of them again; past it, your oldest waiting turn forgets that, so when you answer it the turn asks its agents again where it still needs them, and a review gate's turn still plays its plan and lands what you let through, but its record no longer says which role made the changes from before the gate, and the narrator isn't told the lines its roles had; **Chats whose review cards are watched** (256): past it, the chat watched longest ago stops hearing about its cards until its page asks for them again; **Chats in the live view** (16): how many chats' latest agent turn GM → Live keeps, the one started longest ago let go past it (Live then shows nothing for it until its next turn, and its transcript stays in GM → Workspace); **Agent texts kept once read** (4,000,000 characters): the agents' stored texts (the main agent's session, the GM assistant's conversation, the workspace's files) kept in memory once read, so reading one again takes no trip to storage; past it the text read longest ago goes, and 0 reads every one from storage; and **Messages kept worked out** (65,536): how many of your chats' messages the engine keeps worked out, each with what it did to the world, so working a chat out again starts where it changed rather than from its first message; past it, the message used longest ago is worked out again when next needed. Keep it above your longest chat's messages. Each is at least 1 but **Agent texts kept once read**, which may be 0.
- **Chat kept for its agents** (5 minutes), among **Kept in memory**: the agents of a job (the story's preparation among them) and the story's pages read a chat's messages once and keep them, so the next call and the next agent read none of it again. A message sent, edited, deleted or swiped ends that at once; past this time the chat is read again anyway. At 0 every read asks Lumiverse. What a turn reads to answer you is not kept this way.
- **Throttles**, per connection: requests and tokens per minute, each off by default. A call waits until its connection's minute has room; only one bigger than a whole minute's tokens is refused. **Calls at once** is one gate for every agent call and every prompt-only role call on the connection: a call past the limit waits for one under way to end, and nothing is refused. A connection with no limit of its own uses the global **Calls at once** on **Settings → Speed** (no limit by default).
- **Caching**: the page has every role's switch and **Bust**, then the agents with caching of their own, each opening its page; each agent's own caching is on its page. The stable part of an agent's prompt goes first, with cache breakpoints where the provider takes them (Anthropic's 4 per request, its connection's own prompt caching included: one each for its system messages, its last message and its automatic mode when they're on). Every call of an agent offers the same tool list, so the provider's cache keeps it. On a connection that takes explicit breakpoints, with a third one free and the connection not marking tools itself, LumiRPEngine puts one after the last tool. **Bust** puts a new key at the top of the prompt, so the next call reads nothing from the provider's cache; off sends a new key every call, which also defeats a provider's automatic caching.
- **Acceptance runs**: none of your connections is guarded until you add one here; a connection you add starts marked **Only in acceptance runs**, with ceilings of 300,000 prompt and 30,000 answer tokens per run. A connection so marked is refused unless you've opened a run, here, with a label; an outside agent (the `lumirpengine` CLI or its MCP server) can neither open one nor change these settings: a settings change of its that would leave them otherwise, a reset of the whole Agents section included, is refused whole. A **Model Roulette** connection sends each call on one of its connections, picked at random, so an agent's call on one is refused while any of them is guarded here. An agent's call on a connection LumiRPEngine can't read (your default connection when it can't be read, or one whose settings can't be read, which might be such a roulette) goes ahead, counted against no guarded connection, and while any connection is guarded the log and your diagnostics say so, once for each such connection rather than on every call. While a run is open, each guarded connection's calls count against its ceilings for the run (prompt and answer tokens), and every call first holds its estimated prompt plus its max tokens, so one that would pass a ceiling isn't sent. A model that hasn't reported real usage yet holds its estimate times the **estimate factor** (2). The page and a line at the top of the drawer show what the run has spent, cached and held; **Close the run** keeps its report as the last run's. The guarded connections are kept with the runs, apart from your other settings, so a build from before the agents, which keeps no agent settings, never drops them: back on this one, they are guarded as you left them. If that file can't be read, or was damaged (it is kept aside, as `agent-acceptance.json.corrupt-…`) while your settings kept no copy of the list, agent calls go ahead under the list your settings file keeps (none, if it keeps none), and the log and your diagnostics say so once, naming that list. While the file can't be read no run counts as open, and each call tries the file again; a damaged one lasts until you set the list again here (add a connection, or remove one). Play itself goes on as before.
- **Against the steps engine** (2×), under acceptance runs: the target an acceptance comparison holds agent turns to: per role, an agent turn's tokens against the same scenario's turn on the steps engine, at most this many times. The engine doesn't compare turns itself or stop one past it; each turn's tokens, per role, are in **Settings → Diagnostics**, to compare against this. Any amount above 0; under 1, an agent turn should spend less than the steps engine.

**Settings → Agents → Roles** lists the roles of an agent turn in the order they commit (**Intent**, **Scene and lore**, **Minds**, **Knowledge**, **Director**, **Read**, **Curator**, **Memory**, **Skip beats**, **World steps**), one row each: where its tasks run, anything about how it runs that isn't its default (*one call, no tools*, what it does on a failure, *Off* for a Curator switched off in Pipeline), and how many of its settings are changed; **Main agent** and **Assistant** follow. A row opens the role's page, the same template for every role, the main agent and the assistant:
- **Runs on**: each of its tasks (Intent: *Reading your message*, *Judging your approach*, *A puzzle's answer*; Director: *People's choices*, *The brief and the outline*, *Suggested choices*; and so on) with the connection and model it actually runs on. With no connection of its own (the default), a role runs each task where that task's step runs (Settings → Steps and connections), else on the helper model, else on Lumiverse's default connection; the director's brief and outline run where the brief does, or where the outline does on a turn without one. Then **Connection and model** (*None: each task on its step's*, or a connection and, once picked, its model) and **Reasoning**.
- **What it may do**: its **Tools**, one chip per family, and **What it may change**: *Nothing*, *The story's changes* (the default) or *The GM's edits too*. The **Tools** row reads "N tools, about X tokens of each call's prompt": an estimate from the tools' texts, your own wording included. A world's ids add a little. No tool is cut from any agent; the Curator keeps its default families (items, places, people, money and organizations).
- **What it reads**: what **Settings → Agents → Context** sets for it (below): its **Perspective**, its **Sources** (each under **Each source**: *Starts with*, *When needed* or *Never*) and its **Pinned files**.
- **When it runs and fails**: **When it runs**, for the scene, the minds and the director: *When the turn asks* (the default; Smart persistence may keep the minds and the scene), *Every time, never kept* (the minds and the scene) or *Off* (the turn goes on without the role); **One call, no tools** (off: on, it makes its step's own call, with that step's prompt and answer format) and **When it fails**: *Run its step's one call* (the default), *Go on without it*, or *Stop the turn* (the Intent's default). The Curator's page says whether Pipeline runs it, and opens Pipeline.
- **Budget**: only the limits it sets of its own, each folded to its value with **Every agent's** to give it back; **Set a limit of its own** adds one more at the limit it follows now (a common number where that is no limit). The Curator's page has **Curator ops per turn** here, and its own **Tool rounds per agent** (6), which it ships with, so they aren't a change.
- **Caching**: its own switch and **Bust**; with caching of its own, **Default** follows every role's again.
- **Prompts**: opens its page on Prompts.

While anything on the page differs, **Reset this page** puts the whole role back: its settings, what it reads, its own caching (and the Curator's ops per turn). Whichever **When it fails** you pick, nothing a failed run wanted to change lands, and the main agent decides it for itself when it runs the turn; after the reply, *Stop the turn* stops reading the reply instead, which then waits unread with **Retry read**. Every role's tools read everything, each secret marked with whom it is known to and kept from: what a role works from is a hint on the **Context** page, never a screen.

**The Curator** keeps the world's things and places true to the reply on a chat on agents: who has what and whose it is, the rooms and spots the reply puts people in, the people who walk on, and what a purchase brings. It works after the reply, beside the read, which leaves all of that to it, from the GM's view with every secret marked, the turn's own changes in words and the Place, Gear and People sections (never minds), which list the scene's things and people by the names its tools read, so it names what they show and looks up only what they don't. Each change is one call of one of its tools: making a thing (it looks first, and is refused when the thing is already there), moving it, handing it over (to hold, as a loan, back, or for keeps), making it someone's, letting someone use it, where it's kept, using it up, wearing it, wearing it down, losing it, renaming it, merging two that are one; making or renaming a place; adding a walk-on; a purchase. Each is checked and staged with the turn, lands after the read's, and asks first or applies by the reply's types' modes ([Extraction modes](#extraction-modes)). Its rules, editable in **Prompts → Curator**, say: the reply is canon, change as little as makes the world agree; work from the sections, naming what they list as they name it; find before you make (a make looks first itself); make the changes as soon as you know them, several calls in one answer, lookups together before them; a handover keeps the owner unless the reply says given, sold, traded or kept; a thing too big to carry is placed, never held; say why; a hidden thing the reply reveals is found; make a place only when the reply puts someone there and nothing fits; when unsure, change nothing, and stop once the things are right. Its card on this page adds **Curator ops per turn** (24): past it, its changes are refused, and it is told so; and its budget ships with **Tool rounds per agent** of its own, 6 (every agent's is 10): when its rounds run out before it answers, what it staged and noted still lands, and Diagnostics warns that it stopped short. Taking a thing out of the world needs *The GM's edits too*. The read still gives the things, places, walk-ons and purchases it sees, but holds them aside for the Curator: they land only where the Curator's own work doesn't cover them, when its rounds ran out, its agent failed (beside its prompt-only read), or it never ran (a main agent that didn't run it), each once, never what the Curator staged already; while its work is whole, none of them lands. Switch the Curator off under **Pipeline** (**The Curator keeps the world's things and places**, on by default), and the read reads things, places, walk-ons and purchases itself, as classic does; prompt-only, it is one call of classic's reading of those alone.

A Curator that staged or noted changes and then closed with an answer the engine couldn't read keeps that work: the read's held changes land only for what it didn't stage. A Curator that staged nothing falls back to its prompt-only read, as before. The read keeps what the Curator's tools can't make: without People tools, walk-ons land through the read. A change that names a thing or place another change made, while that change waits for review, waits on the review card with it, and **Apply** lands both.

**Settings → Agents → Context** is the overview of what each role starts with, and the guide the main agent briefs the roles by: one row per role with its perspective, what its prompt starts with (or *its task alone*), what it is never given, and its pinned files, with how many of those are changed. A row opens the role's page at **What it reads**, where they are set: its **perspective**, *Your character's*, *Everyone's, secrets marked*, *The GM's* or *Its character's own* (the character its task is about, as a mind works), a hint in its system prompt that never changes what its tools read; how it gets each source, the **Lore index** and each of the narrator's sections (the Scene, the Minds, the Outcome and the rest, as the narrator would get them then, secrets marked): **Starts with** (in its prompt from the start, in either pipeline), **When needed** (the default: the main agent may give it when the task needs it, and its tools can read it) or **Never** (the main agent is told never to give it); and its **pinned files**, workspace paths one a line, read whole within its pins budget before those `.pins.json` lists for it and those you pinned in GM → Workspace (a path the chat has no file at is passed over). By default the **Director**, which briefs and outlines the reply, starts with the **Scene** (with the scene role's lore and notes so far) and the **Minds**, and the **Curator** with **Place**, **Gear** and **People**, never the **Minds**; every other role starts with its task alone; the intent works from your character's perspective, the minds and the memory from their own character's, the Curator from the GM's, and the rest from everyone's. A role never gets less than this page gives it, whichever pipeline runs it; with a main agent deciding, the main agent is told every role's settings here and decides what else each is told.

**What a role is told.** Each role's agent gets its system prompt (its perspective in it), then your system instructions (AGENTS.md and its own roles/<role>.md), then its instructions with your preset's guidance for its steps (Presets), then its **pinned files**: those its **Context** pins, then those the workspace's `.pins.json` lists for it, then those you pinned in GM → Workspace, each whole within its pins budget (the `tokens` `.pins.json` gives it, else **Pinned files, per role** on the Main agent page), a longer one as its headings; then the lore index when its context starts with it, and its task with the sections its context starts it with. Every role gets its pinned files and its tools, the minds, the memory and a person's choice too. The director's brief and outline, and the suggested choices, are kept whole: a line that carries a secret, or names someone or something your character can't see, goes on marked as a secret kept from your character, never left out; the outline card shows it as a spoiler, and a suggestion that carries one is a spoiler chip. The scene's notes for the narrator all go to the Scene section: one that names someone your character can't see, or carries a secret, goes in marked as a secret, as a Minds line does (left out only while the narrator's **Secrets** setting leaves secrets out). What someone learns from your message before they choose (the knowledge step before the plan) is kept as after the reply: not at all while **What you learn (who knows what)** in **Settings → Changes from the reply** is *Never*. A role at *The story's changes* may write, move, pin or remove any workspace file, `.pins.json` included, which sets what every agent is shown first. On the review card a change to the agents' workspace reads as one line that names no file, as your Journal shows none. A change a role makes to a file under `learned/` or `~/` is made when its turn ends, and never by a turn you cancel.

**Keeping it lean.** What a role reads is the agents' to keep short, never a filter: the start of each prompt stays the same across a turn's calls, so the provider's cache takes it, and an agent can **condense** a long file, a turn's transcript or a lore entry into a short workspace note (one call of its own model, the note opening with what it came from), which lands with the turn and is read meanwhile by the turn's later runs; the main agent condenses for a role it names, so it can point a later task at the note.

**What the roles hold to.** The **intent** reads your message for what it is for, never its first verb: "I get up from the couch, stretch, and sit down next to Danny to ask how his shift went" is the talk with Danny, and getting up and stretching only lead up to it. The **director** briefs and outlines only what the turn's facts, your words and its lookups support, held to the turn as the engine planned it: who is here, who holds what, where things lie, and the engine's clock once your turn's actions are done, with how long they took. The engine then checks each of its lines against the turn as it started and as it ended: a line that has someone here whom the engine has elsewhere, someone gone who is here, someone holding or handing over a thing they don't have, or a thing lying where it isn't, both before your turn's actions and after them, is flagged (a beat whole, the brief a sentence at a time) and told with the run, and the narrator still gets it. A line that fits either stands unflagged, so your turn's own action is told as it happens: "Jordan hands Danny the pocketknife" on the turn you give it to him, or Danny nodding as you head for the door. The check only reads plain statements about people and things your character can see, so a phone call, a memory, a plan, an arrival or a goodbye never trips it. The **read** is told the engine's clock as the Scene tells it, when your message came and now, and measures the time the reply narrates from when your message came: "the church bell rings seven" lands the clock at seven, past what your turn's own actions took, and a time only mentioned (an appointment, an opening hour) moves nothing. It is also told that a thing the world doesn't have yet is made only when someone else hands it over, in the reply's words, or when it is bought.

**What a run gave.** After each run, the main agent is told what it answered and what it gave the engine: the changes it staged (landing when the turn commits, or held for the review card), any it was refused and why, and anything of its answer the engine didn't take, with why and how many: a knowledge run says how many facts and learnings it recorded and, for each kind of refusal, how many ("1 refused (no matters: a new fact says why it matters and to whom)"), a read says which proposals it didn't read and which things its changes name that the world doesn't have ("1 name a thing the world doesn't have (brass key): …"), each only a *Not tracked* note, a director run says which of its lines were flagged and why, each quoted ("1 flagged, still in the reply's prompt (it has someone here whom the engine has elsewhere: "Marcus Delgado leans on the counter beside Jordan.")"), and an answer with nothing in it says so. **Settings → Diagnostics** shows the same words as a record of the role, a warning when something wasn't taken, on the fixed pipeline too, and so does your `diagnostics.log`. The words are on the **Main agent** page of **Prompts**, under *What a run gave*.

**Settings → Agents → Main agent** is on the role page's template: **Runs on** (*Deciding each turn*, while the pipeline has a main agent decide, and *The final review*), its connection, model and reasoning; **What it may do** (its tools: it only reads); **What it reads** (Turn digest, Pinned files per role); **When it runs** (whether it decides turns, opening Pipeline); **Final review** (on or off, what a review that fails does, its time, and the limits the review sets of its own over the main agent's); **Budget** (its own limits only, with the picker); **Caching**; **Prompts**. Settings home's row says what it does now: on a fixed pipeline with the review on it *reviews the narrator's prompt*; it reads *not used* only on a fixed pipeline with the review off. Its pickers never offer **Fresh runs per request**, which only a turn role takes. It is read while **Pipeline** has a main agent decide, and in either pipeline for the final review (below): its **connection** and **model** (your default connection), its **effort** (*Connection's own*), the **tools it reads with** (*Workspace*; they read everything, secrets marked, as every role's do; it changes nothing itself, only through the roles it runs, the notes it condenses for them and its final review's edits of the narrator's prompt), its **turn task**: **Turn digest** (2,000 tokens of what changed since its last turn) and **Pinned files, per role** (2,000 tokens, every role's too where the workspace's pins set none), and its **budget** for each stage it works, every agent's until you set its own. Its system prompt is followed by your AGENTS.md and roles/main.md, and the start of its prompt carries every role's **Context** settings (its perspective, what it starts with, what it is never to be given, its pinned files and its tools), the guide it briefs them by; so does its final review's.

**Main agent → Stages** sets how each stage the main agent decides runs (1 Intent, 3 Scene, minds and decisions, 5 Beats, 7 Read, 8 Memory): **The main agent decides** (the default: it decides the stage in a message of its own) or **Runs on its own** (the stage's roles run as the fixed pipeline runs them, with no message to the main agent, so the turn is faster; its next message lists what ran and what each answered). With stage 3 on its own, the minds start as the turn starts. **Final review → What it is sent**: **Every part** (the default) or **Only what changed**, which works while Smart persistence is on: the parts that haven't changed since the last turn's review get its edits made again, only the changed parts are sent, and when nothing changed no review call is made.

**The final review.** Before every reply of a chat on agents, whichever pipeline plays it, the main agent reviews the prompt the narrator is about to get, after the engine has made the turn's sections and before they go into the prompt: every section (the Outcome, the Scene, the people, money and the rest), the Outline with its brief, and the narrator rules. It checks each line against the world and the turn, and may fix it a line or a block of lines at a time: rewrite a line or a block (a lore entry the Scene tells whole, say), add lines at the end of a section, or take a line or a block out. Each fix answers with the lines it changed and two either side, numbered as the section now reads, and the review reads a section whole only when it asks to, so a review of many fixes stays short. The narrator gets the prompt as it leaves it, which is what Lumiverse's Prompt Breakdown shows for that reply, and GM → Live shows each edit (see [Live](#live)). On the fixed pipeline the main agent runs for this alone, on its connection and model here, with a digest of the chat so far and its pinned files; with a main agent deciding, the review is the start of its stage 6, and its session keeps it. There every stage, the review too, is offered the same tools (a tool that isn't the stage's own answers that it works elsewhere), so the review reads the session your connection's prompt cache holds instead of sending it all again. The turn's record keeps its edits, so a **Re-narrate** tells the narrator the same revised prompt with no model call, and a reply that failed tries again with the same prompt; a **Re-run** is reviewed afresh. Its settings are the **Final review** section of this page: **Review the narrator's prompt** (on), **When the review fails** (*Send the prompt unrevised*, the default, which leaves a Diagnostics record of why; or *Stop the turn*, which stops it before its reply with the chat's Retry), its time (**Final review** in Settings → Timeouts, 5 minutes, shown here too: a large model reviewing from a long session can think for two minutes before its first edit; settings saved with the old 2 minutes move to 5 once), and its own budget over the main agent's for a stage (each limit the main agent's until you change it). A review fails when it runs out of time or budget, or its model call fails; one that edits nothing isn't a failure. Turned off, nothing runs and the narrator gets the prompt as the engine made it. Its texts are on Settings → Agents → Prompts → Main agent, under Final review, each with its Reset.

**One run at a time.** The main agent may ask for several runs in one answer, and they work together, but each thing the engine asks of a role runs once at a time: asking for it again, or for a revision of it, while its run is still working is answered that it is running, so only the run that stands lands what it changed. A run whose request the turn goes on without lands nothing it changed, even when it had answered: when the time for reading the reply runs out before the main agent hands it over, when the main agent fails, or when the turn ends. **GM → Live** shows such a change as dropped, *the turn went on without its run*.

**What the main agent tells a role, and what its session keeps.** Its task and revisions for a run reach each of the run's tasks whole, whatever the task's perspective: what to tell a role is the main agent's to decide, guided by the **Context** page. A role run inside another's (a skip's minds at its beats, the knowledge step before a decision) is told none of it. Its session is read whole: every message of its own and the compaction summary. Only what it reads counts toward **Compact the session past** and is summarized, so a fixed pipeline's digest of its turns, which the main agent never reads (its turn task's digest stands in), is neither. A decision it runs again or revises adds what the people choosing learned first once, from the run whose answer stands.

**Settings → Agents → Assistant** is the GM assistant's own page (GM → Assistant), on the role page's template: **Runs on**, **What it may do** (how its changes land), **What it reads** (everything, outside the story; History tokens), **Budget** (its own limits only, with the picker; never Fresh runs per request), **Caching** and **Prompts** (the GM agent's page). Its **connection** and **model** are your default connection and its model unless you pick one; its **effort** (*Connection's own* by default: the connection's reasoning setting, else yours in Lumiverse; *Set by the model* for a model that picks its own; while **Thinking in agent tool loops** is off, a call that offers tools goes with reasoning off, whatever this says); **How its changes land**, *Each waits for your Apply* (the default) or *At once, with an Undo chip*; **History tokens** (20,000): how much of the conversation goes with each message, the newest first, the older messages left out and the assistant told how many; and its own **budget**: each limit above is every agent's until you change it here, for the assistant alone, and **Every agent's** follows that one again. Nothing here limits what it reads: it works outside the story and reads everything (see [Assistant](#assistant)). The assistant runs outside every turn, so a limit a turn would set when it is none (a call's time, the tokens in and out, the run's time) is no limit at all here, and the page says so. Its texts are on **Prompts**, page GM agent.

### Settings → Speed

**Settings → Speed** (in the **Agents** group, and a card at the top of **Settings → Agents**) gathers every setting that decides how long a turn takes. It has five groups: **Before the reply**, **Remembering past turns**, **Reusing work**, **Calls** and **Pages**. Each row is one setting with **Changed**, its default and **Default**, edited in place. Each row names the page it also lives on, with a way there.

- **Presets**:
  - **Thorough** is the shipped defaults.
  - **Balanced** turns Smart persistence on (3 turns, 30 world minutes), sends the final review only what changed, runs 4 calls at once and 2 knowledge calls at once.
  - **Fast** is Balanced, plus stage 1 and stage 3 running on their own, 3 knowledge calls at once, and the Curator skipped on quiet turns.

  Picking one lists what it will change, each row from → to, before **Apply**. A preset leaves out any row whose setting this build doesn't have. **Back to Thorough** puts back only the rows a preset sets; your other settings stay.
- **Where the last turn's time went**: as in [Diagnostics](#diagnostics).
- **GM page while a turn runs** (**Pages**): *Held* (the default) shows the chat as it was before the turn until **Refresh**; *Live* follows every change.
- **Reuse built views** (**Reusing work**, on): a view built for the chat as it stands is shared by every request until the chat changes (the GM page's parts, the state they read, the recent lines). Off, each request builds its own.

## Character minds

With **character minds** on, the people near the story think for themselves. Each one's mind is its own model call, made as that character and scoped to what they know. By default a mind reads the story as it stands: who they are (their own fields, agendas and secrets), where they are and whom they see there, the lore pinned to them and the lore the turn names, how the latest reply ended and what you say; their usual routine, as context they may weigh; and their own plans, so they keep to what they agreed. Their own memory and life (their summary, notes and what they saw, last thoughts, the turns they recall, their money, today as the planner lays it, and their commitments) they read only if you switch them on in **What a mind reads** ([Settings → Minds](#settings--minds)). Minds are blind to each other, so no mind decides what another character says or does, and none reads what another character's memory says it told them. A mind answers with an inner monologue, a **depiction** (how they come across), what they intend and their reactions, and the narrator is told how to depict it. After the reply, the concrete changes (money, moves) are still read from what the reply actually says, and the characters' memory is written from it.

A character's **plans** come only from what the story states: after the reply, their memory takes the plans the turn sets out (something said aloud, agreed with someone, or scheduled in the story as written), never a guess at what they might do. A mind makes none, since it sees what its character knows, not where the story is going. A plan moves them at its time, may ask you along, and can stop a time skip. In a chat from before, plans a mind made stay in GM mode as thoughts, and do none of this.

A plan with other people in it is **one plan they all share**: "Danny and Priya agree on dinner at the Anchor at seven" reaches both of them (and anyone else in it), whoever's memory took it from the turn, and a second memory of the same turn doesn't add it again. Each of them lives it as their own day allows: someone who lives their own day goes there at its time, and anyone else (someone who doesn't, or a Background character) lets it pass. You're never given a copy: your plans are yours to make, and when their plan asks you along, one of them invites you once. **Accept invitation** books it in your character's day: an appointment at the meeting's time and place (when you know the place), as long as their plan or routine block for it lasts, under the host's name. A Timelapse that follows your routine takes you there, and accepting again books nothing more. A payment or a loan stays the payer's own.

A plan to pay or lend someone money is carried out by the money engine: at the plan's time, the amount goes from the character to the person the plan is with, from their own money as they'd pay anything, and the plan is done. If they can't cover it, nothing moves and the plan is dropped. Their journal says only **Payment** or **Loan**, never what they had in mind.

Each mind is recorded on the turn, so swipes and branches keep it: **Re-narrate** reuses the turn's minds, **Re-run** asks again, and Playtest and Check world never call a model for them.

### The fidelity ladder

Every turn, and at each beat of a time skip, each character gets a focus score and a level from it:

- **Live**: the closest to the story. Only people at hand are Live: in your scene, or named this turn. Anyone else is Near at most, however much of the story they carry.
- **Near**: they think again when something calls for it: they come into the scene, they are named, or their mind is old enough (the Near refresh).
- **Far**: they think now and then, on the Far refresh period, and their plans shape their day if they live their own day.
- **Background**: no mind; they follow their routine.

Who thinks follows your scene and what's in motion:

- **Everyone in your scene** thinks every turn, best on the ladder first, up to your Live limit. Your scene is where you are, or a place up to two levels inside or around it, as long as nothing between is spread out (a town or a region: a place whose parts a drive, a boat or a walk of more than ten minutes joins, or whose parts short walks don't all join, such as a town whose homes and sites have no routes between them). Someone the story put on the fourth floor is with you in the bath of a suite on it, even in a building drawn room by room with short walks between; someone in the room next door, somewhere else in a big building, or somewhere in town, isn't.
- **Someone elsewhere** thinks only while they're caught up in something that could come up soon: a plan or appointment with you within the next day, an invitation to you, an agenda of their own under way, your intent for them in GM mode, a place or activity the story holds them to, or their interrupting a time skip. They think on their level's refresh, or every turn when they're Live, and what they thought is on record, though it never shows in the Minds section. Being named, idling or going about their routine makes no one think.
- A mind that falls due while nothing calls it still keeps the level it fell due at, so a character's routine role never changes with who thinks.
- **Minds think while the engine reads your message**, not after it, so they no longer add their time to the wait before the reply. If your message turns out to be a time skip, the minds started for it stop (a time skip's minds think at its moments instead), and Diagnostics lists their calls as not needed. With the action preview on, they think while the preview waits: **Play** goes straight on with them, and **Cancel** stops them.
- **No mind thinks while nothing would read it**: when you switch the Minds section off for a chat (**GM → This chat → Sections**) and **Their last thoughts** is off in **What a mind reads**, no mind is asked (they're still marked as due, so nothing about who thinks changes when you switch it back on). A refresh you ask for in GM mode still runs.

The score adds up proximity, being named, their feelings toward you, how they figure in the story (quests, secrets, agendas, story roles, rules that name them), what is pending between you (invitations, appointments, plans, commitments), their tier and how recently you saw them. The world sets the weights and thresholds, and who is always Live or never gets a mind ([Character minds in a world](#character-minds-in-a-world)); **GM → Minds** can pin anyone at a level.

### Memory

Each character remembers in four layers: a **witness log** of what they saw happen or were told; **recall** of the turns they were present for (and only those: what was narrated of other people's private thoughts is ignored); **notes** written after a reply, for the levels you pick, which fade with time unless they matter; and **commitments** (promises, debts, grudges, favours, plans with someone) that stay until they are kept, broken or released. Faded and surplus notes can be folded into the character's **summary**, at most once a period for each character and for at most two characters a turn. The folding runs beside the turn's memory notes, on what the character held before the turn, so the reply's reading takes as long as the longer of the two; when a character's memory rewrites their summary that turn, their folding is asked again afterwards, into the new summary. It runs only while something reads what it folds: the memory step after the reply (on for a level in **Memory notes after the reply**), or minds that read **Their summary** or **Their notes and what they saw**. The world sets the defaults; your Settings can override any of them.

### Settings → Minds

**Settings → Minds** opens with a line on what minds are and the **Character minds** switch. Off, no character gets a mind, and the engine's own rules decide invitations as before. On, these sections follow:

- **How many think**: **Live minds a turn**, a number or **no limit**; and **Near and Far refreshes a turn**, how many Near and Far minds may think in one turn besides the Live ones, the most pressing first, or **no limit**. A refresh you ask for in GM mode always runs.
- **Near refresh**: when they come into the scene or it changes; when the latest message or reply names them; every so many turns; when their mind is so many game minutes old. Each number can be turned off.
- **Far refresh**: every so many game minutes, or never.
- **Memory notes after the reply**: a switch for each level (**Live characters who heard you**, and so on) that gets the memory step. On a turn that takes you somewhere else, the people you leave remember it, and the people you meet there don't, since they weren't there to hear it.
- **What a mind reads**: each source a mind may read, with its switch and, while it's on, its limit in tokens, which can be **no limit**. Within its limit a source comes whole, and nothing else cuts it. Who they are, where they are and whom they see, and what they mean to do are always in it.
  - **The story as it stands**, on by default: **Lore pinned to them and to where they are** (no limit); **Lore the turn activates**, the world's entries your message or the latest reply names by a key, an alias, a title or a lookup-table term, as World Info activates them (1,600 tokens); **The latest reply**, how it ended, for those who were there for it (the whole reply; with a number they read its end); and **Your message** (all of it).
  - **What they know**, on by default: **What they know: their own holdings**, while the chat tracks who knows what: the facts they hold and how they came by them, in place of the secrets in who they are. Its limit is **What they know, for each mind (tokens)** from **Knowledge** below, which this row shows and sets too. With **Track who knows what** off it says it reads nothing unless a chat turns tracking on.
  - **The chat as you see it**, off by default: **Recent chat**, the latest messages before the turn as you see them in the chat (hidden JSON blocks left out), oldest first, up to **Recent chat (messages)** and **Recent chat (tokens)**, whichever comes first (8 messages and 3,000 tokens when you turn it on; either can be **no limit**). It includes turns the character wasn't there for: it's the chat as you see it, not what they know. A mind that reads **The latest reply** doesn't get that reply twice: Recent chat gives the messages before it. **Character memory**, **Memory consolidation** and **Time-skip beats** read it too while it's on, with the same limits, and so does the time skip's plan for those whose skip the model plans; their step cards have no Recent chat of their own. The **Context** panel counts the messages each mind was given.
  - **What they usually do and mean to do**, on by default: **Their usual routine** (400 tokens, in whole lines): their routine blocks and their own place and activity schedules, as the world and the story set them, while their routine isn't off ([Routines and schedules](#routines-and-schedules)). It's what they usually do, theirs to weigh, never what they must do: a private block only when it's their own, and never the day the planner lays or the friends it fills free time with. **Their plans** (400 tokens, in whole lines): their open plans, soonest first, with when, where and who else is in it, from what the story's turns stated and what you set in GM mode. A plan they share with others is each one's: in its own words for the one whose memory took it from the turn (and for everyone, once you write its words in GM mode), and for the others as its kind, time, place and people, since one character's memory never speaks for another's. The **Context** panel says how many lines of routine and how many plans each mind got, and when some were left out over the limit.
  - **Their own memory and life**, off by default: **Their summary**, **Their notes and what they saw**, **Their last thoughts**, **Turns they were there for** (its limit is recall's tokens, a memory setting) and **Their life: money, the day and commitments** (today as the day planner lays it). Each can carry what the story never set up: the planner fills a free evening with dinners and friends, and a commitment their memory took from narration that showed another character's mind isn't theirs. Switched on, each is read within its limit; what another character's memory says it told them never reaches a mind.
  - **Their things**, on by default (300 tokens, in whole lines), listed with their own after their life: what they have on them, what they hold for someone else and since when, what of theirs someone else has ("Jordan Ellis has your truck keys (since Tue 2:00 PM)", or lent until when), and what of theirs is missing. A thing taken from them reads as taken, with whoever has it unknown to them: a mind never learns who stole it from this.
  - Settings saved before **What a mind reads** keep their limits: the latest reply's allowance, and, if you set the Character minds step's lore tokens yourself (a **Custom** preset), those as both lore limits; a preset's own lore tokens give way to the defaults above. The memory step after the reply reads the turn within the latest reply's and your message's limits.
- **Advanced: the Minds section budget and time skips**, folded away: **Minds section budget (tokens)**, the narrator's Minds section's own budget, apart from the section budget, so minds never crowd out Scene or People (when the minds don't all fit, the characters lowest on the ladder are left out of it first, and the Stance section tells of them instead); and under **Time skips**, **Beats a skip may stop at** and **Minds at each beat**, either of which can be **no limit**.

Three sections are there whether minds are on or off:

- **Knowledge**: who knows what. The knowledge step runs after each reply, once the reply's reading is in (it reads what the reading found), so it runs whether character minds are on or off. It rides on the story: what happens in a scene is known to everyone who was there through the chat itself, so it records only the few facts, usually none and at most one or two, that would change what someone does, says, hides, or believes about another person (a secret, a revelation or a lie; something a person would act on, hide, trade, or be hurt or helped by; a promise or debt; an open question someone is pursuing). Something said or done in plain view is recorded only when it is one of these, so a revelation or a promise made aloud still is; the story holds the rest, and small talk, advice and description never are. Each new fact says why it matters and to whom, which **GM → Minds** shows beside it; one that doesn't say is left out, and the log says so. A fact is made once: each call of the step is shown the facts already in play and, with **One call per character**, those the calls before it made this turn, and is told to have whoever learns one of them, however it is worded, learn that fact rather than make it again. Should the step say one again anyway (and so may a later turn), worded the same but for case, spacing or punctuation, it is the fact already there, not a new one, so it counts once against the limits below, keeps its first wording and reason, and whoever learns it later simply joins those who hold it. What happens in front of everyone reaches everyone there, your character included, unless the story shows someone missed it (a whisper out of earshot, someone asleep). Someone there for only part of a turn (who arrives during it, is met at its end, or leaves partway) witnesses none of it, and learns what happened in it only when someone tells them.
  - **Track who knows what**: on, each character holds the facts they know; after each reply the knowledge step works out who learned what, minds read what they know, the narrator gets **Who knows what**, and Play shows **What you know**. A chat's own switch in **GM → This chat** wins. While it's on, the memory step no longer records who told whom what: the knowledge step records telling.
  - **Suspicion threshold (%)**, in steps of 5: a fact someone holds less surely than this is a suspicion. A chat takes a new threshold from its next turn.
  - **The knowledge step**: **One call per scene** (the default) works out everyone who could perceive the turn at once; **One call per character** asks about each of them alone, a call each, one after another, so each is shown the facts the ones before it made.
  - **Calls at once** (1 to 8, default 1): how many per-character knowledge calls run together. Each answer is checked as it comes back, and a fact two calls make in the same words is one fact. At 1, calls go one after another, as before.
  - **Limits**: **What they know, for each mind (tokens)**, **Who knows what, for the narrator (tokens)**, **Who knows what: facts per person** and **The knowledge step's context (tokens)**; under **More limits**, **A fact's claim (characters)**, **Facts made in play, per chat**, **People who may hold one fact**, **New facts a turn** (3 by default: most turns need none), **Learnings a turn** and **A fact stays in play for (turns)**. Each is a whole number with a line on what it limits. A new fact counts against the turn's and the chat's limits only once someone learns it. A refusal names the limit it hit. **Who knows what** tells the narrator a fact at a time (who holds it, and who with you doesn't know it), the most relevant first, a secret before anything else; a fact everyone here holds alike, you included, with no one keeping it from anyone, is one line, "Everyone here: …". Over its limit it leaves out whole facts from the end, never part of one.
  - **Texts**: the words knowledge sends a model besides its own prompt, folded by where they appear: **What a mind reads**, **How a fact someone holds reads**, **The narrator's Who knows what** (including **What everyone here holds alike**) and **The knowledge step's context** (including **Facts this turn already made**, **Reusing a listed fact**, which tells the step that a fact it lists is one fact however it is worded, and **What the turn can still record**, the new facts, learnings and room in the chat it has left). Each starts from the shipped text with a **Shipped** or **Your own** badge, and **Save**, **Revert** and **Reset**. Placeholders such as `{{claim}}` must stay: a wording that leaves one out can't be saved. Saving the shipped text unchanged, or an empty one, keeps no copy.
- **Memory**: **Your overrides**, folded away, with a badge counting the ones you set. Each of the world's memory settings (see [Character minds in a world](#character-minds-in-a-world)) says "The world's applies (built in: …)" until you set your own; then it shows a **Yours** badge and **Use the world's**, which goes back to the world's.
- **Prompts**: a row for each minds step (Character minds, Character memory, Time-skip beats, Memory consolidation, Who knows what), with a **Built-in** or **Your own** badge. Open it for the text, with **Save**, **Revert** and **Reset to the default**; the built-in text is under **The built-in default**, inside it. **Save** keeps your own; **Reset to the default** drops it. Saving the built-in text unchanged keeps no copy, so you get later improvements to it. Your own prompt wins over a world's own step template for that step, and the world's template over the built-in. **Who knows what** is the knowledge step's own prompt.
- **Lengths**: how long each text the minds steps write may be, in characters (a mind's monologue, depiction, intent, each reaction, mood and why they'd interrupt a time skip; each memory note, commitment, plan and thing told; their summary; why a time-skip moment is worth stopping at), how many of each list one answer gives (reactions, notes, commitments, plans, things told), and how long your own intent for a character may be in **GM → Minds**. Each step's model is told these lengths after its answer format, whoever worded it, so it writes to them. A longer text is cut at a word and ends in …, and a longer list loses its last items; when that happens the call's entry in the **Context** panel and your diagnostics log say what was cut and that this is where to raise it. What you write in **GM → Minds** is refused past its length, never cut. A length that differs from the default shows the default beside it. Changing one never changes what a past turn recorded.

The **Model use** presets set minds too: **Lean** turns them off; **Standard** runs up to 3 Live minds and 2 refreshes a turn, with memory notes for Live characters; **Full** has no limits, with notes for Live and Near. Picking a preset resets everything above but what a mind reads, knowledge, your prompts, your memory and your lengths; changing any of the rest makes the preset **Custom**. The Minds section's own budget is 1,200 tokens on Lean, 2,400 on Standard and 4,000 on Full, and a mind recalls up to 4,000 tokens of past turns unless the world or your memory overrides say otherwise (either can say **no limit**). Where each minds step runs (its connection, model, effort and max tokens) is in **Settings → Steps and connections**, as for the other steps; what a mind reads, its lore included, is set here, and the memory, time-skip and consolidation steps read only the lore pinned to them in Build. A minds step you haven't set there shows **On**, on the helper model with 8,192 max tokens, because that is how it runs; only its **Enabled** switch turns it off. **Time-skip beats** is the exception: it shows **Off**, and runs only once you turn it on. Minds' calls aren't counted in the presets' step counts above: a turn makes one call per mind that runs, and after the reply one memory call per character who heard your message, at a level you picked, and now and then a consolidation.

### Minds in GM mode

**GM → Minds** starts with the chat's time (**Now**) and **This world's secrecy**, then **The player character**, whose row opens what your own character knows (they have no mind: you decide for them), then **Search by name** and a level filter (**Every level**, or one level). Below, every character but you, closest on the ladder first: their name, their level, when they last thought (or **No mind yet**), a **Pinned**, **Refresh** or **Intent** badge, and their score. Tap someone for their page, with a back button to **Minds**:

- **Fidelity**: their level and score, and each thing that added to it. Under **Pin and refresh**, **Pin their fidelity** opens a page that keeps them at a level in this chat whatever their score (**By score** unpins); its button stays off until you pick something other than what's in force. The author's pin, if any, has its own row. **Refresh their mind next turn** opens a page that asks for a fresh mind on the next turn, whatever the refresh settings say.
- **Your intent for them**: what they mean to do now, as you set it. Its page has **What they mean to do**, in your own words (up to 1,200 characters, or what **Settings → Minds → Lengths** sets), and **How they go about it**: **Their mind decides** (the default), **Reactive** (they answer the scene and what they're asked, and start nothing of their own) or **Proactive** (they pursue their own aims without waiting to be asked). Either can be set alone. Their own mind reads it first, right after who they are, and works from it, so the story follows what they then intend; it is never cut to fit, and no other character's mind, the narrator or anything in Play ever sees it. It stays until you change it: emptying both, or **Clear intent**, clears it, and its edit's **Undo** brings it back. Swipes keep it, as they keep a pin.
- **Latest mind**: when and why it ran, where they were, their mood, monologue, depiction, intent and reactions, and whether it interrupted a time skip. A **To the narrator** badge marks what the narrator got under the world's secrecy, and a warning says when a depiction was kept from the narrator because it gave something away.
- **What they know**: each fact they hold, with how they came by it (**From the start**, **Witnessed**, **Told by** someone, **Inferred** or **Guessed**) and since when, why it matters and to whom (**Matters:**, as the knowledge step said it; a fact from the world, or one recorded before the step said so, has none), what they make of it and how sure they are, whom they keep it from, and what it rests on: another fact they hold, or the line of their witness log itself. A **Suspicion** badge marks one held less surely than the threshold in **Settings → Minds → Knowledge** (70% by default), and a fact that is false or an open question says so. Each row opens its page: **How they came by it**, **Who told them** (a told one needs someone), **What they make of it**, **How sure (percent)**, **Whom they keep it from** (**No one**, **Everyone** or **Some people**) and **What it rests on**, then **Save**, and **Delete holding** at the bottom. **Add a holding** gives them a fact the chat already holds that they don't. Your own character's page has the same list. Each change has its edit's **Undo**, and swipes and Re-runs keep it as they keep any edit.
- **Plans**, **Commitments** and **Memory notes**: each row opens the item's own page to edit it, with **Delete plan** (or **Delete commitment**, **Delete note**) at the bottom, and **Add a plan**, **Add a commitment** and **Add a note** open a page for a new one. A plan has what it is, its kind, when it starts (hours from now) and how long it lasts, where, with whom, a priority from 1 (whenever it fits) to 5 (it must happen) and its status. A plan with others in it is theirs too: adding, editing or deleting it on anyone's page does it for everyone in it (each keeps their own status), someone you take out of it loses it, and someone you add gets it. A plan to **Pay someone** or **Lend money** also has an amount, shown beside its kind, and needs someone it is with: the money goes to the first of them at the plan's time. A plan their mind made in a chat from before shows a **Thought** badge and "a thought from their mind, which drives nothing": it moves no one, pays nothing and stops no skip, and isn't counted among the open plans. A commitment has its kind, its text, who it is with, an optional due time (hours from now) and its status. A note has its text and a salience from 1 to 5, and shows how much it has faded.
- **Summary** and **Witnessed**: what they have folded into their summary, and what they saw or were told.
- **Run their mind now** asks for their mind at once, and **Pinned lore** shows what is pinned to them and to where they are, with **Edit in Build** to change it.

Above the list, **Run minds now** has **Run everyone Live and Near (…)**, which says how many minds it runs and asks first (**Run … minds** or **Cancel**), since each is a model call made at once, and **Choose who to run**: tap people in the list to pick them, or use the bar under the list, which stays above GM mode's tabs, whose **Select all** picks everyone the list shows (its search and level filter as they stand) and a button for each level under **Select a level** exactly the characters at that level, then **Run their minds**. A run asks each mind from the chat so far and their lore, as a turn would (your World Info as it is now, each entry kept as secret as a turn keeps it), four at a time. Its card shows each mind as it goes, **Waiting**, **Thinking**, **Recorded** or **Failed** with why (the time ran out, the call failed, the answer was empty or cut off), with its time, tokens and the pinned lore it read; **Cancel the run** stops it and records nothing. When it ends, what came back is recorded as one edit, and **Undo this run** takes it back. A run between turns reads how the latest reply ended; a run while a reply is being written lands when it finishes. It stops at your **Minds run on demand** limit in **Settings → Timeouts**: 10 minutes unless you change it, or no limit.

Each change is a GM edit like any other: one correction with its own **Undo**. Minds stay in GM mode: nothing a mind thinks reaches Play, the Journal, People or chips. **GM → This chat → Context** lists each mind call with the pinned lore it read and whether any was cut.

### Routines in GM mode

**GM → Routines** shows the routines and schedules everyone follows in this chat. A routine or a schedule is never a script. By default it only informs: the story and planning decide what people do, and only Background characters keep an enforced routine. Even then the story leads whoever shares a scene with you, and no routine interrupts play. The world's routines and schedules are edited in Build (**The world's routines are in Build** goes there); what you change here is this chat's alone.

The list has everyone but you, closest on the minds ladder first, with **Search by name** and a filter (everyone, **Changed in this chat**, **Routine leads**, **Story leads; routine informs**, **No routine**). Each row shows their fidelity and how much their routine binds them, from their routine's role, and a gold badge when this chat changed their routine or schedules: **Changed by you**, **Changed by the story** (a reply), **Changed from your message** (a routine your message stated, added with **Yes**), or more than one ("Changed by you and the story"). Tap someone for their page:

- **How much their routine binds them**, from the role it has for them (the role of their level, as the world sets it, unless this chat sets theirs): **Story leads; routine informs** when it's informative (the story and planning decide what they do, and their routine is only context for their mind and the narrator) or enforced while the planner doesn't lay their day (it needs an agent who acts, at Cast life 3 or higher); **Routine leads** when it's enforced and the planner lays their day; and **No routine** when its role is off or their routine is off in this chat. The lines under it say what the role does, where it comes from and, for an enforced routine, what the world's awareness setting does for them. **In this chat**, **Their routine's role**, **Story and lore awareness** and **In a time skip** open one page with all four: **Follows their routine**, **Follows it, no invitations** or **No routine**; **Their level's default** (naming it), **Informative**, **Enforced** or **Off**; **Their level's default**, **Stay put**, **Follow their routine** or **Planned by the model** ([Routines and schedules](#routines-and-schedules)); and **As the world has it**, **Story first**, **Balanced** or **Routine first**. **Save for this chat** makes it one edit; **Use the world's settings** clears what you set.
- **What the story holds now**: a place or activity the story established, which their routine yields to, and for how long.
- **Planned for the skip**, once a time skip has been planned: what the model planned for them in the current or last skip (**This skip** or **The last skip**), each block with where, how far into the skip and for how long, and the plan's note when the call failed or was skipped. Whenever no block covered them, they kept to their sleep and meals.
- **Their routine**: each block with when and where it happens, whose it is (their own, a group's, a kind's, a selector's, the world's default, the basic needs, or **Changed in this chat**), what it rests on (authored, a lore entry, a sheet field, the story or you, with how long ago) and what it replaces. Tap a block to change it for this chat with the same form as Build's, **Switch it off for them**, or, for a change this chat made, **Use the world's block** (or **Remove the block** when only this chat has it). The block's page shows the lore entry's quote, with **Open the entry** in Build. **Add a block for this chat** adds one only this chat has.
- **Their schedules**: each schedule that applies to them, the most specific owner first for each thing schedules set, with what it rests on, and **In force**, **Shadowed** (a more specific schedule wins where both run) or this chat's change. Tap one to give them **Their own version** in this chat, with Build's schedule form, or put it **Off for them** until you clear it or for some hours; **Use the world's schedule** drops the change.
- **Today**: their day as the planner laid it, each block with where, why it is there (their routine, a schedule, a plan, an appointment, a trip, an invitation, free time) and the reasons it gave.
- **Invitations**: the ones they made and received, each with where it came from: a plan the story made, a block of their routine (with its source), you, or one recorded before invitations needed a ground.
- **Changes made in play**: each block or schedule a reply or your own message changed in this chat, with where it came from (**The story** for a reply, **From your message** for one you said **Yes** to), the turn it came from (its id shows under **Show ids**) and how long ago. A block added this way is in force from the next day, and its row says **In force from the next day** until then. Tap one in force to keep it, change it or go back to the world's.

Each change is a GM edit like any other: one correction with its own **Undo**, and the list's badge follows it.

## Extraction modes

After the narrator writes, the engine reads the reply for changes the story implies. Each kind of change is a **type**, and each type has a **mode**.

| Mode | What happens |
|---|---|
| **auto** | The change is checked and applied, and an **Undo** chip appears. |
| **confirm** | A chip asks you first, with **Apply** and **No**. If you ignore it, it expires when you send your next message and nothing is applied. |
| **never** | The change is ignored. |

| Type in Settings (as a Mode change chip names it) | What it covers | Starting mode |
|---|---|---|
| **Time passing** (Time skips) · `time_advance` | The story jumps ahead ("three hours later"). Up to [Skips without asking](#skips-without-asking) (a day by default) applies with the world caught up (under confirm, when you tap **Apply**), whether or not the clock is paused or a fight is under way; a longer jump becomes a **Run timelapse** chip in either mode, and one of more than 366 days runs at most 366 days at a time, each step ending with **Continue timelapse**. No time the story tells is dropped | auto |
| **Moving somewhere** (Moves) · `location` | Someone moves. Moves are story first: a move the reply tells lands even where no route leads, whatever the world's travel simulation says (it holds only **Go to** to the routes), and of anyone, a body included. The reading is offered the places you can see by name, the ones the reply and your message name first, then the nearest, so a room deep inside a big building is named as readily as the town; a move it gives by a place's name goes to the place by that name you know, the nearest one when two share it. A reply that sends someone to their usual spot ("Danny heads out to his usual lunch place") moves them to the place their routine has for now, as you could know it: never a private part of their routine or a place you haven't found, and never someone the reply doesn't send anywhere. A reply that has you somewhere you know of, other than where the engine has you, without telling the move (it takes it as given) gets an **Apply** chip for your move there, whatever the mode (none on **never**): a place the story only implies may be a flashback, a phone call or a misread, so you stay where you are until you tap **Apply**, and your answer doesn't count toward learning. None when your own action this turn took you elsewhere: what you do comes before what the reply takes as given. A move the engine can't keep gets a **Not tracked** receipt in either mode: a move of someone you don't know, or to a place you don't know or the world doesn't have. It shows the reply's words as written and an id only as "someone you don't know" or "a place you don't know" ("Not tracked: Someone you don't know went to a place you don't know"), the same whether the reply made them up or the world keeps them from you | auto |
| **New places the story brings in (a room, a booth, a new bar)** (New places) · `place_make` | The reply puts someone in a room, a spot or a new place the world doesn't have, and says what it's in ("slides into the back booth at the Rusty Anchor"): the place is made there, found first if the world has one by that name there (so "the booth" next turn is that booth), and they move in. A new venue in town asks first unless you pin the type. A reply makes at most the world's places per reply (2) and a chat the world's places per chat (64), both in **Build → World**; one past either gets a **Not tracked** receipt | auto |
| **Conditions (hurt, tired…)** (Condition changes) · `condition` | A status changes (tired, injured, soaked) | auto |
| **Learning a fact** (New facts) · `knows` | You learn of a public person, thing or place the reply shows you, or hear of a place you didn't know (never a secret one). A fact told only in words, or one naming someone or somewhere you don't know, has no place in the engine: it gets a **Not tracked** receipt, the words as the reply wrote them ("You learn: The ferry stops running at nine.") and an id you don't know only as "something you don't know", and the story keeps it | confirm |
| **Where things are (picked up, put down, put away, fetched)** (Things moved, picked up or put down) · `item_move` | Something is picked up, put down, put into something or somewhere, or shown in someone's hands: the thing the reply's words mean, found where the story has it. A thing of yours left elsewhere that the reply has you use is **brought along**, with a receipt saying where it was. A built-in thing moves only when you tap **Apply** | auto |
| **Handing things over (holding, lending, giving back)** (Things handed over, lent or given back) · `item_hand` | Someone hands a thing over to hold, lends it, or gives it back, and it stays whose it was. Holding it lets the receiver use it, unless they could already (a mechanic handed the shop's tool at the shop) | auto |
| **Whose things are (gifts, sales, trades)** (Things changing owner) · `item_own` | A thing becomes someone else's: a gift ("Keep the jacket"), a sale, a trade, or the reply saying whose it is now | confirm |
| **New things the story brings in** (New things) · `item_make` | The story acts on a thing the world doesn't have yet (picks it up, puts it away, hands it over, uses it), so it is made where the story has it, whose the story makes it. One that costs more than the world's claim cap or is bigger than an armful asks first, unless you pin the type | auto |
| **Using things up** (Things used up) · `item_use` | Some or all of a thing is used up | auto |
| **Putting on or taking off** (Things put on or off) · `item_wear` | Something is put on or taken off | auto |
| **Wear and damage to things** (Wear and damage) · `item_condition` | A thing wears, breaks or is mended, as far as the reply says | auto |
| **Losing things, or having them stolen** (Things lost or stolen) · `item_lose` | A thing is lost, or stolen into someone's hands while it stays its owner's | confirm |
| **Purchases** · `purchase` | A narrated purchase, priced from the catalog. When the seller has the thing (you sell Marcus your old guitar), that same thing changes hands and the price goes to the seller, with no sales tax. Something bought that's too big to carry goes to the buyer's home, never into their hands | confirm |
| **Money transfers** (Payments) · `transfer` | Money moves between people, or between someone's own accounts | confirm |
| **Feelings toward others** (Feelings) · `regard` | Someone's feelings change, as far as the reply says, each feeling staying between −100 and 100 | confirm |
| **Quantities** · `quantity` | A count changes (ammunition, supplies) | confirm (no starting mode of its own; Settings shows *From the field's bound*) |
| **New people** (New characters) · `spawn` | A new character appears (up to the world's limit, 24 by default). A name someone you know already has is that person, so no one is added and a **Not tracked** receipt says so; a thing introduced as a character gets one too. A name only someone you don't know has is left out without a word, so it never tells you such a person exists | confirm |
| **Life and death (dying, dead, collapsed…)** (Life and death) · `life` | Someone dies, is dying, collapses or comes round (what you already knew is left out) | confirm |
| **Routines (a new job, a habit, a move…)** (Routine changes) · `routine` | The reply says someone's week has changed for good: a new job or shift, a new habit, one they gave up, or a move to a new home. It changes their routine in this chat from the next day (what they gave up, and a move, at once), and GM → Routine lists it among the changes the story made, with its turn. Only someone the reply names (or you), at places and with people you know of; a habit only mentioned, or what is already their routine or schedule, changes nothing, and so does a new shift that clashes with their schedule unless the reply says it takes its place. A character's thoughts never count | auto |
| **Votes on organizations' motions (for, against, abstaining)** (Votes on motions) · `vote` | Someone votes on an open motion you are in, in so many words; applied, the vote is cast, and a vote that carries the motion carries out what it decides. Never one they lean toward or promise | confirm |
| **Answers to requests for approval (approved, denied, withdrawn)** · `approval` | Someone answers a request for approval you asked or may answer; applied, an approval does the act then. Only someone up its chain of command may approve or deny it, and only its requester withdraws it | confirm |
| **A stream earning more or less for a while, as the story tells it** (Changes to revenue streams) · `revenue` | What the reply says of a revenue stream whose author lets the agents adjust it, quoted: a factor of 0.25 to 4 on each of its payments, or an amount on each, for 1 to 31 days, at most four in force on a stream, and what they bring from outside the world in a month at most the world's cap on unlisted prices. Applied, the stream pays so until the days run out | confirm |
| **A project's step set back or costing more, as the story tells it** (Project delays and costs) · `project_note` | What the reply says of a project under way: a step not yet done set back by up to 30 days, or costing more at its end, up to the world's cap on narrated prices. The story never finishes, starts or drops a step | auto |
| **What you learn (who knows what)** · `learn` | While the chat tracks who knows what, the knowledge step after the reply finds your character learning a fact that matters: seeing or hearing it in front of them (unless the story shows they missed it), or being told it. What your character makes of things is yours, so it never has them work something out or doubt it. Applied, it shows in **Story → What you know**, and the narrator's **Who knows what** tells it as known to your character. The reply's own reading never proposes it | auto |
| **Secrets the story reveals to you** · `reveal` | While the chat tracks who knows what, the knowledge step finds the story revealing a secret to your character. Applied, the secret opens on your screens (its quest in **Quests**, and **What you know**). The reply's own reading never proposes it | confirm |

Beyond the mode, every field has a limit on how much a single change may be, and **the strictest rule always wins**. Fields a world marks as locked are never offered to the model. A reply that tells this turn's own action again is read as that action, never as a second change. A purchase takes in the seller's handover and the payment, so one sale is not applied three times, and a payer has to be an account.

The strict reading rules are: time jumps only for an explicit jump or stated duration; knowledge only for a public person, thing or place you newly learn of, never a sentence; feelings only when the prose states a change; handovers only between two characters; routines only for a lasting change the prose states, never a one-off, a plan, a wish or a habit only mentioned.

**Things.** The reading names things in the reply's own words, and the engine finds the thing they mean: one your character can see before one they can't. When the words fit more than one, a **Which one?** chip offers each, and **No**. A thing hidden in the scene that the words name is found, never made twice. A change naming someone you don't know gets a **Not tracked** receipt, unless its type is *Never*. Pins you set before these types existed carry over to them once: picking up and putting down to **Where things are**, handovers to **Handing things over**, and using up, wearing and losing to theirs; picking up and putting down pinned differently ask (*Confirm*).

**Settings → Changes from the reply** also sets what the reading is told and keeps of things, under **Things in the reply**:

- **Things the read is shown** (40, from 1 to 200): how many things the reading is told of, each with whose it is, its size and where it's kept: those the reply or your message names first, then those around your character, then your character's own.
- **A change's why, at most** (120 characters, from 20 to 500): the longest reason a change to a thing keeps, as the reply gave it.
- **Things made from a reply, at most** (6, from 0 to 500): how many things one reply may bring into the world. One more gets a **Not tracked** receipt instead; 0 makes none.

### Learning on your own model

The starting modes come from measurements on a small model. Your model may do better or worse, so the engine **learns from how you answer**, on each connection separately, with no extra model calls:

- Each time you confirm or reject a change, or undo an automatic one, that counts. Something applied automatically and not undone by the next turn also counts as accepted. An ignored confirm chip counts nothing, and so does your answer to the **Apply** chip for a move a reply only implies (see **Moving somewhere** above).
- When a `confirm` type reaches **90% accepted over its latest 30 outcomes** on a connection, it flips to `auto` there, and a **Mode change** chip tells you ("Purchases now apply automatically") with an **Undo** button.
- When an `auto` type's undo rate in the latest 30 passes **10%**, it flips back to `confirm`, with the same chip ("Purchases need your confirm again").
- **Undo** on either chip restores the previous mode and holds it for another 30 outcomes.

You can pin any type to **Auto**, **Confirm** or **Never** in **Settings → Changes from the reply** (under *Replies*): setting a mode pins it, and **Unpin** hands it back to learning. Each type shows where its mode comes from: *Pinned by you*, *Learned on <connection> (n kept, n rejected)*, *Default* (with *learning (n kept, n rejected)* once anything is counted) or *From the field's bound*. A pinned type is still counted but never flipped. The order of precedence is: your pinned mode, then what was learned on the turn's connection, then the starting mode, then the field's own bound.

### The GM assistant's changes

**Settings → Changes from the reply** has a second section, **The GM assistant's changes**: what an agent (the GM assistant) may change on its own, by type. No reply proposes these, so the engine's reading of a reply never uses them. Each type has **Auto**, **Confirm** or **Never**, where its mode comes from and **Unpin**, as the reply's types do:

| Type | What it covers | Starting mode |
|---|---|---|
| **Adding people or things** | Someone or something new. The assistant can't add a person once the chat has brought in as many new people as the world allows (its spawn cap, in **Build → World**), counting everyone the chat brought in, gone or not | confirm |
| **Removing people** | Taking a person out of the world | confirm |
| **Destroying things** | Taking anything but a person out of the world | confirm |
| **Money adjustments** | Adjusting someone's money, setting or adding to a money field, or correcting one | confirm |
| **Quest and secret stages** | Moving a quest or a secret to a stage | confirm |
| **Other GM edits (fields, tiers, minds, routines, sections, the clock)** | Every other edit the GM can make | auto |
| **Workspace notes** | The assistant's files in the chat's workspace: writing, editing, moving, deleting or pinning one | auto |
| **Where things are kept** | Where a thing goes back to when it's put away | auto |
| **Who may use things** | Letting someone use a thing, taking that back, and standing permissions to use someone's things | auto |
| **Things' names, descriptions and sizes** | A thing's name, the other names the story calls it by, its description and its size | auto |
| **Two things merged into one** | Two things that are one, made one (things made in play only, unless it's your own edit) | confirm |
| **Things removed from the world** | Taking a thing out of the story | confirm |

With the assistant set to apply at once, an **Auto** change applies with its **Undo**, a **Confirm** change waits for your **Apply**, and a **Never** change is refused. Your own GM edits are never held back by these. They learn from how you answer as the reply's types do, counted for each agent on each connection apart.

The **workspace** is the chat's own files, kept for the GM: notes that follow the chat's turns as everything else does, so a swipe, a Re-run or a Branch carries them, that can be pinned, and that every agent reads (GM → Workspace); the learned notes, under `learned/`, stay as they are whatever a swipe or an Undo does, and your global notes, under `~/`, are shared by every chat of yours. The assistant can copy a lore entry into a file there (under `lore/`) or draft one under `lore-drafts/`, but it never writes your World Info books or the world's lore: you apply a draft yourself, in **Build**, with Build's own **Save**.

## The Context inspector

**GM → This chat → Context**, under *Inspector*, shows what the engine put into the prompt for each generation. It doesn't need the GM gate, because it shows only what you may know. At the top are any warnings: the world's book wasn't attached to the chat or some of its section entries were missing (both put right for the next turn), your preset has a setting that causes trouble, or a damaged journal was replaced (with where the old file is kept). The **Older generation** and **Newer generation** buttons step through earlier generations. **Activation** reads **Activation confirmed** once Lumiverse reports every section the engine switched on as activated, and **Not confirmed** until then or when one wasn't, with a **Section budget** bar of the tokens used. **Engine sections** lists the sections it filled in (Outcome, Scene, People and so on) with their sizes and whether each was **Delivered**, **Not delivered** or **Unchecked**. A section too big for what was left of the section budget loses whole lines from its end until it fits, and its row says how many ("3 lines left out: over its caps or the section budget"; its own caps count there too). One whose first line alone doesn't fit shows **Clipped** and "Left out: over the section budget", and is never shown as delivered. The Minds section has a budget of its own: when some of its lines don't fit, its row says how many were left out ("2 lines left out: over its own budget"), and when none fit it is **Clipped** with "Left out: over its own budget". Sizes are the token counts of Lumiverse's tokenizer for each section's exact text, counted for your main model. Until Lumiverse has counted a section, or if that takes more than a second, the engine uses an estimate that stays above the real count. The [call meter](#the-call-meter) comes last.

**Lore the helpers read** lists, for each model step of the generation, what it read of the world's lore ([Lore for the helpers](#lore-for-the-helpers)): its searches (the prefetch and each lookup) with how many entries each found, the entries it was given with their tokens (and *cut to fit* when shortened), its lookups and whether they went as tools or the ask form, and why a lookup didn't happen. It shows only what you may know. A secret entry isn't there at all: not listed, and not counted in what a search found. An NPC's choice shows only whose choice it is (*Dev's choice*), never what they might do: it reads *private*, its lookups show as —, and nothing it found is counted or listed. A choice made only by people you haven't met or can't see isn't listed. A step that may read a secret shows its lookups as — with how many entries they found, never what it asked. Any other search that would name a secret, a secret entry, someone you haven't met or a place you don't know is withheld, unless you or the reply said those words that turn; a name counts as it does in a judged approach's reason (see [Lore for the helpers](#lore-for-the-helpers)), so someone counts by any word of their name, however short.

**Minds** lists the generation's minds calls ([Character minds](#character-minds)): whose mind it was, which step (a mind, a memory, a time skip's beats or consolidation), their level, when it ran (before the reply, after it, or in a time skip), whose prompt it used (the world's, yours or the built-in), and how it went, **recorded** or **failed**, with its seconds and tokens. Never a word of what a mind thought or said, nor whether the narrator got it: that is in **GM → Minds**. A call for someone you can't see or haven't met isn't listed, even when they are in the room with you.

With **Time-skip beats** turned on, its call before a time skip is listed there too, as the step alone: it thinks as no one, so it shows no one's name and no level.

### The People section

**People** tells the narrator who is in your scene ([The fidelity ladder](#the-fidelity-ladder) says what your scene takes in): your character first, by name, with any needs pressing on them from Cast life 4 ("Alex Rivera (you): hungry, tired, lonely"), then each person there you can see, marked by their tier when they're in your party ("Danny Okafor (party companion) is here."), what they're doing when you may see it, and how they regard you. The Minds and Stance sections read your scene the same way.

### The Stance section

**Stance** tells the narrator how the people with you stand toward you and what they are about, in a line or two each. It lists only the people you can see in your scene: someone hidden from you gets no line, and no line names them.

- **What they know of you:** whether they know you (a **Knows** relation to you, or regard for you) and how they regard you, in your world's words for regard ("warm", "cold"), never numbers. From Relationships 3 it also names any side of their regard that differs from the whole: "regard: warm (trust: cold)".
- **What they are about:** what they are doing, as the People section says it; for each public agenda they have, its cue, or its current step as **Play → People** shows it; and, for someone who lives their own day, the part of today they chose themselves (an NPC choice), the one under way or the next: "Decided: See friends at 18:00".
- **Cues:** how they behave while a secret of theirs stays hidden, as the world's author wrote it: never the secret, only how it shows ("evasive about the cellar"). A cue comes from the current stage of a track they own, or from their own **Cues** in Build, and it goes once its secret is out.

The section opens with a short note for the narrator: these lines say how people behave, and it never says outright what a cue only hints at. Whose knowledge the narrator has is the narrator's knowledge (see [What the narrator is fed](#what-the-narrator-is-fed)). A cue that would carry a secret you haven't found, or name someone or something you don't know, is left out on its own; the rest of the section stays.

Stance comes after the facts on the section budget (Outcome, Scene, People, Money and the rest), so on a small budget it is left out before them; only the newer Place and Property sections come after it and are left out first. Its cues come last of all: they go in only when the budget still has room once every section has its place, and otherwise Stance goes without them. The inspector's size for Stance, and the budget it shows as used, count Stance's people lines only, because the cues change with secrets you haven't found. To see it more often, raise **Engine budget (tokens)** in **Settings → Model use** (under **Advanced: budgets and the engine budget**), or give it a lower rank in the world's own sections. The engine's helpers (the brief, the outline and suggested choices) don't read it: a cue is a hint for the narrator to play, not a fact to state.

### Your lore in world chats

Your own lore books work in a world chat as they do anywhere else in Lumiverse: their entries activate by Lumiverse's own rules (keywords, constant entries, groups, recursion and the World Info budget), and LumiRPEngine never switches one on or off by writing to your book. It changes an entry only for the prompt at hand, in two cases. An entry with a secret line in its text (see **Secrets in your World Info** under [World lore](#world-lore)) reaches the narrator with each secret line marked as a secret, saying who knows it or whom it is kept from ("*its text* [secret; known to Marcus Delgado]"), or without that line, as the narrator's knowledge says (see [What the narrator is fed](#what-the-narrator-is-fed)); an entry that is only one secret line is left out of that prompt when its line is. An entry with the same text as a secret line (the copy a character's card brought, say, or one Lumiverse builds from a card for the prompt) reads as that secret. And an entry an earlier version of LumiRPEngine kept secret with its own fields, or one a secret entry of the world was indexed from, stays off until its gate holds, then comes on, as before. If the engine can't tell where the chat stands, every secret stays out of that prompt. If it doesn't answer in time, Lumiverse leaves every secret line out by itself, so no secret line reaches the prompt; but an entry kept secret only by something outside its own text (one a secret entry of the world was indexed from, or a copy with a secret line's text and no line of its own) then goes in as stored, so write the secret line into that entry too, or switch the copy off. Nothing waits for your other books, and a book LumiRPEngine can't read switches nothing off. The engine's helpers and minds also read your books as lore ([World Info as lore](#world-lore)). The Context inspector doesn't list your entries.

- **Your books are written only when you press for it:** in **Build → Lore → World Info entries** (a secret line into an entry's text, or the entry's own fields) and in [Secrecy in your World Info](#secrecy-in-your-world-info). No turn, save, commit or Upgrade switches one of your entries off or writes anything into it. An entry an earlier version of LumiRPEngine stored switched off for a secret stays off in its book, in chats that play no world too, until you move its book to secret lines, which lists those entries for you to switch back on.
- The engine switches on only the sections it fills in, in the world's own book, and keeps every other entry there switched off, even one you switch on by hand.
- The sections share the section budget in rank order. The Outcome section is never left out; a later section too big for what is left loses whole lines from its end until it fits.
- World Info recursion follows your own settings, so a section's text can trigger one of your entries, as any other entry's text can.
- If the engine stalls, the world's sections are missing from that turn's prompt (they stay switched off) and the Context inspector has no entry for it; a turn the engine couldn't plan in time is also marked **Not fully simulated**.
- The world's own lore (Build → Lore) is kept in the world. The engine's helpers read it ([Lore for the helpers](#lore-for-the-helpers)), and the narrator gets the lore about where your character is in the **Place** section.
- Your World Info is read as lore live, each entry as its book has it now, and nothing is copied into the world: **World Info as lore** under [World lore](#world-lore) says which books, and [Settings → Lore](#settings--lore) narrows them.

### Settings → Lore

**Settings → Lore** (in the **World Info** group) says which of your World Info books LumiRPEngine reads as lore, and how its agents find an entry in them. Each part has its default beside it, and **Reset** puts it back.

- **Books LumiRPEngine reads:** **Every book a chat reads** (the default: the world's **Lore books**, the chat's own books and your global ones) or **Only the books I tick**, which lists your books to tick. A ticked book is still read only where a chat reads it anyway. A ticked book you have since deleted stays listed as no longer one of your books, for you to untick. World Info folders can't be picked: Lumiverse doesn't tell extensions which folder a book is in.
- **Lore index:** the agents find a World Info entry by a **short id** in the lore index, one line per entry with its id, its title and what it holds. An entry with a keyword that matches the **Tag pattern** (a regular expression, by default `^lr\d{4}$`, so a keyword such as `lr0101`) goes by that keyword, its tag; any other entry gets an id of LumiRPEngine's own, the **Id prefix** (`lre`) and a number padded to **Id digits** (4), such as `lre0007`. An entry keeps its id for good, even as other entries come and go, so an id an agent wrote down still names the same entry. **Holds line (characters)** is how much of an entry's first words stands beside its title (100; 0 for the title alone).
- **Index tokens** (1,500): how much of a role's prompt the index may take, for a role whose context starts with the **Lore index** (**Settings → Agents → Context**), cut there and saying how many entries it left out. Any other agent asks for the index with its `lore_index` tool when it needs it.
- **Your own index entry:** pick the book and the entry of yours that already indexes your lore, and the agents get its text, as written, instead of the index LumiRPEngine makes; an id it names that is a tag reads that entry. **Clear** goes back to LumiRPEngine's index.
- **Index entry kept in sync:** the entry **Build → Lore → Build an index entry** wrote (see **The lore index** under [World lore](#world-lore)), which LumiRPEngine writes again whenever the index changes. **Stop** leaves the entry in its book as it stands, at once, even while a turn is under way; delete it there once you no longer want it. An entry you build while a turn runs is the one kept from then on.

### JSON blocks in replies

Some presets ask the AI to end every reply with a block of JSON between `<json>` and `</json>`, such as a tracker or a ledger. In a world chat LumiRPEngine keeps those blocks out of Lumiverse's World Info scanning and hides them in the chat, and the engine's own helpers read the reply without them. Your preset's own regex scripts still read every reply as the AI wrote it, so a preset that draws its block as a tracker keeps its tracker (LumiRPEngine hides only a block still showing as raw JSON, once every other display script has run), and one that strips old blocks from the prompt still finds them. The block itself stays exactly as the AI wrote it, so anything that reads it, such as the `{{jsonBlock}}` macro, still finds it. Regex scripts in the regex panel's LumiRPEngine folder do this, on new replies, older ones and edited ones: two in each world chat, and two global ones that change nothing but the wrapper LumiRPEngine saves around a block. To see the blocks, turn off **Hide JSON blocks in world chats** in **Settings → Preset** (under *Your preset*), which removes the hiding; World Info still skips the blocks. Turning it on again brings the hiding back, starting with the chat you have open. Deleting a world chat removes its scripts, and deleting a world removes its chats' scripts for good. Uninstalling LumiRPEngine leaves the scripts where they are, and they keep working together, so replies read as before; delete them all from the regex panel if you want them gone, and the saved replies then show their blocks, with World Info still skipping them.

### Your preset

The preset Lumiverse generates a world chat with has a say in it: the selected preset, else the one the chat's connection links, with any blocks a preset profile switches on or off for that chat. If Lumiverse can't say which preset that is, the engine stops before the reply and the chat says why. A block of it holding `{{outlet::lre_scene}}` (or `lre_outcome`, `lre_people` and so on) puts that section there instead of where the world places it; switched-off blocks named **RPEngine · extract**, **RPEngine · brief** and so on give that helper of the engine style and rules for your preset; and in world chats a switched-off **RPEngine · narrator** block adds your rules to the narrator's, after the engine's own, which every reply in a world chat gets: the order of authority first (your instructions, then what the story has established, then the sections, the world as it stands), then, under it, what the marks on facts your character doesn't know mean. Saving another preset changes nothing for the chat; saving this one between the engine's run and the reply makes the engine run again only when the save moves an outlet or changes a guidance block or the narrator rules. **Settings → Preset** (under *Your preset*) shows the **Active preset**, its **Outlets** and its **Guidance blocks** (each marked **Missing**, **Empty**, **Read** or **On**), and **Add RPEngine blocks to this preset**, which adds the missing guidance blocks, switched off and holding notes on what each is for, under an **RPEngine** category. RPE reads the preset as it is, and its **Prompt** page shows how it is cut into parts.

### Delivery verification

Activation isn't delivery: an entry can be switched on and still not reach the prompt. Opening the Context inspector runs a **test prompt build**, a dry run that sends nothing and saves nothing, and searches it for each section of the latest generation by its heading (such as "[Scene]"): a section it finds is **Delivered**, and one it doesn't is **Not delivered**. Outcome, Montage and Outline come only from a real turn's plan, which a dry run doesn't make, so the test build leaves them **Unchecked**, as it does a section left out over the budget. The check tells you whether a section reached the prompt, not where in the prompt it landed.

Anything the engine inserts into the prompt, a name, a price, a number, a model's output, an Import or Refine proposal, has every brace replaced with a look-alike (｛ and ｝), its control characters removed (Lumiverse's macro engine turns some of them back into braces on a later pass) and its `<user>`, `<char>` and `<bot>` shorthands broken with look-alike angle brackets (＜ and ＞), so it can never form a Lumiverse macro, however many times it is processed. The same goes for the labels and the text of the sections and for the messages the engine writes into your chat. Your own authored lore is not changed: it keeps its macros, as it would in a native World Book (see [Import and export](#import-and-export) for reviewing a world someone else made).

## Authoring

You build worlds in **Build**, which works at phone width as well as on a desktop. Each page opens scrolled to the top, and each has a back button at its top left.

- **Worlds.** Build opens on your worlds. Each row shows the world's name and how many revisions it has, with a **This chat** badge on the world the open chat plays; tap it to open the world. Its **More for** *name* button (⋯) holds **Export** and **Delete**, and Delete asks first. Where chats play the world, it names them ("Delete “*name*”? 2 chats play it: “*a*”, “*b*”. Their stories stay as played, at the revision each plays, and the chats play no world until you attach one."): a deleted world's chats keep their whole record and the revision they were at, and **Attach** gives them a world again, which comes in from that revision as an [Upgrade](#revisions-and-upgrade) does. A world no chat plays goes with all its revisions. The **Review before playing** card, after an import that needs one, sits at the top (see [Import and export](#import-and-export)).
- **New world** opens a page of its own. Under **Start from**, pick **Blank** or one of the [templates](#templates), each with a line on what it is. The **Name** field takes the template's name until you type your own; then tap **Create world**. **Or bring one in** has **Import a file** (an lre-world-v1 file, as Export writes it).
- **The world bar.** An open world has a bar that stays at the top as you scroll: the back button, the world's name, the state of its draft (**Uncommitted** or **All committed**, with its error and warning counts) and **Commit**. Its **Draft actions** button (⋯) holds **Discard changes**. See [Revisions and Upgrade](#revisions-and-upgrade).
- **The world home** lists the world's parts, **World**, **Calendar**, **Realism defaults**, **Tiers**, **Time defaults**, **Ruleset**, **Lore lookup table**, **Minds** and **Routines and the story**, each with a line on what it holds and a badge for its issues. Then come its collections in seven groups: **People & things** (**People**, the characters; **Organizations**, the companies, households, groups and trusts; **Things**, see [Things in Build](#things-in-build); **Money**, the money records; then Kinds, Classifications, Relations), **Places** (Places, Maps, Routes, Grids), **Daily life** (Routines, Schedules), **Rules** (Actions, Checks, Rules), **Story** (Tracks, Lore), **Engine** (Data tables, Sections, Step templates, Packs) and **Advanced** (**All entities**, every entity in one list), each with a count and an issue badge. Last are its **Tools**: **Problems**, **Import from lore**, **Index this text**, **Build / Refine with AI**, **Describe a map**, **Propose routines from lore**, **Playtest**, **Check world** and, when the chat you have open isn't a world chat yet, **Attach to this chat**. A tool that has run shows how its last run went (**Running**, **Done**, **Failed**, **Cancelled** or **Interrupted**).
- **Collections** list their items by name, with a search box once there are more than eight. **New** asks for the name of the new item and makes its id from the name ("Old Miller Tom" becomes `old_miller_tom`, with `_2` added if that id is taken). You can change the id under **Advanced**, or in the open with **Show ids** on. **Continue** opens the new item's page, and **Create** adds it to the draft. Data tables have no New: new tables come from packs, and you open one to edit this world's copy of it.
- **Changing many at once:** **Entities** and **Places** have **Select**. Tap rows to pick them; the bar under the list has **Select all** (what the search shows), a button for each tier under **Select a tier** (Entities: exactly that tier's entities) and **Clear**. **Change…** opens one change for everyone picked, saved to the draft at once: for entities, **Tier**, **Detail in one area** (a level override, or **From the tier** to remove it) and **Pin lore**; for places, **Default scene type** (**Set it for …** with a type typed or picked, or **Remove their scene type**; a place a map draws keeps the map's) and **Pin lore**. Every control starts on **Choose…**, and its button stays off until you pick a value; each card says how those it reaches stand now. An entity's tier, level and pins go to the people picked, unless you turn on **Also the … picked that aren't people**. A pin is added where it isn't pinned already, from the world's lore or a World Info book. Only what changes is written.
- **An item's page** is headed with its name. **Save to draft** and **Revert** sit in a bar that stays at the bottom of the page, and a part's page has the same bar. Your first keystroke in a field counts as a change, so **Save to draft** lights up at once. Its **Item actions** button (⋯) holds **Delete**, which asks first and names the item ("Delete the entity “Rosie Alvarez”? References to it will show in Problems.").

Ids stay out of sight unless you turn on **Settings → Display → Show ids**: then each item's id shows under its name in the lists, and Problems and the tools show their paths, codes and keys.

For the formulas you write in requirements, checks, derived fields and rules, see [EXPRESSIONS.md](EXPRESSIONS.md); for every kind, field, effect, function and lint code the packs offer, the [reference](REFERENCE.md); and for small worlds to copy from, [RECIPES.md](RECIPES.md).

### Editors and lint

Every part of the world has an editor built from its definition: a page of sections, each field with its label over it, then its help, then any problem with it. A reference to something in the world is picked by name; the line under a choice names its pack, or its id when two share a name. Money is typed as the currency writes it ("$12.50"), a length of time like "1 h 30 min" (a rule's cooldown, a route's time, a scene's tick), and a moment of the world's clock with a date picker (a day number and a time on a calendar of your own). A list of things (effects, a relation's fields, appointments, trips, chores, conditions, bills, a kit's pieces) shows a row for each, which opens as a sheet, with buttons to add, reorder and remove; a list the editor knows no shape for stays JSON. A new entry in a keyed list asks for a name and takes its id from it. An optional field says "· optional". Some parts get visual editors: maps are drawn and grids painted (see [Maps](#maps)), a schedule's day is a bar of slots and a track's stages a graph (see [Schedules and tracks](#schedules-and-tracks)), a routine block's time is a bar across the day (see [Routines in Build](#routines-in-build)), and formulas have an **expression** editor.

What you can define:

- **Kinds**: types of things, with single inheritance and typed fields (number, text, yes/no, choice, reference, list, dice, money, date, duration), each with a default, limits, **bands** (numbers shown as words), **derived** formulas, a lock, a limit on model-proposed changes, and a **visibility**: public, known by particular characters, or secret. A field's **Min** and **Max**, **Enum values**, **Ref kind**, **List of** and **Bands** show only for the types they fit, or when they already hold a value. A kind of yours with the id of one of the packs' kinds adds to it: your fields join the pack's (yours win where both define one), your label is shown, and it builds on what the pack's does unless you set **Extends**. So a Person of your own with only a Grit field still has the pack's Home, Bed and Place. Words you wrote in such a field before, an Employer given as a business's name or a Bed as "the couch", stay as you wrote them: Problems warns that they name nothing in the world, and the world still commits and imports; a reference field your own kind defines must name something. Problems warns when your **Extends** leaves some of the pack's fields behind (a Vehicle of yours that extends Item loses a container's Capacity), at the kind whose **Extends** does it, naming the fields and saying how to keep them. A kind of yours that holds things (something in the world is inside one of them, or an action gives, hands over or lends things to one) but isn't built on a container, a vehicle or a person is told too, since a transfer, a gift or a GM's Put inside into it is refused; and so is a kind of yours whose things the world moves (one of them is inside something, or an action moves, gives or lends one) but that isn't built on Item, since nothing can take it, give it or put it in anything: give it **Extends** Item. A lend counts only in an action that requires the lender to carry the thing (`carries(self, item)`), since a lent vehicle stays where it is parked.
- **Relations**: containment, ownership, use grants ("assigned to", loans, with an end time), worn or equipped, knowledge, membership, regard, routes, kinship and your own, with the kinds allowed at each end, cardinality, symmetry and an **inverse** (the relation the other side holds back). The relation picker lists the packs' relation types beside the world's own.
- **Entities**, **places** (with hierarchy and time zones), **routes** with travel modes, and optional **grids** with terrain. Entities, places, tracks and fields each have a **visibility**. **known_by** shows the thing to whoever knows it: a `knows` relation to it, or matching its **Known by**, a [selector](#selectors) (the members of a group, people of a kind or tier, chosen ids) that the editor shows beside a known_by visibility. A place can also be **discoverable**: hidden from the player until they have been there, are told of it or have it in `known_places`. A visibility the engine doesn't recognize, or a Known by it can't read in full, hides the thing rather than showing it, and lint says so. A point-of-view character with no place of its own starts at the world's start place, and from Space 3 arriving at a place drawn on a grid puts them on its cell. An entity's fields come grouped by realism domain, as its kind defines them; a derived field is shown, not edited, with a line saying what it reads; and a value its kind doesn't define sits under **Advanced: other values**. A relation's fields are typed by its relation type, and its **Until** is a moment of the world's clock.
- **Cues**, on a person: what the narrator sees them do while a secret of theirs stays hidden ("evasive about the cellar"), each standing for one secret: a secret lore entry (until its gate holds), a track (while it keeps something hidden: a truth not yet revealed, or the track itself) or a story flag (until it is set). Write how they act, never the secret; the stage cues of the tracks a person owns work the same way. They reach the narrator in the [Stance section](#the-stance-section).
- **Characters**: a person (any entity whose kind is a person, or extends one) opens on its own **Character page** rather than the generic form. A new one is made in the generic form first, then opens there. Its sections, in order:
  - **The header**: the name, the kind and tier, and **Player character** on yours.
  - **Who they are**: the name, kind, tier, classes and tags, how they start (life), and the fields of who they are (birthday, occupation, region, wealth and the kind's own). Each has its own input, with a plain label and its help under it; a number shows its range; a list of words such as traits is a list of chips.
  - **Where they live**: **Home**, **Sleeps** and **Where the story starts them**, each picked by the place's path, and **Starts inside** for a vehicle they start in (see [Picking a place, a bed or a list of words](#picking-a-place-a-bed-or-a-list-of-words)).
  - **Their day**: **Lives their own day (agendas and plans)**, their routines (each opens with **Edit their routine**) and schedules, each shown by its label or in words ("Weekdays 6:30 AM–12:00 PM: Alder Automotive, …"), and the fields of their day (activity, appointments, trips, chores).
  - **Work and money**: **Works for**: **A place** (a shop or workplace in the world, picked by its path) or **A business** picked by name; then the money fields (how they pay keeps its own editor).
  - **People**: their relations as **Family**, **Feelings**, **Groups** and **Other**, each with remove; **Add someone** picks how they're tied and who, people and groups searched by name. A relation's own details are under All fields.
  - **Stats**: the sheet's numbers and conditions.
  - **Things they have**: what they own and have, in groups: **Outfit**, **Carried**, **Wardrobe** (by holder), **At home**, **Elsewhere**, **Lent out** and **Holding for others** (At home leaves out what Wardrobe lists), each row with **Move…**, **Give…**, **Lend…** and **Remove**; **Give them a thing** opens **Add a thing** already set to them. Their vehicles and property open their pages.
  - **Secrets**: visibility, Known by and cues.
  - **Lore pinned to them**.
  - **All fields**, folded: the whole generic form, for anything the sections don't lay out.
  
  **Save to draft** saves only what you changed, so an edit made in another window to anything else stands; **Revert** drops your edits.
- **Maps**: the inside of a place, drawn on a grid or as linked places, which make that place's places, grid and routes (see [Maps](#maps)).
- **Actions**: a label and a **Description** (a line on what the action does: the helper that reads typed messages is told it, so it knows when your words mean this action), parameters, requirements (each with a **Hard lock** switch, below), an optional dice **check** with outcome bands (the odds shown to players are computed exactly from it), effects per outcome, a duration, and the activity it starts. A ref parameter can say **Only candidates matching** a [selector](#selectors): the Actions sheet offers only those, and typed text or a request naming anyone else is refused.
- **Hard locks.** A requirement is plain unless you switch on **Hard lock** under it. When a player types an action whose plain requirement isn't met, their words still happen in the story: the engine plays none of the action, the reply plays the words as plain roleplay, and a receipt says why. A hard lock refuses the attempt instead (**…: couldn't**), and the reply is told it failed. So mark hard every lock, every puzzle's solution and every guard on a secret: the safe that opens only once its code is found, the door that needs its own key, the papers someone keeps out of reach. **Existing worlds' locks are soft until you mark them:** a world made before hard locks has none, so every lock in it plays as roleplay for anyone who types past it, until you open the action here and switch on **Hard lock** for its requirement. The starter worlds come with theirs marked. Check lists, as a suggestion, each requirement that reads like a lock but isn't hard (`soft_lock`): on an action about a door or a container, one that the actor carries a named thing (a key), or a stage or flag the requirement reads together with one of the action's choices. A hard lock keeps its action in the Actions sheet while it's locked, even a step the story hasn't come to, so a typed attempt finds it; that's why a story step on its own (a stage or a flag the action needs before it's offered at all) is never suggested: it stays hidden until the story gets there, and marking it hard would tell the player it exists.
- **Rules**: "when this happens and that is true, do this", with run-once and cooldowns. A cascade stops at eight steps.
- **Tracks**: quests, secrets (revealed by conditions only), puzzles with attempts and a hint ladder, and NPC agendas.
- **Routines**: how people's days usually go, owner by owner (everyone's, a group's, a kind's, a selector's or someone's own), as blocks at a set time, within a window or optional, each resting on what you wrote, a lore entry or a sheet field (see [Routines in Build](#routines-in-build)).
- **Schedules**: calendar recurrence plus tables of fixed values or weighted choices, for shifts, timetables, opening hours, weather, bills, paydays and price paths. A schedule's owner is an entity, a group (its members), a kind, or a [selector](#selectors). When several set the same field of the same person, the most specific owner wins while its slot runs: their own schedule, then a group's, then their kind's, then a selector's; when the more specific slot ends, the field returns to the less specific one still running.
- **Lore**: what the world knows, as entries with a title, text, keywords, aliases, topics and links, where each came from, and secrets with a gate and the helpers that may read them, plus a lookup table of terms and topics (see [World lore](#world-lore)). It is kept in the world and the engine's helpers read it; the narrator gets the entries about where your character is, in the **Place** section.
- The **calendar** (Gregorian or your own) and **realism** defaults, with the band caps under the band labels.
- **Tiers**: the world's own ranks of importance, on the **Tiers** part. Each has a **Label**, a **Band** (Leads, Supporting or Background), a place in the order (its move up and move down buttons), an optional description, and whether its people live their own day (agendas and plans) by default. Type a name under **New tier's name** and tap **Add tier** to make a new one (its id comes from the name, under **Advanced**); a tier still in use can't be removed. **New people join** picks the tier for people the story introduces (by default the first Background tier). An entity's editor picks its tier from these, and its **Lives their own day** choice can follow the tier ("As its tier"); lint flags an entity whose tier the world doesn't have.
- **Section templates**: each section's **Guidance**, the text the narrator reads ahead of the section's lines, and where the section goes. **Build → Sections** lists every section the world has, and each one's page opens with its guidance. `{{lines}}` marks where the lines go and must stay in it: the field says so, and **Save to draft** waits until it does. A template may use only that marker; any other macro, control character or `<user>`-style shorthand in one is a lint error (`section_macro`), because it would run beside the engine's own values. Each built-in section's guidance tells the narrator what the section holds and that its lines are the authoritative, specific record to write from: the exact items, vehicles, rooms and their contents, people, amounts and conditions, used as given whenever the prose touches them, never a summary or a generic stand-in (and the Outcome's lines are narrated as having happened this turn, the Outline's beats are written in order, and so on). None of them outranks your own instructions or the story, which the narrator's rules put first. A world with no sections of its own gets the built-in ones, each marked **Built-in** in the list: its page shows its guidance, prefilled with the built-in text, with **Reset to default**, and saving keeps only the wording you changed (the world's `sectionTemplates`), so the sections you leave alone keep the built-in text and nothing else about the world changes. Once a world lists sections of its own, it gets only those, plus the built-in Minds unless it lists its own, so list `stance` to keep it; a listed section with a built-in section's id also has **Reset to default**. **Position** is `{"type":"chat_depth","role":"system","depth":1,"direction":"from_end"}` (that many messages from the end of the chat, or from its start), `{"type":"outlet","name":"…"}` (only where a `{{outlet::…}}` macro of that name stands, in your preset or a card, and nowhere else) or `{"type":"native"}` (where the section's entry sits in the world's book, which you can move in Lumiverse's World Info editor; a new one starts before the character). When you change a section's Position and commit, its entry in the world's book moves to match.
- **What a section is fed**: each section in **Build → Sections** also has **In the prompt** (off leaves it out), **Whose knowledge**, **At most lines** (its first lines), **At most tokens** (lines past it are cut from the end) and **Sources** (the renderers that fill it; none, its own). A section with none of these set goes in as it always has. See [What the narrator is fed](#what-the-narrator-is-fed).
- **Step templates**: your own wording for a model step, per purpose (interpret, NPC choices, puzzle answers, judging your approach, brief, outline, extract, suggested choices), with an output (text, decision, or `effects` for the steps that read changes, which run after the reply only) and the signals it runs on (a template's own signals replace the step's). A player's own prompt for the step (**Settings → Steps and connections**) wins over it. The brief, outline, extract and suggested-choices templates, and every `effects` template, can use engine placeholders: `{{world}}`, `{{pov}}`, `{{place}}`, `{{clock}}`, `{{scene}}`, `{{present}}`, `{{facts}}`, `{{outcome}}`, `{{beats}}`, `{{brief}}`, `{{user}}`, `{{reply}}`, `{{recent}}`, `{{gear}}` (what your character carries and wears), `{{money}}` (your character's money), `{{property}}` (what your character owns or may use), `{{positions}}` (who is here with your character, and what they're doing) and `{{knowledge}}` (what your character knows of the quests); each of the last five is what the section that tells it has now, empty in a world without that section. Two more go only to some steps: `{{usual}}`, the usual routines of the people with you, in extract, suggested-choices and `effects` templates, and `{{sections}}`, the sections the narrator has this turn, in an outline template. An interpret template fills in only `{{world}}` and `{{pov}}`, and none at all on a Decision connection; NPC-choice, puzzle-answer and judge templates are sent as you wrote them, with nothing filled in. The template's page lists the placeholders its purpose is given under **Placeholders**. `{{brief}}` is this turn's brief (an outline template that uses it waits for the brief; it's empty in the brief step itself), and `{{recent}}` is the Journal's last five lines before this turn, where each ✎ fix or GM edit you can see takes a line of its own. They are all player-visible: a line that would reveal an unrevealed truth is dropped before the call. A template whose output can't be used by its purpose, an unknown signal and a placeholder its step isn't given are lint warnings, and so is a blank template (it replaces the built-in instructions with none). Changes a step template proposes with `effects` are checked and bounded exactly like extraction, and money in them is stated as a plain amount in the world's currency (8 for $8), which the engine converts. Which steps run at all is still your choice, in **Settings → Steps and connections**. A world made with an earlier version keeps its step templates, except those for lore selection and for the contradiction check, which no longer exist; a secret entry's readers and the lore pinned to steps drop the contradiction check too.
- **Step templates for minds**: the four minds steps (Character minds, Character memory, Time-skip beats, Memory consolidation) take templates too, with output **text**. They can use `{{character}}`, `{{world}}`, `{{pov}}`, `{{place}}` and `{{clock}}`, and the engine always adds the answer format after them. Time-skip beats think for no one, so there `{{character}}` is empty, `{{place}}` is where your character is and `{{clock}}` is when the skip starts. A minds template runs on the minds' own cadence, so its signals are ignored, and only the first template of a step (by id) with any text is used, a blank one being skipped; lint says so for each. **Build → World → Minds** lists them and starts new ones from the built-in prompts (see [Character minds in a world](#character-minds-in-a-world)).

Formulas use a small, safe expression language: numbers, comparisons and a fixed set of functions. They can't run code and they always produce a finite number. `table(id, key, column)` reads a cell of one of the world's data tables, and `table_step(id, value, column)` reads the row whose number key is the largest at or below `value` (a level table keyed by XP, say). `roll(notation)` works only in an effect's arguments (a value that starts with `=`): the effect's event records the result, so a replay or a swipe sees the same number. Anywhere else a roll would change the odds you were shown and what the engine reads, so it is a lint error and reads as nothing. For classifications and groups: `kind_is(e, 'person')` (a kind or one inheriting from it), `in_class(e, 'role', 'antagonist')` (a value or one nested under it), `has_tag(e, 'smuggler')`, `member_of(e, harlows)` (directly or through a member group), `tier_of(e)` and `band_of(e)` (the band its tier belongs to).

In the **expression editor**, the **Operators and functions** button (ƒx) opens the operators in groups (**Arithmetic**, **Compare**, **Logic**) and every function a formula can call there: the math built-ins, the engine's own and the formulas of the packs the engine runs, taken from the engine itself, so the list never names one that doesn't exist. It also lists the **Names** the formula can read, by what the world calls them (their ids show with **Show ids** on), with find boxes for long lists. On a desktop these open under the formula; on a phone, where they wouldn't fit, ƒx opens the formula on a page of its own, and the **Open the formula editor** button opens that page at any width (**Done** closes it). As you type, chips suggest the ids, fields and functions that start with what you typed, and the names every formula can read (`self`, `pov`, `here`, `clock`, `hour`, `daypart` and the rest of the clock); tap one to put it in. Under every formula a line says in plain words what it reads and uses ("Reads Sam's cash and the hour; uses min."). An action's **Effects per outcome** are a list of effects for each outcome, with the check's outcomes suggested. An effect's **arguments** are edited one by one: each is a **Value** (`self`, `might`, `2`, or JSON for a list or an object) or a **Formula**, worked out when the effect runs; **Add argument** adds one by name, and its remove button takes it away. Only an argument's formula offers `roll(`.

#### Picking a place, a bed or a list of words

A character's places (their home, where the story starts them, where they are) show as paths, from the widest place in to the place itself: "Riverton › Elm Street › Elm Street Apartments › Floor 1 › **Apartment 2B**", with **Change…** beside it. **Change…** opens the place picker, headed with what you're picking and where it is now:

- **Search by name or where it is.** Each word you type matches the start of a word in the place's name or in the places around it, so "elm 2b" finds the Apartment 2B on Elm Street, and "kitchen elm apartments 2b" picks that flat's Kitchen out of every Kitchen in the world. Each result shows its path; the places whose own name has every word come first, then the shallower ones. **Enter** picks the first. A search shows at most 60, and says so: type another word to narrow it.
- **Suggestions**, before you type: the places the page thinks likely, such as where they are, their home or where your character is.
- **Browse** opens among the current place's neighbours, or at the top when there's none. **All places** and the places above it form a trail you tap to go back up. Each row says how many places it holds; **Choose** picks it, and **›** opens it.
- **Secret** marks a place the player doesn't see, and **Hidden until found** one they find by going there or being told of it.
- Where the place may be left empty, a button at the bottom clears it (**No home**, say).

**Sleeps** shows where they sleep, with its own **Change…**. Its picker offers what's likely first: **Sleeps at home** (no bed of their own; they sleep in their home, the default), then **Rooms in their home** and **Things in their home** (the beds, bunks, sofas and hammocks first), then **Somewhere else**: **Another place…** opens the place picker, and **A thing somewhere else…** finds a bed, a sofa or a hammock anywhere by its name or by where it is, each with its place's path. A part built into a vehicle (a pickup's truck bed, a glovebox) is never offered: it holds cargo, not a sleeper.

**A list of words** (traits, likes, things they'd say) shows as chips. Type one and press **Enter** or tap **Add**; **✕** on a chip removes it. Spaces are tidied, and a word already there, in any case, isn't added twice.

### Schedules and tracks

**Schedules.** A schedule's page starts with **Who and what**: **It applies to** either **Someone, a group or a kind**, picked by name under **Owner** (or **No one: a world process (weather, prices)**), or **A selector**; and **It sets**, the field or relation each slot's value sets (place, activity, weather, price…). **Repeats** is **Daily**, **Weekly** (tap the weekdays), **Monthly** (tap the days of the month) or **Yearly**, with **Not on** chips for the calendar's holidays and **From** and **Until** dates. **The day** draws the slots on a bar across the 24 hours. Tap a slot, on the bar or in the list under it, to open its sheet; drag a handle on the bar to move where one slot ends or the next starts (arrow keys move a focused handle by 15 minutes). The **Slot** button adds one. A slot's sheet has **Starts** and **Ends** (an end at or before the start runs past midnight), **Its value**, either **Fixed** or a **Weighted choice** (each **Choice** with its **Weight** and its share), **Given the slot before (Advanced)**, and **Remove slot**. A slot with bad times, no value or weights that add up to nothing, and slots that overlap, are marked **Fix**, and **Save to draft** says how many there are ("Save to draft · 2 to fix").

**Tracks.** A track's page starts with **The track**: **Label**, **Kind** (**Quest**, **Secret**, **Puzzle** or **Agenda**), **Who knows of it** (**Everyone**, **Those who know**, with a **Known by** selector, or **Secret**), **Whose it is**, picked by name (or **GM**: the world's own story, no one's in it, so no one starts out knowing it, knowing someone never reveals it, and its cue is something anyone may notice; GM mode shows it as the GM's), and **Checks roll while it's in progress**. **Stages** shows the stages as a **List** or a **Graph** (**Show the stages as**); a phone starts on the list and a desktop on the graph, which runs from the start at the top down. The start is marked **Start**, a stage nothing leads to says **Can't be reached** (a banner counts them), and on the graph a dashed red way is how a stage fails. Tap a stage to open its sheet:

- its fields: the label, the cue the player sees, the secret truth, the **Objectives**, a gate, hints, the effects on entering, rewards and a puzzle's answer. Each objective has **What the player reads**, the line they see in their quests, and **Met when**, its condition; one with no words shows the player its condition, so give it some. **Add objective** adds one, and **Add an item check** sits with them (see Items in Build);
  - **Rewards** are granted as the track leaves the stage onward (not when it fails); an end stage, which is never left, grants its rewards as the track reaches it. **Effects on entering** apply on reaching the stage either way.
  - **Hints**: on a puzzle stage with an answer, the player earns one for each wrong answer given at that stage; on any other stage, every hint is there as soon as the track reaches it. Either way the player opens them one at a time on the quest sheet, and the narrator is told only the opened ones.
  - A **cue** is what the player can notice at the stage, told in their quests. On a track a person other than the player character owns (**Whose it is**), while it still keeps something from the player character (a truth not yet revealed, or the track itself), the cue is how that person behaves instead: Stance tells it under their name while they're in the scene, and the quests never show it.
- **Make it the start** (the start says **Where the track starts** instead);
- **Next stages**: each **Goes to** a stage picked by name, **When** its condition holds, and the first that holds wins. The move buttons reorder them, **Open** *stage* opens the one it goes to, and **+ Next stage…** adds one;
- **Failing**: **It can fail**, then **It fails when** and **Then goes to**;
- **Delete stage**, which waits until another stage is the start.

The **Stage** button asks for the new stage's **Name** and what it **Comes after** (it is added there as a next stage, with "true" as its condition until you change it), then **Add the stage**.

On both pages the **Item actions** button (⋯) holds **Delete**, **JSON (Advanced)** shows the whole schedule or track as the world file keeps it, and **Save to draft** and **Revert** sit in the bar at the bottom. A stage's page has the track's own **Save track to draft** and **Revert track**: a stage is saved with its track.

**Items in Build.** A place's page lists its **Things here**, the furniture and what lies loose there, with **Add a thing here**; each row opens its thing, marked **Hidden until searched** when it is. A thing's page has **Hidden until searched**: on, it stays hidden until your character searches where it lies or opens what holds it (it must lie somewhere first), and **Visibility** reads the same; **Save to draft** keeps it. A thing's page leaves out what only a person has (life, cues, the Lumiverse character, their own day). A stage's **Add an item check**: pick **Has it with them**, **Has one like it** or **It lies at**, the thing (and the place), and **Add objective** writes the objective with the world's own ids and a plain line for the player ("Have the spare relay with you"), which **What the player reads** may change.

### Things in Build

**Build → Things** (under **People & things**) is the world's things, read as a chat would start, with **Search things** (by the words the story would use: names, the other names a thing goes by and its type). **By place** is a tree of places, what lies there and what holds what (Port Alder › 114 Harbor Street › Kitchen › Refrigerator), each row with its size, whose it is and its badges (**Hidden until searched**, a problem); **By owner** lists each owner with their things **On them**, **At home** and **Elsewhere**, and **Nobody's**. **+ Add a thing** asks four things:

1. **Name.**
2. **What is it?** Start with the kind: **Item**, **Clothing**, **Container**, **Kit**, **Weapon**, **Armor**, **Ammo** or **Vehicle**. Each kind lists the catalog types of its category; a pick fills in its size and details. A kind with no type picked sets the category (clothing, weapon, armor or ammo) or makes a container. Or say how big it is, each size with an example: **Pocket**, **Hand**, **Armful**, **Heavy**, **Furniture** or **Built-in**. **Vehicle** opens the vehicle picker and makes a vehicle of a class, with its make, model and year.
3. **Where is it?** One picker: places by their path, people, and the things that hold things, each with whose it is and where ("Toolbox (Jordan's, at Alder Automotive)"); one that can't take it says why.
4. **Whose?** A person, a household, a business or a group, or **Nobody**, with a line saying what that means ("Presumed the household's at 114 Harbor Street").

**Kept at** follows where it is unless you change it, and **Create** opens the thing. A thing's page has **Where it is** and **Kept at** (the place, holder or **On its owner** it goes back to), each with **Change…**; **Whose**; **Who may use it**, a chip for each ground someone may use it on ("The Kowalczyks · members", "Alder Automotive staff · at Alder Automotive", "Alder Automotive customers · while it trades"), each grant with when it ends and ✕, and **Let someone use it**; **What it is**: its type, size, the other names the story may call it (**Also called**), its description, quantity (from Gear 2), condition and slot (from Gear 3), **Sleep in or on** and **Consumable**; **Holds**, its contents with **+ Add inside** and the largest size it **Takes things up to**; **Secrecy**; and **Advanced**, every field, its relations and its id. It never shows a tier, a life or its own day.

**Problems** flags a thing too big to carry that the world puts on a person ("Refrigerator is on Jordan Ellis: too big to carry. Put it at 114 Harbor Street?") and one both lying somewhere and inside something, each with **Fix**; an authored thing no size answers for is a note only.

**The world's things settings** are the gear pack's (**Things settings** on Build → Things opens **Build → Packs → gear**): **Everyday carry** (who carries what from the start: by default people carry a wallet, keys and a phone; each rule a selector and the types it gives, and an empty list carries nothing), **Sizes by name** (each name's size, its other names, what it holds, and *Clothing at home rests in it*; the shipped names are marked, each change has its own reset, and there are **Use the shipped names** and a bring-back for names taken out: a fridge, a washer or a sofa is Furniture and a TV Heavy, while a mini fridge, a microwave or a hair dryer keeps its own size), the Gear section's **Not with you** lines (3), **Put away** (**Put things away**, on; **After (hours out of sight)**, 24; **From Gear level**, 3) and **Tidy up** (**Tidy impossible holds every turn**, on). The rest is the pack's config, below its fields: `sizes` (`ceiling`, the largest thing a person carries, Armful; `byCategory`, a catalog category's size; `byVolume`, the upper bounds in litres of Pocket, Hand, Armful and Heavy, 0.5, 5, 40 and 400; `byName`, the sizes by name), `access` (the implied rules: `household`, `housemates`, `partners`, `staff` and `customers` on, `family` off) and `fetchMinutes` (how far on foot a fetch reaches, 3). Whatever the config leaves out reads as its default. **Build → World** sets how many places the story may make, beside the people replies may introduce: **Places one reply may make** (2) and **Places a chat may make in play, in total** (64), each from 0 (none) to 500.

**Containers** and **Saved outfits** are rows there too: what a holder that isn't a person does when it lacks room or load on your own paths (*Refuse*, the default, or *Off*), how deep things nest (8), and how many outfits and loadouts a person keeps (12, from 0 to 50). Each shows **Changed** with its **Default**.

### Organizations in Build

**Build → Organizations** (under **People & things**) lists the world's organizations grouped **Companies**, **Households**, **Groups** and **Trusts** (and a party, where the world authors one), each with how many belong to it and a badge for its issues, with a search box past eight. **+ New** asks what it is (a company, a household, a group of one of the world's group kinds, or a trust) and its name, and makes its id from the name.

An organization's page has, from the top:

- **Basics**: its name, kind, structure, its places and hours, who knows of it, and **Change what it is** (below).
- **People**: its members by **Member of**, each with a title, a position, a rank and since when, and **Add a member**; its **Staff**, the jobs it employs, with **Add a job** (worker, title, position, how they're paid and the rate); its owners, each with a role, a share, units and a class; and **Acting for it**, the delegations to it, with **Let someone act for it** (who, the powers, the limits and who granted it).
- **Money**: its accounts as Money lists them, its pay schedule and payroll account, its dues, its payouts, its invoice terms, its **Depreciation** (the world's, none, or straight-line; see [Money in Build](#money-in-build)), and its **Revenue streams** (below).
- **Projects**, for a company, a group or a trust: its authored projects, each with its status, steps and cost, **Add a project** (a template, a name, and planned, or under way as the story begins or from a date) and **Remove**; a project's steps are edited on its own page.
- **Ownership**, for a company: its share classes and the votes a decision needs.
- **Decisions**: how its motions count their votes (shares or members), their quorum, pass mark and days, its standing votes, and how many days a request for approval waits.
- **Property and entry**: its properties, and its places with who may enter them.
- **All fields**.

The organization's own fields save with **Save to draft**, as a patch of only what changed; a member's link, a job and a delegation are someone else's, each saved to the draft from its own sheet at once.

**Problems** tells an organization's mistakes in plain words, most with **Fix**, a draft patch of only what it changes: a position or a delegation naming a power there isn't (Fix takes it out); a position reporting to one the organization doesn't have, or up the line to itself (Fix has it report to no one); more holders than seats (Fix makes a seat for each); a member or a job holding a position the organization doesn't define (Fix takes it off); a delegation giving more than its granter may (Fix cuts it to what they hold, or ends it); owners counting units whose shares don't match them (Fix sets each share from its units); a company that passes its profit to its owners held in part by a household, a group or the party, so that no one pays income tax on that share (no Fix: who should be taxed is yours to say; a trust holding it pays as trusts do); a group of the `company` kind with owners or staff, which a business would run (Fix makes it one); a place's entry that isn't one of the five, or keys that aren't a list of words; and, as a note, a household member titled "head" with no Head position (Fix writes one out, holding every power, held by them).

**Change what it is**, under Basics, makes a company a trust or a trust a company, previewed first: a company's managing owners become its trustees and its owners of worth its beneficiaries by their shares, while its units and classes go; a trust's beneficiaries become owners by their shares, and its trustees hold a new **Director** position with every power, each with a membership made where they have none, while a trust's own terms and votes by trustees go. The preview lists the fields only the old kind reads, which are dropped. It is refused, with why, for a listed company, a company no owner manages, a trust with no beneficiary, and while something names it where only its old kind may stand (a company's invoices). It is saved to the draft as one patch (save or revert your other changes first), and a chat takes it with its Upgrade as one change ([Revisions and Upgrade](#revisions-and-upgrade)).

**Revenue streams**, under Money (and in **GM → Orgs → Money**), list what earns for it, each with about what it makes a month. **Add a stream** starts one from a template: Walk-in customers, One product, A catalog, Oil royalty, Gas royalty, Water royalty, Fees or Custom. Its form shows only what its type needs: price, units and buyers; a catalog's items (up to 50, each with its price, units, margin and tax category); a royalty's production (steady, declining as a well does, or month by month), its price (set, month by month, or an asset's market price), its share, deductions, severance tax and how many months after production it pays; or a formula. Then how it swings (busier and quieter months and days, growth a year, and a ± spread, 0 by default), when it pays, and whether **The agents may adjust it** in play; beside it, its first twelve months as it would pay them. Units and buyers take a formula (`=…`) read when it pays. A stream is a standing payment of its own, so it lists with the world's flows too. In play, a business's money page lists its streams with this month, last month, the next twelve months and any adjustment in force.

### Routines in Build

A **routine** is how someone's days usually go: when they open the shop, eat with the family or go to the gym. It is something the world can rely on, never a rule, and it never outranks the story. What it is to the engine is the role **Routines and the story** gives each level (below): by default it only informs Live, Near and Far characters, as context their mind and the narrator may weigh while the story and planning decide what they do, and Background characters keep to theirs. Whatever the role, a routine never moves anyone out of a scene with your character, contradicts what's been told or interrupts play, and a chat changes anyone's routine as it goes, through the story or the GM.

**Where a character's day comes from**, most binding first: what the story has established (where they are, what they're doing, a trip, a plan under way); their commitments (schedules such as shifts and school, appointments, trips); the plans the story or the GM gave them; their routine; and their basic needs, sleep and meals. A character with no routine has the rest of that list, and nothing more: no outings, visits or invitations are made up for them.

**Build → Daily life → Routines** lists the routines by whom they're for, the most specific first: **Someone's own**, **A group's** (a family, a household, a crew), **A kind's**, **By selector** and **Everyone's** (the world's default). Where routines set the same block for someone, the most specific wins: their own, then a group's, a kind's, a selector's, then everyone's. **Routines and the story**, at the top, opens the settings. **New** names a new routine, as in any collection; a routine without a name goes by whom it's for.

A routine's page has:

- **Whose routine**: **It's for** picks the level (**Someone**, **Group**, **Kind**, **Selector** or **Everyone**), then who, and an optional **Name**.
- **Their days**, for anyone but everyone: **Have a routine** or **No routine**. With no routine, their days come only from the story, the GM, their commitments and their basic needs, and no broader routine applies to them. With a routine, **Keeps the blocks of broader routines** says whether a group's, a kind's and everyone's blocks still apply where this one doesn't set the same block.
- **Blocks**, in the order of the day, each with its kind, its time, its place and where it comes from. The **Block** button adds one, and **Change a basic need or a broader block…** copies sleep, a meal or another routine's block in, to change it for whom this routine covers. Tap a block to open its sheet.
- **Turned off here**: blocks that don't apply to whom this routine covers, such as breakfast for a night owl. **Turn off a block…** adds one, and its undo button puts it back.
- **Where this routine comes from**: what its blocks rest on unless a block names its own.

A block's sheet has:

- **What it reads as** ("Opens the shop") and **What they're doing**: sleep, a meal, work, socialize, leisure or the world's own activity.
- **When it happens**: **At a set time** (the same time on each day it's on), **Within a window** (somewhere in its window, wherever the day has room; **How often** can make it **A few times a week**, with **Times a week**) or **Optional** (only likely, with **How likely**: **Rarely**, **Sometimes**, **Often** or **Usually**, and the first thing a busier day leaves out). The bar across the day shows when it runs, or for a window when it may start: drag either end, or type the times. **How long** takes a length such as "1 h 30 min".
- **How much it matters**, **Least** to **Most**: when blocks want the same free time, the one that matters more goes first.
- **Where**: **At home**, **At work**, **Wherever they are**, **At the home of whoever they're with**, **A chosen place**, or **The nearest place of a type**.
- **Who with**: a [selector](#selectors) that names people or groups, and **Asks them along**, which makes the block an invitation, made only as **Routines and the story** allows.
- **Which days**: weekdays, days off, holidays, birthdays (their own or a friend's), seasons and weather; with none set, every day.
- **Needs and privacy**: the needs it meets, and **Private**, which shows it to the player only as "Busy".
- **Where it comes from**: **Written here**, **From lore** (an entry picked by its title, with **The words it rests on**; the entry's row opens it) or **From a sheet** (whose sheet, and which field). The list shows each block's source too.
- **Its id (Advanced)**: a block with the id of a basic need (`sleep`, `breakfast`, `lunch`, `dinner`) or of a broader routine's block replaces it. A block added on the page takes its id from what it reads as.
- **Remove block**.

**Save to draft** (**Create routine** for a new one) and **Revert** sit in the bar at the bottom, and the **Item actions** button (⋯) holds **Delete**. A routine's problems show on its page, and **Problems** opens a block's problem on that block's sheet.

**Routines and the story** (in **World**, and at the top of Routines) tunes what routines are to the engine, and how aware they are of the story and its lore:

- **What a routine is, by closeness**: for **Live** (in the scene with you), **Near** (close to the story), **Far** (off screen, still followed) and **Background** (everyone else), **Informative** (the story and planning decide what they do; their routine is only context their mind and the narrator may weigh; the default for all but Background), **Enforced** (their routine and schedules run their day when the story doesn't, and never pull them out of a scene with you; Background's default) or **Off** (not used at all, not even as context). The GM can set it apart for one character in a chat. The world's schedules (weather, prices, bills, paydays, opening hours) and pay run whatever you pick.
- **In a time skip**: for each level, how those whose routine is informative spend a time skip: **Stay put**, **Follow their routine** (Background's default) or **Planned by the model** (the default for Live, Near and Far; the model plans everyone it covers at the skip's start). A level whose routine is enforced or off says it applies only to characters the GM makes informative. The GM can set it apart for one character in a chat ([Routines and schedules](#routines-and-schedules)). **Plan a skip** picks how the model is asked: **All in one call** (the default, and cheaper) or **One call per person, at once**, which keeps each answer small for a large cast or a long skip; if one person's call fails, the others are still planned and the GM's note says how many weren't.
- **The narrator**: **Tell the narrator people's usual routines** (on by default), for the characters in the scene whose routine isn't off, as context it may use, never what they must do ([Routines and schedules](#routines-and-schedules)).
- **Timelapse from the Actions sheet**: **Follow my routine by default** (off by default), where the Timelapse sheet's **Follow my routine** starts ([Timelapse](#timelapse)).
- **Story and lore awareness**, for those whose routine is enforced: **Story first** (enforced routines bend for the story's plans and the GM's, and what the story sets holds until it's released), **Balanced**, the default (Background characters keep their enforced routine, others bend it for the story's plans, and what the story sets holds for the rest of that day), or **Routine first** (enforced routines hold, bending only as **How plans bend routine** allows, and what the story sets holds until their next block). The GM can set it apart for one character in a chat.
- **Invitations**: whom routine blocks may invite: **Off**, **To the player only** (the default) or **Between everyone**. A block invites only when it names its company and asks them along, so nobody is invited out of nowhere; plans from the story and the GM's invitations need no setting.
- **Sociability**: **Reserved**, **Usual** or **Outgoing**, how fast the social and fun needs grow.
- **How plans bend routine**: which plans made in the story may move work or school: **Only plans that must happen**, **Strong plans**, **Strong plans, and set-time plans for the cast** (the default) or **Strong plans, and set-time plans for anyone**.

**Save to draft** saves them all, and **Use the defaults** takes the world's own settings out again: a world never tuned keeps none.

### Maps

A **map** draws the inside of one place, its `scope`: a manor's floor as rooms on a grid, or a town as places linked by routes. It is a small JSON document in the **lre-map-v1** format (its JSON Schema is `src/shared/lre-map-v1.schema.json` in the source), which you draw in the map editor, write as JSON there, or describe in your own words for the AI to write (see [Describe a map](#describe-a-map)). A map makes its places (inside its scope), its grid and its routes for you, and keeps them in step with it: they show in the Places, Grids and Routes lists as the map's, read-only, with **Edit in map** to change them where they come from. Delete the map and they go with it. A place, grid or route you made yourself always wins: a map that would make one with the same id leaves yours alone, and the lint says so.

A **grid map** is rows of characters, one per square, all the same length. The `legend` says what a character means: a terrain (`{"terrain": "grass"}`, with `"blocked": true` for one you can't cross), a place's area (`{"place": "kitchen"}`), a door on a room's edge (`{"door": true, "place": "kitchen"}`: a door that belongs to the kitchen, so whoever stands in it is in the kitchen, and that joins the kitchen to the room on its other side; it should touch the kitchen, or the map gets a warning), a zone (`{"zone": "cellar"}`) or a thing lying on that square (`{"item": "lantern"}`, which must be a thing of the world on one square only, and not inside something; add `"place"` to say which room's floor it is, otherwise the room around it). Without a legend entry, `#` is a wall, `.` floor, `+` a door and a space nothing. A place stands at its `at` square, or in the middle of its area (its floor, not its doors). `cellMeters` says how many metres a square is across (80 when you leave it out), for walking times. In the map editor, the **Room door** brush paints a door of the room you pick.

```json
{
  "id": "manor_ground",
  "scope": "manor",
  "title": "Ground floor",
  "layout": [
    "#############",
    "#ppppp#kkkkk#",
    "#ppppp+kkkkk#",
    "#ppppp#kkkkk#",
    "###+#####+###",
    "#hhhhhhhhhhh#",
    "#####+#######"
  ],
  "legend": {
    "p": { "place": "parlour" },
    "k": { "place": "kitchen" },
    "h": { "place": "hallway" }
  },
  "places": [
    { "id": "parlour", "name": "Parlour" },
    { "id": "kitchen", "name": "Kitchen" },
    { "id": "hallway", "name": "Hallway" }
  ]
}
```

A **node map** sets its places in a frame of `size` by their `at` (leave it out and the place goes on a ring), and its `links` are the routes between them, each with its `minutes` and, if you like, its `mode` (`walk` unless given, or `road`, `offroad` or `water`). Link every place on the map or none, because once a place's inside has links, travel there follows only them.

```json
{
  "id": "old_town",
  "scope": "town",
  "title": "Old town",
  "size": [100, 100],
  "places": [
    { "id": "square", "name": "Market square", "at": [50, 50] },
    { "id": "inn", "name": "The Gull Inn", "at": [25, 30] },
    { "id": "docks", "name": "Docks", "at": [80, 75] }
  ],
  "links": [
    { "from": "square", "to": "inn", "minutes": 3 },
    { "from": "square", "to": "docks", "minutes": 6 }
  ]
}
```

Without the grid system, walls and doors are drawn, not walked: travel inside a place follows its routes, or, with none, a short walk between any two of its places.

The world's own map settings are in its **World** part in Build. **Display** is how a new chat's map starts out, **Known** or **Fog** (players can switch it on their map). **Grid movement** turns on the **grid system**: Space and travel is at least level 3 in every chat of the world, and on a grid, people stand on squares. **Travel simulation: Go needs a route** is off by default, so **Go to** goes anywhere, walking where it can, else at the time a road or a boat would take, else in 15 minutes. On, **Go to** needs a walking route. Either way a move the reply tells always lands, since the story stands as told; grid steps keep their walls, and nobody's routine changes. **✎** on your place moves you there as a real move: you leave what you were doing, and on a map you stand where you arrive.

- Arriving at a place puts you on its square, or else on a door at the edge of the map, and you walk from there with **Walk here** on the map or the **Move on the map** action, around walls and through doors.
- A floor plan without `links` gets walking routes through its doors, between rooms a walk joins without crossing a third room, so **Go to** the kitchen walks there in the time the walk takes. A room no door leads to gets a warning.
- **Sight radius** (3 squares unless you change it) is how far you see; walls block it, and Fog shows only the squares you've seen.
- A thing drawn in the legend starts on its square. To take it, wear it or use it there, you have to be on its square or next to it: rules and actions can ask the same with `reach(self, thing)`, and `on_grid(thing)` says whether it lies on a square.
- A rule can fire when someone steps into a zone or onto a square: `{"zone": "cellar", "in": "manor"}` or `{"cell": [4, 2], "in": "manor"}` (every room's area is also a zone named after the room). It fires for you unless `who` picks others (a selector, like `{"kind": ["guard"]}`), once each time they step in, never while they stay.

**Travel times**, also under the map settings, tune how long getting about takes in the world's chats. Left empty, every route takes the minutes written on it. A **speed** by way of travel (on foot, by road, off road, on water, in km/h) times every route of that way by its distance instead, and a **speed by vehicle class** (the vehicles table's class, such as `atv_utility`) does the same for that class's vehicles, whichever way they go. **Getting a vehicle going** and **Parking it** (a minute each by default) add to every trip, and **Longest walk to fetch a vehicle** (30 minutes by default) is how far you'll walk to one parked elsewhere. New moves and drives take the new times; what's already happened in a chat stays as it was.

The **map editor** (open a map from **Maps**, or **New**) has four tabs.

- **Draw.** A new, empty map asks **What kind of map?**: **Grid map** or **Node map**, each starting from a small example you can change. On a grid map the tools are **Paint** (drag to paint with the brush), **Fill** (tap a cell to fill the area around it) and **Move** (drag a place to its cell). Pick the brush from the chips: wall, floor, door and nothing, then the legend's own. **Room** and **Brush** add a room or a legend entry, and **Edit brush** opens the one you're painting with. On a node map the tools are **Move** (drag a place; tap one to select it), **Link** (drag from one place to another to join them with a way) and **Add** (tap where the new place goes). Two fingers pan and zoom. **Undo** takes back the last edit.
- **Rooms** (**Places** on a node map) lists the map's places, then its **Legend** on a grid map or its **Ways** on a node map, and the **Map** with its **Edit** button. Each opens a sheet: **Map settings** (**Title**, **Draws the inside of**, the size in cells and **Cell size (metres)**, or **A fixed frame** for a node map), a place (its name, its cell or position, its room and the brush that paints it, **Its inside is drawn by**, and its id under **Advanced**), a brush (what **It paints**: wall, floor, door, terrain, a room, a zone or a thing lying there) or a way (**From**, **To**, **Minutes**, **By** and **Both ways**).
- **Preview** draws the map as Play will show it, every place shown, with each problem marked on it: a solid mark for an error, a dashed one for a warning.
- **JSON** is the map as lre-map-v1 JSON, with **Grid template**, **Node template** and **Tidy**, which lays the JSON out a field per line, and line numbers marked where the problems are. Every edit in the other tabs rewrites the JSON, laid out as Tidy lays it out.

The editor checks the map as you edit, against the format and against the rest of the world. Above the tabs it says "A valid lre-map-v1 map." or counts the errors and warnings, which open into the list: rows of different lengths, a character the legend doesn't know, a place outside the grid, a link to a place that doesn't exist, an id another place already has. Tap a problem to open the place, way or brush it is about, or its line in the JSON tab. The **Item actions** button (⋯) holds **Start from a grid template**, **Start from a node template** and **Delete**; a template asks before it replaces a map you have drawn. **Save to draft** (**Create map** for a new one) and **Revert** sit in the bar at the bottom.

A **grid** that no map draws has a page of its own: **Size** (**Width (cells)**, **Height (cells)** and **Cell size (metres)**) and **Paint**, with the tools **Pan**, **Terrain**, **Blocked** and **Zone**, the terrain and zone chips to paint with, **Add zone** for a new zone, and **Undo**. Its **Item actions** button holds **Delete**, and **JSON (Advanced)** shows the grid. A place that sits on its parent's grid has an **On** *parent*'s **grid** card on its page, above the bar at the bottom: tap a cell to put it there.

### Rulesets and profiles

**Build → Ruleset** is a list of the ruleset's parts: **Start from a profile…**, **How checks are decided**, **Level floors**, **Progression**, **Sheet Designer**, **Combat** and **JSON (Advanced)**. Each row says how that part stands and counts its issues, and each opens its own sheet, with **Save to draft** and **Revert** at the bottom.

A profile is a ready-made ruleset to start from; once applied, everything it added is yours to edit. Tap **Start from a profile…** and pick one:

- **Modern**: present-day adventure on a d20. Six abilities (10 is average) with modifiers of (score − 10) ÷ 2 rounded down; **Defense** of 10 + Dexterity + a level bonus + an equipment bonus; twelve skills with ranks (Athletics, Drive, Stealth, Computers, Investigate, Repair, Notice, Read People, First Aid, Persuade, Deceive, Intimidate); Fortitude, Reflex and Will; **Action Points**, which **Push a skill** spends to add 1d6 to the roll, and which **Rest for the night** and each new level refill (the sheet's row says so); HP from Constitution; Reputation; and levels at 0, 1,000, 3,000, 6,000 XP and so on. A world that applied Modern before Rest for the night refilled them takes it by applying Modern again and choosing **Take the profile's** for Rest for the night and the sheet.
- **Classic**: classic fantasy on a d20. Base attack on a good, average or poor progression and saves on good or poor ones (each character's progressions are fields), thirteen skills with ranks, Armor Class, HP from a hit die, and the same XP scale.
- **Fifth**: heroic fantasy on a d20. A proficiency bonus by level (+2 to +6), skills at proficiency 0, 1 or 2 (2 is expertise, twice the bonus), two proficient saves, Armor Class, **Dodge** (attacks on you roll with disadvantage until your next turn), and hit dice: a **Short rest** spends one to heal, a **Long rest** heals fully and regains half. A well-told approach earns advantage rather than a bonus.
- **Custom**: the least to grow from: one stat (Skill), one check (**Try something**), an HP pool whose max is a formula, a plain attack, and a progression with level 1 only.

Every d20 profile has **Use a skill** and **Make a save**, with one option per skill or save in the Actions sheet and its exact odds; the difficulty is moderate (10) unless the action is given easy, hard, very hard or a number. In an attack a natural 1 always misses and a natural 20 is a critical (the damage dice roll twice). **Total defense** (Dodge in Fifth) lasts until your next turn, and **Rest for the night** heals (in Modern it also refills Action Points). Each profile also brings Balanced checks, a Sheets 3 floor so modifiers count under any realism, a sheet for **Play → Me → You** and combat rules; the focus stays on skills and talking your way through, and fights are there when the story needs them.

**What applying does.** The picker previews the profile on your draft: the fields it adds to your character's kind (in the starters, Character), and the checks, actions, data tables and ruleset parts it adds. Anything already in the world with the same id and different content is listed by name (its path shows with **Show ids** on) with **Keep mine** (the default) or **Take the profile's**, and items that already match are counted. A kind that overrides one of those fields with its own definition keeps it. Everyone of that kind reads the new fields' defaults; nobody's stored values change unless a new field can't hold one (a level of 0 under a minimum of 1), and the preview lists those. **Apply to draft** writes it all at once and lint runs as usual; applying the same profile again changes nothing ("Nothing to apply"). Save or revert your own ruleset edits first.

**Adapting it.** A profile is ordinary data, so you change it like anything else:
- **Level your own way.** The XP thresholds are a column of the profile's level table (Modern levels, Classic levels, Fifth levels) or a list in **Progression**; change them, set a **Top level**, or add effects **On each level gained** (Modern refills Action Points there, Fifth adds a hit die). Custom starts at level 1 only: give it thresholds such as "0, 100, 250".
- **XP trackers.** XP is a number field. Add it with the `award` effect from quest rewards, track stages, rules (one on `combat.end` rewards a fight) or action outcomes, or correct it with ✎ on the sheet; the level follows it, up when it crosses a threshold and down when it falls below one. A second progression on other fields (milestones, renown) runs beside the first.
- **Skills and saves** are rows of the profile's skills and saves tables: add a row (its label and the modifier field it adds) and a number field of the same id for the ranks, and **Use a skill** offers it. Any action can do the same: a text parameter with a `table` offers that table's rows.
- **Everything else** is a formula or a field: Defense or max HP, a check's bands, each character's `damage` dice, the action economy, the sheet.

The profiles are original implementations of common tabletop mechanics: neutral names and numbers, no rules text.

### Checks, pools, progression and sheets

These are the pieces a tabletop ruleset is built from. They work the same whatever ruleset you write.

- **Checks.** A check is dice plus outcome bands. A band can test the total (`min`, `max`), the **natural** die (`natural: {min: 20}`: the face of the first die when the dice start with a single kept die, such as `d20` or `2d20kh1`), and the **margin** over a **DC** (`margin: {min: 5}`, the total minus the DC). Natural bands are tried first. A check with a `dc` and no bands has two outcomes: `failure` below the DC and `success` at or above it. **Advantage** is a formula that gives 1, 0 or −1: it adds a die to the natural die and keeps the higher (`d20` becomes `2d20kh1`) or the lower, and advantage and disadvantage cancel a die at a time. An **opposed** check names its opponent with `vs` (by default the action's `target`): the opponent rolls another check (`vs.check`), this check's dice with their own modifier (`vs.modifier`), this same check (neither), or doesn't roll and has a **passive** score that becomes the DC (`vs.passive`). Whatever you write, the odds in the Actions sheet stay exact and are the real ones; the sheet says "with advantage" and the DC beside them. An outcome's effects can read the roll as `roll.natural`, `roll.total`, `roll.modifier`, `roll.dc`, `roll.margin` and `roll.outcome`.
- **Anyone can roll.** The `check` effect (`{check, actor, target?, outcomes}`) lets a rule or another effect have someone else roll, such as a guard noticing you. Their roll never moves yours, and it is recorded for Check world and the GM.
- **What counts as success.** A check can list **Outcomes that count as success** (`pass`; by default `success` and every band after it) and its **Outcome when not rolled** (`auto`). [How checks are decided](#how-checks-are-decided) reads both.
- **Pools.** Give a number field a `pool` (`max`: another field, often a derived one, or a formula) for hit points, mana or stress. Effects keep it between its `min` (0 unless set) and its max, a pool nobody has set yet is full, and the `recover` effect restores it by an amount, by the pool's own `recover` formula, or to the max. A pool is still a field, so undo, swipes and Keep treat it like any other.
- **Progression.** In **Build → Ruleset**, **Progression** lists the ruleset's progressions. Type a name under **New progression** and tap **Add**; tap a progression to open it in its own sheet. A progression has a **Label** (the sheet and the Journal show it), the kind it **Applies to** (or any kind with both fields), an **XP field** and a **Level field (set from XP)** picked from that kind's number fields (derived fields aren't offered, since the engine sets the level), and the XP each level needs, from level 1 (usually 0), from **A list** ("0, 300, 900, 2700") or **A data table**'s column, read in row order. A **Top level** is optional. **On each level gained** is a list of effects, edited like a rule's, and **Remove progression** deletes it. A level follows its XP, both ways. Whenever an effect, a quest reward, a narrated change, a ✎ fix or a GM edit raises someone's XP past a threshold, the engine raises their level one level at a time and runs those effects for each, with `level` (the new level) and `from` (the level before the gain); the Journal says "You reached Level 3." for your character. When their XP falls below their level's threshold, the level drops straight to the one the XP reaches and runs nothing, so what earlier levels gave stays, but a pool whose max falls with the level (Modern's HP and Action Points) comes down to its new max at the same time, so a later rest never takes it lower; the change reads "Your HP is now at most 8", and the Journal says "You dropped to Level 1 (from 3)." A drop only ever lowers a pool: one already lower when the drop lands (a fix you made while a reply dealt damage, or a later reply after you swipe an earlier one) stays as it is. To change a level, change XP: the sheet's ✎ on the level row corrects XP, the GM view shows the level as **Set from XP** with nothing to edit, and a rule or effect that writes the level, or spawns someone with one, is refused. Someone spawned with XP starts at the level it reaches. The `award` effect (`{entity, progression, amount}`) adds XP. Lint names a character whose level isn't the one their XP reaches, which would move there the next time their XP changed.
- **The sheet.** The ruleset's sheet lays out **Play → Me → You**: sections of rows, each a field, a formula with a label, a pool (shown as a bar of current over max) or a progression (the level, with a bar of XP toward the next level). Without one, You shows the Sheets pack's stats as before. Lay it out in the **Sheet Designer**. The **Section** button adds a section, its **Section title** renames it, and its move up, move down and remove buttons order or remove it. **+ Add a row…** picks what a row shows: **A formula with a label**, a pool, a progression, or one of your character's fields (inherited and derived ones too). Each row opens its own page, with what it shows, its label (a formula is written in the expression editor with your character as `self`) and **Remove row**, and its move up and move down buttons order it. **Preview**, at the bottom of the Sheet Designer, shows the sheet as your character will see it when a new chat starts, from the ruleset as you edit it, before you save.
- **Level floors.** A ruleset's math may need more than the realism settings give: stat modifiers count only from Sheets 3. **Level floors** (for example Sheets 3) raise every character's effective level in that domain to at least the floor, whatever the preset, tier caps or overrides say. The world's **Realism defaults** in Build name the floors ("This world's ruleset needs Sheets & checks 3.") and don't offer a level below one, and **GM → This chat → Realism** shows the same line, each domain at the level actually in force, and starts that domain's slider at its floor. Lint notes when the world's realism defaults sit below a floor.

**Lint** runs as you edit and speaks plainly. It catches broken references, wrong types, cycles, unreachable quest stages, quest conditions that name a thing, a person or a place the world doesn't have (a typo'd id in `carries`, `at` or `knows`, which never holds), sections that are too big, labels that leak secrets, secret truth in public lore, secret lore without a gate or readable by a step that writes prose, a lore entry whose **Secret** is neither on nor off in its file (such as `"true"` in quotes; it is kept secret until fixed), cues that give their secret away, name someone or something the player may not know (they are left out while the player doesn't) or can never show (no secret, two, or one that hides nothing), lookup-table terms and topics that lead to missing entries, lead nowhere or contradict an alias, a section template that holds a macro other than `{{lines}}`, step templates that could never run, unbalanced postings, double-booked commitments (two schedules setting the same field of the same people at once, with the same precedence), classification values that don't exist or nest in a loop, several values on a one-value classification, relations between kinds their type doesn't allow or beyond its cardinality, kinship written on one side only, conditions that depend on something frozen after a demotion, a Timelapse cap smaller than one time slot, rules for checks that hold a value they can't (each plays as the nearest it can), rules for checks that name a preset but set values their own way (they play as Custom), a sure thing that doesn't start above too hard to try, checks that roll only where marked with nothing marked, checks whose success the rules can't tell, a character whose level isn't the one their XP reaches, and problems with the active preset (see [Troubleshooting](#troubleshooting)).

### Combat and encounters

Fights are part of a ruleset, and only a world whose ruleset has **Combat** (**Build → Ruleset → Combat**) has any: without it there is no Fight, no Combat tab in the Actions sheet and nothing changes. Combat is all data, so a Modern, Classic or Fifth-style fight is the same engine with different numbers.

- **The rules.** The **initiative** check everyone rolls when a fight starts (highest first; equal totals go by the initiative modifier, or the **tie-break** formula, then you, then your side, then id order); the **action economy**, budgets such as Action, Bonus, Move or Reaction with how many each fighter gets and whether they refill each turn or each round (a reaction is spent off your own turn, so it never keeps your turn open); the **HP field**, a pool; when someone is **down** (by default at 0 HP) and the condition they then have (it goes on the character sheet through the Sheets pack); the **sides** (who is hostile to whom, by default a `hostile` relation, and who fights beside whom); when the fight is **over** (by default when one side has no one standing); after how many rounds in a row with no attack and no one hurt it **peters out** (3 by default; 0 never); whether your turn ends once nothing you could do fits what's left (the default) or only on **End turn**; everyone else's **tactics** (whom they attack first, when they defend); and whether others' HP shows as **wounds** ("hurt", "badly hurt"; the default) or exact numbers. Build → Ruleset → Combat names each choice's default in its label, so one left unset reads as what it plays as. With wounds, nothing you or the narrator reads gives someone else's HP as a number: the Encounter card, the chips and the Journal say how hurt they are ("Goblin: badly hurt", "Goblin takes damage"), and the narrator is told the same. Your own HP is always exact, and so is everyone's in GM mode.
- **Combat actions.** Any action becomes a combat action with a **cost** (budget → how many; Attack 1, or Attack 1 and Move 1 for a full attack). An attack is an ordinary action whose check reads the target's defense (a DC such as `target.defense`, or an opposed roll) and whose hit outcomes take `roll()` damage off the HP pool, which stops at 0 and at its max. Mark it **attack** or **defend** for everyone else's tactics. A combat action that **starts a fight** (an ambush) is offered only when none is under way; every other one needs a fight, your turn and enough left.
- **Starting and ending.** **Fight** (in the Actions sheet's Combat tab) starts one against someone; rules and actions can use the `combat.start` effect (named foes, a selector, or everyone here hostile to you) and `combat.end`. Formulas read a fight with `in_encounter`, `is_foe`, `is_ally`, `is_down`, `has_turn`, `standing(side)`, `encounter_round()` and `budget_left`; a rule on `combat.end` can award XP.
- **Playing a fight.** While one is under way, the Encounter card in **Play → Now** shows the round and whose turn it is, the turn order with HP (yours exactly, others' as the rules allow) and conditions, what you have left, **Combat actions (n)**, which opens the Actions sheet's Combat tab with your attacks, their targets and exact odds, **End turn**, and this round's acts. When your turn ends, everyone else takes theirs in initiative order before the reply, so the narrator is told the round in order (who did what, the hits and damage, who is down) and then whose turn it is. With **NPC choices** on, the decision model may pick among their legal actions; otherwise they defend when hurt and attack the most threatening foe. It's all part of your turn's record, so swipes, Keep and Re-run treat a round like any other turn. A foe you can't see is "someone": never named, never a target. GM mode's **Fight** page, from the **Fight under way** card in **World**, shows everyone, HP exact.
- **Time, leaving and stalemates.** A fight takes no time on the clock: while one is under way, your turns move no clock and run no one's day, whatever you do (talking, a skill, an action that takes minutes), and no one in the fight walks off to keep a schedule. A time jump the narration tells still applies, as the story tells it, and so does **✎** on the clock. A skip you ask for yourself (typed, such as "we wait it out until morning", or from the Timelapse sheet) ends the fight first: it breaks off as time moves on (the Journal says "The fight broke off as time moved on"), then the time passes as asked. A skip that can't run (the clock is paused) ends nothing: the fight goes on. The action that starts a fight still takes its own time: a twenty-minute stalk that ends in an ambush moves the clock twenty minutes. Walk out of the place and the fight is over for you, with no winner: the card and the fight's actions in the Combat tab close, **Fight** is offered again right away, and nothing of it goes on without you. Three whole rounds in a row in which no one attacks and no one is hurt (the ruleset's **stalemate**; 0 turns it off) and the fight peters out. A missed attack is still an attack, so holding Total defense while a foe keeps swinging doesn't end the fight, and your own **Over when** rule gets its turn. Everyone else guards when hurt only while a foe has gone for them this round or the last, and a stance such as Total defense or Dodge shows in the round's acts like any other act. **Hostile to** is the combat pack's own relation, held on both sides. The card shows who is down at any Sheets level; the down condition shows on the character sheet from Sheets 4. With fights settled by the policy, the Combat tab shows what it makes of each attack (No roll needed, The story decides) rather than dice odds for your own; everyone else's attacks always roll. Lint warns when that policy's retry rule waits for a new scene: the same attack comes round every turn, so a miss would stand for the rest of the fight.
- **Dice or the policy.** Everyone else's combat actions always roll, whatever the world's adjudication policy says: no one chose them, and no judge reads their approach. Your own roll every time by default too, so a missed attack can be tried again next turn. Any other check made during a fight follows the policy as usual. Set **Attacks and other fight checks are settled by** to **policy** to have your own combat actions follow the policy too: a fight takes no time on the clock, so for a retry rule that waits for a change each new round counts as one, while one that waits for a new scene keeps a missed attack refused for the rest of the fight.

### Classifications, groups and kinship

**Classifications** are your own ways of sorting a world's people and things, in **Build → Classifications**. Each one (a *facet*: a story role, an allegiance, a disposition) has a label, says whether an entity holds one value or several (**Several values per entity**), an order and a description, and lists its **Values**. Type a label under **New value's label** and tap **Add value**; its id comes from the label, under **Advanced**. Tap a value to open it: its **Label**, a **Description** and, if you like, the value it is **Nested under** ("Villain" under "Antagonist"), so anything that asks for Antagonist also takes the villains, and **Remove value**. An entity's editor has a field for each classification: a chip per value, which for a one-value classification acts as a single choice while there are few values (tapping the chosen one clears it) and becomes a picker when there are many. Beside them are its **Tags**, free-form labels shown as removable chips: type one and press Enter or tap **Add** (a comma adds too), and the box suggests the tags already in use. A vehicle's **Vehicle class** (`pickup_midsize`, a row of the vehicle classes table its fuel, range and costs come from) belongs to the Vehicles pack, not to your classifications.

A classification is for you and the GM side unless you tick **Players can see this classification**. Until then its values never reach Play, so a "Villain" stays a secret; tags never show in Play at all. **Play → People** groups and filters only by the classifications players can see.

**Groups** are entities too: factions, families, households, companies, or a kind of your own that extends **Group**, and so are businesses, so every one is an organization with positions (see [Organizations in Build](#organizations-in-build)). Someone joins with a **member of** relation, which can carry their **Title** (`role`), their **position**, **rank** and **since** date; a group can belong to a larger group (a household in a family), and membership counts through it, a business's owners and staff with it. A group's regard toward someone, `faction_regard(group, who)`, is its own regard if it has one, else the mean of its members' (people and things, through member groups too). A schedule owned by a group runs for its members.

**Kinship** comes with the Relationships pack: **parent of** and **child of** are each other's inverse, and **sibling of**, **spouse of** and **partner of** are symmetric. The engine keeps both sides: a link written by an effect brings its other side with it (and an unlink takes it away) as one change, a spawned person's relations bring theirs, and a link you author on one side only is completed when a chat starts (lint warns, naming the missing side). The same holds for your own relation types with an inverse or symmetry. A link that would break its type's kinds or cardinality ("Betrothed to" one at a time, say) is refused with the reason.

### Selectors

A **selector** picks entities by rule, with one editor wherever you need one (a schedule's owner, an action parameter's candidates, a **Known by**): **Kind** (or one inheriting from it), **Tier**, a value of each classification (or one nested under it), **Tag**, **Member of** (directly or through a member group) and **Only these entities**. What a constraint holds shows as removable chips: tiers and classification values are chips you tap on or off, tags are typed, and kinds, groups and entities are added from a picker by name. An entity must pass every constraint you set, and within one constraint any of its values will do; a selector with nothing set matches everyone. Where a selector is optional, **Add a selector** starts one and **Remove** takes it away.

### Character minds in a world

**Build → World → Minds** sets how the world's characters think (see [Character minds](#character-minds) for what they do in play). Nothing here is required: a world without it plays with the defaults, and **Use the defaults** takes the world's own settings out again. **Save to draft** saves the whole form and **Revert** drops your changes; the three sit in the bar at the bottom. Problems show in the group they belong to.

- **What the narrator gets of a mind** (secrecy): two cards, the one in force marked **In use**.
  - **Full**, the default: the narrator also gets each character's inner monologue, what they mean to do, their reactions and their mood, with the description unchecked, told to show them through behaviour and never say them outright. Richer, more consistent characters, but a careless narrator could let a thought or a secret slip.
  - **Brief**: the narrator gets only a short description of how each character comes across (what they do, how they look and sound). It is checked first, and left out if it would give away a secret or name someone the player hasn't met. Their private thoughts and what they mean to do stay in GM mode. The safest choice against spoilers, with less for the narrator to work with. A world that chose Brief keeps it.

  Either way, the player never sees a mind.
- **Thresholds**: the lowest score for **Live**, **Near** and **Far**; under Far is Background. Live should be at least Near, and Near at least Far; out of order, the lower ones are raised to match.
- **Focus weights**: the points each thing adds to a character's focus score. **What counts toward the score**, folded away, holds being in the player's scene, close by, named in the latest message or reply, strong feelings toward the player either way, knowing the player, owning a quest, secret or agenda under way, each track that names them, being named by a world rule, each pending invitation, appointment, plan or commitment with the player, and being seen with the player lately; **Tier bands**, also folded away, holds the points for each tier's band. Under **Roles**, **Add a role value** picks a classification value by name (an antagonist, say) to add points for, and a value nested under it counts as it; each has a remove button (**Remove** *value*). Weights run from −100 to 200; a negative one pushes a character away.
- **Who always, and who never**: **Always Live** and **Never a mind (wins over Always Live)**, two [selectors](#selectors). A selector with nothing set picks no one here (not everyone), and the player's own character never gets a mind.
- **Memory defaults**: how many witness-log lines, recalled turns (the latest, and the ones that best match the moment, with their token budget), notes and open commitments a mind sees; how many notes a character keeps before the oldest are folded into their summary; how fast a note's salience halves (in days of game time) and the salience under which it has faded; whether faded and surplus notes are folded into the summary, and at most how often for each character (in days of game time). A player's own **Settings → Minds → Memory** wins over these.
- **What they know from the start**: what each person, your own character included, knows when a chat starts: a lore entry, a stage of a track (with its truth), or one of someone's fields. Under **Add something someone knows**, pick **Who knows it**, then add what they know. Each fact has what they make of it, **How sure, from 0 to 1**, and **Whom they keep it from** (**No one**, **Everyone** or **Some people**, picked by name), with a remove button. It seeds what everyone knows in every chat, and what they learn in play goes on from there. A World Info entry's own secret marks say who holds them, so they aren't listed here.
- **Step templates**: a row for each of the world's own prompts for the five minds steps. For a step with none, **New template** starts one from the built-in prompt and opens it in the step-template editor; a template already there opens the same way. **New template** stays off while this form has unsaved changes, with "Save or revert your changes first." Only a step's first template (by id) with any text is used: a blank one is marked **Blank: not used**, and any other but the one used **Not used**. A world's template wins over the built-in prompt, and a player's own prompt (Settings → Minds) wins over the world's.

### Lore books

Each world has its own book, **"LumiRPEngine · *your world*"**, created for you. It holds only the engine's sections: their entries are stored switched off and only the engine turns them on, so a section the engine can't fill never leaks into the prompt. Lore entries an earlier version of LumiRPEngine copied into the book are removed the next time you commit or Upgrade the world, attach it to a chat, or play a turn in one of its chats.

The world's own lore lives in the world, not in a book: see [World lore](#world-lore). LumiRPEngine writes into your own lore books only when you press for it: in **Build → Lore → World Info entries** (a secret line into an entry's text, or the entry's LumiRPEngine fields) and in [Secrecy in your World Info](#secrecy-in-your-world-info).

Secret truth never lives in a World Book. It stays in the world and enters a section only at the moment it's revealed.

### World lore

**Build → Lore** holds what the world knows. It is kept in the world and travels with it when you export it. The engine's own helpers read it ([Lore for the helpers](#lore-for-the-helpers)), and the narrator gets an entry when it links where your character is, in the **Place** section. The list's search box finds entries by title, alias, topic, keyword or text, and badges secret entries, where an entry came from, and an earlier version's copies of a World Info entry (**Old copy · not read**). Its **Tools** open the **Lookup table**, **Index this text** and [Secrecy in your World Info](#secrecy-in-your-world-info), and say that your World Info is read live, with nothing to copy or refresh. Below them come **Lore books**, the **Lore index** and **World Info entries**, each described below. **New** asks for the new entry's name and takes its id from it, as in any collection.

**Entries.** An entry's page has its **Entry** (a title and its text), then **How it's found**: **Aliases** (other names its subject goes by, such as *the old lighthouse*), **Topics** it belongs under (*Harbor law*) and **Keywords**, each a row of removable chips (type one and press Enter or tap **Add**; the topic and keyword boxes suggest the ones your other entries use), then **links** to the places and people it is about, and optionally a **group** and a **priority**. Then come its **Source** and **Secrecy**. A problem with one of these fields shows under it. Its **Item actions** button (⋯) holds **Delete**, which names what leads to it ("Terms and topics that lead to it will show in Problems."). The text is kept exactly as you wrote it, macros included, as in any World Book; that is why a world from someone else is worth a look before you play it (see [Import and export](#import-and-export)).

The **Source** card says where an entry came from: written in Build (as is every entry made before sources existed), saved from a chat (it names the message), or written by an indexing job from text you pasted. An entry an earlier version of LumiRPEngine copied from one of your World Info books says **Old copy** and names the book and the entry: LumiRPEngine now reads the book entry itself, as the book has it, so the copy isn't read. A pin of the copy reads the book entry, and so does an older agent turn that gave the narrator the copy: its Re-narrate tells the book entry in its place. Its secrecy still holds the book entry back; delete the copy once you no longer need that.

**The lookup table.** **Lore → Lookup table** (also the **Lore lookup table** part on the world home) is the routing LumiRPEngine's own lore searches follow, whether you or an indexing job wrote it. It shows **Terms** or **Topics**. **Terms** lead to entries: *the old lighthouse* → *Breakwater Lighthouse*. One term can lead to several entries (*the Okafors* → each of the family's entries): each term's **Leads to** shows its entries as removable chips, with a picker to add one, and **Add a term** takes a **Term** and the entry it **Leads to**. Terms are matched in lowercase with their spacing tidied, which is how the editor stores them. **Topics** are a table of contents: each has a label and its **Entries** in order, and **Add a topic** makes one. Suggestions come from your entries: their titles, aliases and keywords as terms (**Add all aliases as terms** takes every alias at once), and the topics they name (**File them all**); **Add** takes one. Nothing is saved until **Save to draft**. Lint flags a term or topic that names an entry that doesn't exist, a blank term or one that leads nowhere, a term written in a way lookups won't match, and a term that is one entry's alias but leads somewhere else.

**Who may read it.** Under **Secrecy**, **Who may read it** is *public* (anyone) or *Known by*: read only once your character knows every person and place the entry links (lint warns about one that links nothing). An exported world keeps it as the entry's `visibility` (`public` or `known_by`), which the published `lre-world-v1` schema accepts. Tick **Secret** to keep an entry hidden. A secret entry may hold the secret itself, and it needs a **gate**: a formula, revealed once it holds (lint refuses a secret without one). Until there is one, the form offers examples made of your world's names, a quest's next stage, someone your character may come to know and where the story starts, each with what it means: tap one to use it. Until the gate holds, only the helpers you tick under **Helpers that may read it before the gate holds** may see it: NPC choices, puzzle answers and so on, one by one, and none by default. The scene brief, the prose outline, suggested choices and extraction write what the narrator or you see, so they never read a secret before its gate holds and aren't offered; an entry that names one from before still shows it, with a note, so you can untick it, and lint warns too. **Whose own secret it is** names the people it belongs to: their [minds](#character-minds) read it, before the gate holds and after. A character's mind reads a secret entry only then, or when one of their **Cues** stands for it; linking them doesn't make it theirs, and your character uncovering a secret tells no one else. A public entry never holds a secret's truth; lint refuses one that repeats a quest's or secret's truth.

**World Info as lore.** The engine's helpers, minds and agents read your World Info as lore, live, with the same rules as the world's own entries: the books attached to the chat, your global books and the world's **Lore books** (**Build → Lore → Lore books**: **Add a book**, or remove one with ✕). [Settings → Lore](#settings--lore) can narrow these to the books you tick. A character's or persona's book is read only when you add it to **Lore books**: LumiRPEngine can't see which books Lumiverse scans for a character or persona. The world's own engine book never is. Two of your books can share a name: a character's card brings its own copy of each book it carries, beside the one you imported on its own. Every book picker tells such books apart: **came with a character**, else the day it was added, and **(in this chat)** for one attached to the open chat. Each entry's title is its comment and its keywords are its own; one switched off in its book is skipped unless it has LumiRPEngine's fields. Each secret line in an entry is read as a secret of its own, by the minds of the people it is known to, and the rest of the entry as the entry reads. Nothing is copied into the world: an entry an earlier version of LumiRPEngine copied in isn't read, and its book entry is read in its place, with the book's text as it is now, whether or not its book is attached to the chat. A book that can't be read gives the helpers nothing that turn, and the log says so. A listed book that was deleted shows **Missing**, and each turn's log names it; remove it. **Lore books** is part of the world: commit, then **Upgrade** a chat, to use a change in it. A **Re-read** or **Retry read** reads your books as they are at that moment.

**The lore index.** The agents find your World Info entries by the **short id** each has in the lore index: one line per entry, with its title and what it holds (see [Settings → Lore](#settings--lore) for how the ids are made). An agent asks for it with its `lore_index` tool, or gets it in its prompt when its context starts with it (**Settings → Agents → Context**), and every lore tool takes an entry by its short id. **Build → Lore → Lore index** shows each book the world reads with how many of its entries the index lists, and **What an index entry holds**, the index as it would stand in a book. To keep the index in one of your books, pick it under **Book to write the index entry into** and press **Build an index entry**: LumiRPEngine writes one new entry there, always on and kept out of recursion, listing every entry, a secret or restricted one with its title and what it holds inside its own secret mark, kept from everyone, and writes it again whenever the index changes. Nothing is written until you press it. One such entry is kept in sync at a time; **Settings → Lore → Index entry kept in sync** stops it, leaving the entry in its book.

**World Info entries.** Under **Build → Lore**, pick a book and tap ✎ on an entry to open its form under the entry. It shows the entry's secret lines, each with its text, **Known to …** and **Kept from …**, a **Whole entry** badge when the whole entry is one secret, and any line that starts like a secret but can't be read, with why. **Make this entry secret** (on an entry with no secret lines) makes its whole text one secret; **Add a secret line** adds one at its end, with its text and names; on each line, **Change names** picks who it is **Known to** and whom it is **Kept from**, **Everyone but those who know it** or **Only the people picked**, then **Save the names**, and **Make public** turns it back into plain text. Each of these writes into the entry's own text in your book at once (it's the book's, not the world's: no commit needed) and changes nothing else of it. Below them, **Who may read it**, **Links** and **Aliases** are the entry's LumiRPEngine fields, the people and places picked by name: **Save to the book** writes them into the entry, and **Take the fields off** removes them. An entry an earlier version kept secret with its own fields shows them (the secret switch, the gate, the holders and the helpers) read-only, with a note that its book's secrecy moves to secret lines on [Secrecy in your World Info](#secrecy-in-your-world-info); until then it keeps working as before and takes no secret lines here. An entry an earlier version kept switched off as a copy of a secret says which entry it follows.

**Secrets in your World Info.** A secret in one of your entries is a line in the entry's own text (the buttons above write it for you):

- `{{// Secret: …}}`: kept from everyone;
- `{{// Secret (known to Marcus Delgado, Priya Nair): …}}`: known to those people, kept from everyone else;
- `{{// Secret (kept from Jordan Ellis): …}}`: kept from those people (`everyone` may stand alone);
- `{{// Secret (known to Marcus; kept from Jordan Ellis): …}}`: both, in either order.

A name is a person's name, their id, or a word of their name no one else has (*Marcus*, while he is the only Marcus), and *you* or *the player character* is the character you play; a name that fits no one, or more than one, is kept as you wrote it, and Build flags it on the secret's row. A secret kept from such a name could be kept from your character, so the narrator is told it is kept from your character too, and while the narrator's knowledge is your character's, or leaves secrets out, it is left out: fix the name to have it told as you meant. Once your character learns a secret in play (told it, finding it out, or revealed), it is theirs, and the narrator is told it as something they know, whatever its line says. `Secret`, `known to` and `kept from` may be in any case, and the text after the colon may run over several lines. An entry that is only one secret line is a secret as a whole. Lumiverse leaves every secret line out of every prompt by itself, in every chat, whether or not LumiRPEngine runs, so a secret line never slips through; in a chat that plays no world, though, its words still count for Lumiverse's keywords and World Info budget before it goes. In a world chat LumiRPEngine gives the line to the narrator, marked as a secret, or leaves it out, as the narrator's knowledge says (see [Your lore in world chats](#your-lore-in-world-chats)); the minds of the people it is known to read it as their own secret; no helper reads it, and nothing you read shows it. A secret line can't hold `{{` or `}}` or a backslash before a brace, can't end in `}` or `\`, and keeps everything before its colon on one line, since Lumiverse would end it in the wrong place: the buttons refuse such a text, and a line that breaks these rules is never read as a secret, is left out of whatever LumiRPEngine hands on, and shows under its entry with why. Any other entry with the same text as a secret line, spacing aside (such as the copy a character's card brought), reads as that secret wherever LumiRPEngine reads both.

**Index this text.** In the world's tools and in the Lore list, **Index this text** turns text you already have into lore: paste a setting document, a history or your notes. The helper splits it into entries in the text's own words; each is marked as written by the AI and counts as reviewed once you apply it. One run reads about 72,000 characters. Your World Info needs no indexing: it is read live, as above.

It works like [Import from lore](#import-from-lore). A proposal that repeats a title the world's lore already has, or has no title or no text, is dropped, links to anything that isn't one of the world's people or places are removed with a note, and a reply with nothing usable is asked once more. You review the proposals: each shows its title, where it came from, its aliases, topics and links, and the lookup terms it adds. **Apply n to the draft** adds the ticked entries and files their terms and topics in the lookup table; your books are never edited. **Commit** when you are happy with the result. Like the other tools, the job keeps its last run.

**Save to lore.** In **Play → Story → Journal**, **Save to lore…** opens a small form, and **Save the latest reply** keeps the latest reply as a lore entry in the world's draft. To keep only part of the chat, select it and tap **Use selected text**, or paste it, then **Save the excerpt**. The entry's source names the message it came from (the latest message holding the excerpt, or the latest reply if the excerpt isn't in the chat word for word), and its form opens in Build so you can give it a title, aliases and topics before you commit.

### Secrecy in your World Info

LumiRPEngine used to keep a secret of your World Info by storing fields of its own on the entry and switching the entry off in its book, and it kept each twin of a secret (another entry with the same text) switched off with it. Secret marks replace that: a line in the entry's own text, such as `{{// Secret: …}}`, `{{// Secret (known to Marcus): …}}` or `{{// Secret (kept from Jordan Ellis): …}}`. Lumiverse leaves a mark out of every prompt by itself, and LumiRPEngine gives the narrator what a mark holds, marked as a secret. **Build → Lore → Secrecy in your World Info** (a tool in the Lore list) moves your books to marks, one book at a time, each step only when you press it. A book you haven't moved keeps its old secrecy, as before.

1. **Look at what moves.** The page lists your books, the ones the world reads first, and says which hold anything to move; those with nothing to move are folded away at the end. Open a book to see what its move would do. Looking writes nothing.
   - **Become secret marks**: each secret's whole text goes into one mark, known to the people its **Whose own secret it is** named (by their ids), and the page shows the mark. A mark has no gate and no early readers, so the page says what no longer applies: the gate that opened the secret, and the helpers that could read it before then.
   - **Marked already**: a secret whose text holds marks already. LumiRPEngine reads those, and its text stays as it is.
   - **Twins become secret marks**: each copy LumiRPEngine kept off with a secret. Its text becomes the same mark as its secret's, so Lumiverse leaves it out of any prompt LumiRPEngine doesn't mark, and it then goes back on or off as its note says it stood. A copy whose text can't be a mark stays off and is listed under **Can't become a mark**.
   - **Old fields only**: an entry that keeps nothing secret but still carries LumiRPEngine's secrecy fields. Its links, aliases and **Known by** stay.
   - **Can't become a mark**: a secret whose text a mark can't hold, with why (for instance, a macro in it, whose braces would end the mark early). It keeps its old secrecy: change its text in Lumiverse's World Info panel, then look again.
   - **Switched off by LumiRPEngine**: the secrets it stored switched off. Nothing records how each stood before, so the move leaves them off and lists them for you to switch on in Lumiverse's World Info panel.
2. **Apply to this book.** Before it changes anything, LumiRPEngine keeps a copy of every entry the move lists, whole (its text, keywords, whether it is switched on, and its extension data), in its own storage. Then it writes each secret's mark and puts each twin back exactly as its note says. Lumiverse indexes each changed entry afresh once, as it does after any edit. The old fields stay on the entries for now, but an entry that holds a mark is read by its marks, so the book reads the new way at once.
3. **Check, then confirm.** Look over the marks on the page, and play a turn or two. When the book reads right, **It reads right: take the old fields off** removes LumiRPEngine's old secrecy fields (the secret switch, the gate, the holders and the early readers) from its entries. A secret that couldn't become a mark keeps them.

**Restore secrecy as it was** puts the book back exactly as it stood before its move, every entry the move listed byte for byte, whenever you press it, after Apply or after Confirm: the copy stays. It asks first, naming the entries you changed since the move (it replaces each change, and keeps a copy of it with the backup) and those you deleted (it can't bring those back). Each step works on the book as the page last read it: if the book changed since, the step is refused and the page reads the book again. If Lumiverse refuses a write, the entry says why: one Apply couldn't write keeps its old secrecy, and pressing Confirm or Restore again finishes what didn't.

### Import from lore

Already have lore books? **Import from lore**, a tool inside a world, builds a starting world from them:

1. Pick the books.
2. The engine reads them on a background task and turns them into proposals: entities, places, rules and more. When the helper's reply has nothing usable (it comes back empty, isn't valid JSON, lists no proposal the engine can read, or proposes nothing for entries that have text), the engine asks once more with a reminder of the format (Refine with AI does the same, though there an empty answer stands); only if that reply fails too does the job say "The helper proposed nothing (or its reply wasn't valid JSON)." Each call's outcome and time go to your diagnostics log.
3. You review the proposals. Each has a checkbox and a confidence badge (70% or more start ticked), **Select all** and **None** are there, and **Show value** reveals what a proposal would set, its sources named by entry and book (*Lighthouse · B2 Chat Lore*). Under each one, **From lore: *book name*** names the book it came from (the entry it cites is linked back to), and **Assumed from a table: *field*, …** lists any fields the lore didn't state and the engine filled from a table. An entity the lore gave no kind takes the world's one base kind, **Thing**, says **Assumed kind: thing** and starts unticked whatever its confidence, so a person filed as a thing is applied only if you tick it; a world with more than one base kind drops it instead, and the job says why. There is no per-proposal edit step: accept or reject, then edit what you accepted in the editors.
4. **Apply n to the draft**, in the bar at the bottom of the page, adds the ticked proposals as entities; the books are linked, never edited. **Commit** when you are happy with the result.
5. Anything the books don't say is left to the data tables and to [detail on demand](#detail-on-demand). A field that comes from a table (an occupation, a region, a catalog item) which the lore leaves out or names loosely takes the best-matching table row, and its proposal says so. A thing's catalog item is looked for in the gear tables (what it is) before the price catalog (what it costs), so a "Truck bed" becomes the pickup bed the Containers table marks as part of a vehicle, never a bed sheet set.

### Build / Refine with AI

**Build / Refine with AI** is a tool inside a world. You can ask the AI to draft or improve parts of it, and it returns **proposals**, never direct changes: you review them as you would an import, tick the ones you want (each has a confidence badge, and proposals at 70% or more start ticked) and apply them to the draft. A change to an existing entity that leaves out its kind keeps the kind it has. Proposals are checked by the world checker as part of this, and the work happens in the background, so you can keep editing.

Each tool remembers its last run, with its status and its proposals, so you can leave Build, reload or restart and come back to review them. Its page shows the run under **Status**, with **Run again with new input** folded below it, and then the **Proposals**, each by name, with **Apply n to the draft** in a bar that stays at the bottom of the page. An import saves its proposals as it reads each part of the books. A job the server or the extension stopped while it ran shows as **Interrupted**: the proposals it made before it stopped are still there to review and apply, and **Run it again** starts it over with the same books or request. **Dismiss** clears a finished job; the engine keeps only the last few anyway.

### Describe a map

**Describe a map** draws a map from your own words. Pick the place it maps, give the map an id if you like, and describe the inside ("A small inn: the taproom at the front, the kitchen behind it through a door, and a corridor down the side to the stable yard."), then tap **Draw the map**. The AI writes an lre-map-v1 map, and the engine checks it as the map editor would; if it has problems, the AI gets one more try with the list of them. You get one proposal with a preview of the map, ticked when it came back clean and unticked, with what's still wrong, when it didn't. Applying it puts the map in your draft like any other, where the map editor can fix the rest; nothing reaches your chats until you commit. Like the other tools, it remembers its last run.

### Proposing routines from lore

**Propose routines from lore**, a tool inside a world, gives your people and groups [routines](#routines-in-build) and schedules from what the world already says about them: its lore entries, their sheets (role, occupation, workplace, home, traits, description), their families, households and clubs, and who they know. It proposes two kinds of thing:

- **Schedules**: shifts, school and class timetables, services. A shop's opening hours become the shifts of the people who work there.
- **Routine blocks**: habits.
  - **Fixed** blocks happen at a set time ("dinner at six").
  - **Flexible** blocks happen somewhere in a window, every day or a few times a week ("most mornings", "twice a week").
  - **Optional** blocks happen only sometimes ("now and then").

A household's dinner, a club's evening or worship with a congregation comes back as the group's block, with its members as company.

**Sources.** Every block and schedule cites the lore entry or sheet field it rests on, with a short quote from it. The engine checks the quote against that text, and anything without a source that holds is never proposed. So someone the lore says nothing about gets no routine at all, and a habit the lore states loosely comes back flexible or optional, never fixed.

**What it reads.** It reads only what may reach the player: public lore, entries whose secret is already out, public sheets and places. Secret lore and secret people never go into it, so nothing it proposes gives a secret away.

**How much lore each one gets.** Each person and group gets their lore in whole entries, the most about them first, up to **Lore each person or group reads** in **Settings → Steps and connections → Build jobs** (8,000 tokens to start; turn on **all of it** for everything, however long). No entry is ever cut short. When the limit leaves entries out, the job says whose and how many of theirs it read. People whose lore fills the limit get a helper call of their own.
**What the engine checks.** Each proposal is checked against your draft:

- its places, people and company exist;
- its times can be;
- anyone it names as company is tied to the person, belongs with them, or is named with them in the cited text;
- its label names no one.

When the AI got any of this wrong, it gets one more try with the list of what to fix.

**What it points out.** A proposal can clash with something already there:

- a schedule or a routine block the person already has;
- another proposal;
- the opening scene, by taking someone away from where the story starts them.

The clash is pointed out in its problems, and it never replaces what you wrote. Something the world already has is left out.

**Using it:**

1. Choose whom for: **Everyone** (every person and group the job may read, never a secret one or your own character), or **Choose** and tick people and groups.
2. It runs in the background, with its progress and **Cancel**. It reads a few people and groups at a time, within the same twelve helper calls an import makes; a pass that stops before everyone was read says so and offers **Continue**, which starts the next pass where this one stopped. Apply what you want from a pass first: the next pass's proposals replace it.
3. The review groups the proposals by the person or group they're for. Each says what it sets (a block's kind, time, length, days, place and company; a schedule's field, how it repeats and its slots) and what it rests on, **From lore:** *entry* or **From a sheet:** *name · field*, with the quote; tap a lore entry's title to read it in place. Under it is what checking it against the draft found: a clash with their schedules or routine, a place or company it can't have, pulling someone away from where the story starts them, or a routine or schedule of the same id already in the draft. A proposal with any of these starts unticked; the rest start ticked at 70% or more. A proposal that doesn't rest on a lore entry or a sheet field is never offered, and the review says how many were left out.
4. **Edit** opens a proposal in Build's own routine or schedule form. **Done** keeps the edit and ticks it, and **As proposed** drops it. The edit is what's applied, and anything wrong with its shape shows before you apply.
5. **Apply n to the draft** writes the ticked proposals into the draft, each as a routine or schedule of its own, where the editors can change them; nothing reaches your chats until you **Commit**. A schedule comes as a pair, where they are and what they're doing: apply both.

Like the other tools, it remembers its last run, and your ticks and edits stay while you open a lore entry or another page and come back, until you reload.

### Money in Build

A world's money lives in its entities and the economy pack's config, and every part of it has a form. Amounts are typed as the world's currency writes them. Lists inside a money field (owners, splits, shares, lots, deductions and payment-preference tiers) show a row for each entry that opens it as a sheet, with a button below to add one (**Add owner**, **Add split** and so on); weekdays and months are chips to toggle, and days of the month are typed in a row. Problems show in place, beside the field, row or switch they belong to.

- **Accounts** (kind `account`): its owners (each with a role, owner, joint, custodian, beneficiary, trustee or authorized user, and a share of its worth), its **product** (one of the world's products, or a built-in class: cash, checking, business checking, savings, money market, certificate of deposit, trust, escrow, credit card, charge card, line of credit, loan, mortgage, brokerage or retirement), its opening balance, status and any terms it sets itself over its product's (limit, APR, APY, statement and due days, minimum payment, a loan's payment and months left, what secures it, its overdraft policy and autopay).
- **Payment cards** (kind `card`): the account, the holder, debit, credit or charge, its state, the card item in the holder's gear, the last four digits and its daily limits.
- **Flows** (kind `flow`): standing payments, income and one-offs, with their amount (a fixed amount, a formula, a table cell, a range, a percentage of a balance, what's due on a credit account, or a revenue stream's model, which [Build → Organizations](#organizations-in-build) edits), schedule, splits, shares among a household (**Split equally among a household's members** fills them in), what happens when the money isn't there, and what they belong to.
- **Jobs** (kind `job`), **businesses** (kind `business`), **trusts** (kind `trust`), **holdings** (kind `holding`) and **property** (kind `property`), each with its own fields: a job's employer (any party: a business, a household, a group, a trust, the party or a person), pay, schedule, position, deductions and where pay goes; a business's owners, the places it trades at (chips, added with **Add a place…**), its terms and its days, and for its taxes the **Earnings and profits it had kept when the story begins**; a trust's terms (its settlor, whether it can be taken back, what it is for, the day it ends, and whether its trustees decide each on their own or together); a holding's asset, lots and where its payouts go; a property's place, owners, value and costs. An organization's people, positions and money also have its page in [Build → Organizations](#organizations-in-build).
- **Mineral interests**: a property set to **mineral** (**What it is**) is a mineral or royalty interest: it pays its property tax, but no insurance or upkeep, and no housing market moves its value. Give it a **valuation** (present value, cap rate or multiple) and link its royalty streams to it, and its value follows what they will pay, recorded on the 1st of each month; with streams and no valuation, it is valued at their present value over 30 years at 10%.
- **Depreciation**: **Build → Packs → economy → Books** sets it for the world, **None: costs are expensed** (the default) or **Straight-line, with cost depletion**, and each organization's **Depreciation** field overrides it. A property's **Capital cost**, **Useful life (months)**, **In service from**, **Salvage value** and **Reserves (units)** drive it: straight-line takes its cost less its salvage over its life, month by month, from the month after it went into service, and a mineral interest's cost depletes by the units its royalty streams produced each month against its reserves. Each posts on the 1st for the month just ended, to the owners whose books depreciate, by their shares. A project's capitalized steps post as capital spending only where its organization depreciates; elsewhere they're expensed. People's own things never depreciate.
- **People**: their payment preferences, in order, with the amounts and categories each tier is for.
- **Households**: a household is a group (kind `household`) its members join with **Member of**; shared costs are flows with shares.
- **Places**: **Add a cash machine** adds a cash machine (its bank, its surcharge, the most it pays out at once, and whether it takes deposits), and **Add a bank branch** a bank branch (its bank).
- **Build → Packs → economy**: under **Prices and region**, the **Narrated-price cap**, the **Claim cap** (the most a thing you say you have may cost before a capped Make it real asks first; see [Player claims](#player-claims)) and the world's **Region** (whose cost of living and tax rates price things for anyone who doesn't name a region of their own; **Not set: the national averages**); its **Living costs** (a week's spending for each wealth band; set any band and the world's amounts replace the catalog-priced defaults for every band, so a band left empty spends nothing); its **Taxes**: the **Corporate tax rate** a C corporation pays on its profit (the federal 21% when it's left empty); **Tax on kept earnings** (off by default: on, a C corporation pays the personal holding company tax or the accumulated earnings tax on 15 April for what it kept the year before; see [Ownership and Taxes](#ownership-and-taxes)); the unemployment taxes an employer pays on its staff's wages, each left empty for the data tables' figure, which its hint gives: **FUTA rate before the credit (%)** (6), **FUTA wage base** ($7,000.00), **FUTA credit for state tax (%)** (5.4), **SUTA rate (%)** (each employer's state's new-employer rate, the world's region's shown; an organization's own rate, its field **Its state unemployment tax rate (%)**, comes first) and **SUTA wage base** (each employer's state's); and the **Exempt from sales tax** row, which opens a page with **Taxed** or **Exempt** for each spending category (an exempt category is never taxed, wherever it's sold) and **Done**; its **Books**: **Depreciation** (above); its **Shares**: **Units a company starts counting in**, the units a company whose owners hold their shares in percent counts them in the first time it issues shares, buys some back or sets its classes (1,000 when it's left empty), **Listing: the most the public pays, times its worth a unit** (the most the public pays a unit for a company's new units on the market, as a multiple of its books a unit, or once it's listed of its market price; 1 when it's left empty, no more than they're worth, and never less than 1), and how a listed company's price moves: **Listed prices: return to the books, a year** (how fast its price returns to its books a unit; 2 when it's left empty, 0 lets it wander), **Listed prices: swings, % a year** and **Listed prices: moving with the market, 0 to 1** (1 moves it by the stock market alone, the same in every chat; both as the finance tables' single stock when left empty); under **Money features**, the **Feature switches** row, which opens a page of its own with a switch for each money feature the world turns on or off (under any preset), and **Done**; its own **Products** (each with its class and whatever it sets: limit, rates, interest method, statement and due days, minimum payment, fees, rewards, daily limits, hold days, overdraft and term) and its **Assets** (each with its class, symbol, starting price and price path, payouts, and a bond's or an option's terms). A product or an asset is a row that opens as a sheet, with **Remove product** (or **Remove asset**) and **Done**; **Add product** and **Add asset** ask for a name and make the id from it. What a product leaves out comes from its class's defaults in the finance tables.
- **Build → Economy → Cash**: the world's cash rule, **From Money 4**, **Needs a machine** or **Anywhere**; a chat may set its own (GM → This chat → **Money**).

#### When a loan bills

A loan bills only once it is set up as owing and its people are followed at Money 3 or more. In Build, enter the loan's **Amount owed** (it is stored as a negative balance); give it a **due day** (else it uses its product's statement day), and a **payment** or the **months left** if you know them. Each month at 12:01 AM on its due day it adds the month's interest and asks for that month's payment (or, with neither set, the payment that pays it off over its term; with no term at all, everything at once), due by the end of that day. Autopay pays it then; otherwise you're reminded that morning, and a payment missed past midnight is late: a late fee, and then the late stages on the credit report. A loan added after 12:01 AM on its due day first bills the next month.

### Data tables

Prices, pay, taxes, rents, vehicles, gear and the calendar's holiday rules come from **data tables**. The defaults are **Modern US**: approximate figures reflecting roughly 2025–26, recalled from public sources and rounded, not live data. Prices are national averages; each state's cost-of-living multiplier scales them when something is priced. Included are about 340 priced items in 19 categories, 56 occupations with pay and typical schedules, the 50 states and DC with tax rates (state unemployment tax among them), rents, electricity and minimum wage, finance parameters (credit cards, deposit accounts, loans, investing, payroll deductions, federal tax, unemployment taxes), the tax-exempt statuses an organization may hold (a charity, a church, a social club), 19 vehicle classes, 90 gear items, 34 containers, 5 kits, and federal holidays, common observances and school-year and work-shift patterns. One table is exact rather than approximate: the stock index's history, the Thrift Savings Plan C Fund's month-end closes from May 2003 to October 2026, copied from its published file, which the market follows (see **Investments** under [Your money](#your-money)).

You can edit your world's copy of any table in **Data tables**. The list shows each table's pack, how many rows it has and whether the figures are *approximate*, *exact* (copied from their published source) or *authored*; new tables come from packs, so there is no New. A table's page has **About this table** (its schema, revision and source), then its **Rows** as a grid, 25 to a page, with **Search rows** and the first column kept in view as you scroll sideways; a row with a problem shows how many. Tap a row's name to open it as a sheet with a field per column (money in the world's currency), where your changes go into the table as you type; **Delete row** asks first, and **Done** closes it. **New row** asks for the row's name and takes its id from it (or asks for the id, in a table whose rows have no name). **Save table** puts the table in the draft, saying how many problems it still has, and **Revert** sits beside it; saving makes a new revision of the table. Each world revision pins the table revisions it uses, so a running chat never sees a table change until you [Upgrade](#upgrade). Swapping in another region's table isn't in the editor yet.

**Data updates.** A world keeps the tables it was made with, so a newer LumiRPEngine's tables (more rows, sizes and what a holder takes, aliases such as "TV" for a television, a new table like the stock index's history) don't reach it on their own. While this build ships newer versions than the draft has, **Build → World** opens with a **Data updates** card, and the world home's **World** row says **Data updates**. Each table reads **Newer shipped version** for a copy you never changed, with the rows and columns the new version adds and changes; **New table** for one the world doesn't have; or **Changed by you · newer shipped version available** for a copy you changed. **Apply** puts the newer versions of the tables you never changed, and the new ones, in the draft. A table you changed is never replaced: tap it to review what the shipped version changes and what you changed since the version your copy came from, then **Keep mine**, or **Use the shipped version**, which asks first and puts the shipped table in its place. A world that keeps none of the shipped tables is offered none. A template's own tables (Brindlemere's and Harrowmere's prices, categories, goods and vehicle classes) have newer versions of their own: a world made from **Fantasy town** or **Mystery manor** on an earlier build reads **Newer Fantasy town version** (or **Mystery manor**) for a copy it never changed, and Apply puts that template's newer table in, never the Modern US one; a table rewritten past them stays the world's. As with any change, **Commit** makes it a revision, and each chat takes it when you [Upgrade](#upgrade) it: **GM → This chat → Pins and upgrade** marks the chat's tables that have newer shipped versions, with **Open Data updates** while the world doesn't have them yet.

### Templates

In Build, tap **New world** and pick a card under **Start from** (**Blank**, **Fantasy town**, **Mystery manor** or **Modern slice-of-life**). The **Name** field takes the template's name until you type your own; then tap **Create world**. A template is a normal world with its own cast, schedules, quests, rules, weather and belongings, plus two dozen or so lore entries on its places, people and history (kept in the world, where the engine's helpers read it; the brief and the prose outline draw on its public entries for the narrator); change anything you like. Its puzzles show in **Play → Story → Quests** once you have come across them: the study safe when you step into the study, the vestry tiles when you find the vestry floor, the township truck when the shop takes the job.

- **Fantasy town** is *Brindlemere*, a river-ford market town on the eve of harvest, on a made-up calendar of thirteen moons. It starts on the **Story** preset and counts money in bits, marks and crowns.
- **Mystery manor** is *Harrowmere Hall*, a storm-bound fenland country house in October 1934, in pre-decimal money. It starts on the **Grounded** preset and has clues to find and a locked safe to open.
- **Modern slice-of-life** is *Port Alder*, a Lake Michigan harbor town in September 2026, in US dollars. It runs on the **Simulation** preset: payroll, rent, a garage, a boat and a shop to look after, a small index-fund account to invest in (Invest and Sell investments, on the Simulation preset), a school term for the landlady's youngest granddaughter, and a neighbor away on a weekend trip.

### Problems, Check world and Playtest

**Problems** runs the full lint on the draft and reports every problem in plain language: each row has a badge for how bad it is (**Error**, **Warning** or **Info**), the message, and where it is by name ("Entities › Rosie Alvarez"); its path and code show only with **Show ids** on. Filter by **All**, **Errors**, **Warnings** or **Info**, tap **Open** to jump to the thing (an item, or the part of the world it is in: a tier problem or the tier new people join opens **Tiers**), and **Check again** after a fix. **Check world** plays the world forward in the background and shows a transcript of each day as the author's view: every event, worded with names (no ids or bookkeeping, up to 40 lines), including people the player hasn't met and secrets, so you can check off-screen routines. Dropped changes, failures and leaks go in the notes. **Playtest** plays through the world in the background, without touching any chat: pick the number of turns (1 to 50), optionally give it a script, and tap **Run playtest** for a transcript and an **Issues found** list; its transcript shows each turn as a player would see it. A scripted line can say an action in its own words, with filler words in between ("walk down to the coffee shop"); when several actions fit, the one whose details the line fills is used. An action's words count only when you are the one doing it, in the first person ("I", "we", "let's") or as a command; a line that negates it ("I don't want to walk to the market"), says it of someone else ("Danny says he'll walk over to the marina"), muses about it ("I wonder whether to buy a boat", "I might", "thinking about") or uses it in an idiom ("I pay attention") triggers nothing. Playtest and Check world make no model calls: nothing reads typed text, judges an approach or decides an NPC's close call, so checks roll and close calls go by the numbers. Like looking in GM mode, they leave nothing recorded.

Each day of **Check world** also lists its slowest simulated processes ("Slowest processes: economy.money 1.2 s · …"): the five slowest, each at least 1 ms. This is wall-clock time. It is never in the day's notes, its reply or its events, and a kept job keeps it across a restart.

### Import and export

Each world's **More for** *name* button on the Worlds page holds **Export** and **Delete**, and **Import a file** on the **New world** page brings one in. Worlds export in the published **`lre-world-v1`** format and import from the same; the world's own lore travels inside the file, and your World Books stay separate. A world is one document, so it can be copied, shared and read by other tools: its JSON Schema is `schemas/lre-world-v1.schema.json`. It carries its own **currency** (a code, symbol, minor unit, number of decimals and display format), which is why a fantasy town can count in *bits* and the mystery manor in pence (*12d*); every amount in the drawer and the chips is written with it. Importing never changes your settings.

**Imported worlds list any macros in their lore for your review.** After an import, a **Review before playing** card appears above the list of worlds if the file's lore, section templates or step templates contain macros, control characters or `<user>`, `<char>` or `<bot>` shorthands (all things Lumiverse's macro engine acts on): "“*name*” came from a file. Its lore and templates contain macros that Lumiverse runs when it builds a prompt; open each one and check it before you play." Each row names the macro, control character or shorthand and where it is, and **Open** takes you to that lore entry, section or step; **Done reviewing** puts the card away (it is not kept, so open the Lore list and look for double braces if you come back later). The toast says how many items there are to review. Nothing is changed for you: macros in lore you write yourself are kept as written, just as in any World Book.

### Revisions and Upgrade

You edit a world as a draft: the world bar in **Build** says **Uncommitted** while the draft has changes, with its error and warning counts, and **Commit** makes it a new revision. While errors remain, **Commit** says how many block it, and tapping it asks you to fix them first. **Discard changes**, under the bar's **Draft actions** button, throws away every uncommitted change once you confirm with **Discard**. When you **commit**, the draft becomes a new revision; revisions that a chat depends on are never deleted automatically. A chat stays on the revision it was started on. Once you commit a newer revision of its world, **GM → This chat → Pins and upgrade** shows the world, the chat's revision and its pinned generators and tables, and offers **Upgrade to the latest revision**. (An imported world is a new world, so it brings no Upgrade. An extension update changes no stored revision either: the newer data tables it ships reach a world through **Data updates** in **Build → World**, as a revision you commit; see [Data tables](#data-tables).)

A world has one draft, shared by every window and device you use. When something else changes the draft while Build has it open (another window, or an outside agent working through the `lumirpengine` CLI or its MCP server), Build reloads it and tells you: *This world's draft changed in another window, or an outside agent changed it; Build reloaded it.* Changes are made one at a time, so none is lost. A draft begun before the world got a newer revision can't be committed over it: discard it and start again from the latest revision.

If you were editing an item when it changed elsewhere, Build keeps your edits, even text you are still typing in a field: the world bar says *This item changed elsewhere while you were editing it.* and offers **Load theirs** (the other change, in the form) and **Keep mine**. Saving over the other change asks you first (**Save mine**). A discard or a commit of a draft that changed while Build was asking you is refused, and Build reloads the draft so you can look again. Build also reads the draft again when it reconnects or you come back to the page, in case it missed a change.

Each world keeps a **history** of how each of its revisions became the latest: created, committed in Build, committed by an outside agent (with the note it gave), or imported. Worlds made before this was kept start their history at their next commit. The `lumirpengine` CLI and MCP server show the history, and they can put an earlier revision back into the draft for you to commit again.

#### Upgrade

Upgrade moves a chat to the newer revision from the next turn forward. It is recorded like any other change, so it works with swipes, branches and Re-narrate. Detail that has already been shown stays as it was; the new tables and generators apply to anything not yet shown. Nothing ever changes silently.

**Upgrade to the latest revision** first shows what the revision brings into the chat, entity by entity: people and things that are new, ones the revision no longer has, and each change to the rest. That covers fields such as appointments, home or employer, plus name, tier, classes, relations, an account's opening balance, and **Placement**: where something is and the map cell it lies on, taken or kept as one, so a thing a new map lays in a cell, or moves, goes there. Something you picked up or put away counts as moved in play, and anything still in someone's hands or inside a container stays there: the Upgrade never lays it on the map. The rest of the world's definitions (places, routes, rules, actions, checks and the like) aren't listed: they come with the revision, as in a new chat. Each change shows the chat's value, the revision's, and why it is set the way it is:

- **Unchanged in play:** nothing in the chat has touched it, so it's set to **Take**.
- **Changed in play:** a turn, a fix or a GM edit changed it, so it's set to **Keep**. That holds even if it later changed back.

Flip any change with its **Take**/**Keep** toggle. **Take all unchanged** resets the untouched ones to Take; **Review changed in play** lists only the touched ones. Someone the revision no longer has stays in the chat unless you pick **Remove**. **Upgrade with these picks** applies it.

A company made a trust, or a trust a company (Build's **Change what it is**), is one change, **Change what it is**: from its kind and owners to its new ones, the fields it drops and whom it seats. Its pick takes or keeps all of it, so the organization never ends up half one kind and half the other.

A change that would give a secret away (someone or somewhere your character doesn't know, a quest kept from you) reads **A hidden change** until you tap **Show spoilers**, as GM mode waits behind its own gate. This gate is the preview's own: it starts closed each time, whatever the GM gate is doing. Its toggle works either way, and its pick applies like any other.

A quest sitting at a stage the revision removed goes back to its first stage, with a Journal note. If an earlier Upgrade moved the chat without bringing these changes in, Pins and upgrade says so (**Not everything came in**) and offers the Upgrade even at the latest revision.

## Your agent's MCP server

Your own agent (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor…) reaches your worlds through the `lumirpengine` MCP server ([MCP.md](MCP.md)). It follows LumiRPEngine: when you update LumiRPEngine, a server on the same machine as Lumiverse loads the new build's tools and guide by itself, with no reinstall.

Your agent can also try a chat in a **sandbox**: a scratch copy of it on the agent's machine, fast-forwarded months at a time to see the bills, pay and standing payments month by month, or given typed turns such as "send $500 to Danny". Nothing done there reaches your chat, and no model runs. It needs your say-so to look behind the curtain, since the copy holds everything, secrets included. When you edit a world through your agent, it also sees what each edit brought into the world's checks and what's new in the draft since the last save.

**Settings → MCP server** (under *Your agent*) shows the setup that follows LumiRPEngine, with this extension's own path, under each runtime that runs it, each with **Copy**: first `bun <path>/dist/lumirpengine.js mcp`, for Bun 1.4 or newer, which Lumiverse itself runs on, so it is always on Lumiverse's machine; then `node <path>/dist/lumirpengine.js mcp`, for Node 22 or newer. Give one to your agent's harness as the command for its lumirpengine server. On a machine with only Bun, use the Bun one: installing with npm, or running with npx, needs Node. Below it is each server that has connected from your account, with the harness that runs it, its machine, its builds and whether it is connected now (Claude Code's and Codex's servers on one machine are two lines; a restarted one keeps its line): **Update now** asks one that hasn't reloaded yet to do it, and **Forget** drops one you no longer use. A server running as more than one process (two Claude Code windows on one machine) says how many, *2 processes*, and its line is the one furthest behind: it says *reloaded* only once all of them have.

**Lumiverse can run the server too.** In Lumiverse's Settings → MCP Servers, add a Stdio server with the command `bun` (or `node`) and the arguments `<path>/dist/lumirpengine.js, mcp` (the same path): Lumiverse runs only `node`, `bun`, `deno` and `python` there, not `lumirpengine`. Sign in once first, as the user Lumiverse runs as. It is listed like any other server, as *lumirpengine for lumiverse*, and follows each update. Lumiverse, though, keeps the tool list it read when it connected, so once the server has reloaded, its line offers **Reconnect in Lumiverse**. Pressing it asks the server to finish any tool call in progress and exit; Lumiverse starts it again about a second later, with the same command, and reads the new tools. The line waits for it to come back (Settings → Timeouts → **MCP server reconnect**, 1 min) and then reads updated; if it isn't back in time, the line says so, and you can **Disconnect** it and **Connect** it again in Lumiverse's Settings → MCP Servers. LumiRPEngine never adds or changes a Lumiverse MCP server. A server set up before this button existed doesn't know how to exit when asked, so its line asks you to reconnect it by hand that one time.

To have the line check Lumiverse's own tool list rather than take the server's word for it, switch on LumiRPEngine's **Mcp Servers** permission in Lumiverse's Extensions tab (only an owner or admin can). With it, LumiRPEngine reads which of your Lumiverse MCP servers hold LumiRPEngine's tools, and nothing more of them: only those servers' names show on the line. Without it, **Reconnect in Lumiverse** still works, and the line says Lumiverse's own list can't be checked.

**After an update** a toast says so, once, and LumiRPEngine's tab in the drawer opens on **LumiRPEngine updated**, with a line for each server:
- *reloaded this build's tools*: nothing to do;
- a server Lumiverse runs that *reloaded this build's tools, but Lumiverse's MCP client keeps the list it got when it connected*: press **Reconnect in Lumiverse** (above);
- *hasn't reloaded yet*: it does within a minute, or press **Update now**. Right after the update no server has checked in with the restarted LumiRPEngine yet, so the line says so: a running one takes the new tools at its next check, and a stopped one when it next starts;
- *can't follow*: why, and what to do, such as installing again on another machine (**Copy the install**) or using the setup above (**Copy the setup for Bun 1.4 or newer**, or **for Node 22 or newer**). A server never loads tools other users of the machine could change: when Lumiverse's extensions folder, or the extension's files in it, can be written by anyone but their owner, the line names the path, and the fix is to make it writable by its owner only (on Linux and macOS; Windows isn't checked);
- *from before MCP servers followed LumiRPEngine*: update it once (`npm i -g github:TheLiquorPriest/LumiRPEngine`), or switch it to the setup above.

The notice stays until you press **Dismiss**, and comes back with the next update. If LumiRPEngine's tab was open when it updated, Lumiverse switches the drawer to another tab while it reloads the extension (on a phone, the RPEngine button can end up just past the edge of the tab strip): open RPEngine again to see the notice.

## Troubleshooting

**A reply came back empty, and the turn says "degraded" with a Retry chip.**
The model returned nothing, which reasoning models do when their thinking uses up the token limit. The engine won't regenerate on its own. Raise **max tokens** to **1,500 or more** in your preset or connection settings, then tap **Retry**. The world checker warns you when a preset's max tokens are below 1,500 with a reasoning model, and **Settings → Caveats** shows the warning too.

**The helper steps fail, or return nothing.**
The same limit applies to the helper calls (reading typed text, reading replies, minds). A reasoning model needs several thousand tokens, because its thinking counts toward the limit; every step starts at 8,192. Set the step's **Max tokens** in **Settings → Steps and connections** if a step's replies still come back cut off; it warns you below 4,096.

**Settings → Caveats says it's still checking.**
Caveats checks your preset against the open chat with a test prompt build, which takes a while on a long chat. The page and its row on the Settings list say **Checking your preset…** until it answers; if the build is still running after the **Preset check** time (1 minute to start, in **Settings → Timeouts**), the page says so and the next visit picks up the answer, and if the build fails, the page says the preset's warnings aren't known. Settings itself never waits for it.

**Settings → Caveats says a step's connection or model is gone.**
Caveats checks the helper model's connection and every switched-on step's own connection and model before your preset: a connection you deleted in Lumiverse, or a model its connection no longer lists, would make that step's call fail and stop the turn. The warning's **Open Steps and connections** takes you there to pick another (or open **Helper model** from its top row). A connection that doesn't list its models within the **Preset check** time leaves its steps unchecked, and Caveats says so as information, not a warning; open it again in a moment. When Settings saves a value other than the one you gave (out of range, say), the page says what it kept.

**The AI keeps writing a JSON block, or the replies look wrong.**
Some presets contain a **JSON-ledger block**: a block that makes the narrator append a JSON object (gold, inventory) to its replies. It can confuse reasoning models, and it competes with the engine's own record of money and belongings. The world checker warns you if the active preset has one, and **Settings → Caveats** shows the warning too. Use a preset without that block for world chats.

**A section turns up outside my preset's blocks.**
Your preset places some of the world's sections with `{{outlet::lre_…}}` but not all of them, so the ones it leaves out go in where the world places them. **Settings → Caveats** names the missing outlets; add them to the block where the others are.

**A section says "not delivered".**
The test prompt build didn't find it in the prompt. Check where the world's book and your preset place it (a position your preset doesn't assemble never reaches the prompt), then open **GM → This chat → Context** ([The Context inspector](#the-context-inspector)) again, which runs the check again.

**The chat is paused.**
You edited an earlier message of yours. Choose **Keep** or **Discard** (see [Editing an earlier message of yours](#editing-an-earlier-message-of-yours)). Until you do, new turns are written without simulation.

**The world doesn't match what the reply says.**
You edited the reply, or continued it in a way the engine couldn't read. Tap **Re-read edit** on that reply (or **Retry read** for a stopped Continue).

**A warning says a saved file was damaged.**
A saved file the engine couldn't read, or couldn't make sense of, is kept next to the original as `<name>.corrupt-<time>` (a second damaged copy of the same file gets its own time), and the engine carries on: settings fall back to their defaults, and the chat's journal, extraction learning, chips, plans or inspector history, your jobs or the call meter start fresh. A damaged journal is also reported in the Context inspector's warnings, with where the file is kept. The records saved on your messages still apply; edits, pauses and discards that only the journal held may be gone. A single record on a message that has the wrong shape (from an imported or forked chat, or another extension writing to the same message) is skipped instead: the message reads as if the engine had not simulated it, and the inspector's warnings name the message and swipe. Nothing is deleted.

**Something shows "pending".**
You opened detail or made a change while a reply was streaming. It lands right after the reply finishes.

**A Timelapse stopped early.**
Something interrupted it, and the chip says what: **Timelapse stopped: an invitation** (or a bill, a declined payment, an incident, a quest event, a world rule, someone arriving, an overdue loan, a need, a weather alert), with a **Continue** button that carries on to the original target. The Montage and the Journal say the same. A **Continue timelapse** chip means it hit the safety cap, and a Timelapse that couldn't run at all says why in the turn's changes ("Timelapse not run: …").

**My own lore entries are missing.**
Your entries follow Lumiverse's own rules, and LumiRPEngine never switches one off in your book. For the prompt at hand, it leaves out a secret line, or an entry that is only one, when the narrator's knowledge leaves that secret out; and an entry an earlier version kept secret with its own fields, or one a secret entry of the world was indexed from, stays off until its gate holds. An entry an earlier version stored switched off for a secret stays off in its book until you move the book on [Secrecy in your World Info](#secrecy-in-your-world-info) and switch the entry back on. Otherwise, check that the book is attached to the chat, the entry is enabled and its keywords match, and Lumiverse's World Info budget leaves room for it.

**I imported a world and I'm not sure what's in it.**
A world file is someone else's content, and its lore and templates may contain Lumiverse macros (double braces such as `{{…}}`), which Lumiverse runs whenever it builds a prompt, in a World Info entry or here. LumiRPEngine keeps your own authored text exactly as written, macros included, and never lets a value it inserts (a name, a price, what a model said) form a macro. Besides double braces, Lumiverse's macro engine also acts on control characters and on the `<user>`, `<char>` and `<bot>` shorthands, so the import lists those too. After an import it lists what it found: a **Review before playing** card appears above the world list, with each item as "Review this macro before playing: …", the place it sits in (`lore.<id>.content`, `sections.<id>.template`, `steps.<id>.template`) and an **Open** button for it. Open each, delete or rewrite what you don't want, and tap **Done reviewing** before you attach the world to a chat. The card is shown only right after the import. The step templates' own placeholders (`{{pov}}`, `{{facts}}` and the like) appear in the list too; they belong to the engine and are harmless. Section templates may use only `{{lines}}`; the world checker reports any other macro, control character or shorthand in one as an error.

**A step never seems to run, or the call meter shows fewer calls than I expected.**
A step runs only when one of its signals fires and its phase's budget still has calls. Check the step's **Enabled** switch and its **Signals** (under **Advanced: signals and lore**) in **Settings → Steps and connections**, and the budgets under **Advanced: budgets and the engine budget** in **Settings → Model use** (**Calls** 0 means no model at all in that phase). Lean has NPC choices switched off, so it never asks a model about them.

**The prompt is too long, or the chat history disappeared from it.**
The engine's fixed overhead plus your context is over the connection's limit. Pick a smaller preset (Lean), lower the budgets in **Settings → Model use**, or use a connection with a larger context.

**The map closed the drawer.**
On a phone the map goes fullscreen, so opening it closes the drawer first. Reopen the drawer when you're done. If the drawer can't be closed, Play opens on **Places** instead, where the map is.

**A step failed and the engine did something simpler.**
That's the fallback: a failed decision call goes to the helper, then to the engine alone, and a step that passes its deadline uses its deterministic fallback. The turn still finishes. Check the call meter and your connections.
