## How it works in one page

A **world** is a description of a place and the things in it: people, belongings, money, vehicles, locations, schedules, rules and quests. You attach a world to a chat, and the chat becomes a **world chat**. Everything the engine knows about the story so far is kept in two places: the world itself, and a small **record** saved on every AI reply that says what happened during that turn.

Every turn goes the same way:

1. **You act.** You type, tap an action in the Actions sheet, tap the map, or tap a chip under a reply.
2. **The engine runs first.** When you press Send (or Regenerate, swipe or Continue), Lumiverse starts the reply, and the engine stops it before anything is written: the status line says **Running the engine…**. The engine works out how much time passes, settles your action (its requirements, its dice check if it has one, its effects, how long it takes), and steps the world forward: NPCs go about their day, bills fall due, fuel burns. With minds on, each character present thinks first.
3. **The engine writes the facts.** It writes the established facts as short lines in the world's own sections (Outcome, Scene, People and so on) and puts them in the world's lore book, where the prompt picks them up; then it starts the reply again by itself, with Lumiverse's own Regenerate or Continue. A new reply after a reply only you can start, so then the line says **Ready: press Send for the reply**.
4. **The narrator writes.** The AI writes the reply, with the outcome already decided. Its instructions are to use the facts' numbers and not invent prices, balances, quantities, fuel or times.
5. **The engine reads the reply.** It looks for changes the story implies (someone bought a coffee, moved to the kitchen, learned a public fact), checks each one against its limits, applies or asks about it, runs the people who are off screen, and saves the turn's record. The sections are emptied from the lore book once the reply ends.

If anything the engine needed fails (reading your action, a character's mind, the time skip), nothing is generated: your message gets a card saying what failed and why, with **Retry** and **Diagnostics**. A dropped connection or a busy provider is asked again twice on its own first. **Retry** asks only what failed: the minds that already answered are kept, as long as nothing they thought from changed. If the chat changes after the engine ran and before the reply starts (you edit your message, change one of the engine's settings, switch presets, or run Impersonate), what it prepared is never used stale: nothing is generated, and your message gets a card saying so, whose **Retry** runs the engine again and then writes the reply. Lumiverse saving its own settings (a drawer or a panel you moved) changes nothing the engine prepared.

With the chat open in two windows, only one starts the reply: the one you pressed Send in, or the first to open the chat. The other window's line says **Already started in another window**. If Lumiverse ran the reply for a window or client where the engine isn't running, the window showing the chat starts it instead. A window that was asleep when the engine finished starts the reply when you come back to it ("Ready; reopen the chat to continue").

On a Lumiverse too old to stop a generation, the chat, Settings and Diagnostics say **This Lumiverse can't stop generation; engine state may be missing**: replies then go out without the engine's sections.

The AI proposes; the engine decides. Because the decisions and the numbers belong to the engine, they stay consistent however many times you swipe, edit or branch.

Where things live: the world's definition lives in LumiRPEngine's own storage, and your lore stays in your World Books, which work as usual: LumiRPEngine doesn't manage World Info for now. The world adds one lore book of its own, named "LumiRPEngine · *your world*", which holds only the sections the engine fills in each turn. Records live on the replies themselves, so they follow swipes, branches, exports and deletions, and a backup journal in extension storage restores them if something overwrites them.

## Play

### The drawer

Open the **LumiRPEngine** tab in the Lumiverse drawer. The mode bar at the top moves between **Play**, **GM**, **Build** and **Settings**, and stays there in every mode. The mode you're in has a ring around it, gold in GM.

**Play** is the chat you're in. A bar along the bottom of the drawer holds five places:

| Place | What it holds |
|---|---|
| **Now** | The hub: where you are, what needs you, the fight while one is under way, and who is here. See [Now](#now). |
| **Me** | **You**, **Money**, **Garage** and **Gear**. See [Me](#me). |
| **People** | The people you know. See [People](#people). |
| **Places** | The map. See [The map](#the-map). |
| **Story** | **Quests**, **Journal** and **Calendar**. See [Story](#story). |

A place that holds more than one part shows them as tabs along its top. A place shows only while it has something to show: Now and Story always do, and a place whose packs the world doesn't use is left out. Everything follows your [realism](#realism) levels: a domain that is switched off has nothing to show, and a domain at a low level shows less. A dot on a place, or on one of its tabs, means detail is waiting there.

Details open as sheets inside the drawer: a person, a place, an item, an account or a quest. Each sheet has a back button at its top left. The back button or Escape goes back one level, and moving to another place, or opening another chat, closes them all.

If the chat isn't a world chat, Play says **Not a world chat** and lists **Your worlds**, each with an **Attach** button. Tap **Attach** and confirm in the **Attach world** box. **Open Build** takes you to the editors. A chat keeps the world it started with: attaching the same world again only re-attaches its lore book, and attaching a different one is refused with "This chat already plays another world. Start a new chat for this one."

The other modes: **GM** shows and edits everything, secrets included (see [GM mode](#gm-mode)), **Build** is where you make worlds (see [Authoring](#authoring)), and **Settings** holds your own defaults. Settings opens on a list of pages in groups: **Getting started** (**Helper model**, **Model use**, **Steps and connections**, **Minds**), **Defaults for new chats** (**Realism**, **Checks**, **Time**), **Replies** (**Changes from the reply**, **Swipes**), **Your preset** (**Preset**), **Display** and **About** (**Caveats**, **[Diagnostics](#diagnostics)**). Tap a page to open it; its back button, or Escape, returns to the list. **Realism**, **Checks** and **Time** there set up chats you start from now on; the chat you have open has its own in GM mode, and each of those pages says so, with **Open GM → This chat** to go there. **Settings → Display → Show ids** is off by default. Turn it on to see the id the engine and the world's files use beside each name, in Build's lists, in Problems and in GM mode. Play never shows them: it names things only as your character knows them.

What you can see is always filtered to what your point-of-view character knows. Facts are public, known by particular characters, or secret, and a secret stays out of every place, sheet, chip, choice and Journal line until the story reveals it.

- **People.** Someone you can't see (a secret, or someone known only to particular characters whom you don't know) is never listed: not under **Here**, in **People** or as an action's target, even standing in the room, and the narrator isn't told they're there. You know the people you have dealt with (anyone you have a relation with, or whose things you hold) and anyone you're with. When someone leaves, People keeps what you saw: where and when you were last together, never where they are now or what they're up to.
- **Places.** Some places are hidden until you find them: they stay off the map, the travel list, the place fix and the narrator's facts until you have been there or hear of them. Once found, they stay found. A reply can tell you of one ("you hear about the old boathouse"): that's a **New facts** chip like any other. A secret place is never learned this way; the story reveals it.
- **What you've seen.** The engine keeps a record of every place you have been and of whom you last saw where, written as the story moves people around, so an existing chat builds it from its history.

### Now

**Now** is where Play opens. From the top:

1. **Banners.** **Paused** while the chat waits on an edit of yours, with the Keep report inside it (see [Editing an earlier message of yours](#editing-an-earlier-message-of-yours)), or **A turn is running** while a reply is being written: changes you make then apply when it finishes.
2. **The scene card**: the time and the part of the day (with the time scale when it isn't 1×, and **Clock paused** while the clock is paused), your place, the scene type with how long the last turn took, the weather and your cash. A value you can correct, the time among them, has an edit button (✎) beside it; see [Fixing a value](#fixing-a-value).
3. **Needs you**: the Keep report, then every decision under a reply that lapses when you send your next message, with how many there are. Tap one to see its reply: on a phone the drawer closes and the chat scrolls to it, and on a desktop the chat scrolls. If that reply isn't loaded in the chat, a notice asks you to scroll to it.
4. **The Encounter**, while a fight is under way: the round and whose turn it is, the turn order with HP and conditions, what you have left this turn, **Combat actions (n)**, which opens the Actions sheet on its **Combat** tab, **End turn**, and this round's acts. See [Combat and encounters](#combat-and-encounters).
5. **Here**: the people with you. Tap someone to open their person sheet.
6. **Glances**: short cards from the world's packs, each with **Open** to go to its part. **Coming up** shows your next two commitments, **Story** the next objective of the quest in hand, **Garage** low fuel, an overdue loan or service due, **Money** your money's alerts and then what's coming up within about three days (three rows at most, each opening the account or payment it names), **Gear** things you lent that are overdue, and **You** your conditions. A glance with nothing to say doesn't show.
7. **Quick actions**: **Act** opens the Actions sheet, **Timelapse** opens it on its Timelapse tab, and **Map** goes to **Places**.

### Me

**Me** has a tab for each part of your own life:

| Tab | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **You** | Your name, where you are and what you're doing, and your tier; your conditions; then your character sheet in sections: stats, skills and derived values, as numbers or as words, depending on the level. A pool such as HP is a bar of current over max that turns red at a quarter or less, and a level shows a bar of XP toward the next one. A value you can correct has an edit button. |
| **Money** | Your money hub (see [Your money](#your-money)): an overview, then a row for each piece of your money you have (accounts and cards, standing payments, jobs, businesses, investments, property, credit and your household), each opening its own page, shown as simply or as fully as your Money level says. In a new chat it shows the balances the world gives you to start with, under a **Not opened yet** banner, and the cash in the status line and the pill matches; opening the tab puts those amounts on your books, so the figures don't change when it does (during a reply they go on when the reply finishes). |
| **Garage** | A card for each vehicle: fuel (marked low when it is), condition, location and home base, odometer or engine hours, who has it on loan (with **Overdue**), and upcoming service. A location or home base you don't know about reads **Somewhere**. |
| **Gear** | What you have, sorted into **Owned**, **Worn**, **Carried** (the one that opens first) and **Stored**, with container loads, kits ("Kit 3 of 5") and what you have lent out (shown under **Owned**, without your equip slot, since it is with the borrower). A container's chevron button opens and closes what's inside it. A container whose contents haven't been listed yet offers **Look inside**, which opens it (and, once shown, what's in it stays). Tap an item for its item sheet: where it is and what holds it, its owner, slot and quantity, whom it is lent to and until when, its condition, a kit's pieces and what's missing, and a container's load and contents. |

### Your money

**Play → Me → Money** is your money hub. What it shows follows your [realism](#realism): at Money 1 just a wealth band; from Money 2 your cash and accounts; from Money 3 cards, standing payments, income and bills; and at the top level (Money 4) the full books. Below the top level the books stay simple: balances, what's due, a few recent lines. The full books add each account's terms, statements, pending lines and holds, what your net worth is made of, cash flow by category and the next 30 days, investment lots with their gains, your paycheck's taxes and deductions, a business's books and sales, and your credit report. A money feature the world switches on (see [Realism](#realism)) shows its own part of these wherever it runs, and one it switches off shows nowhere, the top level included.

The hub opens on the overview. A large figure leads: your net worth, with your wealth band under it (at Money 1, just the band), and a **Money** *n* badge that adds **· full books** when it shows them. Under it come **Cash on hand** and, in the full books or wherever investments or property run for you, **Assets** and **Owed**. Each alert is a banner, with **Open** when it names an account or a standing payment. Then, at the levels that show them, **Coming up**, **Recent**, **What it's made of** (the full books) and **Cash flow** (by category and the next 30 days in the full books); a **Coming up** row opens the payment or account it names. Under **Your money**, a row for each other section you have gives a one-line summary and opens that section's page; **Needs you** marks one that wants your attention.

Each page opens over the drawer with a back button, and Escape goes back too. Every control opens a page of its own: fill it in, then tap its one button, which says what it does (**Pay** *amount*, **Start the lease**, **Release**). While something is missing, or a box holds what it can't send (a 0 in **Every Nth time**, say), the button waits and the page says why just above it, even when that box is folded away under **More options**. Bookkeeping you do alone (buying or selling investments, exercising an option, where payouts go, moving money between your own accounts, paying a card or loan, autopay, freezing or replacing a card, standing payments, how you pay) happens at once, with no message, no reply and no time passing, and a chip naming what you did with **Undo** (GM → This chat → Edits lists it as your action, not a correction); one that can't happen says why, and nothing changes. Anything else (buying something, paying someone, cash at a machine, business and property) plays as your turn, and the story tells it.

- **Accounts**: every account you hold or share, by kind (cash, bank accounts, cards and credit lines, loans, investment accounts), each row with its balance and what's due, or a loan's payment a month and the months left. Tap one for its page: its balance, what's available, its limit (with how much of it is used) and rate, what's due, a loan's payment and months left, its owners when it's shared, its cards and its lines, each naming who it paid or who paid it (no one, for money between your own accounts). From Money 3, **Pay** pays what a card, a line or a loan owes, from the account you pick: the amount due, the whole balance, or another amount you type. **Transfer** moves money to another of your accounts, and **Withdraw cash** and **Deposit cash** move cash in and out of a bank account. At the top level, in a world that places bank branches or cash machines, that needs one where you are, and the page says so: a branch of the account's bank, or a cash machine. Taking cash out of a machine needs a card on the account (another bank's machine adds its fee), and paying cash in needs the bank's own machine that takes deposits, which holds the deposit for a few days. A world that places none leaves banking offstage, and cash moves anywhere. The **Autopay** row opens a page that sets how a credit account pays itself (the minimum due, the statement balance, the whole balance or a fixed amount, from an account you pick of its debtor's own money: theirs to spend, or theirs by its worth, so a custodial card pays from the custodial savings). **New standing payment**, on the page of an account you manage that can pay one, opens **New standing payment or one-off** with that account as **From**. A card opens its own page, where **Freeze**, **Unfreeze**, **Report lost** (it stops working at once) and **Replace** each open a page that asks you to confirm. **How you pay** lists your payment preferences, in order: a way of paying (cash, a debit, credit or charge card, checking, savings, or one card or account), for amounts and categories that fit; the first tier that fits and covers a purchase pays it, and if none does, your cash, debit card, credit card or charge card, whichever covers it, pays (savings, a money market or a trust account pays only when a tier names it or you do). Its **Change** button opens the page where you set them.
- **Flows**: every standing transfer, bill, income and one-off, grouped **Active**, **Paused** and **Ended**; tap one for its page. **New standing payment or one-off**, at the top of the page, sets one up: a name, from one of your accounts, to one of your accounts, someone you know or someone outside the world by name, an amount, and when: once on a day, or every day, week (on a weekday), month (on a day, or the last day) or year. Under **More options**: every second (or Nth) time, the time of day (09:00 when you don't say), an end date or a number of times, what happens on a weekend or a day-off holiday, a category, and what it does when the money isn't there: skip that time, pay what there is, try again later, pay from another account, go into overdraft as the account allows, or carry it to the next time. On a flow's page, **Edit**, **Pause**, **Resume** and **Delete** each open a page; an edit can rename it, and a time, end date, number of times or category you empty is gone. One that comes from a job, a lease or a loan says so on its page, and is changed there. Rent you pay under a lease also has **End the lease**, which gives notice: the lease ends today. An invoice billed to you, or to a business you run, has **Pay now**, except where the rule below for money you only manage keeps the business's money from the sender's till: an invoice from a business of yours or your household's, or from one whose till you hold a card on, to a business you don't own outright; otherwise it's paid on its due date if the money is there.
- **Jobs**: a job's page shows its pay, schedule, next payday, gross pay each payday, the hours worked, the last paycheck, its **Deductions** (with taxes, in the full books) and **Pay goes to**. From Money 3, **Quit** leaves it.
- **Businesses**: a business you own or run. Its page shows its profit, where it trades (**Trades at**), its **Accounts**, its owners, its books (revenue, expenses, payroll, taxes and profit), month by month, its sales, its invoices, its payroll and its taxes. From Money 3, if you manage it, its buttons are **Hire** (someone you know; not you or your household where you run it for others without a share of it, nor, unless it's wholly yours, anyone else whose pay would go into an account you can spend from yourself, such as one you're custodian of, unless that account is wholly theirs whom the business is for), **Issue an invoice** (to someone you know or someone outside by name; its due date follows the business's own terms unless you give one), **Owner's draw** (for an owner or a joint owner, never from a corporation), and **Pay out profit** by share (with no amount, its profit since the last payout). A job on its payroll opens a page with **Raise** and **Let go** (only **Let go** for someone that rule refuses, where their job's pay splits count as where their pay goes), and an invoice row opens **Void**, or **Pay** for one billed to you. If you own a share, **Your share of its profit goes to** opens a page where you say where it goes (reinvested, to one of your accounts, or to the usual place), and where investments run, **Sell a stake** sells part of it to someone you know or an outside buyer. Its **Accounts** are the ones you hold among its own and, from Money 3 if you manage it, the rest of them, its loan too: each opens its page, where you set its autopay, handle its cards and set up a standing payment from it, though **Pay**, **Transfer** and the cash buttons still take a role of your own on the account. Any others are counted, not named. Money you only manage pays for the business, never for you: a standing payment from it, or falling back on it, can't go to you, your household or anything of yours (though a business you own outright may pay your other businesses), nor, unless you own the business outright, into an account you can spend from yourself, one of its own you hold a card on included; a loan's autopay draws only on the business's own money.
- **Investments**: what you hold, at its price now. A holding's page shows its worth, cost and gain (and its lots in the full books), an option's strike and expiry, and **Distributions go to**, which says where its payouts go (reinvested, to an account, or to the usual place). Where investments run for you (from Money 4), the holding's page has **Buy more** and **Sell** (all of it when you give no amount), and the Investments page has **Buy something new** from what's on the market. A buy says which account pays for it and a sale where its money goes; by default, that's the account it's held in, else your usual account. Where options run, a buy can be a call or a put with its strike and expiry, and **Exercise** takes the contracts you choose (all of them by default) at the strike: a call's strike is paid from the account you pick, and a put's is paid into it.
- **Properties**: what you own, and what's for sale. A property's page shows its value, your share, your equity, its mortgage, **Rent goes to** (which of your accounts the rent lands in), its leases and its running costs (tax, insurance, upkeep); one for sale that isn't yours shows only its listing: its value, where it is when you know the place, and its asking price. From Money 3, **Let it** starts a lease with a tenant you know, a lease row opens **End the lease** (the tenants of a managed property come and go on their own), and **Sell** sells it (its mortgage is paid off from the price first). A property for sale has **Buy**, paid from the account you pick, with a down payment; what the down payment leaves is a mortgage, over the term you pick under **Mortgage over**, from the terms the world's lenders offer (30, 20 or 15 years in the Modern US data, 30 years where a world sets none), with the usual one picked to start. The mortgage is a lender's, as if you had applied for it: it sets the rate by your credit, lends no more than the property is worth, and wants at least 3 % down; if it says no, nothing is bought. The mortgage pays the sellers, and what they owed on it is paid off first.
- **Credit**: your score with its band, on a meter from 300 to 850 (its history, what makes it and your report in the full books), and the credit you could apply for, each with its rate; an offer's row opens its **Apply** page.
- **Household**: what the shared costs come to a month, who you share a home with and each one's share, the shared accounts and the shared costs, each opening its page, which offers changes only where standing payments run for you.

A holding's **Held in** and a property's **Mortgage** open that account's page.

Every control here is something your character does: it plays as your next turn, like an action from the Actions sheet, and the reply narrates it. Your role on an account decides what you can do with it: an owner or a joint owner spends from it and manages it, a custodian or a trustee spends from it and manages it but its worth isn't theirs, a beneficiary sees it and counts its worth but can't spend it, and an authorized user spends with a card but manages nothing. If you own or run a business, you also manage its accounts by that role: you set up and change their standing payments, set their autopay and handle their cards, though you spend the business's money for yourself only with a role of your own on the account, such as an authorized user's card. Money you only manage pays for whom it's for, never for you. That's a business's or a trust's money you hold no spending role on, and the business or trust paying as itself, whatever your role on it. A standing payment from it, or falling back on it, can't go to you, anyone in your household, a business or a trust one of you owns or benefits from (or one that those own in turn), or an account one of those counts as theirs, nor to someone outside typed as your name or your household's, in any capitals or spacing. Unless the money is wholly yours, it can't go into an account you can spend from yourself either (with an authorized user's card, or as its custodian or trustee), even one that's someone else's, unless that account is wholly theirs whom the money is for: as trustee of your daughter's trust and custodian of her account, you may pay her. A payment to someone by name is judged by the account it goes into, their deposit account, so paying someone whose only account you're custodian of is paying into that account; an invoice's payment is judged by the till of the business that sent it, and a worker's pay by their deposit account and their job's pay splits. It still pays whom it's for, such as a trust's beneficiary, even one in your household. Money that's wholly yours, a business you own outright, pays your other businesses and trusts too, though never you or anyone in your household: what you take out for yourself goes by a draw or a salary. The hub leaves out what it can't pay, and says so on the page when a name you type is yours or your household's. Working there gives you no say over its money.

### People

**People** lists **Here** (the people with you) and then **People you know**. Each row shows their role, what they're doing or when and where you last saw them, how they feel about you, and any life state other than alive. **Filter and group** opens **Group by** (the world's classifications that players may see, a story role, say, or their groups or their tiers) and filters by those classifications' values and by group, with **Clear filters** (see [Classifications, groups and kinship](#classifications-groups-and-kinship)). **Your groups**, at the bottom, lists the groups your character belongs to and your role in each.

Tap someone for their person sheet: where they are, or **Last seen** (from Cast life 2) with where and when you last saw them; their **Next** commitment; **Today**, a 24-hour timeline of where they will be, for someone who lives their own day, where **Why this plan?** gives the reasons for each block: a fixed commitment, the need pressing them ("hungry (hunger 62)"), a close call ("close call, Maddie chose"), or "a close friend"; **How they see you**, each axis in words; **What they're after**, their public agendas; their **Groups**; and **Known as**, the classifications you can see. Agendas show only when they're public, each on its own: time someone spends on an agenda that isn't public shows in their day as **Busy**, for "their own business", and someone working on one of their agendas is doing "agenda" only when all their agendas are public, else they are "busy" (here, in the Journal and to the narrator).

**Their usual day**, folded under **Today**, says where each block of that day comes from, as far as you may know: **Usually · their routine**, **Usually · from lore:** with the lore entry's title (only an entry you may read; a block resting on any other reads as their routine), **A commitment** (a shift, an appointment, a trip), **As the story has it**, **A plan of theirs** or **Free time**, with **Why?** for the reasons. It shows only for someone whose day Cast already shows, and never more than Cast does: a private block, or one spent on an agenda that isn't public, reads **Busy**, and no place or company shows. It's what they'd usually do, not a fact about what they're doing now: what happens in the story always comes first.

**Where are they?** on a person sheet corrects where they are, as **✎** on your own place does: it offers only the places you may know of, moves them there for real (out of anything that held them, and what they were doing stays behind), and its **Corrected** chip has an **Undo**.

### Story

**Story** has three tabs.

- **Quests**: quests, puzzles, secrets and agendas, sorted into **Active**, **Done** and **Failed**, each row with its stage and its objectives (n of m). Tap one for its quest sheet: where it stands (its stage and cue), its objectives, what you've learned, and its hints: **Show a hint**, then **Show another hint**, one at a time. Only what you know is shown. To answer a puzzle, type it ("the code is 1887"); while more than one is open, say which in words from its row or its sheet, or the answer is turned down and doesn't count as a try.
- **Journal**: a running list of what changed, newest first, including the full list behind every Montage, plus notes about changes the engine didn't apply and why. It lists what was applied: a change you undid, or one a later switch dropped, isn't shown as made. A group of changes dropped together (a purchase and its payment) is one note, worded like its Keep report row, with "(2 linked changes)" when the group has more than one change. A turn's entry lists the same lines as its change chip, at most 8; when more exist, money, quest and signal lines are kept and routine ones (activities, other people's moves, needs) go first. Filter by **All**, **Notes & montages** or **Not applied**. **Save to lore…** keeps the latest reply, or an excerpt you select or paste, as a lore entry for the world (see [World lore](#world-lore)).
- **Calendar**: the date and the clock, today's holidays and what is coming up, with **Pause clock** / **Resume clock** and **Timelapse…**. This chat's time controls are in GM mode, and the button at the bottom, **This chat's time controls are in GM → This chat**, takes you there (see [Time controls](#time-controls)).

### The status line and the pill

On a phone, a **status line** sits just above the message box: the time (with the pause icon when the clock is paused, and the time scale as n× when it isn't 1×), your place and your cash, what the engine is doing while a turn runs ("Running the engine…", "Asking 3 minds…", "Ready; reopen the chat to continue", "Already started in another window", "Reading the reply…", with the seconds it has taken), and **Paused** while the chat waits on an edit. When the engine's last turn failed, a red **!** beside the line opens [Diagnostics](#diagnostics). **Act** beside it opens the Actions sheet. Tap the line for the **World** sheet.

On a wider screen, and on a phone where Lumiverse has no room above the message box, a small **pill** floats over the chat instead. You can drag it, and it remembers where you left it (the phone and the desktop remember it separately, and when the window crosses between phone and desktop width, the pill moves to that layout's place straight away; until you move it, it stays centred as its content changes). It shows the time (with the pause icon when the clock is paused, and the time scale as n× when it isn't 1×), your place, the weather and your cash. On screens narrower than 760 px it collapses to the time and a **Show place, weather and cash** button. While a turn runs it says what the engine is doing and for how long (collapsed, a pulse and the seconds); **Failed: …** in red means the last turn's work failed, and opens [Diagnostics](#diagnostics). **Paused** means the chat is waiting on an edit. Tap the pill for its details; its **Fix a value** button opens them too.

The World sheet and the pill's details show the same things: the time and the part of the day, your place, the scene (and the last turn), the weather and your cash, then **This turn** (calls, seconds and tokens: the [call meter](#the-call-meter)), the clock button (**Pause clock** or **Resume clock**), and the **Map** and **Actions** buttons.

#### Fixing a value

Tap the edit button (✎) next to a value to correct it: the time, your place, the weather, your cash or the scene type. You'll find it in the World sheet, in the pill's details and on the scene card in **Now**. A correction like this is a **fix**. It is saved with the reply like anything else, so it survives swipes and branches in the same way, and the status line, the pill and the details show the corrected value at once.

- **Time** sets the date and the time of day, in the world's own calendar (a date picker in a real-world calendar, the calendar's own months otherwise) and as the clock reads where you are, starting from now. A date the calendar doesn't have says which days the month has.
  - **A later time** passes as a time jump the story tells does (see [When the story jumps ahead](#when-the-story-jumps-ahead)): the world lives the time in between, so bills fall due, people follow their schedules and you follow your routine, with nothing stopping it and no Montage. When too much happens before then to run in one go, nothing changes and you're told to use a Timelapse, which gets there in steps. During a fight it changes nothing: a fight's rounds take no clock time.
  - **An earlier time** puts the clock back. Nothing that already happened in between is undone, and none of it happens again when the clock passes that time again: a bill already paid isn't paid twice, nobody's schedule moves them again, and no one's mind goes back over it. Past where the clock had been, the world carries on as before. The clock can't go back before the story starts.

  Its **Corrected** chip says how it moved the clock ("Clock moved on 5 h 15 min", "Clock set back 3 h"), and its **Undo** puts the clock and everything the time in between changed back.

- **Weather** has a fix from Weather level 2, where the sky is simulated; you pick one of the climate's states (Clear, Overcast, Rain and so on) for the region you're in. The weather still follows its climate, so your correction holds only until the climate next changes the sky. During a scripted spell of weather, the corrected sky holds until the spell ends.
- **Scene type** lists the four built-in scene types (conversation, action, exploration and downtime) and those of the places you know. Any other, such as one only a place you don't know about has, is never listed, nor named as the place default's, even when the world gives it a tick of its own. When the scene you're in has such a type, the details and the scene card call it **Conversation**, the engine's default scene, though the clock keeps that type's own tick. Your pick holds until an action, a rule or another fix sets the scene type, even when you move. **Place default** goes back to the scene type of wherever you are.
- **Cash** has a fix from Money level 2. Amounts are typed in the world's own money: "5" is 5 bits in Brindlemere, 5d in Harrowmere and $5.00 in Port Alder, with as many decimals as the currency has. When your cash is held in cash accounts (your wallet), a cash fix doesn't overwrite a number: it moves the difference between your wallet and an outside **Correction** account, so **Play → Me → Money** shows it as a correction and the books still balance. In a new chat whose accounts aren't on your books yet, the fix puts them on first, so the difference is measured from the cash you start with. Undo and swipes treat it like any other fix.

Every ✎ fix is checked before it is saved: a value outside the field's range (energy above 100, cash below zero) or not among its choices is refused with the reason, and nothing changes. A saved fix shows a **Corrected** chip on the latest reply, with **Undo**. A fix you make while a reply is streaming waits for the reply to finish; if the reply changed things so the fix no longer fits, a chip says it didn't apply.

### Under each reply

Under each reply the engine ran, a quiet line gives the world's date and time once that turn ended, for the swipe you're looking at, as the clock reads where your character is ("Tue 15 Sep 2026, 12:25 PM"). Times you see read on a 12-hour clock, and temperatures in °F. Under it the engine tells you what it did and asks what it needs to ask. Each thing it says is a **chip**, and a chip shows in one of three ways:

- **The receipt**: one quiet line for what the turn did, at most three parts long (anything that went wrong first, then the time, money, the roll and the changes), then "n more". Tap it for the full list, where every chip shows with its own buttons (**Undo**, **Edit amount** and so on).
- **Decision cards**: a card for each thing that needs your answer, with its buttons. A card marked **Answer before you send** lapses when you send your next message. The Keep report is a card of its own.
- **Offers**: small buttons after the cards for a next move you can take or leave: a Timelapse, the rest of your message, a suggested choice, or **Swipe as…**.

A reply's chips belong to the swipe they came with: they show, and act, only while that swipe is shown, and they go when you delete it, along with what they would have told the next reply.

### The Actions sheet

The **Actions** sheet opens from the input bar's **World actions…** button, from **Act** on the status line and in **Now**, and from **Actions** in the World sheet and the pill's details. It lists the actions that are possible right now, each with its targets, its minutes and price, and, for actions with a dice check, the **exact odds** of every outcome as one bar, with each outcome's chance in words beside it (success in bold). You see the odds before you commit, and they are the real ones: the roll that follows uses exactly those faces. An option that isn't possible stays in the list with a lock and says why in plain words ("Not possible here and now" when a requirement isn't met). When the world's rules for checks decide without dice, the option says so instead of showing a bar: **No roll needed**, **Too hard to try** or **Retry needs a change**; **The story decides (odds 35%)** keeps the bar as the guidance (see [How checks are decided](#how-checks-are-decided)). Before your first turn in a chat, options are judged at your own realism settings, so an option offered isn't declined when the turn runs.

- **Actions**: whatever the world offers here, such as **Go to**, **Move on the map** (with the grid system), **Take**, **Drop**, **Give**, **Wear or equip**, **Take off**, **Use**, **Eat**, **Rest**, **Go to bed**, **Chat with**, **Lose something** (the cash in a lost wallet goes with it), **Transfer between accounts** and the world's own. **Go to** lists the places you can see, nearest first (a route away, or a place inside or around where you are). Typed, it brings along the people with you it names: "Danny and I head to the market" takes Danny, "we" takes everyone here; and your party (people the GM marks as travelling with you) always comes along. **Attempt** lists one option per stat on your sheet, each with its odds. **Take** from a kit asks for the kit, then a piece still in it, with how many are left ("Socket, 3/8 in drive (8 left)"). **Accept invitation** lists only the people who invited you.
- **Buy**: what is sold where you are, one priced option for each offer, at the world's price for that place.
- **Pay** and **Lend**: money and belongings between you and others (moving money between your own accounts is **Transfer between accounts**, under Actions; a world's own action whose id ends in a part starting with `transfer`, the part after its last dot or the whole id when it has no dot, gets a **Transfer** tab). A money option asks for an **Amount** in the world's currency (the box shows its symbol or unit, and its limits), with only as many decimals as the currency has. **Lend** asks for the item, then the person, and has a number box for the days (in typed text you can say an end instead; see [Typed text](#typed-text)). An amount or a number outside its limits says what it has to be.
- **Drive**: pick a vehicle you can use, then the destination.
- **Timelapse**: duration buttons, *until* targets and interrupt overrides. See [Timelapse](#timelapse).
- **Combat**: in a world with combat, the actions that start a fight (**Fight**, and any ambush the world has) against whoever they may target; during a fight, your combat actions against each standing fighter you can see, with their exact odds, and **End turn**, under the round, whose turn it is and what you have left. While it's your turn in a fight, the sheet opens on this tab. See [Combat and encounters](#combat-and-encounters).

Actions that need a second pick show a second list, indented under the pick it follows; choosing again at any step replaces that pick and the ones after it. Only the tabs that have entries appear. The header holds **Pause clock** / **Resume clock** and a close button; the button at the foot of the sheet names your choice (for example "Drive: *vehicle* → *destination*"). Picking something, here, in a panel such as Money or on the map, doesn't play it yet: it waits for your next message, and the status line (on a desktop, the pill) says **With your next message:** and the action. Send your message and it plays that action, with your words as how you go about it, and the engine settles it first, then the narrator describes a result that is already decided. Picking the same thing again, or ✕, clears it; picking another replaces it; **Send now** plays it at once with no words. Bookkeeping you do alone, such as moving money between your own accounts, still happens at once (see [Your money](#your-money)).

The input bar has four buttons for the world: **World actions…**, **Timelapse…**, **Pause clock** / **Resume clock** and **World map**. They work only in a world chat.

### Typed text

You don't have to use the Actions sheet. Every message you type is read by a small helper call, which works out whether your character does something, which of the world's actions it is, and the details. No list of words decides whether a message is an action, so you can put it however you like. The action still has to satisfy its requirements. A thing that matches nothing in the world, or nothing your character knows of, is up to the **Player claims** setting (below); under **Hold me to the world**, and always for money, people and places, the action is left out instead of playing one that would only fail: the reply shows **Pay: couldn't** (with the action's own name) and why, for example "Rena isn't anyone in this world", and the next reply is told it didn't happen. The rest of the message still plays, so "I pull on my work jacket and walk down to Alder Automotive" walks you there even when the world has no jacket. One message can hold several actions ("I walk to Bluebird Coffee, buy a latte and sit by the window"): up to five play in order in the same turn, each from where the one before left you, and their times add up. The turn stops early at a check (it rolls, and you see how it fell before going on), at an action that fails (a requirement, a declined payment), or when something comes up: you go down (dying, or worse), a fight, a long activity such as sleep that offers or runs a Timelapse, being moved somewhere you didn't go, or anything you'd stop a Timelapse for (an invitation, someone arriving), as long as you could see it. Then the reply offers the rest as a chip whose **Send** sends it as your next message, so it plays as its own turn. If it got it wrong, tap **Not an action?**: that undoes it, and a Re-run treats the text as plain roleplay. Reading a message costs one call on every preset, whether or not it holds an action; an action you pick from the Actions sheet or a chip needs none.

**Things you say you have.** When an action uses a thing the world doesn't have, as far as your character knows ("I pour Danny a coffee" with no coffee anywhere), the [**Player claims**](#player-claims) setting decides. **Make it real** (the default) adds it, in your hands, plays the action and shows **Added: a coffee** with **Undo**; food and drink are used up once given or used. **Ask first** asks before the reply: **Coffee isn't in this world yet. Add it and give it?**, with **Add** and **Not an action**. **Hold me to the world** leaves the action out with **Give: couldn't**, as above. Whatever the setting, money is never made from what you say you have, keys, weapons and documents always ask first, and with a cap anything costing more than the world's claim cap asks too. A thing you add never stands in for one the world has: an added key doesn't open a door that needs a particular key, and adding one never tells you whether such a thing exists.

**Routines you state.** When your message says that someone's week has changed for good, your character's ("I start nights at the marina Monday") or someone else's that they told you of ("Marcus says he's started swimming on Tuesdays"), the engine never changes it on its own: your message gets **Would you like to add this to your routine?** (or **to Marcus's routine?**), naming who, what, when and where. **Yes** adds it to their routine in this chat from the next day, and **Undo** takes it back; **No** leaves their routine as it was. When the place you name fits several places you know ("the lake"), the card offers each (**At Lakefront Park**, …) instead of **Yes**, and the one you pick is where it happens; when it fits none you know of ("the community pool"), the card says so, and **Yes** adds it with no set place. GM → Routines shows it at once among the changes the story made, by its own name, marked **From your message** with your message as its turn (a change the reply made reads **The story**). A question ("do you still swim on Tuesdays?"), a passing mention, a one-off or a plan asks nothing, and neither does a change already in their routine or someone your message doesn't name.

**Attempts.** When your message tries something whose outcome is uncertain and no action covers it ("I try to talk the bouncer into letting me in", "we sneak past the guard"), the reading can treat it as a check of your character's own sheet (**Try something**): a skill your sheet has, how hard it looks, the companions who join in ("we") and the people it goes up against. A question about someone counts too: "I watch Marcus as he answers: is he lying?" rolls your read of people against his deceit. The people it goes up against set how hard it is, rolling against you when everyone involved rolls, and each companion rolls their own; their numbers stay hidden, so the receipt shows only your own roll and how each companion did, and the narrator is told how it went and to narrate it that way. Whether and when this happens is the chat's rules for inferred checks (see [Inferred checks](#inferred-checks)): **Suggest** asks first with a **Roll Persuasion?** card, **Roll automatically** just rolls, and **Off** reads your message as it was read before inferred checks: a message that says it uses a skill ("I use my Mechanics skill on the engine") is read as the world's own **Use a skill**, **Attempt** or the like and rolls that action's check, and an attempt no action covers plays as roleplay. An action of the world's own that rolls its own check keeps it: the same attempt is never rolled twice.

A message can also take a move for granted. "I sit down next to Marcus" while your character is at the market has you somewhere you aren't, so it is read as going first to where you last saw Marcus, as if you had said so; the same goes for something that belongs to another place ("at the shop's counter"). And when the last reply's chip held the rest of your message, a message that takes those actions as done ("I set my chips and soda on the counter") plays them first, in order. If the reading misses a move anyway and the reply has you somewhere the engine doesn't, the reply gets an **Apply** chip for that move: see **Moving somewhere** in [Extraction modes](#extraction-modes).

What the helper reads besides your message: what your character can do now (the world's own actions and the built-in ones, each with a line on what it does, what it takes, such as the people and things it can name, and what it needs, in words), where and when you are, who is there, the people your message names who aren't with you (with where and how long ago you last saw them), what you carry, your vehicles, the invitations waiting on your answer, what your last message left unplayed and the places you know, all as your character knows them, and the messages before yours, so a line that hangs on the last reply ("I take what she offered", "I follow him out") is read from it. A stage direction ("[Tommy heads to the roof]") or the story told about your character counts as what you do; a line that is only speech, or out of character ("((OOC: …))"), plays as roleplay, unless it tells time to pass (below). When it can't tell what you mean, it reads no action rather than guess. How far back it reads is the **Recent chat** setting on the *Interpret typed input* card in **Settings → Steps and connections**: the last 8 messages and about 3,000 tokens unless you change it (either can be **no limit**, and switching it off reads none); a long reply that doesn't fit whole is read from its end.

Typed text can also let time pass ("wait until noon", "sleep until the next morning", "skip ahead to Friday at 6 PM"). An out-of-character time skip is always read as one, whatever else your message says: "OOC: over the next hour, I sort the stock", "(OOC: skip ahead two hours)", "[OOC] later that evening". So is time your character spends on something no action covers ("I spend the next hour sorting the stock"). What you say happens in that time is what fills it, and the reply tells it: the turn is a Timelapse of that span, run just as one from the **Timelapse** tab is (with character minds on, a [time skip](#character-minds)), never the activity you describe at its usual length. The span is read from your own words, the same way every time: a length ("over the next hour", "for the next two hours", "half an hour", "an hour and a half"), a vague one ("a moment", "a few moments" and "a few minutes" are 5 minutes, "a little while" 15, "a while" 30, "a few hours" 3 hours, so "a few minutes later" skips 5), a part of the day ("skip ahead to evening", "later that night", "tomorrow morning"), a moment of your own day ("until dinner", "until lunch", "until breakfast", "until bedtime", "until morning": to when your character's day has that meal, goes to bed or wakes up, or, when it doesn't say, to the evening, midday, morning or night), your next shift or commitment ("until my next shift"), or a day and time you name. A message that doesn't ask for time to pass is never made a Timelapse by the time words in it: "“I'll be back in an hour,” I tell Danny" is only speech. Time you let pass in character after your actions ("…then wait two hours") is offered as a chip once they have all played, as described above.

A length of time up to [Skips without asking](#skips-without-asking) (a day unless you change it) runs as the turn's own Timelapse, with your interrupt policy and a Montage; a longer one is never run unasked: a **Timelapse 3 d?** chip offers it, and **Timelapse** runs it. An *until* runs as the turn's own Timelapse however far off it is, so "sleep until my next shift" runs to the shift even when it is days away (a long run still stops at the safety cap, with a **Continue timelapse** chip). A day and time you name runs to exactly then, in the time where your character is (the time the status line shows), however far off it is; a time that has already passed, or one more than 366 days away, doesn't run, and the Journal says why ("Timelapse not run: that time has passed"). The Actions sheet's **Timelapse** tab has no day-and-time entry; type it instead. The narration can still skip ahead on its own, as described in [When the story jumps ahead](#when-the-story-jumps-ahead).

Some details can be said in your own words. A loan ends when you say it does: "I lend Danny my truck until 7 tonight", "for 3 hours", "until Friday", "tomorrow morning". A said end wins over the days. Words that name no time fall back to the days you gave, and a lend with neither is declined ("… names no time; say how many days or until when"). A piece of a kit can be named by its words in any order ("the metric combination wrench"): filler words and plurals don't count, at least half the words must match, and a lone number doesn't.

### Prices and purchases

When the story has you buy something, the engine prices it from the world's catalog, or from the item's category band with a little seeded variance. A purchase described in your own words ("a round of beers", "a large pepperoni pizza for delivery") is priced from a catalog row only when the row names it: whole words (plurals count), the item's main noun among them, preferring the row that holds most of its words and then what the place sells. A "desk lamp" is not a desk, a "coffee machine" is not a coffee, and "lantern oil" is not vegetable oil (a row that is another kind of the thing doesn't name it); with no row that fits, the stated price is used, then the category. A word that tells none of the catalog's kinds apart leaves the place to choose: "two large coffees" are two cafe drip coffees at a coffee shop, taxed as prepared food, and a bag of ground coffee at the grocery store. Brindlemere and Harrowmere price their categories in their own money (bits and pence). For something the catalog doesn't list you can state the price yourself, and the engine uses it. In worlds set to *lenient* pricing, a price the narrator states is accepted if it falls inside the band, and clamped (with a chip) if it falls outside. A price the narration gives for something the catalog doesn't list is capped at the world's **narrated-price cap** (by default the top price of its dearest catalog item or category, or 500 of its currency without either), and the chip says so ("narrated $12,000.00, capped at $6,000.00"). To set it, open **Build → Packs → economy**: the **Narrated-price cap** field takes it as the world's currency writes it ("$6,000.00"), shows the default it uses when left empty, and Problems warns about a cap that isn't a whole number above zero. Two things bought are two purchases: when the reply bundles your own purchase with something else ("a coffee and a bran muffin"), your part is dropped and the rest is priced on its own.

**How you pay.** A purchase can say how it is paid, whether you pick Buy or write it: "with my credit card" pays on a card; "my debit card", "checking" or "from the bank" pays from checking; "from savings" pays from savings; "in cash" or "from my wallet" pays from your wallet. A card you don't have falls back to checking, and any other missing account falls back to the way the engine would pay anyway. The same holds for a purchase the narration describes: "paid with my debit card" charges checking, and if the reply names something someone owns as the payer (a debit card in the wallet), its owner pays the way its name says. A named source pays only when it is the payer's own cash, checking, savings or card account; a loan or brokerage account named as the source is ignored and the usual way is used. When you type a Buy and the reading of your line leaves the way of paying out, your own words fill it, but only when you say it as a payment: after "with", "on", "using", "by", "from" or "in" ("pay with my credit card", "in cash", "from savings"). A mention you rule out ("not with cash") is skipped and the last mention counts, so "not with cash, with my debit card" pays by debit card, and "I buy a credit card holder for $12" is something you buy, not how you pay.

**Money handed over.** When the reply has someone hand over money ("Danny hands me $20 for gas", "a twenty", "a $20 bill"), the engine moves that amount from their money to yours and never turns cash into an item. Cash handed over goes into the wallet of the person who gets it (or their checking, if they carry no cash), and comes out of the giver's wallet when it holds enough, otherwise the way they'd usually pay. Only an item that is money as a whole counts: "my $300 jacket" or "a $20 gift card" is a thing with a price, handed over as a thing. A payment in the story is between people, or between someone's own cash, checking, savings or card accounts ("I move $100 from checking to savings" moves it; nothing is lost or made up). A payment to or from a thing, a loan or an investment account doesn't happen. If a change like that, or any other the engine would have applied by itself, can't apply (for example, there is no amount), a **Didn't apply** chip says so instead of letting it vanish, and the next reply is told, in the chip's own words with names as written, that it didn't happen.

**When you can't pay.** From Money level 2 up, a payment the account can't cover is declined: nothing changes hands and no item is created. If you chose **Buy** or **Pay** in the Actions sheet, the action still runs, the narrator is told "it didn't go through (… doesn't have $X available)" and to narrate it failing, and the reply shows a **Buy: declined** chip with the reason (no buttons; it lapses at your next message). If an action's own requirement fails (**Pay** requires that you can afford it), you get **Pay: couldn't** instead. If the narration claims a purchase your money can't cover, the engine checks it after the reply, declines it and shows **Couldn't pay for** the item, with three choices:

- **Pay another way** retries from savings, then card, checking and cash, and applies the first that covers it. If none does, a notice says "No other way to pay covers it." and the chip stays.
- **Remove it** tells the next reply that the item was narrated but never happened, and to leave it out.
- **Accept**, which is also what ignoring the chip does, tells the next reply that the payment was declined and that you don't have the item.

That note goes only to the next turn and its swipes. It is saved with the chat, so it still reaches that turn if the server or the extension restarts in between. At Money level 1 nothing is declined: payments overdraw, and the books track it in bands.

### The map

The map lives in **Play → Places**. A bar along its top holds the **breadcrumbs**, the **List of places** button and the **Full screen** button. Drag to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom, and use the **Zoom in**, **Zoom out** and **Fit map** buttons. **List of places** lists the places you know at the level on screen, with where you stand with each and the quickest way there.

Tap a place, on the map or in the list, for its place sheet: whether you're here, inside it, have been there or have only heard of it, and **Getting there**, where each travel option shows its mode (walk, road, off-road or water), time, vehicle and cost, or why it isn't available ("No route from here."). Choose one and the move or drive is sent. A place with places inside it offers **Open** *place*, and **Show on the map** takes you back to it on the map. A place you can't make out reads **Somewhere** and offers nothing.

**Full screen** opens the map over the chat, as do the **World map** button in the input bar and **Map** in the World sheet and the pill's details. On a desktop it is a floating widget (480 px wide, resizable, with **Fullscreen** and **Close map** buttons); the phone and the desktop remember its size and position separately. On a phone (a screen up to 600 px wide, or any touch screen), opening it closes the drawer first and goes fullscreen; if the drawer can't close, Play opens on **Places** instead. Over the chat, a place's details dock over the map rather than opening a sheet; Escape closes the dock first, then the map. Choosing a way to travel closes the map. Place names never overlap: a name sits beside its dot, or further out on a short line, on two lines, or shortened (the full name shows in the place's sheet or dock when you tap it); on a crowded map a name may be left off, and its dot stays tappable.

The map shows one place at a time, with the places directly inside it (a town's streets and shops, a manor's rooms) and the routes between them. It opens where you are: on your own place when there are places inside it, otherwise on the nearest place around you that has a map or a grid, or else on the place just around yours. The **breadcrumbs** lead back out, a level at a time, up to the **World** (the places at the top of the world). When the place on screen is the one you stand in, its breadcrumb says **you are here**. The map draws the places you know and the places you're inside. A stranger's home you were let into keeps its name while you're anywhere in it, its kitchen as much as its hall; a secret place you're inside, or a room of one, shows only as **Somewhere**, never by its name, and so it does on the status line and the pill, on the scene card, on your sheet and in People. A room inside a place you don't know about is as unknown as that place, and nothing offers a way there. Travel isn't limited to the place on screen: the Actions sheet and a typed move reach any place you know, and so does the map from any of its levels. From Space and travel level 2, a place with a grid shows its walls, doors and terrain behind its rooms, and from level 3 the square you're standing on.

**Known** and **Fog**, on the map, choose how it shows what you know, for this chat only (until you pick, the world's choice). **Known** draws a place you've heard of but never been to as a dashed ring, and its sheet says so. **Fog** dims the places you haven't been to and lays fog over what you haven't seen: the squares you haven't had in sight when the world uses the grid system, or else the rooms you haven't been in. Under fog there is nothing to find: the map is sent without what lies there, and a room you don't know about is just more fog. With **Known**, a room you don't know about is solid nothing on the grid. Things lying on a grid's squares show as small amber diamonds only where you see them now: within your sight when the world uses the grid system, or else in the place you're in. Something you saw earlier isn't shown, so the map never tells you where it went. Tap the square to see what lies there.

When the world uses the **grid system**, you stand on a square of the grid you're on, and walls and doors are walked: tap a square of that grid and its card offers **Walk here** with the time it takes, going around the walls and through the doors, those under fog included, or says "No way there from here.". Walking into a room puts you in it, but never into a room you don't know about, nor through one. You see a few squares around you (the world sets how far), and walls block the view.

### Detail on demand

The world doesn't store every coin in every wallet. When you look at something that has no detail yet (open a trunk, check what a character is carrying, audit an account, open **Play → Me → Money**), the engine generates it then, consistent with what is already known: a character known to be "rich" gets a balance inside the rich band. Once shown, it is recorded and never changes. Previews in the inspector are generated from the same seed but are not recorded, and GM mode shows what isn't generated yet as it stands (an account not opened yet shows the balance it will open with). If a recorded detail is removed from the story, for example by a branch taken before it was shown, it is generated again from the same seed the next time you look, and it can come out differently if what's known about it has changed since.

## GM mode

**GM** mode shows everything the engine knows, including secrets, truths you haven't discovered and people who were removed, and lets you change any of it. It wears a gold frame, so you can tell at a glance that you're behind the curtain. Looking never changes your chat; only the edits you save do. The [GM guide](GM-GUIDE.md) covers running a chat from here.

### The gate

The truth sits behind a spoiler gate, **Spoilers ahead**: tap **Show the truth** to open it. While it is open, a bar at the top of every page behind it says **The truth is showing**, with **Hide** to put it away. The gate closes again when you tap **Hide**, when you leave GM mode, and when you open another chat. What it guards is fetched only while it is open, so none of it travels with what Play shows you. This chat's rules and the Inspector don't need the gate.

### The GM bar

A gold bar along the bottom of the drawer holds the GM's places: **World**, **Places** and **Story**, then a place for each pack that adds one (**Minds**, **Routines** and **Money**), then **This chat**. All but **This chat** are behind the gate.

- **World**: every person, item, vehicle, group and account in the world, including the ones spawned during this chat and the removed ones, with **Search by name** and a kind filter. Badges mark **You**, **Removed**, a life state other than alive, anything that isn't public (**Known by**, **Secret**) and **Claimed**, a thing you said you had that the engine made real (see [Player claims](#player-claims)), and a row says when someone lives their own day or was spawned here. Tap one to open its page (see below). **Spawn** opens a page that adds someone or something new to this chat: a name, a kind, a place and a tier. While a fight is under way, a **Fight under way** card leads the list; tap it for the **Fight** page, with everyone in the fight, seen or not, in initiative order, with HP exact and the round's acts. While there are claimed things, **Claimed by the player** comes next: each one still in the world, by name, with who holds it or where it lies ("held by Sam Rivera", "at Rosie's Diner"). Tap one to open its page, where **Remove from the world** takes it out of the story; one removed, or a drink or a meal used up, leaves that list. Kinds and the world's places are the world's own definitions: the button at the bottom of the list, **Kinds and places are the world's: edit them in Build**, takes you to Build.
- **Places**: every place, under the place it's in, with its visibility (public, discoverable, secret and so on).
- **Story**: every quest, secret and agenda, with its stage and the stages that carry a truth (the one it's at is marked **Now**). Pick a stage under **Stage** to move the track there at once.
- **Minds**: every character's place on the fidelity ladder and why, their latest mind, plans, commitments, notes and summary, with pins and edits (see [Minds in GM mode](#minds-in-gm-mode)).
- **Routines**: every character's routine and schedules as this chat has them, how much their routine binds them now, today as laid, their invitations and what the story changed, with per-chat changes (see [Routines in GM mode](#routines-in-gm-mode)).
- **Money**: anyone's money, as their own money hub with the GM's controls (see [Everyone else's money](#everyone-elses-money)).
- **This chat**: this chat's rules, its edits and the inspector (see [This chat](#this-chat)).

**Changing many at once.** **Select** (beside **Spawn**) turns the **World** list into a list you pick from: tap rows to pick them, and the bar under the list shows how many you've picked, with **Select all** (everyone the list shows, its search and kind filter as they stand), **Select a level** (exactly the characters at one fidelity level) and **Clear**. **Change…** opens a page with one change for everyone picked: **Tier**, **Detail in one domain** (a level, or **From the tier**), **Their routine** (its role in this chat, or **As the world has it**), **Pin their fidelity** (or **By score**), **Your intent for them** and **Move to a place**. A routine, pin or intent change goes to the characters among them only, and a move to anyone not removed; each **Apply** says how many it reaches. A change is sent as edits of at most 64 each, each edit with its own **Undo**; if one is refused, the ones before it stay and the rest aren't sent. **Done** stops picking. What you pick stays in GM mode: nothing in Play shows it.

### An entity's page

An entity's page has a back button to **World**, and Escape goes back too. Its **Fields** show what its kind defines, with each number's range: a lock marks a field never offered to a model, and a value worked out from others ("Worked out from other values") is shown but not edited. Tap a field's edit button to change it, with **Save** or **Cancel**: a choice or a reference to someone or somewhere gives a picker, and money is typed in the world's currency. **Add to a value** adds to a number, an amount or a duration (a minus takes away, and the result stays inside the field's range: adding 50 to an energy of 80 gives 100). Below come:

- its designed sheet (as **Play → Me → You** shows yours);
- **Where it is**: where it is now, with **Bring to** *your character*'s **place** in one tap; **Move to a place**, whose search box narrows the places as you type; put it inside something or take it out; for a person, **Travels with** *your character*, with **Join the party** and **Leave the party** (a party member comes along on every move your character makes: Go to, driving, a move the reply tells, unless the same reply sends them elsewhere); an account can also **Correct the balance**, with **Add** and **Take**. Each of these is an edit with its own **Undo**;
- **Relations**: each relation's edit button loads it into **Add or change a relation**, where **Add or update** saves it with its fields, each checked against its range, and its remove button removes it;
- **Simulation**: its tier, whether it lives its own day, and each domain's level, or *From the tier*;
- **Life**: its life state and who learns of a change, where **Also tell** picks people and each **Don't tell** *name* button takes one off again;
- **Classes and tags**: its value of each of the world's classifications, the ones players can't see included, and its tags; **Save** sets them for this chat.

**Remove from the world**, at the bottom, takes an entity out of the story without deleting it: it disappears from Now, People, the Actions sheet, what the narrator is told, schedules and the money processes, and its relations, and everyone's relations to it, go. Whatever it holds goes with it. No rule, schedule or zone picks it out any more, it no longer counts in its group's regard, and a removed group's schedules stop moving its members. **Restore** brings it back where it was, without the relations it lost (undo the removal instead to get those back too); someone who lives off-screen picks up from then, without catching up on the time they were away. Your own character can't be removed.

Every change you save is checked first: a value outside its range, a choice that isn't one of its values or a relation that doesn't fit is refused with the reason, and nothing changes (only **Add** keeps its result inside the range instead). A save is one correction, however many things it changes: a **Corrected** chip appears on the latest reply with **Undo**, and swipes and Re-runs treat it like a ✎ fix (see [Re-run and Re-narrate](#re-run-and-re-narrate)). Saved while a reply is streaming, it lands when the reply finishes.

A classification or tag you set here holds in this chat only, over what the world says: rules, schedules and **Known by** selectors match it from then on, and an Upgrade keeps it unless you pick the revision's. A classification players can't see stays out of Play, and tags never show there.

### Everyone else's money

**Money** shows anyone's money: pick a person, a business, a trust or a household from **Whose money**, a list that gives each one's kind and Money level under their name (with a search box when there are more than 8), and their money hub opens under it at their own Money level, as yours does in Play, with the GM's controls in place of the player's actions. Its pages open over the hub, each with a back button, and Escape goes back too. On an account's page, **Add money** and **Take money** make a correction against an outside account, so the books still balance; **Status** sets it to open, frozen, collections, charged off, closed, paid off or matured; the **Autopay** row sets how it pays itself, from its debtor's own accounts whichever party's page it was opened from; **Account fields** holds every field the account declares, each with its edit button; and **Close** closes an account that holds and owes nothing (its cards are cancelled), and otherwise says why it can't. A hold's row opens its **Release** page, to release it early, and a card's page has its **Card state** (active, frozen, lost or cancelled) and **Card fields**. A standing payment's page can edit it (its name too), set its status (paused, ended and so on) or delete it, and **New standing payment or one-off** adds one. A job can be ended (**End the job**) or edited, and **Add a job** gives the person a new one, from one of the world's businesses picked by name or **An outside employer (type the name)**. An invoice's state can be set, and a holding's price too (**Set its price**, for securities, funds, crypto and other assets: an option follows its underlying). Where a holding's payouts, a property's rent or an owner's share of a business's profit go can be set for the person at any level, **Add a property** gives them one of their own at a value, and **Add a member** and **Remove a member** change a household (where households run for them, from Money 3 unless the world switches them, **Add to a household** puts a person who has none in one). Jobs, Properties and Household have their rows for this even while they're empty, once the person has money to show (Household only where households run: switched off, no household shows, theirs included). A person whose accounts haven't been opened yet shows the balances the world gives them to start with. None of this reaches your chat: other people's paydays and bills never appear in your chips, Journal or Montage, and their declines never stop your timelapse.

### This chat

**This chat** is a list of pages, each with a back button to the list:

- **Rules**: **Realism** (see [Promote and demote](#promote-and-demote)), **Checks** (see [New chats and this chat](#new-chats-and-this-chat) and [Inferred checks](#inferred-checks)), **Time** (see [Time controls](#time-controls)), **Sections** (see [What the narrator is fed](#what-the-narrator-is-fed)) and **Pins and upgrade** (see [Revisions and Upgrade](#revisions-and-upgrade)). These change the chat you have open; Settings holds the defaults for new chats.
- **Behind the curtain**: **Edits**, the corrections in force on this version of the story, worded in full, newest first, and any change that didn't apply after a reply, with the exact reason. It is behind the gate.
- **Inspector**: **Context**, what each reply was given, and the call meter. See [The Context inspector](#the-context-inspector).

### What the narrator is fed

Each section the narrator reads (Outcome, Scene, People and the rest) has settings. The world sets them in **Build → Sections**, and **GM → This chat → Sections** sets them for the chat you have open: tap a section, change what you like and tap **Save for this chat**, or **Use the world's** to go back to the world's. Each save is a GM edit with its own **Undo**.

- **In the prompt**: off leaves the section out.
- **At most lines**: only its first lines go in.
- **At most tokens**: lines past it are cut from the end.
- **Sources**: the renderers that fill the section, by name; none means its own. `location`, `calendar` and `weather` fill Scene, `people`, `routines` and `present_gear` People, `stance` and `cues` Stance, `conditions` Outcome, and `money`, `gear`, `vehicles`, `quest`, `place`, `property` and `minds` their own sections. A custom section with sources, say a **Weather** section with `weather`, gets those lines too.
- **Whose knowledge**:
  - **What the player character knows** (the default): what every section has always told.
  - **Also what the people with him know**: facts he doesn't know but someone with him does, such as a person in the room only Maya knows is there.
  - **Everything**: every fact, secrets included.

  A fact your character doesn't know is marked for the narrator ("[unknown to you; known to Maya]", "[unknown to you; GM only]"), and the narrator is told that marked facts are only for steering the story: never to reveal, narrate as known or let your character act on. The engine's own screens never show them: Play, chips, the Journal and the Context panel's sizes show only what your character knows. But marked facts are in the prompt, so anything in Lumiverse that shows the prompt or the engine's book shows them too; choosing a wider scope is choosing to put them there.

  What each section adds: People, someone here your character can't see (and, from what people carry, a thing on them he can't see); Scene, the place around his and nearby exits he doesn't know; Stance, people's private agendas; Money, the money of the people with him; Gear, a thing on him he doesn't know of; Vehicles, cars at his site he can't see; Quest, quests and secrets he doesn't know; Place, hidden things here, hidden ways out and, under **Everything**, who is next door; Property, other people's property.

Two sections are new, both on unless the world or the GM switches them off, and ranked last, so a small budget leaves them out first. A world that lists its own sections gets them only when it adds them.

- **Place**: the place's description, the things lying there your character can see (at most 12), the rooms inside it and the ways out.
- **Property**: the property your character holds a part in (his own, or his family's), where it is, his role and share, and who else owns it.

People also tells what each person with your character visibly wears and carries (never what is in a bag or a pocket), at most 6 things each.

## Swipes, edits and branches

A world chat survives all the ways people mess with a chat. Here is exactly what each one does.

### Re-run and Re-narrate

There are two ways to retry a reply:

- **Re-run** plays the turn again with a **fresh roll**. Your typed text keeps the reading it was given, but the engine draws new dice, so a failed check can succeed. This is the default.
- **Re-narrate** keeps everything the engine decided (the same dice, the same outcome, the same events) and only asks for **new prose**. Use it when you like what happened but not how it was told.

Which one a plain swipe uses is a setting (**Settings → Swipes**, under *Replies*: *Re-run (new roll)* or *Re-narrate (same outcome)*). Either way, the **Swipe as…** chip on a reply has **Re-run** and **Re-narrate** buttons for a single swipe. Anything you fixed by hand (✎) or changed in GM mode carries over to the new swipe either way, where it still makes sense: Re-narrate checks it against the same outcome, and Re-run against the new turn, after everything the new roll did. One that no longer fits (the new roll spent the money it corrected, or the thing it changed is gone) is dropped with a **Not carried** note in the Journal, and a correction you undid stays undone. With Re-narrate, any detail you opened on the original reply carries over too, and anything that depended on results of the original narration is dropped with a note in the Journal.

### Switching to an earlier swipe

Swipe back to an earlier version of a reply, or delete or add a swipe on an earlier message, and the turns after it are **automatically rebased**: the engine re-checks each of their results against the new situation. Something that no longer makes sense (an item you never bought, a payment under a different balance) is dropped, with a note. A purchase or payment is one unit, so the money and the item are kept or dropped together. Nothing pauses and nothing is erased.

A **Keep report (n)** chip lists what was dropped. For each entry you can **Convert** it (keep the effect as a fixed correction; it is your own adjustment, so it is kept even if it leaves an account short). It also lists anything that was done on a different branch of the story as "made on another branch", and shows converted entries as "kept".

Everything is reversible: switch back to the original swipe and the world is exactly as it was. Anything you do while you are on a different swipe, such as a fix, a chip confirmation or a new turn, counts only while you stay on that version of the story. A new turn on a rebased path builds on the rebased version.

### Editing the AI's reply

Fixing a typo, a name or a trimmed line is routine, and the world doesn't care. **When you edit an AI reply, the world stays as it was**: nothing pauses and nothing is re-read. If the edit changes what *happened*, tap **Re-read edit** on that reply and the engine re-reads the new text, replacing what it had read from the old one. A change you had already applied from the old read's chips is undone with it and offered again from the new text, if the new text still tells it, so nothing is charged twice. While the chat is paused, or when the message is a switched-away view, the read is refused without calling a model and the chips stay as they were; Keep or Discard, or switch back, first. Keeping is the default.

Re-read edit, Retry read and **Undo** run only on a reply that is on its own timeline. If you have swiped away from the timeline the reply was made on, they are refused and ask you to switch back first. One case can't be fixed by switching back: if an earlier message or swipe the reply depended on changed while the reply was written (deleted, or its text replaced), the reply was read without it, so Re-read edit, Retry read and Undo are always refused on it. If it is the latest reply, **Re-run** it to play the turn again; an older reply can't be changed this way.

### Editing an earlier message of yours

If you change what *you* wrote earlier in the chat, every turn after it was built on something that no longer exists, so the chat **pauses**: a **Paused** banner appears at the top of **Play → Now**, and a card under your edited message asks "Edited message: keep or discard later turns?" (the same card sits inside the banner):

- **Keep**: the later turns are re-checked against the edited message, as in the swipe case above, with a Keep report.
- **Discard**: the later turns' records are removed for good. Their messages stay in the chat as plain text, and the world forgets what happened in them. This cannot be restored, not even after a restart.

While the chat is paused, a new turn is written but gets no simulation (it is marked *degraded*), and fixes and confirmations are refused until you choose. If the edit lands while a reply is streaming, that reply is left unread. A swipe that finishes while the chat is paused carries none of the fixes and GM edits of the swipe it replaced: each gets a **Not carried** note in the Journal.

**Edit and Send** on an earlier message works in a world chat as a branch: Lumiverse makes a new chat from the edited message, and its reply is that chat's latest. In place, the reply would be written onto the older message after yours, which the engine can't prepare, because Lumiverse can start only the last reply again. So an in-place Edit and Send on an earlier message stops with **The engine didn't finish, so nothing was generated**, and nothing is written: your edit stays and pauses the chat as above. Instead, use Edit and Send as a branch (Lumiverse's setting to branch on Edit and Send), or regenerate the last reply.

### Continue

When you use Lumiverse's Continue on the latest reply, the engine reads only the new text and applies what it says, as one more step of that turn. Continue always continues the latest reply: when the engine has run first, it starts the Continue again with Lumiverse's own, which knows only the latest. Some Continues can't be read that way: a Continue on an older message (only possible through Lumiverse's API, and only where Lumiverse can't stop a reply), on a message the engine never simulated (a greeting), one you stopped half-way, or one where Lumiverse re-rendered the earlier text. These are accepted as they are, like an edit. The world stays as it was until you tap **Re-read edit** (or **Retry read** for a stopped Continue). Before the reply starts, a Continue shows the world as it was, because the engine learns which message you're continuing only when the reply ends.

### Undo, retry and what lapses

- **Undo** covers the latest reply. Earlier changes go through Keep or Discard. Each ✎ fix and GM edit on the latest reply has its own **Undo**, on its **Corrected** chip.
- **Apply / No chips** from a reply expire when you send your next message, and nothing is applied.
- **Timelapse chips** (offers and Continue), **suggested choices**, **your other action** and the **contradiction** chip lapse at the next send as well.
- An empty reply is *degraded* and shows **Retry**. The engine never regenerates on its own.

### Branches

A Lumiverse chat branch carries the world with it. Records that belong to the branch you're on apply; changes made on another branch stay stored but do nothing until you go back. When you delete a reply, the settings changes recorded on it (realism, time controls, Upgrade) move to the nearest earlier reply, and its other-branch changes are dropped with it.

## Time controls

Time is deterministic: nothing here needs the AI.

Every turn has an **elapsed time**. It is one of: the length of a Timelapse, the length of the action you took (a drive, a rest), or the **default tick** of the current scene type multiplied by the **time scale**. The **+n min** chip in the reply's receipt shows it, the scene card in **Play → Now** says how long the last turn took, and the narrator is told it in the Scene section ("This turn took 20 min.", or "No time passes this turn." while the clock is paused).

### Ticks, scale and pause

- **Tick per scene type.** The time a turn takes when nothing else sets it, before the time scale. The defaults are **conversation 2 minutes**, **action 1 minute**, **exploration 10 minutes** and **downtime 30 minutes**. Worlds can add scene types. A tick is a whole number of minutes: a fractional one, such as a world's half minute, plays as the nearest whole minute, and one under half a minute as no time at all (with **Use my settings** on, a world's own scene type that your settings don't list keeps the world's tick as written). The time scale can still make a turn's time a fraction of a minute ("This turn took 0.5 min."). The scene type is set by actions and by places, or by a ✎ fix.
- **Time scale.** A multiplier on default ticks only, from 0.25× to 10×, default 1×; a tick after scaling is never longer than a day. Action durations, travel times and Timelapses stay exact. When it isn't 1×, the status line, the pill and the scene card show it.
- **Clock pause.** While paused for the scene, no time elapses (neither ticks nor action durations) until you resume it or the scene changes. If a scene change started the clock again, **Pause clock** pauses it for the new scene. The status line and the pill show the pause icon, and the scene card says **Clock paused**. A Timelapse asks you to resume first. A time jump the narration describes still applies (see [When the story jumps ahead](#when-the-story-jumps-ahead)). Pause and resume with **Pause clock** / **Resume clock** in the World sheet or the pill's details, in the Actions sheet's header, on the input bar, or in **Play → Story → Calendar**.

Where to change them:

- **Settings → Time** fills in, for **new chats**, what a world's own time defaults leave out (always the time scale); with **Use my settings instead of a world's defaults for new chats** on, it replaces them.
- **GM → This chat → Time** changes **this chat**. **Play → Story → Calendar** has a button that takes you there.

Both have the same sections: **Default tick per scene type** (with **Time scale**), **Timelapse interrupts**, **Speed through** and **Montage and limits**; **Settings → Time** also has [Skips without asking](#skips-without-asking). A change saves as you make it; there is nothing to apply.

A change in a running chat applies from the next turn forward, is recorded like any other change, and respects swipes and branches. Earlier turns keep their recorded times. Changing Settings later leaves existing chats alone, except for **Skips without asking**.

### Skips without asking

**Settings → Time → Skips without asking** is how long a skip may run inside one turn before the engine asks you first. It covers a length of time you type ("for the next three hours", "OOC: skip ahead two hours") and a jump the reply tells ("three hours later"). It is a day by default; set it in minutes, from 0 (every such skip asks) up to 366 days. A longer skip doesn't run: it becomes a chip that runs it as a Timelapse when you tap it (**Timelapse** for a skip you typed, **Run timelapse** for one the reply tells). An *until* you type ("until dinner", "until my next shift"), a day and time you name, a Timelapse you start yourself and a ✎ fix of the clock never ask. It is a setting of yours, not a chat's: every chat follows it from its next turn, and turns already played keep the time they took.

### Timelapse

A Timelapse skips ahead while the world keeps living: bills fall due, you commute to work, NPCs go about their days. Open it from the **Timelapse…** button in the input bar (it opens the Actions sheet on its **Timelapse** tab), from **Timelapse** in **Play → Now** and **Timelapse…** in **Play → Story → Calendar**, or from a chip. The sheet has a **For** row of duration buttons (with a **Custom** length in minutes, hours, days or weeks), an **Until** list, and **Interrupts for this run**, folded away until you open it.

**Follow my routine**, below the targets, says what your own character does while the time passes: on, they go about their usual day (work, meals, sleep, as their routine and schedules have it); off, they stay where the story has them while the world goes on. It starts at the world's default, which is off unless the world's **Routines and the story** says otherwise, and holds for that Timelapse only.

**Targets** are either a duration (minutes, hours, days, weeks) or *until* something: next morning or another part of the day, your next shift or commitment, arrival (when the current trip ends), a date and time, a quest reaching a stage, or "until something happens".

**Interrupts.** While the time passes, some things should stop it. Each kind has a policy: **stop**, **note** or **ignore**. The kinds are invitations, bills and declines, incidents, quest and rule triggers, NPC arrivals at your place, overdue loans, needs thresholds and weather alerts. The defaults:

| Policy | Kinds |
|---|---|
| **stop** | invitations, declines, incidents, quest triggers, arrivals |
| **note** | bills and paydays, rule triggers, overdue loans |
| **ignore** | needs thresholds and weather alerts |

The first *stop* you could see ends the Timelapse at the end of that time slot; something hidden from you never stops it, so a stop never gives a secret away. *Notes* go into the Montage. The same world and the same policy always stop in the same place, and you can override the policy for a single Timelapse in the sheet.

**The safety cap.** One Timelapse segment is limited, by default, to **600 agent-days** (the people who are simulated in detail, plus you, times the days) and **5,000 events**; you can lower either but not raise it. A longer request ends at the last whole time slot inside the cap, and a **Continue timelapse** chip carries on from there. Segments add up exactly as if you had done it in one go, as long as nothing else changes the world between them. A Timelapse can reach at most 366 days ahead.

**NPC choices** during a Timelapse use the numbers only; the AI isn't called. A Timelapse that can't run at all (the clock is paused, its time has passed or is more than 366 days ahead, or there is no upcoming shift or commitment to run to) simulates nothing, and the turn's changes say why ("Timelapse not run: …").

### Montage

A Timelapse turn is narrated from a **Montage**: the key events the point-of-view character would know (about people and places you have met; the weather counts when it is your region's), plus totals (money in and out per account at your Money level, distance travelled, anything you can see that crossed a band, such as how someone feels or a need going from calm to frantic, and net changes to your own and your belongings' other numbers; a field shown in bands is told by its band, never its number). A field that changed several times in one batch, such as an hourly temperature, is listed once at its latest value. The narrator is told to narrate the interval from the Montage and not add events; when a short Timelapse has nothing for the Montage, the Montage is left out and the narrator is told only not to add events. The Outline never points the narrator at a section it doesn't have that turn. The Journal keeps the full list, with spans worded in days, hours or minutes. Under **Montage and limits** you can choose the **Montage granularity**: **Key events** (the default), **Per day** or **Per week**; it is shortened to fit the budget.

### Speed through and Timelapse offers

For long activities (sleep, a work shift, travel, waiting), the engine can skip the dull part. When an activity that has a policy has more time left than its threshold (**When longer than (min)**), one of two things happens, per activity type (**Speed through** in **Settings → Time**, or in **GM → This chat → Time** for this chat):

- **Offer a timelapse** (the default): the turn takes only the action's own time, and a chip offers a Timelapse to the end of the activity.
- **Run it**: the turn runs that Timelapse itself, with your interrupt policy and a Montage.

New settings give every listed activity a policy (offer, past 30 minutes), and a world can set its own for any activity; settings you saved with an earlier version keep the policies they had and aren't filled in. An activity without one (in a chat started before it had one, a world's own activity that the world leaves unset, or one your saved settings don't list) passes within the turn however long it takes: its row says *passes within the turn* and shows no mode or threshold, and picking either gives it a policy.

When the narration implies an activity the engine hadn't seen start, you always get the offer chip, because the engine never starts a generation by itself. Activities shorter than the threshold simply elapse. The offer reads "Timelapse to 07:00 (8 h 55 min)?" (with the weekday when it is another day); the **Timelapse** button on it runs the Timelapse. A threshold can be anything from a minute to a week (1 to 10,080 on the stepper); a threshold outside that, such as a 0 an earlier version let you save, reads as the nearest end.

### When the story jumps ahead

If the reply says "three days later", the engine can read that as a time jump. The reading counts all the time the reply says passed in the turn, and only the part *beyond* what the turn already used is added: after a 15-minute action, "an hour later" adds 45 minutes, and a reply that tells less time than the action took adds none. It adds up to [Skips without asking](#skips-without-asking) at once (a day unless you change it), and runs the whole interval so the world catches up. A longer jump, or one that can't finish, becomes a chip offering to **Run timelapse** instead, unless it is more than 366 days, which is dropped. The time advance has its own mode (see [Extraction modes](#extraction-modes)): on **auto**, its starting mode, it applies by itself; on **confirm** a chip asks first, and **Apply** runs it. During a fight a time jump never applies, in either mode: a **Didn't apply: +N min** chip says so ("a fight is under way…", with **Remove it** and **Accept**), the next reply is told it didn't happen, nothing is added to the changes chip, and **Apply** on an earlier confirm chip is refused. A paused clock doesn't stop one: the pause holds the turn's own time, not time the reply says passed.

## Realism

Realism is how much of life the engine keeps track of. It is set **per domain**, from level 0 (off) to level 4 (simulation), and the story never needs it: quests and secrets work at any setting. Use as much or as little as your story enjoys.

| Domain | 1 Narrative | 2 Simple | 3 Detailed | 4 Simulation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Money** | A wealth band | Cash plus one account | Accounts, cards (with autopay), recurring income and bills | Interest and APR, statements, minimum payments, loans, investments, withholding |
| **Gear** | Notable items: those the world marks notable, and anything found, bought, handed over or claimed | Items, containers, cash in a wallet | Worn and equipped slots, kits, condition | Wear per use, consumables |
| **Vehicles** | "Runs fine" | Fuel %, location, owner, your family's vehicles | Specs, consumption, odometer, loans and vehicles assigned or lent to you, home base | Maintenance intervals, breakdowns, registration and insurance, marine and off-road specifics |
| **Cast life** | Day-part routines, agendas | Commitments set the day | A day planner | Needs and drives, invitations, NPC-to-NPC encounters, knowledge spreading |
| **Relationships** | One band per pair | One number per pair | Several axes plus appraisal rules | Decay, history, group standing |
| **Space and travel** | Named places | Places, routes and travel times | Grids and positions | Terrain multipliers, mode-specific routes |
| **Calendar and time** | Day parts | Clock and dates | Recurrence and time zones | Holidays, school terms, exceptions |
| **Weather** | Season mood | Climate states | Regional climates | Microclimate effects on travel and rules |
| **Sheets and checks** | Bands as words | Stats plus one check | Skills and derived fields | The full sheet |

The time controls and Timelapse need Calendar at level 1 or higher. At level 1, Timelapse targets round to parts of the day.

**What the narrator is told of vehicles.** The Vehicles section lists what your character may use, each saying whose it is: his own at Vehicles 1, his family's from 2 (with their fuel and range), and those assigned or lent to him from 3 (a loan with when it's due). An owner he doesn't know of reads "someone". A trailer has no tank, so it shows no fuel. Vehicles away from where he is fold into one line, such as "Elsewhere: 12 (8 at Cedar Draw, 4 at the boathouse)". Then other people's vehicles at the same site (a house with its garage and driveway, a marina; not the whole town) that he can see, with whose they are, up to 8.

**Money detail.** Below the top Money level the money hub keeps the books simple, and at the top level it shows them all (see [Your money](#your-money)). Under any preset, a world can turn single money features on or off in **Build → Packs → economy** (**Money features** → **Feature switches**). Each runs from its usual level. Switched on, it runs from the lowest level it can (statements and interest from Money 3, cash machines and daily card limits from Money 2), and the hub shows that feature's own detail there and nothing more of the full books. Switched off, it doesn't run at any level, and its detail is gone from the hub at the top level too.

### Presets

| Preset | Money | Gear | Vehicles | Cast life | Relationships | Space | Calendar | Weather | Sheets | Band caps (Leads / Supporting / Background) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Story** (the default) | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 1 | none; every domain level applies to everyone |
| **Grounded** | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 2 | 2 | no cap / no cap / 1 |
| **Simulation** | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 4 / 3 / 2 |
| **Custom** | any | any | any | any | any | any | any | any | any | any |

Moving any dial yourself makes the preset *Custom*.

### Player claims

When you type that your character does something with a thing the world doesn't have, as far as your character knows ("I light a cigarette with my Zippo", "I hand Rosie a coffee"), the **Player claims** setting decides what happens:

- **Make it real**: the thing is added where your action needs it, in your hands or where you are, and the action plays. With **Ask first above the world's claim cap** on, something whose price is over the world's claim cap asks first instead.
- **Ask first**: the turn waits for your answer, **Add** or **Not an action**, before anything is generated.
- **Hold me to the world**: the action doesn't play, and a chip says why.

A claim never makes money: saying you have $500 adds nothing to your cash. Each preset carries its own setting: **Story** makes it real at any price, **Grounded** makes it real up to the claim cap, and **Simulation** asks first.

It is set in three places, each with the same control (the three modes, and the cap switch, which only Make it real uses):

- **Settings → Realism → Player claims**: yours, for chats you start from now on. Picking a preset there sets it, and changing it makes the preset *Custom*. Like the rest of Settings → Realism, it fills in for a world that sets none, or replaces the world's with **Use my settings instead of a world's defaults for new chats** on.
- **GM → This chat → Realism → Player claims**: the chat you have open, from its next turn; earlier turns keep what theirs did. A chat started before player claims existed plays by its world's, else by what its realism preset carries (a Simulation chat asks first, a Grounded one keeps the cap), and by Story's when its realism is *Custom*.
- **Build → Realism defaults → Player claims**: the world's own, for new chats. **This world sets what new chats make of them** switches it on, starting from what the world's realism preset plays; off, a new chat takes the player's own setting. Check flags one the engine can't read.

**The claim cap** is about the price of a meal: twice the typical price of a meal out in the world's categories table ($23 in Port Alder), or 25 in the world's currency when it has none. A world can set its own as **Claim cap** in **Build → Packs → economy**, typed in the world's currency; left empty, the field shows the default it uses. A cap that isn't a whole amount above zero gets a warning, and the engine then rounds a fraction or uses the default.

In GM mode, **World** marks each claimed thing **Claimed** and lists them under **Claimed by the player** with who holds each, so you can find one and remove it (see [The GM bar](#the-gm-bar)).

### Tiers and bands

Not everyone needs to be simulated in depth. Every character has a **tier**, one of the world's own (a new world has **Leads**, **Supporting** and **Background**; an author can rename them, add more and reorder them in **Build → Tiers**). Each tier belongs to one of three **bands**, Leads, Supporting or Background, and each band has a cap per domain. A character's **effective level** is the lower of the domain level and its band's cap, unless you set a per-character override (**Level overrides** in the character's editor), which can never go above the domain level. Your own character is never capped. In Simulation, Leads run at level 4 while a Background shopkeeper runs at 2.

A tier can make its people **live their own day (agendas and plans) by default**, and a world names the tier **new people join** (by default its first Background tier): people the story introduces, and anything spawned without a tier. How many people the story may introduce in one chat is the world's **People replies may introduce in a chat**, in **Build → World**: 24 unless you set another, up to 500, and 0 for none.

People you mark as **Lives their own day (agendas and plans)** in their entity editor get goals and agendas that feed their choices, even on Story. They are consulted by the AI only on close calls or invitations. Everyone else follows schedules and seeded incidents. Only they live their plans (from Cast life 3): someone else with a mind still thinks, but their plans are never lived, never ask you along, and are dropped once their time has passed.

**Life and death.** Everyone is **Alive**, **Dying**, **Dead**, or in a state the world adds in **Build → World → Life states** (a label, whether someone in it still acts, and how the narrator reads it, such as "lies unconscious"); an entity's editor sets the state it starts in. Someone who doesn't act keeps no routine or day plan, makes no choices and takes no turn in a fight, and their body stays where it lies, still there to find. You learn of it by seeing it happen, by walking in on it, or by being told (the story, a rule or the GM); until then People, Now and the narrator show what you last knew. Rules change it with the `life` effect, and a world's combat can send the fallen into a state of its own (**down** → life state).
A death reaches the Journal on the turn it happened only if you saw it; otherwise it appears when you hear of it. Other people's comings and goings stay out of the Journal (Now and People show who is around), and a reply can't walk a body anywhere. An entity's editor can also say **Who knows how it starts**: everyone else takes it for alive until they find out.

### Promote and demote

A new chat starts from its world's own realism and time defaults, and **Settings → Realism** fills in only what a world leaves out; turn on **Use my settings instead of a world's defaults for new chats** there (it is also in **Settings → Time**) if you always want your own levels, say Story everywhere. A change in Settings saves at once and touches no running chat. To change the chat you have open, use **GM → This chat → Realism**: **Applies to** picks **Everyone** or just the **Leads**, **Supporting** or **Background** band (it names the world's tiers in that band), and each domain has a slider. Before the chat's first turn it shows the levels that turn will use. A single character's level is set on the character in the Build editors (**Level overrides**).

- **Promoting** switches processes on. Anything the character lacks is generated on demand, inside what is already known.
- **Demoting** switches processes off and trims what is shown. The data stays; nothing goes dormant, so promoting again never brings back stale detail. Money is conserved either way.

When you move a slider there, a **migration preview** opens and comes into view, naming the domain the way the rest of the drawer does and saying whose level it is: "*Cast life for supporting characters* drops from level 4 to 2: processes above level 2 stop and their inputs freeze; detail stays stored.", "*Money* rises from level 2 to 4: detail is filled in when first shown." (followed by "People without money detail get it from the pinned tables when you open them."), or "… stays at level 3." Each line is marked with what it does: freezes, rises, gains detail, or a note. The levels it names are the ones the chat plays at: where the world's rules set a minimum level for a domain, a setting below it reads as that minimum. It also lists conditions in your world's rules that depend on something that will stop updating. The buttons are **Apply to this chat**, which has the focus, and **Cancel**, and a toast confirms ("*Money* is now *Simulation*."). The change is recorded like any other and applies from the next turn forward.

### Mixed levels

A character at a low level can still deal with one at a high level: each side records at its own level, and the low-level side's money is represented by an *external economy* account, so the books always balance.

### Other people's money

From Money level 3, the people in the world live on their money as you do. They are paid, they pay their bills, and every Monday they pay a week of living costs that follows their wealth band (a world can set its own amounts; the Modern US data prices them from a weekly grocery basket), so their balances go down as well as up. A bill paid to someone in the world, like your rent to your landlord, lands in that person's own account. The first morning their bills run, the engine opens their accounts with the balances the world gives them. You see all of this only in [GM mode](#everyone-elses-money).

## Routines and schedules

People in a world have days of their own. A **routine** is what someone can be relied on to do on an ordinary day: opening the shop at seven, coffee somewhere three mornings a week, a walk by the lake on a fair weekend. Its blocks can be **fixed** (at a set time), **flexible** (somewhere in a window, or a few times a week) or **optional** (only likely, and the first thing a busy day drops), and someone can have no routine at all. A **schedule** is a commitment with set hours, such as a shift or school. A world's routines can rest on its lore or a character's sheet, and say so.

Routines and schedules are defaults the world relies on, never a script the story has to follow. Where someone's day comes from, most binding first:

1. **What the story established:** where the narration, your actions or the GM put them, a trip, a plan under way, and what the GM says they mean to do.
2. **Their commitments:** schedules, appointments and trips.
3. **Their plans** from the story.
4. **Their routine.**
5. **Their basic needs:** sleep and meals.
6. **Chores, their agenda's steps and things to get back** that the world grounds.

Nothing else fills a day: no outing, party or invitation that nothing in the world or the story grounds. The story always leads the people in it. What someone's routine and their own schedules are to the engine is their **role**, which the world sets for each level of the [fidelity ladder](#the-fidelity-ladder):

- **Informative**, the default for Live, Near and Far characters: their routine and schedules never move or occupy them. The story and context-aware planning (plans the story made, the GM's intent) decide what they do, and their routine is only context their mind and the narrator may weigh.
- **Enforced**, the default for Background characters: the planner lives their routine and their schedules run, the story still first. Off screen, this is where routines matter most.
- **Off**: their routine isn't used at all, not even as context.

**In a time skip** (a Timelapse, a typed span or a skip the reply narrates), someone whose routine is informative spends the time as the world picks for their level, or the GM for them:

- **Stay put**: they stay where the story has them.
- **Follow their routine**: they go about their usual routine for the skip, as an enforced one would be lived, except while a story plan, a trip, the GM's intent or an appointment covers them.
- **Planned by the model**, the default for Live, Near and Far: at the skip's start one helper call plans everyone it covers, from their routine, their plans and the story so far: a few blocks each, for what they do other than simply being at home asleep, and each lives what it planned for them, from the skip's first minute for whatever is under way as it starts. Whenever no block covers them, they keep to their basic needs as their day has them: home to sleep at night, and their meals (one their day has at work they have where they are; it never sends them to work). So does someone it planned nothing for, or everyone when the call fails, runs out of time or is cut off. A replay or a swipe reads the recorded plan rather than asking again, and a skip continued inside the planned time reuses it. **Follow their routine** is Background's default.

An enforced routine is always lived through a skip, and an off one never. Either way the story comes first: no one is moved into or out of a scene with you, and someone arriving where you are is only noted.

Whatever the role, a routine or a schedule never moves anyone out of a scene with you, and no routine interrupts play or stops a time skip. The world's own schedules (weather, prices, bills, paydays, opening hours) and money run whatever the roles say: a job still pays on its schedule when the worker's routine only informs. Your own character follows its routine through a Timelapse only when you turn on its **Follow my routine** ([Timelapse](#timelapse)); a span you spend doing something ("for the next two hours, I sort the stock"), a skip the reply narrates or a time you fix with ✎ leaves you where the story has you.

**Tuning.** A world sets how routines and the story share the day in **Build → Routines → Routines and the story**:

- **What a routine is, by closeness:** the role of each level, **Live**, **Near**, **Far** and **Background**: **Informative**, **Enforced** or **Off**.
- **In a time skip:** for each level, how those whose routine is informative spend a skip: **Stay put**, **Follow their routine** or **Planned by the model**.
- **The narrator:** whether the narrator is told the usual routines of the people in the scene that your character can see and whose routine isn't off (on by default): at the end of **People**, a line or two each ("Maya usually: weekdays, working the counter at Daybreak Café 07:00–15:00"), as background it may use or ignore, never a fact to state. They go in only while the section budget has room, never carry a private block or anything your character can't see, and no helper reads them as fact. For a character whose routine only informs, this and their own mind's **Their usual routine** are the only ways their routine reaches the story.
- **Timelapse from the Actions sheet:** where **Follow my routine** starts (off by default).
- **How strictly people keep to routine** against the story, for those whose routine is enforced: **Story first** (someone the story put somewhere stays there until the story or the GM releases them), **Balanced** (the default: until their night, with Background characters keeping their routine) or **Routine first** (their routine's next block takes them back).
- **Invitations** from routine blocks that ask company along: **Off**, **To the player only** (the default) or **Between everyone**. Plans the story makes, and the GM, invite whatever this says.
- **Sociability:** **Reserved**, **Usual** or **Outgoing**, how fast people want company and fun.
- **How plans bend routine:** which plans the story makes take time from work and school.

In a chat, the GM can change any of this for one character without touching the world: their routine's role, how they spend a time skip, whether and how strictly they keep their routine, one block, or one schedule, each with its Undo.

Where you meet them:

- **Play → People**: someone's usual day, as far as you may know it, and why each block is there ([People](#people)).
- **GM → Routines**: everyone's routine, schedules and day as this chat has them, with what the story changed and the GM's changes ([Routines in GM mode](#routines-in-gm-mode)).
- **Build → Routines**: the world's routines and their settings ([Routines in Build](#routines-in-build)), and routines proposed from the world's lore ([Proposing routines from lore](#proposing-routines-from-lore)). A routine Build can't read, or a setting it doesn't take, isn't saved: the message names the field to fix. Outside agents edit them the same way, with the same checks ([MCP.md](MCP.md)).

## How checks are decided

A check is the dice an action rolls (see [Checks, pools, progression and sheets](#checks-pools-progression-and-sheets)). How much those dice matter, against how you describe what your character does, is up to the world and to you. A world can set its own rules for checks in **Build → Ruleset**, under **How checks are decided**. A world that sets none rolls every check and lets the roll settle it, exactly as before.

### The presets

| Preset | When checks roll | Your described approach | Who settles it | Retrying a failed check |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Narrative-first** | Only risky ones (under 75% to succeed); likelier actions just succeed | The judge reads it as it picks the outcome | The story: a helper picks the outcome, with the exact odds as guidance; the dice when nothing judged it | Needs a change |
| **Balanced** | Every check, except that sure things (95% or more) pass and hopeless ones (5% or less) fail without a roll | Adds up to +2 | The roll; a roll within 2 of another result is a close call, and the narrator may add a cost or partial progress | Needs a change |
| **Crunchy** | Every check | Doesn't count | The roll | Any time |

Crunchy is how the engine has always played. **Custom** lets you set each value, and changing any value of a preset makes it Custom:

- **When checks roll**: every check; only risky ones, below a chance you set; only where marked (an action marked **Its check always rolls**, a place marked **Checks roll here** and the places inside it, or a quest marked **Checks roll while it's in progress** while it is under way and you can see it in your quests, so a secret quest never shows by making the dice roll); or never. A check that isn't asked for succeeds.
- **Sure things pass without a roll** and **Hopeless tries fail without a roll**, each from a chance you set.
- **How the player's approach counts**: not at all, as a bonus up to **The most it counts**, as advantage from a rating you set, or as a DC lower by up to the most it counts (a check without a DC takes it as a bonus). It shifts a check that rolls; the Actions sheet shows the odds before it.
- **Who settles a check**: the roll; the roll, with close calls within the points you set; or the story.
- **Retrying a failed check**: any time; after a change (the minutes you set passing, a new round of a fight, better or worse odds, another way of doing it such as another stat, tool or target, or going somewhere else; rewording the same thing isn't a change); or once per scene (until you leave and come back).

A chance is a check's exact chance of success, as the Actions sheet shows it. Success is the outcomes a check lists as success, else its `success` band and every band after it. A check that names no success has no success to give without a roll, so it rolls whenever its action runs, whatever the rules say about when checks happen (when the story decides, it still does, and the dice do when it can't), and a failed try at it isn't remembered.

When nothing is rolled, the check passes as `success` (when the action and the check have none, as the check's **Outcome when not rolled**, else its likeliest passing band); a hopeless try fails as `failure` (else its likeliest failing band); and a retry that needs a change fails the way it failed before.

### Your approach and the judge

When the rules weigh your words, or leave the check to the story, the **Judge your approach** step reads what you wrote before the reply. It rates your approach from 0 up to the most it counts, with one line on why, and when the story decides it also gives each outcome's odds, which the engine draws from with the turn's seed. It takes one call from the before-the-reply budget, ahead of NPC choices, runs only for your own action with a check the rules would roll or leave to the story, only when you described what you do (an action picked in the Actions sheet with nothing typed isn't judged), and is off in the Lean preset. Its answer is kept in memory with the reading of your message's text (the last 64 per chat, until a restart): a Re-narrate keeps the outcome, and a Re-run of the same message reads the same judgement with a fresh draw while it is kept, or asks again. Without a judgement (the step is off, out of budget or fails), a rolled check rolls as the Actions sheet showed, and a check the story would decide is rolled with the dice instead, and the **Roll** chip says nothing judged your approach. Other people's actions always roll.

### What you see

The Actions sheet says what the rules will do before you commit: **No roll needed**, **Too hard to try**, **Retry needs a change** (or **Retry needs a new scene**), or **The story decides (odds 35%)**, with the odds bar as the guidance. After the turn, the **Roll** chip says what was rolled, or "No roll:" and why, with how your approach was rated when it counted and the close call when there was one. The narrator is told the same: to narrate the result, that a close success may come at a cost or a close failure leave partial progress, or what the story decided.

An inferred check (see [Typed text](#typed-text)) shows the same way, with what it was for, and with how each companion did. When someone it went up against set how hard it was, neither the chip nor the narrator gets their numbers, nor the odds, the margin or the difficulty that would give them away, unless the chat's rules show others' numbers. Whatever the rules, no panel shows them, the Journal and the Context inspector included: their rolls are recorded as hidden checks.

### New chats and this chat

**Settings → Checks** holds your own rules for new chats: a new chat in a world that sets none uses them, and **Use my check rules instead of a world's for new chats** makes every new chat use them. A change there saves at once, and a chat keeps the rules it started with, whatever you change later. **GM → This chat → Checks** shows the rules in force in the chat you have open; change them and tap **Apply to this chat**, or **Cancel**. The change applies from the next turn, and it is recorded like a realism change, so it follows swipes and branches.

### Inferred checks

A typed attempt that no action covers ("I try to talk the bouncer into letting me in", "I keep my face blank while Marcus lies", "we sneak past the guard") can be read as a check of the world's own sheet, rolled and recorded like any check, so the reply follows the dice. How is set beside the rules above, in the same three places and layered the same way:

- **Build → Ruleset → How checks are decided**, under **Inferred checks**: the world's default. Turn on **This world sets how typed attempts are read as checks** to set it, starting from what the world's realism defaults play; leave it off and new chats take the player's own setting.
- **Settings → Checks**, under **Inferred checks**: yours, for new chats. **By the chat's realism** (the default) leaves each new chat to its realism preset; **My own** sets your own, starting from what your realism defaults play. It fills in for a world that sets none, and **Use my check rules instead of a world's for new chats** makes it replace a world's too. A change saves at once.
- **GM → This chat → Checks**, under the rules in force: the chat you have open. Change any value and tap **Review the change** to see what it does, then **Apply to this chat** (or **Keep editing**); **Cancel** drops the edits. It applies from the next turn, earlier turns keep how theirs were read, and it is recorded like a realism change, so it follows swipes and branches.

A chat nobody set one for, a chat started before inferred checks among them, plays by its realism preset at its Sheets level:

| Preset | Read typed attempts | Which attempts | Who rolls | Difficulty | The narrator is told | Others' numbers | Most rolls a turn |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Story** | Roll automatically | Only when the message says it tries | The player character only | Easy to Hard | The result | Hidden | 1 |
| **Grounded** | Roll automatically | Only risky attempts | With companions | Easy to Very hard | The result | Hidden | 3 |
| **Simulation** | Roll automatically | Every uncertain attempt | Everyone involved | Easy to Heroic | The result and margin | Hidden | 6 |

Story rolls by itself rather than asking, so a Story chat never stops to wait on a **Roll** card unless you pick **Suggest a roll**; it rolls only what your message says your character tries, so other messages play as they always did. At Sheets 0 there is no sheet to roll with, so it is off; at Sheets 1 the narrator is told the result alone, and at Sheets 2 the margin at most. A Custom realism takes the preset its Sheets level matches (1 Story, 2 Grounded, 3 and up Simulation).

The values, the same in each place:

- **Read typed attempts as checks**: **Off** (typed text is read as it was before inferred checks: a message that says it uses a skill reads as the world's own skill action, such as **Use a skill** or **Attempt**, and rolls its check, and an attempt no action covers plays as roleplay, the narrator deciding how it goes), **Suggest a roll** (a chip offers the roll, which happens only when you take it) or **Roll automatically**. Suggest and Roll automatically read such a message as **Try something** in place of those actions, so it is never rolled twice. Under Off the other values are greyed out and keep what they were, for when you turn it back on.
- **Which attempts**: **Every uncertain attempt**, **Only risky attempts** (below the risky line of the rules above) or **Only when the message says it tries**.
- **Who rolls**: **The player character only**; **With companions**, each companion who joins in rolling their own; or **Everyone involved**, the people the attempt opposes or targets rolling against it too.
- **Every skill the sheet has**, or turn it off and pick the skills an attempt may roll. Each place lists the skills its world's sheet has: Build the draft's sheet as you edit it, GM the chat's world's, and Settings the world of the chat you have open (with none open there is nothing to pick from). A picked skill a sheet doesn't have stays listed, marked so, until you take it off; a world whose sheet has none of the picked skills reads no attempt as a check.
- **Easiest difficulty** and **Hardest difficulty**: the bands the difficulty is read from, from the attempt and its scene: **Trivial** (DC 5), **Easy** (8), **Moderate** (10), **Tricky** (12), **Hard** (15), **Very hard** (20), **Heroic** (25) and **Nearly impossible** (30). One outside the range is moved to the nearest band in it, and moving one bound past the other takes the other along.
- **What the narrator is told**: **The result** (passed or failed), **The result and margin** (by how much) or **The whole roll** (the dice, the modifier and the total).
- **Others' numbers**: **Hidden** or **Shown**: whether an opponent's or a companion's dice, modifier, total or score reach the narrator and the **Roll** chip. Hidden keeps a hidden stat hidden.
- **After a success, tell the narrator** and **After a failure, tell the narrator**: the line the narrator is given after each result, up to 240 characters. `{who}` reads as who rolled, `{skill}` as the skill, `{attempt}` as what they tried and `{against}` as who or what it was against. A line left empty is the default's ("Narrate it succeeding.", "Narrate it failing."), and **Reset the lines** puts both back.
- **Most rolls a turn**: 1 to 12. Your character's roll comes first, then companions', then opponents'; past the most, an opponent sets the difficulty without a roll.

## Model use

LumiRPEngine does most of its work without the AI. When it does ask a model, it uses small, focused calls, each with its own job. **Model use** presets set which steps may call a model, when, and how many calls a phase may spend. **Settings → Model use** shows them as four cards, **Lean**, **Standard**, **Full** and **Custom**, each saying in plain words what the preset does and then its limits; tap a card to use that preset, and the one in use is marked **In use**.

| Preset | Before the reply | After the reply | Model steps beyond the narrator: most in one turn | Budgets (calls before / after) | Lore lookups (before / after) | Engine budget (sections) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Lean** | Reads every message you type | Reads the reply | 2 (reading your text, reading the reply) | 1 / 1 | none | 2,000 tokens |
| **Standard** (the default) | Reads every message you type; judges your approach when the rules for checks need it; asks about NPC choices on close calls and invitations | Reads the reply; NPC choices off screen on close calls | 4 (reading your text, judging your approach or an NPC choice with you, an NPC choice elsewhere, reading the reply) | 2 / 2 | 4 / 3 | 4,000 tokens |
| **Full** | Reads every message you type, judges your approach when needed, decides NPC choices for everyone present, writes a brief and a prose outline | Reads the reply, decides off-screen NPC choices, checks for contradictions | 8 (adds the brief, the prose outline, the contradiction check, and judging either a puzzle answer or your approach; an answer has no check to judge) | 8 / 4 | 6 / 4 | 8,000 tokens |
| **Custom** | Any step, any trigger, any connection and model | Same | Depends on the steps you enable; the call meter shows it live | Yours | Yours | Yours |

The number is the most steps a turn can run: each step takes one call from its phase's budget when it starts, so a phase never runs more steps than its budget allows. A step can cost more calls than that: each lore lookup is one more (from its phase's lookup budget), and a JSON repair, a Decision call falling back to the helper, or `interpret` asking again after a dropped connection adds one that no budget is charged for. Most turns cost less, because a step runs only when its trigger fires. The call meter counts every engine model call, not the narrator, so it shows each turn's real count. **Lean** is the floor. It keeps the cost down and leaves NPC choices to the engine's own rules rather than a model. Standard is where a model starts deciding close calls.

### The steps

| Step | When it runs | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `interpret` (Interpret typed input) | Before the reply | Reads every typed message as the actions it takes, in order (up to five play in the turn), including a request to let time pass, from what your character can do now, the scene and the messages before (see [Typed text](#typed-text)); what the turn doesn't play is a chip for your next message. |
| `npc.choose` (NPC choices) | Before the reply for people who are with you; after the reply for people elsewhere | Picks what an NPC does on a close call or invitation, from a list the engine enumerates (always including "do nothing"). |
| `puzzle.answer` (Puzzle answers) | Before the reply | Judges a free-text puzzle answer, only for puzzles whose author opted in; the answer is a probability against a threshold. It runs unless you turn it off, on every answer while its Signals are empty. |
| `judge` (Judge your approach) | Before the reply | Rates how well you describe your approach to an action's check, with one line on why, and when the story decides gives each outcome's odds. It runs only when the world's rules for checks need it; see [How checks are decided](#how-checks-are-decided). Off in Lean. |
| `brief` (Scene brief), `outline` (Prose outline) | Before the reply, once the engine has planned the turn | Write a short briefing or a prose outline for the narrator. The engine always writes a deterministic outline from its own beats; the brief and the prose outline stack on top of it in the Outline section. |
| `extract` (Extract changes) | After the reply | Reads the reply for changes. See [Extraction modes](#extraction-modes). |
| `contradiction` (Contradiction check) | After the reply | Checks the reply against the established facts. If it finds a clash, a **The reply contradicts the facts** chip appears, and the next turn is told the correction. |
| `choices` (Suggested choices) | After the reply | Offers next-step options as **Suggested choices** chips. |
| `mind` (Character minds) | Before the reply, and at time-skip beats | One call per character, as that character: their monologue, depiction, intent and reactions. Runs on its own cadence; see [Character minds](#character-minds). |
| `memory` (Character memory) | After the reply | Per character who heard your message, at the levels you pick: the notes and commitments they take from what the reply said, and the plans it states. |
| `skip` (Time-skip beats) | Before a time skip, once you turn it on (it is off in every preset) | Proposes further moments in the skip worth stopping at, beside the ones the engine finds. |
| `consolidate` (Memory consolidation) | After the reply | Folds a character's faded and surplus notes into their summary. |

A step runs when one of its **signals** fires (in **Settings → Steps and connections**, under each step's **Advanced: signals and lore**). `always` fires every turn; `never` turns the step off and wins over the rest. The others:

| Signal | Fires when |
|---|---|
| `typed_input` | The turn's text was typed rather than chosen. A step saved on the retired `classifier` signal runs on this. |
| `reply` | A reply exists (after the reply). |
| `scene_change`, `slot_crossed`, `day_start` | You changed place; the turn crossed a part of the day; it crossed midnight. |
| `beat` | The plan has outline beats. |
| `agents_present`, `present`, `offscreen`, `tie_zone` | Agents are with you; a close call is open for someone with you; for someone elsewhere; for the people this phase decides for. |
| `invitation`, `timelapse`, `conflict` | An invitation was made; the turn was a Timelapse; your action failed, was declined or rolled a failing band. |
| `every_n:k` | Every *k*th assistant turn. |

Prose steps (`brief`, `outline`, `choices`) are shown only what your character could see: the facts they get are the player-visible ones, and any line that would give away an unrevealed truth or a secret lore entry's text is dropped before the call. A question that carries a secret never shares a call with prose.

### The helper model and connections

Every model call the engine makes for itself (reading your actions and the reply, NPC choices, judging, the brief and outline, minds and their memory, time-skip beats, lore lookups) runs on the **helper model**, set in **Settings → Helper model**, the first page under **Getting started**:

- **Connection**: any of your Lumiverse connections. **None** runs each call on the connection Lumiverse marks as your default, with its own model and settings.
- **Model**: for a connection you picked, its model list (or type a name); empty uses the connection's own model.
- **Reasoning effort**: sent with each call as Lumiverse's own reasoning setting, which Lumiverse turns into the provider's field (DeepSeek reads Low to High as High, and Max and Extra high as Max). **Connection's own**, the default, leaves the connection's reasoning setting as it is, and **Off** turns reasoning off.
- **Max tokens**: 8,192 by default, with a warning below 4,096. Reasoning models think before they answer, and the thinking counts toward the limit, so a smaller one can leave the answer empty.
- **Temperature**, **Top P**, **Top K**, **Frequency penalty** and **Presence penalty**: each is **the connection's own** (its preset's value) until you switch it off and set a number. Lumiverse passes each one you set to the provider, which uses those it supports.

Picking a preset never changes the helper model. The Decisions API takes no effort, max tokens or sampling parameters, so steps on it run with their connection's own settings.

Each step can set its own, in **Settings → Steps and connections**, which opens with a **Helper model** row that leads to the page above. Each step is a card that shows what it runs on: *Helper*, the connection, the model, the effort, the max tokens and any parameter set; or *Decision* and its connection; or **Off**. Open it for a line on what the step does, **Enabled**, a **Provider** (*Decision* or *Helper*), a **Connection** and, for a connection you picked, a **Model**; then for a helper step **Reasoning effort**, **Max tokens** and, under **Sampling parameters**, each parameter. Each starts at **Use helper default**, and the card shows the helper model's value beside it; whatever the step sets wins over the helper model's, one by one. Every step but the minds steps has **Recent chat**: **Reads recent chat**, and while it's on how many of the messages before this turn's it reads (**Messages**) and about how many tokens of them (**Tokens**), each a number or **no limit**, the newest first, stopping at whichever limit comes first; the step gets them, oldest first, as a "Recent chat" block after its own input. *Interpret typed input* reads 8 messages and 3,000 tokens unless you change it; every other step reads none until you switch it on. Under **Advanced: signals and lore** are its **Signals** and lore. Nothing is cut or simplified because a model is small; each default is a tap to change. Picking **Lean**, **Standard** or **Full** in **Settings → Model use** replaces every step's settings with the preset's, connections and models included, so pick the preset first; changing a step afterwards makes it **Custom** and keeps the rest.

Each card also has a **Prompt**: your own wording for the step, which wins over the world's step template for it (Build → Step templates), and that over the built-in. It does to the step what a world template does: for reading your typed input and reading the reply it is added before the built-in instructions, which it can't change; for the brief, the outline and suggested choices it replaces the built-in instructions, which it starts from; for NPC choices, contradiction, puzzle answers and judging your approach it replaces the question asked. The answer format and the leak checks never change. **Save** keeps it, **Revert** drops your edits, and **Reset to the default** goes back to the world's template or the built-in. A world's `effects` templates still run beside your prompt. The minds steps' prompts are the same ones as in **Settings → Minds**; *Time-skip beats* has two, its beats' and **The skip plan**'s, the call that plans how the people the model plans spend a time skip. Picking a **Model use** preset keeps every prompt of yours. Beside each prompt, **Placeholders** lists the `{{vars}}` that step is given, when it runs, and what each holds; Build's step templates show the same list.

By default, the helper model handles `interpret`, `extract`, `contradiction`, the prose steps and the minds, and your **Decision connection** (Lumiverse's Decisions API) handles `npc.choose`. The Decisions API takes no reasoning effort or max tokens, so a Decision step runs with its connection's own settings. If a decision call fails, the step falls back to the helper model, and then to the engine alone. A failed call is never blindly retried, and neither a repair of malformed JSON nor a decision falling back to the helper is charged against a budget. The model and connection you pick for a step are the ones its call uses.

### Budgets, deadlines and fallbacks

Budgets are a **number of calls for each phase**: **Before the reply** (reading your text, the NPC choices for people with you, puzzle answers, judging your approach, the brief and the prose outline, and the minds beside them) and **After the reply** (reading the reply, off-screen NPC choices, the contradiction check, suggested choices). You set them in **Settings → Model use** under **Advanced: budgets and the engine budget**, each phase with **Calls** and **Lore lookups**, beside **Engine budget (tokens)** for the sections; how long each phase may take is in [Settings → Timeouts](#timeouts). Calls, lore lookups and the token budgets have no upper limit. Changing a budget makes the preset **Custom**. Each step takes one call from its phase's budget before it starts; when a budget is spent, or its time is up, each remaining step uses its fallback (**Calls** 0 means no model at all in that phase): number-only NPC choices, the deterministic outline, a check rolled as if unjudged (one the story would decide goes to the dice), or plain roleplay plus reading the reply. Steps run by priority, and independent ones run in parallel. Lumiverse stops a Decision call at 30 seconds, its own limit, and the step then uses the helper. Simulation itself is never cut off by a clock; it is bounded by counts (days, agents, events).

Settings saved before these defaults rose keep every value you changed; a value still at an old default (a step's 2,000 max tokens, which now follows the helper model; the old lore tokens, lookup counts and section budgets) reads as the new one.

### Timeouts

**Settings → Timeouts** (under *Getting started*) holds every wait the engine keeps, each with what it limits, what happens when it runs out, its default and its value in seconds. None has a ceiling, and **No limit** lifts one wherever that can't break anything. Picking a Model use preset never changes them.

- **Turns**: **Engine run** (5 min: everything before the reply is generated; when it runs out the run stops, no reply is generated, and the chat says which step ran out, with a Retry), **Lore lookups before the reply** (2 min), **Reading the reply** (5 min: what changed, the contradiction check, people elsewhere, choices, memory), **Lore lookups after the reply** (2 min), **Generation guard** (2 min: Lumiverse's wait for the engine's check, and for its writing of the turn's sections, as a generation starts; Lumiverse caps it at 2 min) and **Time skip plan** (the helper call that plans a time skip for those **Planned by the model**; when it runs out nobody is planned, so they keep to their sleep and meals, the GM's routine page says why, and the skip runs).
- **This tab**: **Engine requests** (5 min: how long the tab waits for each answer) and **Preset check** (1 min: how long Caveats waits for its test prompt before saying it's still running).
- **Jobs and background work**: **Minds run on demand** (10 min), **Import and Refine calls** (10 min each; the lore index, map and routine proposal calls in Build take it too), **Token counts** (30 s; 0 always uses the engine's estimate), **Background lane start-up** and **Background lane heartbeat** (1 min each; Lumiverse caps both at 2 min, and No limit on the heartbeat asks it to check none), and **MCP server reconnect** (1 min: how long **Reconnect in Lumiverse** waits for a server Lumiverse runs to come back with the new tools before its line tells you to reconnect it by hand).

The page ends with **Lumiverse's own limits**, which no setting changes. When a timeout runs out, [Diagnostics](#diagnostics) records it with the setting's name and how to raise it, and a request the tab gave up on says so in its error. Settings saved before Timeouts existed keep the wall clocks you set; one still at an old preset's default rises to the new default. The command line and the MCP server take `--timeout <seconds|none>` (or `LRE_TIMEOUT`), 300 seconds by default.

### Lore for the helpers

The engine's own helpers read the world's lore ([World lore](#world-lore)) to do their jobs: `interpret` resolves names and aliases, `extract` recognises who and what the reply is about, `npc.choose` weighs a person's past and motives, `judge` weighs your described approach against what the world says, and the brief, the outline, the contradiction check and puzzle answers read what bears on the scene. The narrator gets no lore section of its own, but the brief and the prose outline may read the world's public lore to plan the scene, so what they write for it can reflect that lore.

- **Prefetch.** Before a step asks its model, the engine searches the lore with the step's own inputs: your message or the reply, the people and places in the scene, the quests you can see, or the people and options of an NPC choice. What it finds goes into that step's prompt, cut to fit the step's lore tokens. A prefetch costs no call.
- **Lookups.** A step can also ask for more. On most connections it calls the lore tools (search, read one entry, list a topic); on a connection that refused tools once, it asks in a plain form instead (`{"need": [...]}`), and the engine remembers that connection. Each time the model asks, the engine answers and the step is asked again: one more call, a **lore lookup**. Steps on the Decision connection can't use tools, so before deciding they get one lookup on the helper that asks what lore the decision needs.
- **Per step**, in **Settings → Steps and connections**, under each step's **Advanced: signals and lore**: **Lore** (*Off*, *Prefetch*, *Look up* or *Both*), **Lore tokens**, and **Lookups by** (*Auto*, *Tools* or *Ask form*). A step without its own setting takes its preset's.
- **Per phase**, in **Settings → Model use** under **Advanced: budgets and the engine budget**: **Lore lookups**; in **Settings → Timeouts**, how long after the phase starts one may begin. Each lookup takes one from its phase's lookup budget, apart from the phase's calls, and the steps that run first get them first. A lookup starts only while a third of the phase's time is left, so a step still has time to answer. When none is left, the step answers with what its prefetch found. **Lean** makes no lookups.
- **Pinned lore.** In **Build**, a character's page and a place's page (a place drawn on a map too) have **Pinned lore**, and **Pinned lore** in Build's **World** list has a section for each step. **Pin world lore** picks one of the world's entries, and **Pin from World Info** picks one of your World Info books (the ones attached to the open chat first) and then **The whole book** or one of its entries, even one switched off in its book. A whole book is read as it is each time a call reads it: its switched-on entries, in the book's order, so entries added to it later (the chapters another extension writes, say) are read without pinning them again. What is pinned to a character is read first by their mind every time they think; what is pinned to **Character minds** is read by every character's mind, after their own pins, and what is pinned to **Time-skip beats** by every mind at a time skip's beat too, after those; what is pinned to any other step is read first by every call of that step, whatever its **Lore** setting; what is pinned to a place is read first by every helper and mind there, or in a place inside it. **Character memory**, **Memory consolidation** and **Time-skip beats**' own call read only what is pinned to them, and so does the time skip's plan for those whose skip the model plans, which reads what is pinned to **Time-skip beats**. For a helper, pinned text comes first within the step's **Lore tokens**; for a mind, the other minds steps and the skip's plan, within **Lore pinned to them and to where they are** in **Settings → Minds → What a mind reads** (no limit by default). When it doesn't all fit it is cut, and the **Context** panel says so and which limit to raise. Pins are part of the world: commit, then **Upgrade** the chat, to use a new pin in it; a pinned book's text is always read as it is now. A secret entry pinned to a place or a step still reaches only its audience, and a pinned entry the AI wrote waits for your review like any other. **Check** warns of a pin to an entry that is gone, twice over, or that nobody can read, and opens the page it is on.
- **Secrets.** A secret entry reaches only the helpers its audience names, until its gate holds; the brief, the prose outline, suggested choices and extraction never get it, even if ticked. An entry marked **Known by** is read only once your character knows every person and place it links. Nothing a helper writes for you may carry a secret's text: a chip, a Journal line, a section, an action read from your words or the reason given for a judged approach that repeats an unrevealed truth, or a sentence or clause of a secret entry, word for word is dropped, unless the words were yours. The check looks only for clauses of at least four words and 16 characters, so a shorter phrase or a paraphrase isn't caught, which is why a secret never goes to a step that writes for you. The helpers are told only who your character can see there, and a reason for a judged approach that names someone, a quest or a place your character can't see, or a secret entry by its title, is dropped whatever you typed; the rating stands. Names count word for word: *Vex is watching* names a hidden Vex Morrow, while *dangerous* doesn't name a hidden Dan. A plural counts too (*the Okafors* names a hidden Ada Okafor), and so do the accents people leave out (*Zoe* names a hidden Zoë, *Soren* a hidden Søren). Someone whose name is a single letter, such as *Q*, counts as that capital letter standing alone, except *A* and *I*, which are ordinary words. Anything else, a place, a thing, a quest or an entry's title, counts only by all its words in order, so a secret *Under the Chapel* isn't named by *under* alone. A name in another script counts wherever it appears, even inside a word, which catches a language written without spaces (*李雷在监视你*) and a name with an ending added (*Вексом* for *Векс*).

### The call meter

The call meter counts each turn's engine model calls, seconds and tokens, and its lore lookups when there were any. **This turn** in the World sheet and the pill's details shows the latest turn. **Settings → Model use** has a **Call meter** section with rows for **This turn**, **Average** and **95th pct**, and columns for **Calls**, **Tokens**, **Lookups** and **Time**; **GM → This chat → Context** ends with the same meter. If a preset costs more than you want, this is where you see it. The meter is saved for you, so a restart keeps its numbers.

### Engine tokens versus your context

The engine's sections are a fixed overhead in every prompt, up to the engine budget. Nothing checks that budget against your connection's context size, so keep it well inside it: if the fixed part of the prompt is larger than the context, Lumiverse drops the chat history, including your latest message.

### Diagnostics

While a turn runs, the pill, the status line and the top of the drawer say what the engine is doing and for how long. If a step fails, they turn red ("Failed: Planning"); the drawer's banner has **Diagnostics** and **Dismiss**.

**Settings → Diagnostics** (also under About) shows what the engine did in this chat. It can name someone your character hasn't met, so it opens behind **Show diagnostics**. Then:

- **Errors** lists what failed, newest first, with the reason and what to change: for example, an answer cut off at its max tokens tells you to raise **Max tokens** for that step in **Settings → Steps and connections**.
- Each **turn** lists its steps in order (planning, the plan, the outline, filling the prompt, reading the reply, memory) with their time and outcome, and under each the model calls it made: the connection and model, tokens in and out, why the answer ended, and the time. Filling the prompt lists each section, the preset outlet it goes to, and whether it got text.
- **Slow** marks a view or an edit that took over a second.
- **Show** filters to **Problems**, **Model calls** or **Slow**; **Copy** puts the shown records on the clipboard as text, **Download** saves them all as JSON for a bug report, and **Clear** forgets them.

The engine keeps the last 400 records of each chat, so they survive a restart, and writes each as a line to your `diagnostics.log`.

## Character minds

With **character minds** on, the people near the story think for themselves. Each one's mind is its own model call, made as that character and scoped to what they know. By default a mind reads the story as it stands: who they are (their own fields, agendas and secrets), where they are and whom they see there, the lore pinned to them and the lore the turn names, how the latest reply ended and what you say; their usual routine, as context they may weigh; and their own plans, so they keep to what they agreed. Their own memory and life (their summary, notes and what they saw, last thoughts, the turns they recall, their money, today as the planner lays it, and their commitments) they read only if you switch them on in **What a mind reads** ([Settings → Minds](#settings--minds)). Minds are blind to each other, so no mind decides what another character says or does, and none reads what another character's memory says it told them. A mind answers with an inner monologue, a **depiction** (how they come across), what they intend and their reactions, and the narrator is told how to depict it. After the reply, the concrete changes (money, moves) are still read from what the reply actually says, and the characters' memory is written from it.

A character's **plans** come only from what the story states: after the reply, their memory takes the plans the turn sets out (something said aloud, agreed with someone, or scheduled in the story as written), never a guess at what they might do. A mind makes none, since it sees what its character knows, not where the story is going. A plan moves them at its time, may ask you along, and can stop a time skip. In a chat from before, plans a mind made stay in GM mode as thoughts, and do none of this.

A plan with other people in it is **one plan they all share**: "Danny and Priya agree on dinner at the Anchor at seven" reaches both of them (and anyone else in it), whoever's memory took it from the turn, and a second memory of the same turn doesn't add it again. Each of them lives it as their own day allows: someone who lives their own day goes there at its time, and anyone else (someone who doesn't, or a Background character) lets it pass. You're never given a copy: your plans are yours to make, and when their plan asks you along, one of them invites you once. **Accept invitation** books it in your character's day: an appointment at the meeting's time and place (when you know the place), as long as their plan or routine block for it lasts, under the host's name. A Timelapse that follows your routine takes you there, and accepting again books nothing more. A payment or a loan stays the payer's own.

A plan to pay or lend someone money is carried out by the money engine: at the plan's time, the amount goes from the character to the person the plan is with, from their own money as they'd pay anything, and the plan is done. If they can't cover it, nothing moves and the plan is dropped. Their journal says only **Payment** or **Loan**, never what they had in mind.

Each mind is recorded on the turn, so swipes and branches keep it: **Re-narrate** reuses the turn's minds, **Re-run** asks again, and Playtest and Check world never call a model for them.

### The fidelity ladder

Every turn, and at each beat of a time skip, each character gets a focus score and a level from it:

- **Live**: the closest to the story. Only people at hand are Live: in your scene, or named this turn. Anyone else is Near at most, however much of the story they carry.
- **Near**: they think again when something calls for it: they come into the scene, they are named, or their mind is old enough (the Near refresh).
- **Far**: they think now and then, on the Far refresh period, and their plans shape their day if they live their own day.
- **Background**: no mind; they follow their routine.

Who thinks follows your scene and what's in motion:

- **Everyone in your scene** thinks every turn, best on the ladder first, up to your Live limit. Your scene is where you are, or a place up to two levels inside or around it, as long as nothing between is spread out (a town or a region: a place whose parts a drive, a boat or a walk of more than ten minutes joins, or whose parts short walks don't all join, such as a town whose homes and sites have no routes between them). Someone the story put on the fourth floor is with you in the bath of a suite on it, even in a building drawn room by room with short walks between; someone in the room next door, somewhere else in a big building, or somewhere in town, isn't.
- **Someone elsewhere** thinks only while they're caught up in something that could come up soon: a plan or appointment with you within the next day, an invitation to you, an agenda of their own under way, your intent for them in GM mode, a place or activity the story holds them to, or their interrupting a time skip. They think on their level's refresh, or every turn when they're Live, and what they thought is on record, though it never shows in the Minds section. Being named, idling or going about their routine makes no one think.
- A mind that falls due while nothing calls it still keeps the level it fell due at, so a character's routine role never changes with who thinks.

The score adds up proximity, being named, their feelings toward you, how they figure in the story (quests, secrets, agendas, story roles, rules that name them), what is pending between you (invitations, appointments, plans, commitments), their tier and how recently you saw them. The world sets the weights and thresholds, and who is always Live or never gets a mind ([Character minds in a world](#character-minds-in-a-world)); **GM → Minds** can pin anyone at a level.

### Memory

Each character remembers in four layers: a **witness log** of what they saw happen or were told; **recall** of the turns they were present for (and only those: what was narrated of other people's private thoughts is ignored); **notes** written after a reply, for the levels you pick, which fade with time unless they matter; and **commitments** (promises, debts, grudges, favours, plans with someone) that stay until they are kept, broken or released. Faded and surplus notes can be folded into the character's **summary**, at most once a period for each character and for at most two characters a turn. The world sets the defaults; your Settings can override any of them.

### Settings → Minds

**Settings → Minds** opens with a line on what minds are and the **Character minds** switch. Off, no character gets a mind, and the engine's own rules decide invitations as before. On, these sections follow:

- **How many think**: **Live minds a turn**, a number or **no limit**; and **Near and Far refreshes a turn**, how many Near and Far minds may think in one turn besides the Live ones, the most pressing first, or **no limit**. A refresh you ask for in GM mode always runs.
- **Near refresh**: when they come into the scene or it changes; when the latest message or reply names them; every so many turns; when their mind is so many game minutes old. Each number can be turned off.
- **Far refresh**: every so many game minutes, or never.
- **Memory notes after the reply**: a switch for each level (**Live characters who heard you**, and so on) that gets the memory step. On a turn that takes you somewhere else, the people you leave remember it, and the people you meet there don't, since they weren't there to hear it.
- **What a mind reads**: each source a mind may read, with its switch and, while it's on, its limit in tokens, which can be **no limit**. Within its limit a source comes whole, and nothing else cuts it. Who they are, where they are and whom they see, and what they mean to do are always in it.
  - **The story as it stands**, on by default: **Lore pinned to them and to where they are** (no limit); **Lore the turn activates**, the world's entries your message or the latest reply names by a key, an alias, a title or a lookup-table term, as World Info activates them (1,600 tokens); **The latest reply**, how it ended, for those who were there for it (the whole reply; with a number they read its end); and **Your message** (all of it).
  - **The chat as you see it**, off by default: **Recent chat**, the latest messages before the turn as you see them in the chat (hidden JSON blocks left out), oldest first, up to **Recent chat (messages)** and **Recent chat (tokens)**, whichever comes first (8 messages and 3,000 tokens when you turn it on; either can be **no limit**). It includes turns the character wasn't there for: it's the chat as you see it, not what they know. A mind that reads **The latest reply** doesn't get that reply twice: Recent chat gives the messages before it. **Character memory**, **Memory consolidation** and **Time-skip beats** read it too while it's on, with the same limits, and so does the time skip's plan for those whose skip the model plans; their step cards have no Recent chat of their own. The **Context** panel counts the messages each mind was given.
  - **What they usually do and mean to do**, on by default: **Their usual routine** (400 tokens, in whole lines): their routine blocks and their own place and activity schedules, as the world and the story set them, while their routine isn't off ([Routines and schedules](#routines-and-schedules)). It's what they usually do, theirs to weigh, never what they must do: a private block only when it's their own, and never the day the planner lays or the friends it fills free time with. **Their plans** (400 tokens, in whole lines): their open plans, soonest first, with when, where and who else is in it, from what the story's turns stated and what you set in GM mode. A plan they share with others is each one's: in its own words for the one whose memory took it from the turn (and for everyone, once you write its words in GM mode), and for the others as its kind, time, place and people, since one character's memory never speaks for another's. The **Context** panel says how many lines of routine and how many plans each mind got, and when some were left out over the limit.
  - **Their own memory and life**, off by default: **Their summary**, **Their notes and what they saw**, **Their last thoughts**, **Turns they were there for** (its limit is recall's tokens, a memory setting) and **Their life: money, the day and commitments** (today as the day planner lays it). Each can carry what the story never set up: the planner fills a free evening with dinners and friends, and a commitment their memory took from narration that showed another character's mind isn't theirs. Switched on, each is read within its limit; what another character's memory says it told them never reaches a mind.
  - Settings saved before **What a mind reads** keep their limits: the latest reply's allowance, and, if you set the Character minds step's lore tokens yourself (a **Custom** preset), those as both lore limits; a preset's own lore tokens give way to the defaults above. The memory step after the reply reads the turn within the latest reply's and your message's limits.
- **Advanced: the Minds section budget and time skips**, folded away: **Minds section budget (tokens)**, the narrator's Minds section's own budget, apart from the section budget, so minds never crowd out Scene or People (when the minds don't all fit, the characters lowest on the ladder are left out of it first, and the Stance section tells of them instead); and under **Time skips**, **Beats a skip may stop at** and **Minds at each beat**, either of which can be **no limit**.

Two sections are there whether minds are on or off:

- **Memory**: **Your overrides**, folded away, with a badge counting the ones you set. Each of the world's memory settings (see [Character minds in a world](#character-minds-in-a-world)) says "The world's applies (built in: …)" until you set your own; then it shows a **Yours** badge and **Use the world's**, which goes back to the world's.
- **Prompts**: a row for each minds step (Character minds, Character memory, Time-skip beats, Memory consolidation), with a **Built-in** or **Your own** badge. Open it for the text, with **Save**, **Revert** and **Reset to the default**; the built-in text is under **The built-in default**, inside it. **Save** keeps your own; **Reset to the default** drops it. Saving the built-in text unchanged keeps no copy, so you get later improvements to it. Your own prompt wins over a world's own step template for that step, and the world's template over the built-in.

The **Model use** presets set minds too: **Lean** turns them off; **Standard** runs up to 3 Live minds and 2 refreshes a turn, with memory notes for Live characters; **Full** has no limits, with notes for Live and Near. Picking a preset resets everything above but what a mind reads, your prompts and your memory; changing any of the rest makes the preset **Custom**. The Minds section's own budget is 1,200 tokens on Lean, 2,400 on Standard and 4,000 on Full, and a mind recalls up to 4,000 tokens of past turns unless the world or your memory overrides say otherwise (either can say **no limit**). Where each minds step runs (its connection, model, effort and max tokens) is in **Settings → Steps and connections**, as for the other steps; what a mind reads, its lore included, is set here, and the memory, time-skip and consolidation steps read only the lore pinned to them in Build. A minds step you haven't set there shows **On**, on the helper model with 8,192 max tokens, because that is how it runs; only its **Enabled** switch turns it off. **Time-skip beats** is the exception: it shows **Off**, and runs only once you turn it on. Minds' calls aren't counted in the presets' step counts above: a turn makes one call per mind that runs, and after the reply one memory call per character who heard your message, at a level you picked, and now and then a consolidation.

### Minds in GM mode

**GM → Minds** starts with the chat's time (**Now**) and **This world's secrecy**, then **Search by name** and a level filter (**Every level**, or one level). Below, every character but you, closest on the ladder first: their name, their level, when they last thought (or **No mind yet**), a **Pinned**, **Refresh** or **Intent** badge, and their score. Tap someone for their page, with a back button to **Minds**:

- **Fidelity**: their level and score, and each thing that added to it. Under **Pin and refresh**, **Pin their fidelity** opens a page that keeps them at a level in this chat whatever their score (**By score** unpins); its button stays off until you pick something other than what's in force. The author's pin, if any, has its own row. **Refresh their mind next turn** opens a page that asks for a fresh mind on the next turn, whatever the refresh settings say.
- **Your intent for them**: what they mean to do now, as you set it. Its page has **What they mean to do**, in your own words (up to 600 characters), and **How they go about it**: **Their mind decides** (the default), **Reactive** (they answer the scene and what they're asked, and start nothing of their own) or **Proactive** (they pursue their own aims without waiting to be asked). Either can be set alone. Their own mind reads it first, right after who they are, and works from it, so the story follows what they then intend; it is never cut to fit, and no other character's mind, the narrator or anything in Play ever sees it. It stays until you change it: emptying both, or **Clear intent**, clears it, and its edit's **Undo** brings it back. Swipes keep it, as they keep a pin.
- **Latest mind**: when and why it ran, where they were, their mood, monologue, depiction, intent and reactions, and whether it interrupted a time skip. A **To the narrator** badge marks what the narrator got under the world's secrecy, and a warning says when a depiction was kept from the narrator because it gave something away.
- **Plans**, **Commitments** and **Memory notes**: each row opens the item's own page to edit it, with **Delete plan** (or **Delete commitment**, **Delete note**) at the bottom, and **Add a plan**, **Add a commitment** and **Add a note** open a page for a new one. A plan has what it is, its kind, when it starts (hours from now) and how long it lasts, where, with whom, a priority from 1 (whenever it fits) to 5 (it must happen) and its status. A plan with others in it is theirs too: adding, editing or deleting it on anyone's page does it for everyone in it (each keeps their own status), someone you take out of it loses it, and someone you add gets it. A plan to **Pay someone** or **Lend money** also has an amount, shown beside its kind, and needs someone it is with: the money goes to the first of them at the plan's time. A plan their mind made in a chat from before shows a **Thought** badge and "a thought from their mind, which drives nothing": it moves no one, pays nothing and stops no skip, and isn't counted among the open plans. A commitment has its kind, its text, who it is with, an optional due time (hours from now) and its status. A note has its text and a salience from 1 to 5, and shows how much it has faded.
- **Summary** and **Witnessed**: what they have folded into their summary, and what they saw or were told.
- **Run their mind now** asks for their mind at once, and **Pinned lore** shows what is pinned to them and to where they are, with **Edit in Build** to change it.

Above the list, **Run minds now** has **Run everyone Live and Near** and **Choose who to run**: tap people in the list to pick them, or use the bar under the list, whose **Select all** picks everyone the list shows (its search and level filter as they stand) and **Select a level** exactly the characters at one level, then **Run their minds**. A run asks each mind from the chat so far and their lore, as a turn would, four at a time. Its card shows each mind as it goes, **Waiting**, **Thinking**, **Recorded** or **Failed** with why (the time ran out, the call failed, the answer was empty or cut off), with its time, tokens and the pinned lore it read; **Cancel the run** stops it and records nothing. When it ends, what came back is recorded as one edit, and **Undo this run** takes it back. A run between turns reads how the latest reply ended; a run while a reply is being written lands when it finishes. It stops at your **Minds run on demand** limit in **Settings → Timeouts**: 10 minutes unless you change it, or no limit.

Each change is a GM edit like any other: one correction with its own **Undo**. Minds stay in GM mode: nothing a mind thinks reaches Play, the Journal, People or chips. **GM → This chat → Context** lists each mind call with the pinned lore it read and whether any was cut.

### Routines in GM mode

**GM → Routines** shows the routines and schedules everyone follows in this chat. A routine or a schedule is never a script. By default it only informs: the story and planning decide what people do, and only Background characters keep an enforced routine. Even then the story leads whoever shares a scene with you, and no routine interrupts play. The world's routines and schedules are edited in Build (**The world's routines are in Build** goes there); what you change here is this chat's alone.

The list has everyone but you, closest on the minds ladder first, with **Search by name** and a filter (everyone, **Changed in this chat**, **Routine leads**, **Story leads; routine informs**, **No routine**). Each row shows their fidelity and how much their routine binds them, from their routine's role, and a gold badge when this chat changed their routine or schedules: **Changed by you**, **Changed by the story** (a reply), **Changed from your message** (a routine your message stated, added with **Yes**), or more than one ("Changed by you and the story"). Tap someone for their page:

- **How much their routine binds them**, from the role it has for them (the role of their level, as the world sets it, unless this chat sets theirs): **Story leads; routine informs** when it's informative (the story and planning decide what they do, and their routine is only context for their mind and the narrator) or enforced while the planner doesn't lay their day (it needs an agent who acts, at Cast life 3 or higher); **Routine leads** when it's enforced and the planner lays their day; and **No routine** when its role is off or their routine is off in this chat. The lines under it say what the role does, where it comes from and, for an enforced routine, what the world's awareness setting does for them. **In this chat**, **Their routine's role**, **Story and lore awareness** and **In a time skip** open one page with all four: **Follows their routine**, **Follows it, no invitations** or **No routine**; **Their level's default** (naming it), **Informative**, **Enforced** or **Off**; **Their level's default**, **Stay put**, **Follow their routine** or **Planned by the model** ([Routines and schedules](#routines-and-schedules)); and **As the world has it**, **Story first**, **Balanced** or **Routine first**. **Save for this chat** makes it one edit; **Use the world's settings** clears what you set.
- **What the story holds now**: a place or activity the story established, which their routine yields to, and for how long.
- **Planned for the skip**, once a time skip has been planned: what the model planned for them in the current or last skip (**This skip** or **The last skip**), each block with where, how far into the skip and for how long, and the plan's note when the call failed or was skipped. Whenever no block covered them, they kept to their sleep and meals.
- **Their routine**: each block with when and where it happens, whose it is (their own, a group's, a kind's, a selector's, the world's default, the basic needs, or **Changed in this chat**), what it rests on (authored, a lore entry, a sheet field, the story or you, with how long ago) and what it replaces. Tap a block to change it for this chat with the same form as Build's, **Switch it off for them**, or, for a change this chat made, **Use the world's block** (or **Remove the block** when only this chat has it). The block's page shows the lore entry's quote, with **Open the entry** in Build. **Add a block for this chat** adds one only this chat has.
- **Their schedules**: each schedule that applies to them, the most specific owner first for each thing schedules set, with what it rests on, and **In force**, **Shadowed** (a more specific schedule wins where both run) or this chat's change. Tap one to give them **Their own version** in this chat, with Build's schedule form, or put it **Off for them** until you clear it or for some hours; **Use the world's schedule** drops the change.
- **Today**: their day as the planner laid it, each block with where, why it is there (their routine, a schedule, a plan, an appointment, a trip, an invitation, free time) and the reasons it gave.
- **Invitations**: the ones they made and received, each with where it came from: a plan the story made, a block of their routine (with its source), you, or one recorded before invitations needed a ground.
- **Changes made in play**: each block or schedule a reply or your own message changed in this chat, with where it came from (**The story** for a reply, **From your message** for one you said **Yes** to), the turn it came from (its id shows under **Show ids**) and how long ago. A block added this way is in force from the next day, and its row says **In force from the next day** until then. Tap one in force to keep it, change it or go back to the world's.

Each change is a GM edit like any other: one correction with its own **Undo**, and the list's badge follows it.

## Extraction modes

After the narrator writes, the engine reads the reply for changes the story implies. Each kind of change is a **type**, and each type has a **mode**.

| Mode | What happens |
|---|---|
| **auto** | The change is checked and applied, and an **Undo** chip appears. |
| **confirm** | A chip asks you first, with **Apply** and **No**. If you ignore it, it expires when you send your next message and nothing is applied. |
| **never** | The change is ignored. |

| Type in Settings (as a Mode change chip names it) | What it covers | Starting mode |
|---|---|---|
| **Time passing** (Time skips) · `time_advance` | The story jumps ahead ("three hours later"). Up to [Skips without asking](#skips-without-asking) (a day by default) applies with the world caught up (under confirm, when you tap **Apply**), whether or not the clock is paused; a longer jump, up to 366 days, becomes a **Run timelapse** chip in either mode, and one longer still is dropped with no chip. During a fight none applies: a **Didn't apply** chip says so, and **Apply** on an earlier chip is refused | auto |
| **Moving somewhere** (Moves) · `location` | Someone moves. Moves are story first: a move the reply tells lands even where no route leads, unless the world turns on its travel simulation (its map settings in Build's **World** part). A reply that sends someone to their usual spot ("Danny heads out to his usual lunch place") moves them to the place their routine has for now, as you could know it: never a private part of their routine or a place you haven't found, and never someone the reply doesn't send anywhere. A reply that has you somewhere other than where the engine has you without telling the move (it takes it as given) gets an **Apply** chip for your move there, whatever the mode (none on **never**): a place the story only implies may be a flashback, a phone call or a misread, so you stay where you are until you tap **Apply**, and your answer doesn't count toward learning; one that can't happen leaves nothing, no chip and no note to the next reply. None when your own action this turn took you elsewhere (the engine's outcome wins). Any change in **confirm** the engine refuses gets a **Didn't apply** chip, as in **auto**, unless it names something you don't know of | auto |
| **Conditions (hurt, tired…)** (Condition changes) · `condition` | A status changes (tired, injured, soaked) | auto |
| **Learning a fact** (New facts) · `knows` | You learn a public fact, or hear of a place you didn't know (never a secret one) | confirm |
| **Giving things** (Handovers) · `give` | A handover between two characters | confirm |
| **Purchases** · `purchase` | A narrated purchase, priced from the catalog | confirm |
| **Money transfers** (Payments) · `transfer` | Money moves between people, or between someone's own accounts | confirm |
| **Feelings toward others** (Feelings) · `regard` | Someone's feelings change: by at most 10 on each feeling per reply, however often the reply says so | confirm |
| **Quantities** · `quantity` | A count changes (ammunition, supplies) | confirm (no starting mode of its own; Settings shows *From the field's bound*) |
| **New people** (New characters) · `spawn` | A new character appears (up to the world's limit, 24 by default). A name someone you know already has is a duplicate and isn't added (under Auto, a chip says so); a name only someone you don't know has is left out without a word, so it never tells you such a person exists | confirm |
| **Life and death (dying, dead, collapsed…)** (Life and death) · `life` | Someone dies, is dying, collapses or comes round (what you already knew is left out) | confirm |
| **Routines (a new job, a habit, a move…)** (Routine changes) · `routine` | The reply says someone's week has changed for good: a new job or shift, a new habit, one they gave up, or a move to a new home. It changes their routine in this chat from the next day (what they gave up, and a move, at once), and GM → Routine lists it among the changes the story made, with its turn. Only someone the reply names (or you), at places and with people you know of; a habit only mentioned, or what is already their routine or schedule, changes nothing, and so does a new shift that clashes with their schedule unless the reply says it takes its place. A character's thoughts never count | auto |

Beyond the mode, every field has a limit on how much a single change may be, and **the strictest rule always wins**. Fields a world marks as locked are never offered to the model. If a narrated change contradicts the engine's own outcome, the engine's outcome wins. A purchase takes in the seller's handover and the payment, so one sale is not applied three times, and a payer has to be an account.

The strict reading rules are: time jumps only for an explicit jump or stated duration; knowledge only for public facts you newly learn; feelings only when the prose states a change; handovers only between two characters; routines only for a lasting change the prose states, never a one-off, a plan, a wish or a habit only mentioned.

### Learning on your own model

The starting modes come from measurements on a small model. Your model may do better or worse, so the engine **learns from how you answer**, on each connection separately, with no extra model calls:

- Each time you confirm or reject a change, or undo an automatic one, that counts. Something applied automatically and not undone by the next turn also counts as accepted. An ignored confirm chip counts nothing, and so does your answer to the **Apply** chip for a move a reply only implies (see **Moving somewhere** above).
- When a `confirm` type reaches **90% accepted over its latest 30 outcomes** on a connection, it flips to `auto` there, and a **Mode change** chip tells you ("Purchases now apply automatically") with an **Undo** button.
- When an `auto` type's undo rate in the latest 30 passes **10%**, it flips back to `confirm`, with the same chip ("Purchases need your confirm again").
- **Undo** on either chip restores the previous mode and holds it for another 30 outcomes.

You can pin any type to **Auto**, **Confirm** or **Never** in **Settings → Changes from the reply** (under *Replies*): setting a mode pins it, and **Unpin** hands it back to learning. Each type shows where its mode comes from: *Pinned by you*, *Learned on <connection> (n kept, n rejected)*, *Default* (with *learning (n kept, n rejected)* once anything is counted) or *From the field's bound*. A pinned type is still counted but never flipped. The order of precedence is: your pinned mode, then what was learned on the turn's connection, then the starting mode, then the field's own bound.

## The Context inspector

**GM → This chat → Context**, under *Inspector*, shows what the engine put into the prompt for each generation. It doesn't need the GM gate, because it shows only what you may know. At the top are any warnings: the world's book wasn't attached to the chat or some of its section entries were missing (both put right for the next turn), your preset has a setting that causes trouble, or a damaged journal was replaced (with where the old file is kept). The **Older generation** and **Newer generation** buttons step through earlier generations. **Activation** reads **Activation confirmed** once Lumiverse reports every section the engine switched on as activated, and **Not confirmed** until then or when one wasn't, with a **Section budget** bar of the tokens used. **Engine sections** lists the sections it filled in (Outcome, Scene, People and so on) with their sizes and whether each was **Delivered**, **Not delivered** or **Unchecked**. A section that was left out because it went over the section budget shows **Clipped** and "Left out: over the section budget", and is never shown as delivered. The Minds section has a budget of its own: when some of its lines don't fit, its row says how many were left out ("2 lines left out: over its own budget"), and when none fit it is **Clipped** with "Left out: over its own budget". Sizes are the token counts of Lumiverse's tokenizer for each section's exact text, counted for your main model. Until Lumiverse has counted a section, or if that takes more than a second, the engine uses an estimate that stays above the real count. The [call meter](#the-call-meter) comes last.

**Lore the helpers read** lists, for each model step of the generation, what it read of the world's lore ([Lore for the helpers](#lore-for-the-helpers)): its searches (the prefetch and each lookup) with how many entries each found, the entries it was given with their tokens (and *cut to fit* when shortened), its lookups and whether they went as tools or the ask form, and why a lookup didn't happen. It shows only what you may know. A secret entry isn't there at all: not listed, and not counted in what a search found. An NPC's choice shows only whose choice it is (*Dev's choice*), never what they might do: it reads *private*, its lookups show as —, and nothing it found is counted or listed. A choice made only by people you haven't met or can't see isn't listed. A step that may read a secret shows its lookups as — with how many entries they found, never what it asked. Any other search that would name a secret, a secret entry, someone you haven't met or a place you don't know is withheld, unless you or the reply said those words that turn; a name counts as it does in a judged approach's reason (see [Lore for the helpers](#lore-for-the-helpers)), so someone counts by any word of their name, however short.

**Minds** lists the generation's minds calls ([Character minds](#character-minds)): whose mind it was, which step (a mind, a memory, a time skip's beats or consolidation), their level, when it ran (before the reply, after it, or in a time skip), whose prompt it used (the world's, yours or the built-in), and how it went, **recorded** or **failed**, with its seconds and tokens. Never a word of what a mind thought or said, nor whether the narrator got it: that is in **GM → Minds**. A call for someone you can't see or haven't met isn't listed, even when they are in the room with you.

With **Time-skip beats** turned on, its call before a time skip is listed there too, as the step alone: it thinks as no one, so it shows no one's name and no level.

### The People section

**People** tells the narrator who is in your scene ([The fidelity ladder](#the-fidelity-ladder) says what your scene takes in): your character first, by name, with any needs pressing on them from Cast life 4 ("Tommy Kyser (you): hungry, tired, lonely"), then each person there you can see, what they're doing when you may see it, and how they regard you. The Minds and Stance sections read your scene the same way.

### The Stance section

**Stance** tells the narrator how the people with you stand toward you and what they are about, in a line or two each. It lists only the people you can see in your scene: someone hidden from you gets no line, and no line names them.

- **What they know of you:** whether they know you (a **Knows** relation to you, or regard for you) and how they regard you, in your world's words for regard ("warm", "cold"), never numbers. From Relationships 3 it also names any side of their regard that differs from the whole: "regard: warm (trust: cold)".
- **What they are about:** what they are doing, as the People section says it; for each public agenda they have, its cue, or its current step as **Play → People** shows it; and, for someone who lives their own day, the part of today they chose themselves (an NPC choice), the one under way or the next: "Decided: See friends at 18:00".
- **Cues:** how they behave while a secret of theirs stays hidden, as the world's author wrote it: never the secret, only how it shows ("evasive about the cellar"). A cue comes from the current stage of a track they own, or from their own **Cues** in Build, and it goes once its secret is out.

The section opens with a short note for the narrator: it knows only what you know, these lines say how people behave, and it never says outright what a cue only hints at. A cue that would carry a secret you haven't found, or name someone or something you don't know, is left out on its own; the rest of the section stays.

Stance is the last section on the section budget, after the facts (Outcome, Scene, People, Money and the rest), so on a small budget it is the first one left out. Its cues come last of all: they go in only when the budget still has room once every section has its place, and otherwise Stance goes without them. The inspector's size for Stance, and the budget it shows as used, count Stance's people lines only, because the cues change with secrets you haven't found. To see it more often, raise **Engine budget (tokens)** in **Settings → Model use** (under **Advanced: budgets and the engine budget**), or give it a lower rank in the world's own sections. The engine's helpers (the brief, the outline, suggested choices and the contradiction check) don't read it: a cue is a hint for the narrator to play, not a fact to state.

### Your lore in world chats

LumiRPEngine doesn't manage World Info for now. In a world chat your own lore books work as they do anywhere else in Lumiverse: their entries activate by Lumiverse's own rules (keywords, constant entries, groups, recursion and the World Info budget), and the engine never switches one on or off. The Context inspector doesn't list them.

- The engine switches on only the sections it fills in, in the world's own book, and keeps every other entry there switched off, even one you switch on by hand.
- The sections share the section budget in rank order. The Outcome section is never left out.
- World Info recursion follows your own settings, so a section's text can trigger one of your entries, as any other entry's text can.
- If the engine stalls, the world's sections are missing from that turn's prompt (they stay switched off) and the Context inspector has no entry for it; a turn the engine couldn't plan in time is also marked **Not fully simulated**.
- The world's own lore (Build → Lore) is kept in the world. The engine's helpers read it ([Lore for the helpers](#lore-for-the-helpers)); the narrator doesn't get it yet.

### JSON blocks in replies

Some presets ask the AI to end every reply with a block of JSON between `<json>` and `</json>`, such as a tracker or a ledger. In a world chat LumiRPEngine keeps those blocks out of Lumiverse's World Info scanning and hides them in the chat, and the engine's own helpers read the reply without them. Your preset's own regex scripts still read every reply as the AI wrote it, so a preset that draws its block as a tracker keeps its tracker (LumiRPEngine hides only a block still showing as raw JSON, once every other display script has run), and one that strips old blocks from the prompt still finds them. The block itself stays exactly as the AI wrote it, so anything that reads it, such as the `{{jsonBlock}}` macro, still finds it. Regex scripts in the regex panel's LumiRPEngine folder do this, on new replies, older ones and edited ones: two in each world chat, and two global ones that change nothing but the wrapper LumiRPEngine saves around a block. To see the blocks, turn off **Hide JSON blocks in world chats** in **Settings → Preset** (under *Your preset*), which removes the hiding; World Info still skips the blocks. Turning it on again brings the hiding back, starting with the chat you have open. Deleting a world chat removes its scripts, and deleting a world removes its chats' scripts for good. Uninstalling LumiRPEngine leaves the scripts where they are, and they keep working together, so replies read as before; delete them all from the regex panel if you want them gone, and the saved replies then show their blocks, with World Info still skipping them.

### Your preset

The preset Lumiverse generates a world chat with has a say in it: the selected preset, else the one the chat's connection links, with any blocks a preset profile switches on or off for that chat. If Lumiverse can't say which preset that is, the engine stops before the reply and the chat says why. A block of it holding `{{outlet::lre_scene}}` (or `lre_outcome`, `lre_people` and so on) puts that section there instead of where the world places it; switched-off blocks named **RPEngine · extract**, **RPEngine · brief** and so on give that helper of the engine style and rules for your preset; and a switched-off **RPEngine · narrator** block gives the narrator rules in world chats only. Saving another preset changes nothing for the chat; saving this one between the engine's run and the reply makes the engine run again only when the save moves an outlet or changes a guidance block or the narrator rules. **Settings → Preset** (under *Your preset*) shows the **Active preset**, its **Outlets** and its **Guidance blocks** (each marked **Missing**, **Empty**, **Read** or **On**), and **Add RPEngine blocks to this preset**, which adds the missing guidance blocks, switched off and holding notes on what each is for, under an **RPEngine** category. [Presets and RPEngine](PRESETS.md) has every outlet, the guidance each helper takes, and examples.

### Delivery verification

Activation isn't delivery: an entry can be switched on and still not reach the prompt. Opening the Context inspector runs a **test prompt build**, a dry run that sends nothing and saves nothing, and searches it for each section of the latest generation by its heading (such as "[Scene]"): a section it finds is **Delivered**, and one it doesn't is **Not delivered**. Outcome, Montage and Outline come only from a real turn's plan, which a dry run doesn't make, so the test build leaves them **Unchecked**, as it does a section left out over the budget. The check tells you whether a section reached the prompt, not where in the prompt it landed.

Anything the engine inserts into the prompt, a name, a price, a number, a model's output, an Import or Refine proposal, has every brace replaced with a look-alike (｛ and ｝), its control characters removed (Lumiverse's macro engine turns some of them back into braces on a later pass) and its `<user>`, `<char>` and `<bot>` shorthands broken with look-alike angle brackets (＜ and ＞), so it can never form a Lumiverse macro, however many times it is processed. The same goes for the labels and the text of the sections and for the messages the engine writes into your chat. Your own authored lore is not changed: it keeps its macros, as it would in a native World Book (see [Import and export](#import-and-export) for reviewing a world someone else made).

## Authoring

You build worlds in **Build**, which works at phone width as well as on a desktop. Each page opens scrolled to the top, and each has a back button at its top left.

- **Worlds.** Build opens on your worlds. Each row shows the world's name and how many revisions it has, with a **This chat** badge on the world the open chat plays; tap it to open the world. Its **More for** *name* button (⋯) holds **Export** and **Delete**, and Delete asks first ("Delete “*name*” and all its revisions? Chats attached to it stop simulating."). An offer from LumiRPEngine Studio and the **Review before playing** card, when there are any, sit at the top (see [Import and export](#import-and-export)).
- **New world** opens a page of its own. Under **Start from**, pick **Blank** or one of the [templates](#templates), each with a line on what it is. The **Name** field takes the template's name until you type your own; then tap **Create world**. **Or bring one in** has **Import a file** (an lre-world-v1 file, as Export writes it) and **Import from LumiRPEngine Studio**.
- **The world bar.** An open world has a bar that stays at the top as you scroll: the back button, the world's name, the state of its draft (**Uncommitted** or **All committed**, with its error and warning counts) and **Commit**. Its **Draft actions** button (⋯) holds **Discard changes**. See [Revisions and Upgrade](#revisions-and-upgrade).
- **The world home** lists the world's parts, **World**, **Calendar**, **Realism defaults**, **Tiers**, **Time defaults**, **Ruleset**, **Lore lookup table**, **Minds** and **Routines and the story**, each with a line on what it holds and a badge for its issues. Then come its 19 collections in six groups: **People & things** (Entities, Kinds, Classifications, Relations), **Places** (Places, Maps, Routes, Grids), **Daily life** (Routines, Schedules), **Rules** (Actions, Checks, Rules), **Story** (Tracks, Lore) and **Engine** (Data tables, Sections, Step templates, Packs), each with a count and an issue badge. Last are its **Tools**: **Problems**, **Import from lore**, **Index a World Info book**, **Index this text**, **Build / Refine with AI**, **Describe a map**, **Propose routines from lore**, **Playtest**, **Check world** and, when the chat you have open isn't a world chat yet, **Attach to this chat**. A tool that has run shows how its last run went (**Running**, **Done**, **Failed**, **Cancelled** or **Interrupted**).
- **Collections** list their items by name, with a search box once there are more than eight. **New** asks for the name of the new item and makes its id from the name ("Old Miller Tom" becomes `old_miller_tom`, with `_2` added if that id is taken). You can change the id under **Advanced**, or in the open with **Show ids** on. **Continue** opens the new item's page, and **Create** adds it to the draft. Data tables have no New: new tables come from packs, and you open one to edit this world's copy of it.
- **Changing many at once:** **Entities** and **Places** have **Select**. Tap rows to pick them; the bar under the list has **Select all** (what the search shows), **Select a tier** (Entities: exactly that tier's entities) and **Clear**. **Change…** opens one change for everyone picked, saved to the draft at once: for entities, **Tier**, **Detail in one domain** (a level override, or **From the tier** to remove it) and **Pin lore**; for places, **Default scene type** (blank removes it; a place a map draws keeps the map's) and **Pin lore**. A pin is added where it isn't pinned already, from the world's lore or a World Info book. Only what changes is written.
- **An item's page** is headed with its name. **Save to draft** and **Revert** sit in a bar that stays at the bottom of the page, and a part's page has the same bar. Your first keystroke in a field counts as a change, so **Save to draft** lights up at once. Its **Item actions** button (⋯) holds **Delete**, which asks first and names the item ("Delete the entity “Rosie Alvarez”? References to it will show in Problems.").

Ids stay out of sight unless you turn on **Settings → Display → Show ids**: then each item's id shows under its name in the lists, and Problems and the tools show their paths, codes and keys.

For the formulas you write in requirements, checks, derived fields and rules, see [EXPRESSIONS.md](EXPRESSIONS.md); for every kind, field, effect, function and lint code the packs offer, the [reference](REFERENCE.md); and for small worlds to copy from, [RECIPES.md](RECIPES.md).

### Editors and lint

Every part of the world has an editor built from its definition: a page of sections, each field with its label over it, then its help, then any problem with it. A reference to something in the world is picked by name; the line under a choice names its pack, or its id when two share a name. Money is typed as the currency writes it ("$12.50"), a length of time like "1 h 30 min" (a rule's cooldown, a route's time, a scene's tick), and a moment of the world's clock with a date picker (a day number and a time on a calendar of your own). A list of things (effects, a relation's fields, appointments, trips, chores, conditions, bills, a kit's pieces) shows a row for each, which opens as a sheet, with buttons to add, reorder and remove; a list the editor knows no shape for stays JSON. A new entry in a keyed list asks for a name and takes its id from it. An optional field says "· optional". Some parts get visual editors: maps are drawn and grids painted (see [Maps](#maps)), a schedule's day is a bar of slots and a track's stages a graph (see [Schedules and tracks](#schedules-and-tracks)), a routine block's time is a bar across the day (see [Routines in Build](#routines-in-build)), and formulas have an **expression** editor.

What you can define:

- **Kinds**: types of things, with single inheritance and typed fields (number, text, yes/no, choice, reference, list, dice, money, date, duration), each with a default, limits, **bands** (numbers shown as words), **derived** formulas, a lock, a limit on model-proposed changes, and a **visibility**: public, known by particular characters, or secret. A field's **Min** and **Max**, **Enum values**, **Ref kind**, **List of** and **Bands** show only for the types they fit, or when they already hold a value.
- **Relations**: containment, ownership, use grants ("assigned to", loans, with an end time), worn or equipped, knowledge, membership, regard, routes, kinship and your own, with the kinds allowed at each end, cardinality, symmetry and an **inverse** (the relation the other side holds back). The relation picker lists the packs' relation types beside the world's own.
- **Entities**, **places** (with hierarchy and time zones), **routes** with travel modes, and optional **grids** with terrain. Entities, places, tracks and fields each have a **visibility**. **known_by** shows the thing to whoever knows it: a `knows` relation to it, or matching its **Known by**, a [selector](#selectors) (the members of a group, people of a kind or tier, chosen ids) that the editor shows beside a known_by visibility. A place can also be **discoverable**: hidden from the player until they have been there, are told of it or have it in `known_places`. A visibility the engine doesn't recognize, or a Known by it can't read in full, hides the thing rather than showing it, and lint says so. A point-of-view character with no place of its own starts at the world's start place, and from Space 3 arriving at a place drawn on a grid puts them on its cell. An entity's fields come grouped by realism domain, as its kind defines them; a derived field is shown, not edited, with a line saying what it reads; and a value its kind doesn't define sits under **Advanced: other values**. A relation's fields are typed by its relation type, and its **Until** is a moment of the world's clock.
- **Cues**, on a person: what the narrator sees them do while a secret of theirs stays hidden ("evasive about the cellar"), each standing for one secret: a secret lore entry (until its gate holds), a track (while it keeps something hidden: a truth not yet revealed, or the track itself) or a story flag (until it is set). Write how they act, never the secret; the stage cues of the tracks a person owns work the same way. They reach the narrator in the [Stance section](#the-stance-section).
- **Maps**: the inside of a place, drawn on a grid or as linked places, which make that place's places, grid and routes (see [Maps](#maps)).
- **Actions**: a label and a **Description** (a line on what the action does: the helper that reads typed messages is told it, so it knows when your words mean this action), parameters, requirements, an optional dice **check** with outcome bands (the odds shown to players are computed exactly from it), effects per outcome, a duration, and the activity it starts. A ref parameter can say **Only candidates matching** a [selector](#selectors): the Actions sheet offers only those, and typed text or a request naming anyone else is refused.
- **Rules**: "when this happens and that is true, do this", with run-once and cooldowns. A cascade stops at eight steps.
- **Tracks**: quests, secrets (revealed by conditions only), puzzles with attempts and a hint ladder, and NPC agendas.
- **Routines**: how people's days usually go, owner by owner (everyone's, a group's, a kind's, a selector's or someone's own), as blocks at a set time, within a window or optional, each resting on what you wrote, a lore entry or a sheet field (see [Routines in Build](#routines-in-build)).
- **Schedules**: calendar recurrence plus tables of fixed values or weighted choices, for shifts, timetables, opening hours, weather, bills, paydays and price paths. A schedule's owner is an entity, a group (its members), a kind, or a [selector](#selectors). When several set the same field of the same person, the most specific owner wins while its slot runs: their own schedule, then a group's, then their kind's, then a selector's; when the more specific slot ends, the field returns to the less specific one still running.
- **Lore**: what the world knows, as entries with a title, text, keywords, aliases, topics and links, where each came from, and secrets with a gate and the helpers that may read them, plus a lookup table of terms and topics (see [World lore](#world-lore)). It is kept in the world and the engine's helpers read it; the narrator gets no lore section.
- The **calendar** (Gregorian or your own) and **realism** defaults, with the band caps under the band labels.
- **Tiers**: the world's own ranks of importance, on the **Tiers** part. Each has a **Label**, a **Band** (Leads, Supporting or Background), a place in the order (its move up and move down buttons), an optional description, and whether its people live their own day (agendas and plans) by default. Type a name under **New tier's name** and tap **Add tier** to make a new one (its id comes from the name, under **Advanced**); a tier still in use can't be removed. **New people join** picks the tier for people the story introduces (by default the first Background tier). An entity's editor picks its tier from these, and its **Lives their own day** choice can follow the tier ("As its tier"); lint flags an entity whose tier the world doesn't have.
- **Section templates**: how a section's lines are wrapped when they go into the prompt, and where they go. A template may use only the engine's `{{lines}}` marker; any other macro, control character or `<user>`-style shorthand in one is a lint error (`section_macro`), because it would run beside the engine's own values. Stance's template opens with its note to the narrator, which you can reword there. A world with no sections of its own gets the shipped ones; once it lists its own, it gets only those, so list `stance` to keep it. **Position** is `{"type":"chat_depth","role":"system","depth":1,"direction":"from_end"}` (that many messages from the end of the chat, or from its start), `{"type":"outlet","name":"…"}` (only where a `{{outlet::…}}` macro of that name stands, in your preset or a card, and nowhere else) or `{"type":"native"}` (where the section's entry sits in the world's book, which you can move in Lumiverse's World Info editor; a new one starts before the character). When you change a section's Position and commit, its entry in the world's book moves to match.
- **What a section is fed**: each section in **Build → Sections** also has **In the prompt** (off leaves it out), **Whose knowledge**, **At most lines** (its first lines), **At most tokens** (lines past it are cut from the end) and **Sources** (the renderers that fill it; none, its own). A section with none of these set goes in as it always has. See [What the narrator is fed](#what-the-narrator-is-fed).
- **Step templates**: your own wording for a model step, per purpose (interpret, NPC choices, contradiction, puzzle answers, judging your approach, brief, outline, extract, suggested choices), with an output (text, decision, or `effects` for the steps that read changes, which run after the reply only) and the signals it runs on (a template's own signals replace the step's). A player's own prompt for the step (**Settings → Steps and connections**) wins over it. The brief, outline, extract and suggested-choices templates, and every `effects` template, can use engine placeholders: `{{world}}`, `{{pov}}`, `{{place}}`, `{{clock}}`, `{{scene}}`, `{{present}}`, `{{facts}}`, `{{outcome}}`, `{{beats}}`, `{{brief}}`, `{{user}}`, `{{reply}}`, `{{recent}}`, `{{gear}}` (what your character carries and wears), `{{money}}` (your character's money), `{{property}}` (what your character owns or may use), `{{positions}}` (who is here with your character, and what they're doing) and `{{knowledge}}` (what your character knows of the quests); each of the last five is what the section that tells it has now, empty in a world without that section. Two more go only to some steps: `{{usual}}`, the usual routines of the people with you, in extract, suggested-choices and `effects` templates, and `{{sections}}`, the sections the narrator has this turn, in an outline template. An interpret template fills in only `{{world}}` and `{{pov}}`, and none at all on a Decision connection; NPC-choice, puzzle-answer and judge templates, and a contradiction template with a decision output, are sent as you wrote them, with nothing filled in. The template's page lists the placeholders its purpose is given under **Placeholders**. `{{brief}}` is this turn's brief (an outline template that uses it waits for the brief; it's empty in the brief step itself), and `{{recent}}` is the Journal's last five lines before this turn, where each ✎ fix or GM edit you can see takes a line of its own. They are all player-visible: a line that would reveal an unrevealed truth is dropped before the call. A template whose output can't be used by its purpose, an unknown signal and a placeholder its step isn't given are lint warnings, and so are a blank template (it replaces the built-in instructions with none) and a contradiction template whose output isn't a decision (it replaces the built-in contradiction check without being one, so no reply is checked). Changes a step template proposes with `effects` are checked and bounded exactly like extraction, and money in them is stated as a plain amount in the world's currency (8 for $8), which the engine converts. Which steps run at all is still your choice, in **Settings → Steps and connections**. A world made with an earlier version keeps its step templates, except those for lore selection, which no longer exists.
- **Step templates for minds**: the four minds steps (Character minds, Character memory, Time-skip beats, Memory consolidation) take templates too, with output **text**. They can use `{{character}}`, `{{world}}`, `{{pov}}`, `{{place}}` and `{{clock}}`, and the engine always adds the answer format after them. Time-skip beats think for no one, so there `{{character}}` is empty, `{{place}}` is where your character is and `{{clock}}` is when the skip starts. A minds template runs on the minds' own cadence, so its signals are ignored, and only the first template of a step (by id) with any text is used, a blank one being skipped; lint says so for each. **Build → World → Minds** lists them and starts new ones from the built-in prompts (see [Character minds in a world](#character-minds-in-a-world)).

Formulas use a small, safe expression language: numbers, comparisons and a fixed set of functions. They can't run code and they always produce a finite number. `table(id, key, column)` reads a cell of one of the world's data tables, and `table_step(id, value, column)` reads the row whose number key is the largest at or below `value` (a level table keyed by XP, say). `roll(notation)` works only in an effect's arguments (a value that starts with `=`): the effect's event records the result, so a replay or a swipe sees the same number. Anywhere else a roll would change the odds you were shown and what the engine reads, so it is a lint error and reads as nothing. For classifications and groups: `kind_is(e, 'person')` (a kind or one inheriting from it), `in_class(e, 'role', 'antagonist')` (a value or one nested under it), `has_tag(e, 'smuggler')`, `member_of(e, harlows)` (directly or through a member group), `tier_of(e)` and `band_of(e)` (the band its tier belongs to).

In the **expression editor**, the **Operators and functions** button (ƒx) opens the operators in groups (**Arithmetic**, **Compare**, **Logic**) and every function a formula can call there: the math built-ins, the engine's own and the formulas of the packs the engine runs, taken from the engine itself, so the list never names one that doesn't exist. It also lists the **Names** the formula can read, by what the world calls them (their ids show with **Show ids** on), with find boxes for long lists. On a desktop these open under the formula; on a phone, where they wouldn't fit, ƒx opens the formula on a page of its own, and the **Open the formula editor** button opens that page at any width (**Done** closes it). As you type, chips suggest the ids, fields and functions that start with what you typed, and the names every formula can read (`self`, `pov`, `here`, `clock`, `hour`, `daypart` and the rest of the clock); tap one to put it in. Under every formula a line says in plain words what it reads and uses ("Reads Sam's cash and the hour; uses min."). An action's **Effects per outcome** are a list of effects for each outcome, with the check's outcomes suggested. An effect's **arguments** are edited one by one: each is a **Value** (`self`, `might`, `2`, or JSON for a list or an object) or a **Formula**, worked out when the effect runs; **Add argument** adds one by name, and its remove button takes it away. Only an argument's formula offers `roll(`.

### Schedules and tracks

**Schedules.** A schedule's page starts with **Who and what**: **It applies to** either **Someone, a group or a kind**, picked by name under **Owner** (or **No one: a world process (weather, prices)**), or **A selector**; and **It sets**, the field or relation each slot's value sets (place, activity, weather, price…). **Repeats** is **Daily**, **Weekly** (tap the weekdays), **Monthly** (tap the days of the month) or **Yearly**, with **Not on** chips for the calendar's holidays and **From** and **Until** dates. **The day** draws the slots on a bar across the 24 hours. Tap a slot, on the bar or in the list under it, to open its sheet; drag a handle on the bar to move where one slot ends or the next starts (arrow keys move a focused handle by 15 minutes). The **Slot** button adds one. A slot's sheet has **Starts** and **Ends** (an end at or before the start runs past midnight), **Its value**, either **Fixed** or a **Weighted choice** (each **Choice** with its **Weight** and its share), **Given the slot before (Advanced)**, and **Remove slot**. A slot with bad times, no value or weights that add up to nothing, and slots that overlap, are marked **Fix**, and **Save to draft** says how many there are ("Save to draft · 2 to fix").

**Tracks.** A track's page starts with **The track**: **Label**, **Kind** (**Quest**, **Secret**, **Puzzle** or **Agenda**), **Who knows of it** (**Everyone**, **Those who know**, with a **Known by** selector, or **Secret**), **Whose it is**, picked by name, and **Checks roll while it's in progress**. **Stages** shows the stages as a **List** or a **Graph** (**Show the stages as**); a phone starts on the list and a desktop on the graph, which runs from the start at the top down. The start is marked **Start**, a stage nothing leads to says **Can't be reached** (a banner counts them), and on the graph a dashed red way is how a stage fails. Tap a stage to open its sheet:

- its fields: the label, the cue the player sees, the secret truth, objectives, a gate, hints, the effects on entering, rewards and a puzzle's answer;
- **Make it the start** (the start says **Where the track starts** instead);
- **Next stages**: each **Goes to** a stage picked by name, **When** its condition holds, and the first that holds wins. The move buttons reorder them, **Open** *stage* opens the one it goes to, and **+ Next stage…** adds one;
- **Failing**: **It can fail**, then **It fails when** and **Then goes to**;
- **Delete stage**, which waits until another stage is the start.

The **Stage** button asks for the new stage's **Name** and what it **Comes after** (it is added there as a next stage, with "true" as its condition until you change it), then **Add the stage**.

On both pages the **Item actions** button (⋯) holds **Delete**, **JSON (Advanced)** shows the whole schedule or track as the world file keeps it, and **Save to draft** and **Revert** sit in the bar at the bottom.

### Routines in Build

A **routine** is how someone's days usually go: when they open the shop, eat with the family or go to the gym. It is something the world can rely on, never a rule, and it never outranks the story. What it is to the engine is the role **Routines and the story** gives each level (below): by default it only informs Live, Near and Far characters, as context their mind and the narrator may weigh while the story and planning decide what they do, and Background characters keep to theirs. Whatever the role, a routine never moves anyone out of a scene with your character, contradicts what's been told or interrupts play, and a chat changes anyone's routine as it goes, through the story or the GM.

**Where a character's day comes from**, most binding first: what the story has established (where they are, what they're doing, a trip, a plan under way); their commitments (schedules such as shifts and school, appointments, trips); the plans the story or the GM gave them; their routine; and their basic needs, sleep and meals. A character with no routine has the rest of that list, and nothing more: no outings, visits or invitations are made up for them.

**Build → Daily life → Routines** lists the routines by whom they're for, the most specific first: **Someone's own**, **A group's** (a family, a household, a crew), **A kind's**, **By selector** and **Everyone's** (the world's default). Where routines set the same block for someone, the most specific wins: their own, then a group's, a kind's, a selector's, then everyone's. **Routines and the story**, at the top, opens the settings. **New** names a new routine, as in any collection; a routine without a name goes by whom it's for.

A routine's page has:

- **Whose routine**: **It's for** picks the level (**Someone**, **Group**, **Kind**, **Selector** or **Everyone**), then who, and an optional **Name**.
- **Their days**, for anyone but everyone: **Have a routine** or **No routine**. With no routine, their days come only from the story, the GM, their commitments and their basic needs, and no broader routine applies to them. With a routine, **Keeps the blocks of broader routines** says whether a group's, a kind's and everyone's blocks still apply where this one doesn't set the same block.
- **Blocks**, in the order of the day, each with its kind, its time, its place and where it comes from. The **Block** button adds one, and **Change a basic need or a broader block…** copies sleep, a meal or another routine's block in, to change it for whom this routine covers. Tap a block to open its sheet.
- **Turned off here**: blocks that don't apply to whom this routine covers, such as breakfast for a night owl. **Turn off a block…** adds one, and its undo button puts it back.
- **Where this routine comes from**: what its blocks rest on unless a block names its own.

A block's sheet has:

- **What it reads as** ("Opens the shop") and **What they're doing**: sleep, a meal, work, socialize, leisure or the world's own activity.
- **When it happens**: **At a set time** (the same time on each day it's on), **Within a window** (somewhere in its window, wherever the day has room; **How often** can make it **A few times a week**, with **Times a week**) or **Optional** (only likely, with **How likely**: **Rarely**, **Sometimes**, **Often** or **Usually**, and the first thing a busier day leaves out). The bar across the day shows when it runs, or for a window when it may start: drag either end, or type the times. **How long** takes a length such as "1 h 30 min".
- **How much it matters**, **Least** to **Most**: when blocks want the same free time, the one that matters more goes first.
- **Where**: **At home**, **At work**, **Wherever they are**, **At the home of whoever they're with**, **A chosen place**, or **The nearest place of a type**.
- **Who with**: a [selector](#selectors) that names people or groups, and **Asks them along**, which makes the block an invitation, made only as **Routines and the story** allows.
- **Which days**: weekdays, days off, holidays, birthdays (their own or a friend's), seasons and weather; with none set, every day.
- **Needs and privacy**: the needs it meets, and **Private**, which shows it to the player only as "Busy".
- **Where it comes from**: **Written here**, **From lore** (an entry picked by its title, with **The words it rests on**; the entry's row opens it) or **From a sheet** (whose sheet, and which field). The list shows each block's source too.
- **Its id (Advanced)**: a block with the id of a basic need (`sleep`, `breakfast`, `lunch`, `dinner`) or of a broader routine's block replaces it. A block added on the page takes its id from what it reads as.
- **Remove block**.

**Save to draft** (**Create routine** for a new one) and **Revert** sit in the bar at the bottom, and the **Item actions** button (⋯) holds **Delete**. A routine's problems show on its page, and **Problems** opens a block's problem on that block's sheet.

**Routines and the story** (in **World**, and at the top of Routines) tunes what routines are to the engine, and how aware they are of the story and its lore:

- **What a routine is, by closeness**: for **Live** (in the scene with you), **Near** (close to the story), **Far** (off screen, still followed) and **Background** (everyone else), **Informative** (the story and planning decide what they do; their routine is only context their mind and the narrator may weigh; the default for all but Background), **Enforced** (their routine and schedules run their day when the story doesn't, and never pull them out of a scene with you; Background's default) or **Off** (not used at all, not even as context). The GM can set it apart for one character in a chat. The world's schedules (weather, prices, bills, paydays, opening hours) and pay run whatever you pick.
- **In a time skip**: for each level, how those whose routine is informative spend a time skip: **Stay put**, **Follow their routine** (Background's default) or **Planned by the model** (the default for Live, Near and Far; the model plans everyone it covers at the skip's start). A level whose routine is enforced or off says it applies only to characters the GM makes informative. The GM can set it apart for one character in a chat ([Routines and schedules](#routines-and-schedules)). **Plan a skip** picks how the model is asked: **All in one call** (the default, and cheaper) or **One call per person, at once**, which keeps each answer small for a large cast or a long skip; if one person's call fails, the others are still planned and the GM's note says how many weren't.
- **The narrator**: **Tell the narrator people's usual routines** (on by default), for the characters in the scene whose routine isn't off, as context it may use, never what they must do ([Routines and schedules](#routines-and-schedules)).
- **Timelapse from the Actions sheet**: **Follow my routine by default** (off by default), where the Timelapse sheet's **Follow my routine** starts ([Timelapse](#timelapse)).
- **Story and lore awareness**, for those whose routine is enforced: **Story first** (enforced routines bend for the story's plans and the GM's, and what the story sets holds until it's released), **Balanced**, the default (Background characters keep their enforced routine, others bend it for the story's plans, and what the story sets holds for the rest of that day), or **Routine first** (enforced routines hold, bending only as **How plans bend routine** allows, and what the story sets holds until their next block). The GM can set it apart for one character in a chat.
- **Invitations**: whom routine blocks may invite: **Off**, **To the player only** (the default) or **Between everyone**. A block invites only when it names its company and asks them along, so nobody is invited out of nowhere; plans from the story and the GM's invitations need no setting.
- **Sociability**: **Reserved**, **Usual** or **Outgoing**, how fast the wish for company and fun grows.
- **How plans bend routine**: which plans made in the story may move work or school: **Only plans that must happen**, **Strong plans**, **Strong plans, and set-time plans for the cast** (the default) or **Strong plans, and set-time plans for anyone**.

**Save to draft** saves them all, and **Use the defaults** takes the world's own settings out again: a world never tuned keeps none.

### Maps

A **map** draws the inside of one place, its `scope`: a manor's floor as rooms on a grid, or a town as places linked by routes. It is a small JSON document in the **lre-map-v1** format (its JSON Schema is `src/shared/lre-map-v1.schema.json` in the source), which you draw in the map editor, write as JSON there, or describe in your own words for the AI to write (see [Describe a map](#describe-a-map)). A map makes its places (inside its scope), its grid and its routes for you, and keeps them in step with it: they show in the Places, Grids and Routes lists as the map's, read-only, with **Edit in map** to change them where they come from. Delete the map and they go with it. A place, grid or route you made yourself always wins: a map that would make one with the same id leaves yours alone, and the lint says so.

A **grid map** is rows of characters, one per square, all the same length. The `legend` says what a character means: a terrain (`{"terrain": "grass"}`, with `"blocked": true` for one you can't cross), a place's area (`{"place": "kitchen"}`), a zone (`{"zone": "cellar"}`) or a thing lying on that square (`{"item": "lantern"}`, which must be a thing of the world on one square only, and not inside something; add `"place"` to say which room's floor it is, otherwise the room around it). Without a legend entry, `#` is a wall, `.` floor, `+` a door and a space nothing. A place stands at its `at` square, or in the middle of its area. `cellMeters` says how many metres a square is across (80 when you leave it out), for walking times.

```json
{
  "id": "manor_ground",
  "scope": "manor",
  "title": "Ground floor",
  "layout": [
    "#############",
    "#ppppp#kkkkk#",
    "#ppppp+kkkkk#",
    "#ppppp#kkkkk#",
    "###+#####+###",
    "#hhhhhhhhhhh#",
    "#####+#######"
  ],
  "legend": {
    "p": { "place": "parlour" },
    "k": { "place": "kitchen" },
    "h": { "place": "hallway" }
  },
  "places": [
    { "id": "parlour", "name": "Parlour" },
    { "id": "kitchen", "name": "Kitchen" },
    { "id": "hallway", "name": "Hallway" }
  ]
}
```

A **node map** sets its places in a frame of `size` by their `at` (leave it out and the place goes on a ring), and its `links` are the routes between them, each with its `minutes` and, if you like, its `mode` (`walk` unless given, or `road`, `offroad` or `water`). Link every place on the map or none, because once a place's inside has links, travel there follows only them.

```json
{
  "id": "old_town",
  "scope": "town",
  "title": "Old town",
  "size": [100, 100],
  "places": [
    { "id": "square", "name": "Market square", "at": [50, 50] },
    { "id": "inn", "name": "The Gull Inn", "at": [25, 30] },
    { "id": "docks", "name": "Docks", "at": [80, 75] }
  ],
  "links": [
    { "from": "square", "to": "inn", "minutes": 3 },
    { "from": "square", "to": "docks", "minutes": 6 }
  ]
}
```

Without the grid system, walls and doors are drawn, not walked: travel inside a place follows its routes, or, with none, a short walk between any two of its places.

The world's own map settings are in its **World** part in Build. **Display** is how a new chat's map starts out, **Known** or **Fog** (players can switch it on their map). **Grid movement** turns on the **grid system**: Space and travel is at least level 3 in every chat of the world, and on a grid, people stand on squares. **Travel simulation: moves need a route** is off by default, so moves are story first: a move the reply tells always lands, and **Go to** goes anywhere, walking where it can, else at the time a road or a boat would take, else in 15 minutes. On, a move the reply tells across a gap no route spans didn't happen, and **Go to** needs a walking route. Grid steps keep their walls either way, and nobody's routine changes. **✎** on your place moves you there as a real move: you leave what you were doing, and on a map you stand where you arrive.

- Arriving at a place puts you on its square, or else on a door at the edge of the map, and you walk from there with **Walk here** on the map or the **Move on the map** action, around walls and through doors.
- A floor plan without `links` gets walking routes through its doors, between rooms a walk joins without crossing a third room, so **Go to** the kitchen walks there in the time the walk takes. A room no door leads to gets a warning.
- **Sight radius** (3 squares unless you change it) is how far you see; walls block it, and Fog shows only the squares you've seen.
- A thing drawn in the legend starts on its square. To take it, wear it or use it there, you have to be on its square or next to it: rules and actions can ask the same with `reach(self, thing)`, and `on_grid(thing)` says whether it lies on a square.
- A rule can fire when someone steps into a zone or onto a square: `{"zone": "cellar", "in": "manor"}` or `{"cell": [4, 2], "in": "manor"}` (every room's area is also a zone named after the room). It fires for you unless `who` picks others (a selector, like `{"kind": ["guard"]}`), once each time they step in, never while they stay.

The **map editor** (open a map from **Maps**, or **New**) has four tabs.

- **Draw.** A new, empty map asks **What kind of map?**: **Grid map** or **Node map**, each starting from a small example you can change. On a grid map the tools are **Paint** (drag to paint with the brush), **Fill** (tap a cell to fill the area around it) and **Move** (drag a place to its cell). Pick the brush from the chips: wall, floor, door and nothing, then the legend's own. **Room** and **Brush** add a room or a legend entry, and **Edit brush** opens the one you're painting with. On a node map the tools are **Move** (drag a place; tap one to select it), **Link** (drag from one place to another to join them with a way) and **Add** (tap where the new place goes). Two fingers pan and zoom. **Undo** takes back the last edit.
- **Rooms** (**Places** on a node map) lists the map's places, then its **Legend** on a grid map or its **Ways** on a node map, and the **Map** with its **Edit** button. Each opens a sheet: **Map settings** (**Title**, **Draws the inside of**, the size in cells and **Cell size (metres)**, or **A fixed frame** for a node map), a place (its name, its cell or position, its room and the brush that paints it, **Its inside is drawn by**, and its id under **Advanced**), a brush (what **It paints**: wall, floor, door, terrain, a room, a zone or a thing lying there) or a way (**From**, **To**, **Minutes**, **By** and **Both ways**).
- **Preview** draws the map as Play will show it, every place shown, with each problem marked on it: a solid mark for an error, a dashed one for a warning.
- **JSON** is the map as lre-map-v1 JSON, with **Grid template**, **Node template** and **Tidy**, which lays the JSON out a field per line, and line numbers marked where the problems are. Every edit in the other tabs rewrites the JSON, laid out as Tidy lays it out.

The editor checks the map as you edit, against the format and against the rest of the world. Above the tabs it says "A valid lre-map-v1 map." or counts the errors and warnings, which open into the list: rows of different lengths, a character the legend doesn't know, a place outside the grid, a link to a place that doesn't exist, an id another place already has. Tap a problem to open the place, way or brush it is about, or its line in the JSON tab. The **Item actions** button (⋯) holds **Start from a grid template**, **Start from a node template** and **Delete**; a template asks before it replaces a map you have drawn. **Save to draft** (**Create map** for a new one) and **Revert** sit in the bar at the bottom.

A **grid** that no map draws has a page of its own: **Size** (**Width (cells)**, **Height (cells)** and **Cell size (metres)**) and **Paint**, with the tools **Pan**, **Terrain**, **Blocked** and **Zone**, the terrain and zone chips to paint with, **Add zone** for a new zone, and **Undo**. Its **Item actions** button holds **Delete**, and **JSON (Advanced)** shows the grid. A place that sits on its parent's grid has an **On** *parent*'s **grid** card on its page, above the bar at the bottom: tap a cell to put it there.

### Rulesets and profiles

**Build → Ruleset** is a list of the ruleset's parts: **Start from a profile…**, **How checks are decided**, **Level floors**, **Progression**, **Sheet Designer**, **Combat** and **JSON (Advanced)**. Each row says how that part stands and counts its issues, and each opens its own sheet, with **Save to draft** and **Revert** at the bottom.

A profile is a ready-made ruleset to start from; once applied, everything it added is yours to edit. Tap **Start from a profile…** and pick one:

- **Modern**: present-day adventure on a d20. Six abilities (10 is average) with modifiers of (score − 10) ÷ 2 rounded down; **Defense** of 10 + Dexterity + a level bonus + an equipment bonus; twelve skills with ranks (Athletics, Drive, Stealth, Computers, Investigate, Repair, Notice, Read People, First Aid, Persuade, Deceive, Intimidate); Fortitude, Reflex and Will; **Action Points**, which **Push a skill** spends to add 1d6 to the roll and each new level refills; HP from Constitution; Reputation; and levels at 0, 1,000, 3,000, 6,000 XP and so on.
- **Classic**: classic fantasy on a d20. Base attack on a good, average or poor progression and saves on good or poor ones (each character's progressions are fields), thirteen skills with ranks, Armor Class, HP from a hit die, and the same XP scale.
- **Fifth**: heroic fantasy on a d20. A proficiency bonus by level (+2 to +6), skills at proficiency 0, 1 or 2 (2 is expertise, twice the bonus), two proficient saves, Armor Class, **Dodge** (attacks on you roll with disadvantage until your next turn), and hit dice: a **Short rest** spends one to heal, a **Long rest** heals fully and regains half. A well-told approach earns advantage rather than a bonus.
- **Custom**: the least to grow from: one stat (Skill), one check (**Try something**), an HP pool whose max is a formula, a plain attack, and a progression with level 1 only.

Every d20 profile has **Use a skill** and **Make a save**, with one option per skill or save in the Actions sheet and its exact odds; the difficulty is moderate (10) unless the action is given easy, hard, very hard or a number. In an attack a natural 1 always misses and a natural 20 is a critical (the damage dice roll twice). **Total defense** (Dodge in Fifth) lasts until your next turn, and **Rest for the night** heals. Each profile also brings Balanced checks, a Sheets 3 floor so modifiers count under any realism, a sheet for **Play → Me → You** and combat rules; the focus stays on skills and talking your way through, and fights are there when the story needs them.

**What applying does.** The picker previews the profile on your draft: the fields it adds to your character's kind (in the starters, Character), and the checks, actions, data tables and ruleset parts it adds. Anything already in the world with the same id and different content is listed by name (its path shows with **Show ids** on) with **Keep mine** (the default) or **Take the profile's**, and items that already match are counted. A kind that overrides one of those fields with its own definition keeps it. Everyone of that kind reads the new fields' defaults; nobody's stored values change unless a new field can't hold one (a level of 0 under a minimum of 1), and the preview lists those. **Apply to draft** writes it all at once and lint runs as usual; applying the same profile again changes nothing ("Nothing to apply"). Save or revert your own ruleset edits first.

**Adapting it.** A profile is ordinary data, so you change it like anything else:
- **Level your own way.** The XP thresholds are a column of the profile's level table (Modern levels, Classic levels, Fifth levels) or a list in **Progression**; change them, set a **Top level**, or add effects **On each level gained** (Modern refills Action Points there, Fifth adds a hit die). Custom starts at level 1 only: give it thresholds such as "0, 100, 250".
- **XP trackers.** XP is a number field. Add it with the `award` effect from quest rewards, track stages, rules (one on `combat.end` rewards a fight) or action outcomes, or correct it with ✎ on the sheet; crossing a threshold levels up. A second progression on other fields (milestones, renown) runs beside the first.
- **Skills and saves** are rows of the profile's skills and saves tables: add a row (its label and the modifier field it adds) and a number field of the same id for the ranks, and **Use a skill** offers it. Any action can do the same: a text parameter with a `table` offers that table's rows.
- **Everything else** is a formula or a field: Defense or max HP, a check's bands, each character's `damage` dice, the action economy, the sheet.

The profiles are original implementations of common tabletop mechanics: neutral names and numbers, no rules text.

### Checks, pools, progression and sheets

These are the pieces a tabletop ruleset is built from. They work the same whatever ruleset you write.

- **Checks.** A check is dice plus outcome bands. A band can test the total (`min`, `max`), the **natural** die (`natural: {min: 20}`: the face of the first die when the dice start with a single kept die, such as `d20` or `2d20kh1`), and the **margin** over a **DC** (`margin: {min: 5}`, the total minus the DC). Natural bands are tried first. A check with a `dc` and no bands has two outcomes: `failure` below the DC and `success` at or above it. **Advantage** is a formula that gives 1, 0 or −1: it adds a die to the natural die and keeps the higher (`d20` becomes `2d20kh1`) or the lower, and advantage and disadvantage cancel a die at a time. An **opposed** check names its opponent with `vs` (by default the action's `target`): the opponent rolls another check (`vs.check`), this check's dice with their own modifier (`vs.modifier`), this same check (neither), or doesn't roll and has a **passive** score that becomes the DC (`vs.passive`). Whatever you write, the odds in the Actions sheet stay exact and are the real ones; the sheet says "with advantage" and the DC beside them. An outcome's effects can read the roll as `roll.natural`, `roll.total`, `roll.modifier`, `roll.dc`, `roll.margin` and `roll.outcome`.
- **Anyone can roll.** The `check` effect (`{check, actor, target?, outcomes}`) lets a rule or another effect have someone else roll, such as a guard noticing you. Their roll never moves yours, and it is recorded for Check world and the GM.
- **What counts as success.** A check can list **Outcomes that count as success** (`pass`; by default `success` and every band after it) and its **Outcome when not rolled** (`auto`). [How checks are decided](#how-checks-are-decided) reads both.
- **Pools.** Give a number field a `pool` (`max`: another field, often a derived one, or a formula) for hit points, mana or stress. Effects keep it between its `min` (0 unless set) and its max, a pool nobody has set yet is full, and the `recover` effect restores it by an amount, by the pool's own `recover` formula, or to the max. A pool is still a field, so undo, swipes and Keep treat it like any other.
- **Progression.** In **Build → Ruleset**, **Progression** lists the ruleset's progressions. Type a name under **New progression** and tap **Add**; tap a progression to open it in its own sheet. A progression has a **Label** (the sheet and the Journal show it), the kind it **Applies to** (or any kind with both fields), an **XP field** and a **Level field** picked from that kind's number fields (derived fields aren't offered, since the engine sets the level), and the XP each level needs, from level 1 (usually 0), from **A list** ("0, 300, 900, 2700") or **A data table**'s column, read in row order. A **Top level** is optional. **On each level gained** is a list of effects, edited like a rule's, and **Remove progression** deletes it. Whenever an effect, a quest reward, a narrated change or a ✎ fix raises someone's XP past a threshold, the engine raises their level one level at a time and runs those effects for each, with `level` (the new level) and `from` (the level before the gain); the Journal says "You reached Level 3." for your character. The `award` effect (`{entity, progression, amount}`) adds XP. Levels only go up.
- **The sheet.** The ruleset's sheet lays out **Play → Me → You**: sections of rows, each a field, a formula with a label, a pool (shown as a bar of current over max) or a progression (the level, with a bar of XP toward the next level). Without one, You shows the Sheets pack's stats as before. Lay it out in the **Sheet Designer**. The **Section** button adds a section, its **Section title** renames it, and its move up, move down and remove buttons order or remove it. **+ Add a row…** picks what a row shows: **A formula with a label**, a pool, a progression, or one of your character's fields (inherited and derived ones too). Each row opens its own page, with what it shows, its label (a formula is written in the expression editor with your character as `self`) and **Remove row**, and its move up and move down buttons order it. **Preview**, at the bottom of the Sheet Designer, shows the sheet as your character will see it when a new chat starts, from the ruleset as you edit it, before you save.
- **Level floors.** A ruleset's math may need more than the realism settings give: stat modifiers count only from Sheets 3. **Level floors** (for example Sheets 3) raise every character's effective level in that domain to at least the floor, whatever the preset, tier caps or overrides say. The world's **Realism defaults** in Build name the floors ("This world's ruleset needs Sheets & checks 3.") and don't offer a level below one, and **GM → This chat → Realism** shows the same line, each domain at the level actually in force, and starts that domain's slider at its floor. Lint notes when the world's realism defaults sit below a floor.

**Lint** runs as you edit and speaks plainly. It catches broken references, wrong types, cycles, unreachable quest stages, quest conditions that name a thing, a person or a place the world doesn't have (a typo'd id in `carries`, `at` or `knows`, which never holds), sections that are too big, labels that leak secrets, secret truth in public lore, secret lore without a gate or readable by a step that writes prose, a lore entry whose **Secret** is neither on nor off in its file (such as `"true"` in quotes; it is kept secret until fixed), cues that give their secret away, name someone or something the player may not know (they are left out while the player doesn't) or can never show (no secret, two, or one that hides nothing), lookup-table terms and topics that lead to missing entries, lead nowhere or contradict an alias, a section template that holds a macro other than `{{lines}}`, step templates that could never run, unbalanced postings, double-booked commitments (two schedules setting the same field of the same people at once, with the same precedence), classification values that don't exist or nest in a loop, several values on a one-value classification, relations between kinds their type doesn't allow or beyond its cardinality, kinship written on one side only, conditions that depend on something frozen after a demotion, a Timelapse cap smaller than one time slot, rules for checks that hold a value they can't (each plays as the nearest it can), a sure thing that doesn't start above too hard to try, checks that roll only where marked with nothing marked, checks whose success the rules can't tell, and problems with the active preset (see [Troubleshooting](#troubleshooting)).

### Combat and encounters

Fights are part of a ruleset, and only a world whose ruleset has **Combat** (**Build → Ruleset → Combat**) has any: without it there is no Fight, no Combat tab in the Actions sheet and nothing changes. Combat is all data, so a Modern, Classic or Fifth-style fight is the same engine with different numbers.

- **The rules.** The **initiative** check everyone rolls when a fight starts (highest first; equal totals go by the initiative modifier, or the **tie-break** formula, then you, then your side, then id order); the **action economy**, budgets such as Action, Bonus, Move or Reaction with how many each fighter gets and whether they refill each turn or each round (a reaction is spent off your own turn, so it never keeps your turn open); the **HP field**, a pool; when someone is **down** (by default at 0 HP) and the condition they then have (it goes on the character sheet through the Sheets pack); the **sides** (who is hostile to whom, by default a `hostile` relation, and who fights beside whom); when the fight is **over** (by default when one side has no one standing); after how many rounds in a row with no attack and no one hurt it **peters out** (3 by default; 0 never); whether your turn ends once nothing you could do fits what's left (the default) or only on **End turn**; everyone else's **tactics** (whom they attack first, when they defend); and whether others' HP shows as **wounds** ("hurt", "badly hurt"; the default) or exact numbers. Build → Ruleset → Combat names each choice's default in its label, so one left unset reads as what it plays as. With wounds, nothing you or the narrator reads gives someone else's HP as a number: the Encounter card, the chips and the Journal say how hurt they are ("Goblin: badly hurt", "Goblin takes damage"), and the narrator is told the same. Your own HP is always exact, and so is everyone's in GM mode.
- **Combat actions.** Any action becomes a combat action with a **cost** (budget → how many; Attack 1, or Attack 1 and Move 1 for a full attack). An attack is an ordinary action whose check reads the target's defense (a DC such as `target.defense`, or an opposed roll) and whose hit outcomes take `roll()` damage off the HP pool, which stops at 0 and at its max. Mark it **attack** or **defend** for everyone else's tactics. A combat action that **starts a fight** (an ambush) is offered only when none is under way; every other one needs a fight, your turn and enough left.
- **Starting and ending.** **Fight** (in the Actions sheet's Combat tab) starts one against someone; rules and actions can use the `combat.start` effect (named foes, a selector, or everyone here hostile to you) and `combat.end`. Formulas read a fight with `in_encounter`, `is_foe`, `is_ally`, `is_down`, `has_turn`, `standing(side)`, `encounter_round()` and `budget_left`; a rule on `combat.end` can award XP.
- **Playing a fight.** While one is under way, the Encounter card in **Play → Now** shows the round and whose turn it is, the turn order with HP (yours exactly, others' as the rules allow) and conditions, what you have left, **Combat actions (n)**, which opens the Actions sheet's Combat tab with your attacks, their targets and exact odds, **End turn**, and this round's acts. When your turn ends, everyone else takes theirs in initiative order before the reply, so the narrator is told the round in order (who did what, the hits and damage, who is down) and then whose turn it is. With **NPC choices** on, the decision model may pick among their legal actions; otherwise they defend when hurt and attack the most threatening foe. It's all part of your turn's record, so swipes, Keep and Re-run treat a round like any other turn. A foe you can't see is "someone": never named, never a target. GM mode's **Fight** page, from the **Fight under way** card in **World**, shows everyone, HP exact.
- **Time, leaving and stalemates.** A fight takes no time on the clock: while one is under way, your turns move no clock and run no one's day, whatever you do (talking, a skill, an action that takes minutes), you can't skip ahead (a time jump the narration describes doesn't apply: a **Didn't apply** chip says a fight is under way, and the next reply is told so), and no one in the fight walks off to keep a schedule. The action that starts a fight still takes its own time: a twenty-minute stalk that ends in an ambush moves the clock twenty minutes. Walk out of the place and the fight is over for you, with no winner: the card and the fight's actions in the Combat tab close, **Fight** is offered again right away, and nothing of it goes on without you. Three whole rounds in a row in which no one attacks and no one is hurt (the ruleset's **stalemate**; 0 turns it off) and the fight peters out. A missed attack is still an attack, so holding Total defense while a foe keeps swinging doesn't end the fight, and your own **Over when** rule gets its turn. Everyone else guards when hurt only while a foe has gone for them this round or the last, and a stance such as Total defense or Dodge shows in the round's acts like any other act. **Hostile to** is the combat pack's own relation, held on both sides. The card shows who is down at any Sheets level; the down condition shows on the character sheet from Sheets 4. With fights settled by the policy, the Combat tab shows what it makes of each attack (No roll needed, The story decides) rather than dice odds for your own; everyone else's attacks always roll. Lint warns when that policy's retry rule waits for a new scene: the same attack comes round every turn, so a miss would stand for the rest of the fight.
- **Dice or the policy.** Everyone else's combat actions always roll, whatever the world's adjudication policy says: no one chose them, and no judge reads their approach. Your own roll every time by default too, so a missed attack can be tried again next turn. Any other check made during a fight follows the policy as usual. Set **Attacks and other fight checks are settled by** to **policy** to have your own combat actions follow the policy too: a fight takes no time on the clock, so for a retry rule that waits for a change each new round counts as one, while one that waits for a new scene keeps a missed attack refused for the rest of the fight.

### Classifications, groups and kinship

**Classifications** are your own ways of sorting a world's people and things, in **Build → Classifications**. Each one (a *facet*: a story role, an allegiance, a disposition) has a label, says whether an entity holds one value or several (**Several values per entity**), an order and a description, and lists its **Values**. Type a label under **New value's label** and tap **Add value**; its id comes from the label, under **Advanced**. Tap a value to open it: its **Label**, a **Description** and, if you like, the value it is **Nested under** ("Villain" under "Antagonist"), so anything that asks for Antagonist also takes the villains, and **Remove value**. An entity's editor has a field for each classification: a chip per value, which for a one-value classification acts as a single choice while there are few values (tapping the chosen one clears it) and becomes a picker when there are many. Beside them are its **Tags**, free-form labels shown as removable chips: type one and press Enter or tap **Add** (a comma adds too), and the box suggests the tags already in use. A vehicle's **Vehicle class** (`pickup_midsize`, a row of the vehicle classes table its fuel, range and costs come from) belongs to the Vehicles pack, not to your classifications.

A classification is for you and the GM side unless you tick **Players can see this classification**. Until then its values never reach Play, so a "Villain" stays a secret; tags never show in Play at all. **Play → People** groups and filters only by the classifications players can see.

**Groups** are entities too: factions, families, households, companies, or a kind of your own that extends **Group**. Someone joins with a **member of** relation, which can carry their **role**, **rank** and **since** date; a group can belong to a larger group (a household in a family), and membership counts through it. A group's regard toward someone, `faction_regard(group, who)`, is its own regard if it has one, else the mean of its members' (people and things, through member groups too). A schedule owned by a group runs for its members.

**Kinship** comes with the Relationships pack: **parent of** and **child of** are each other's inverse, and **sibling of**, **spouse of** and **partner of** are symmetric. The engine keeps both sides: a link written by an effect brings its other side with it (and an unlink takes it away) as one change, a spawned person's relations bring theirs, and a link you author on one side only is completed when a chat starts (lint warns, naming the missing side). The same holds for your own relation types with an inverse or symmetry. A link that would break its type's kinds or cardinality ("Betrothed to" one at a time, say) is refused with the reason.

### Selectors

A **selector** picks entities by rule, with one editor wherever you need one (a schedule's owner, an action parameter's candidates, a **Known by**): **Kind** (or one inheriting from it), **Tier**, a value of each classification (or one nested under it), **Tag**, **Member of** (directly or through a member group) and **Only these entities**. What a constraint holds shows as removable chips: tiers and classification values are chips you tap on or off, tags are typed, and kinds, groups and entities are added from a picker by name. An entity must pass every constraint you set, and within one constraint any of its values will do; a selector with nothing set matches everyone. Where a selector is optional, **Add a selector** starts one and **Remove** takes it away.

### Character minds in a world

**Build → World → Minds** sets how the world's characters think (see [Character minds](#character-minds) for what they do in play). Nothing here is required: a world without it plays with the defaults, and **Use the defaults** takes the world's own settings out again. **Save to draft** saves the whole form and **Revert** drops your changes; the three sit in the bar at the bottom. Problems show in the group they belong to.

- **What the narrator gets of a mind** (secrecy): two cards, the one in force marked **In use**.
  - **Brief**, the default: the narrator gets only a short description of how each character comes across (what they do, how they look and sound). It is checked first, and left out if it would give away a secret or name someone the player hasn't met. Their private thoughts and what they mean to do stay in GM mode. The safest choice against spoilers, with less for the narrator to work with.
  - **Full**: the narrator also gets each character's inner monologue, what they mean to do, their reactions and their mood, with the description unchecked, told to show them through behaviour and never say them outright. Richer, more consistent characters, but a careless narrator could let a thought or a secret slip.

  Either way, the player never sees a mind.
- **Thresholds**: the lowest score for **Live**, **Near** and **Far**; under Far is Background. Live should be at least Near, and Near at least Far; out of order, the lower ones are raised to match.
- **Focus weights**: the points each thing adds to a character's focus score. **What counts toward the score**, folded away, holds being in the player's scene, close by, named in the latest message or reply, strong feelings toward the player either way, knowing the player, owning a quest, secret or agenda under way, each track that names them, being named by a world rule, each pending invitation, appointment, plan or commitment with the player, and being seen with the player lately; **Tier bands**, also folded away, holds the points for each tier's band. Under **Roles**, **Add a role value** picks a classification value by name (an antagonist, say) to add points for, and a value nested under it counts as it; each has a remove button (**Remove** *value*). Weights run from −100 to 200; a negative one pushes a character away.
- **Who always, and who never**: **Always Live** and **Never a mind (wins over Always Live)**, two [selectors](#selectors). A selector with nothing set picks no one here (not everyone), and the player's own character never gets a mind.
- **Memory defaults**: how many witness-log lines, recalled turns (the latest, and the ones that best match the moment, with their token budget), notes and open commitments a mind sees; how many notes a character keeps before the oldest are folded into their summary; how fast a note's salience halves (in days of game time) and the salience under which it has faded; whether faded and surplus notes are folded into the summary, and at most how often for each character (in days of game time). A player's own **Settings → Minds → Memory** wins over these.
- **Step templates**: a row for each of the world's own prompts for the four minds steps. For a step with none, **New template** starts one from the built-in prompt and opens it in the step-template editor; a template already there opens the same way. **New template** stays off while this form has unsaved changes, with "Save or revert your changes first." Only a step's first template (by id) with any text is used: a blank one is marked **Blank: not used**, and any other but the one used **Not used**. A world's template wins over the built-in prompt, and a player's own prompt (Settings → Minds) wins over the world's.

### Lore books

Each world has its own book, **"LumiRPEngine · *your world*"**, created for you. It holds only the engine's sections: their entries are stored switched off and only the engine turns them on, so a section the engine can't fill never leaks into the prompt. Lore entries an earlier version of LumiRPEngine copied into the book are removed the next time you commit or Upgrade the world, attach it to a chat, or play a turn in one of its chats.

The world's own lore lives in the world, not in a book: see [World lore](#world-lore). LumiRPEngine never edits your own lore books.

Secret truth never lives in a World Book. It stays in the world and enters a section only at the moment it's revealed.

### World lore

**Build → Lore** holds what the world knows. It is kept in the world and travels with it when you export it. The engine's own helpers read it ([Lore for the helpers](#lore-for-the-helpers)); the narrator doesn't get it yet. The list's search box finds entries by title, alias, topic, keyword or text, and badges secret entries, where an entry came from, and entries whose book has changed. Its **Tools** open the **Lookup table** and the two indexing tools. **New** asks for the new entry's name and takes its id from it, as in any collection.

**Entries.** An entry's page has its **Entry** (a title and its text), then **How it's found**: **Aliases** (other names its subject goes by, such as *the old lighthouse*), **Topics** it belongs under (*Harbor law*) and **Keywords**, each a row of removable chips (type one and press Enter or tap **Add**; the topic and keyword boxes suggest the ones your other entries use), then **links** to the places and people it is about, and optionally a **group** and a **priority**. Then come its **Source** and **Secrecy**. A problem with one of these fields shows under it. Its **Item actions** button (⋯) holds **Delete**, which names what leads to it ("Terms and topics that lead to it will show in Problems."). The text is kept exactly as you wrote it, macros included, as in any World Book; that is why a world from someone else is worth a look before you play it (see [Import and export](#import-and-export)).

The **Source** card says where an entry came from: written in Build (as is every entry made before sources existed), indexed from one of your World Info books (it names the book and the entry), saved from a chat (it names the message), or written by an indexing job from text you pasted. When an entry indexed from a book no longer matches its World Info entry because you edited the book since, the card says so and offers **Refresh from book**, which puts the book's new text in the form for you to **Save to draft**. An entry whose book entry was deleted says so and keeps its text.

**The lookup table.** **Lore → Lookup table** (also the **Lore lookup table** part on the world home) is the routing LumiRPEngine's own lore searches follow, whether you or an indexing job wrote it. It shows **Terms** or **Topics**. **Terms** lead to entries: *the old lighthouse* → *Breakwater Lighthouse*. One term can lead to several entries (*the Okafors* → each of the family's entries): each term's **Leads to** shows its entries as removable chips, with a picker to add one, and **Add a term** takes a **Term** and the entry it **Leads to**. Terms are matched in lowercase with their spacing tidied, which is how the editor stores them. **Topics** are a table of contents: each has a label and its **Entries** in order, and **Add a topic** makes one. Suggestions come from your entries: their titles, aliases and keywords as terms (**Add all aliases as terms** takes every alias at once), and the topics they name (**File them all**); **Add** takes one. Nothing is saved until **Save to draft**. Lint flags a term or topic that names an entry that doesn't exist, a blank term or one that leads nowhere, a term written in a way lookups won't match, and a term that is one entry's alias but leads somewhere else.

**Who may read it.** Under **Secrecy**, **Who may read it** is *public* (anyone) or *Known by*: read only once your character knows every person and place the entry links (lint warns about one that links nothing). An exported world keeps it as the entry's `visibility` (`public` or `known_by`), which the published `lre-world-v1` schema accepts. Tick **Secret** to keep an entry hidden. A secret entry may hold the secret itself, and it needs a **gate**: a formula, revealed once it holds (lint refuses a secret without one). Until then, only the helpers you tick under **Helpers that may read it before the gate holds** may see it: NPC choices, the contradiction check, puzzle answers and so on, one by one, and none by default. The scene brief, the prose outline, suggested choices and extraction write what the narrator or you see, so they never read a secret before its gate holds, even when ticked; ticking one shows a note, and lint warns too. **Whose own secret it is** names the people it belongs to: their [minds](#character-minds) read it, before the gate holds and after. A character's mind reads a secret entry only then, or when one of their **Cues** stands for it; linking them doesn't make it theirs, and your character uncovering a secret tells no one else. A public entry never holds a secret's truth; lint refuses one that repeats a quest's or secret's truth.

**Indexing jobs.** Two tools turn text you already have into lore. Both are in the world's tools and in the Lore list:

- **Index a World Info book**: pick one of your books. Each entry with text that is switched on becomes a proposed lore entry with a title, aliases, topics, links to the world's people and places, and lookup terms. The entry keeps the book's own text and keywords, and its source remembers the book entry, so a later edit to the book shows up as described above. Entries already in the world's lore are skipped, so running it again reads only what's new; one run reads up to 72 entries. The world's own engine book can't be indexed.
- **Index this text**: paste a setting document, a history or your notes. The helper splits it into entries in the text's own words; each is marked as written by the AI and counts as reviewed once you apply it. One run reads about 72,000 characters.

Both work like [Import from lore](#import-from-lore). A proposal that cites no entry of the book, repeats a title the world's lore already has, or has no text is dropped, links to anything that isn't one of the world's people or places are removed with a note, and a reply with nothing usable is asked once more. You review the proposals: each shows its title, where it came from, its aliases, topics and links, and the lookup terms it adds. **Apply n to the draft** adds the ticked entries and files their terms and topics in the lookup table; your books are never edited. **Commit** when you are happy with the result. Like the other tools, each job keeps its last run.

**Save to lore.** In **Play → Story → Journal**, **Save to lore…** opens a small form, and **Save the latest reply** keeps the latest reply as a lore entry in the world's draft. To keep only part of the chat, select it and tap **Use selected text**, or paste it, then **Save the excerpt**. The entry's source names the message it came from (the latest message holding the excerpt, or the latest reply if the excerpt isn't in the chat word for word), and its form opens in Build so you can give it a title, aliases and topics before you commit.

### Import from lore

Already have lore books? **Import from lore**, a tool inside a world, builds a starting world from them:

1. Pick the books.
2. The engine reads them on a background task and turns them into proposals: entities, places, rules and more. When the helper's reply has nothing usable (it comes back empty, isn't valid JSON, lists no proposal the engine can read, or proposes nothing for entries that have text), the engine asks once more with a reminder of the format (Refine with AI does the same, though there an empty answer stands); only if that reply fails too does the job say "The helper proposed nothing (or its reply wasn't valid JSON)." Each call's outcome and time go to your diagnostics log.
3. You review the proposals. Each has a checkbox and a confidence badge (70% or more start ticked), **Select all** and **None** are there, and **Show value** reveals what a proposal would set, its sources named by entry and book (*Lighthouse · B2 Chat Lore*). Under each one, **From lore: *book name*** names the book it came from (the entry it cites is linked back to), and **Assumed from a table: *field*, …** lists any fields the lore didn't state and the engine filled from a table. An entity the lore gave no kind takes the world's one base kind, **Thing**, says **Assumed kind: thing** and starts unticked whatever its confidence, so a person filed as a thing is applied only if you tick it; a world with more than one base kind drops it instead, and the job says why. There is no per-proposal edit step: accept or reject, then edit what you accepted in the editors.
4. **Apply n to the draft**, in the bar at the bottom of the page, adds the ticked proposals as entities; the books are linked, never edited. **Commit** when you are happy with the result.
5. Anything the books don't say is left to the data tables and to [detail on demand](#detail-on-demand). A field that comes from a table (an occupation, a region, a catalog item) which the lore leaves out or names loosely takes the best-matching table row, and its proposal says so.

### Build / Refine with AI

**Build / Refine with AI** is a tool inside a world. You can ask the AI to draft or improve parts of it, and it returns **proposals**, never direct changes: you review them as you would an import, tick the ones you want (each has a confidence badge, and proposals at 70% or more start ticked) and apply them to the draft. A change to an existing entity that leaves out its kind keeps the kind it has. Proposals are checked by the world checker as part of this, and the work happens in the background, so you can keep editing.

Each tool remembers its last run, with its status and its proposals, so you can leave Build, reload or restart and come back to review them. Its page shows the run under **Status**, with **Run again with new input** folded below it, and then the **Proposals**, each by name, with **Apply n to the draft** in a bar that stays at the bottom of the page. An import saves its proposals as it reads each part of the books. A job the server or the extension stopped while it ran shows as **Interrupted**: the proposals it made before it stopped are still there to review and apply, and **Run it again** starts it over with the same books or request. **Dismiss** clears a finished job; the engine keeps only the last few anyway.

### Describe a map

**Describe a map** draws a map from your own words. Pick the place it maps, give the map an id if you like, and describe the inside ("A small inn: the taproom at the front, the kitchen behind it through a door, and a corridor down the side to the stable yard."), then tap **Draw the map**. The AI writes an lre-map-v1 map, and the engine checks it as the map editor would; if it has problems, the AI gets one more try with the list of them. You get one proposal with a preview of the map, ticked when it came back clean and unticked, with what's still wrong, when it didn't. Applying it puts the map in your draft like any other, where the map editor can fix the rest; nothing reaches your chats until you commit. Like the other tools, it remembers its last run.

### Proposing routines from lore

**Propose routines from lore**, a tool inside a world, gives your people and groups [routines](#routines-in-build) and schedules from what the world already says about them: its lore entries, their sheets (role, occupation, workplace, home, traits, description), their families, households and clubs, and who they know. It proposes two kinds of thing:

- **Schedules**: shifts, school and class timetables, services. A shop's opening hours become the shifts of the people who work there.
- **Routine blocks**: habits.
  - **Fixed** blocks happen at a set time ("dinner at six").
  - **Flexible** blocks happen somewhere in a window, every day or a few times a week ("most mornings", "twice a week").
  - **Optional** blocks happen only sometimes ("now and then").

A household's dinner, a club's evening or worship with a congregation comes back as the group's block, with its members as company.

**Sources.** Every block and schedule cites the lore entry or sheet field it rests on, with a short quote from it. The engine checks the quote against that text, and anything without a source that holds is never proposed. So someone the lore says nothing about gets no routine at all, and a habit the lore states loosely comes back flexible or optional, never fixed.

**What it reads.** It reads only what may reach the player: public lore, entries whose secret is already out, public sheets and places. Secret lore and secret people never go into it, so nothing it proposes gives a secret away.

**How much lore each one gets.** Each person and group gets their lore in whole entries, the most about them first, up to **Lore each person or group reads** in **Settings → Steps and connections → Build jobs** (8,000 tokens to start; turn on **all of it** for everything, however long). No entry is ever cut short. When the limit leaves entries out, the job says whose and how many of theirs it read. People whose lore fills the limit get a helper call of their own.
**What the engine checks.** Each proposal is checked against your draft:

- its places, people and company exist;
- its times can be;
- anyone it names as company is tied to the person, belongs with them, or is named with them in the cited text;
- its label names no one.

When the AI got any of this wrong, it gets one more try with the list of what to fix.

**What it points out.** A proposal can clash with something already there:

- a schedule or a routine block the person already has;
- another proposal;
- the opening scene, by taking someone away from where the story starts them.

The clash is pointed out in its problems, and it never replaces what you wrote. Something the world already has is left out.

**Using it:**

1. Choose whom for: **Everyone** (every person and group the job may read, never a secret one or your own character), or **Choose** and tick people and groups.
2. It runs in the background, with its progress and **Cancel**. It reads a few people and groups at a time, within the same twelve helper calls an import makes; a pass that stops before everyone was read says so and offers **Continue**, which starts the next pass where this one stopped. Apply what you want from a pass first: the next pass's proposals replace it.
3. The review groups the proposals by the person or group they're for. Each says what it sets (a block's kind, time, length, days, place and company; a schedule's field, how it repeats and its slots) and what it rests on, **From lore:** *entry* or **From a sheet:** *name · field*, with the quote; tap a lore entry's title to read it in place. Under it is what checking it against the draft found: a clash with their schedules or routine, a place or company it can't have, pulling someone away from where the story starts them, or a routine or schedule of the same id already in the draft. A proposal with any of these starts unticked; the rest start ticked at 70% or more. A proposal that doesn't rest on a lore entry or a sheet field is never offered, and the review says how many were left out.
4. **Edit** opens a proposal in Build's own routine or schedule form. **Done** keeps the edit and ticks it, and **As proposed** drops it. The edit is what's applied, and anything wrong with its shape shows before you apply.
5. **Apply n to the draft** writes the ticked proposals into the draft, each as a routine or schedule of its own, where the editors can change them; nothing reaches your chats until you **Commit**. A schedule comes as a pair, where they are and what they're doing: apply both.

Like the other tools, it remembers its last run, and your ticks and edits stay while you open a lore entry or another page and come back, until you reload.

### Money in Build

A world's money lives in its entities and the economy pack's config, and every part of it has a form. Amounts are typed as the world's currency writes them. Lists inside a money field (owners, splits, shares, lots, deductions and payment-preference tiers) show a row for each entry that opens it as a sheet, with a button below to add one (**Add owner**, **Add split** and so on); weekdays and months are chips to toggle, and days of the month are typed in a row. Problems show in place, beside the field, row or switch they belong to.

- **Accounts** (kind `account`): its owners (each with a role, owner, joint, custodian, beneficiary, trustee or authorized user, and a share of its worth), its **product** (one of the world's products, or a built-in class: cash, checking, business checking, savings, money market, certificate of deposit, trust, escrow, credit card, charge card, line of credit, loan, mortgage, brokerage or retirement), its opening balance, status and any terms it sets itself over its product's (limit, APR, APY, statement and due days, minimum payment, a loan's payment and months left, what secures it, its overdraft policy and autopay).
- **Payment cards** (kind `card`): the account, the holder, debit, credit or charge, its state, the card item in the holder's gear, the last four digits and its daily limits.
- **Flows** (kind `flow`): standing payments, income and one-offs, with their amount (a fixed amount, a formula, a table cell, a range, a percentage of a balance, or what's due on a credit account), schedule, splits, shares among a household (**Split equally among a household's members** fills them in), what happens when the money isn't there, and what they belong to.
- **Jobs** (kind `job`), **businesses** (kind `business`), **trusts** (kind `trust`), **holdings** (kind `holding`) and **property** (kind `property`), each with its own fields: a job's pay, schedule, deductions and where pay goes; a business's owners, the places it trades at (chips, added with **Add a place…**), its terms and its days; a holding's asset, lots and where its payouts go; a property's place, owners, value and costs.
- **People**: their payment preferences, in order, with the amounts and categories each tier is for.
- **Households**: a household is a group (kind `household`) its members join with **Member of**; shared costs are flows with shares.
- **Places**: **Add a cash machine** adds a cash machine (its bank, its surcharge, the most it pays out at once, and whether it takes deposits), and **Add a bank branch** a bank branch (its bank).
- **Build → Packs → economy**: under **Prices and region**, the **Narrated-price cap**, the **Claim cap** (the most a thing you say you have may cost before a capped Make it real asks first; see [Player claims](#player-claims)) and the world's **Region** (whose cost of living and tax rates price things for anyone who doesn't name a region of their own; **Not set: the national averages**); its **Living costs** (a week's spending for each wealth band; set any band and the world's amounts replace the catalog-priced defaults for every band, so a band left empty spends nothing); its **Taxes**: the **Corporate tax rate** a C corporation pays on its profit (the federal 21% when it's left empty), and the **Exempt from sales tax** row, which opens a page with **Taxed** or **Exempt** for each spending category (an exempt category is never taxed, wherever it's sold) and **Done**; under **Money features**, the **Feature switches** row, which opens a page of its own with a switch for each money feature the world turns on or off (under any preset), and **Done**; its own **Products** (each with its class and whatever it sets: limit, rates, interest method, statement and due days, minimum payment, fees, rewards, daily limits, hold days, overdraft and term) and its **Assets** (each with its class, symbol, starting price and price path, payouts, and a bond's or an option's terms). A product or an asset is a row that opens as a sheet, with **Remove product** (or **Remove asset**) and **Done**; **Add product** and **Add asset** ask for a name and make the id from it. What a product leaves out comes from its class's defaults in the finance tables.

### Data tables

Prices, pay, taxes, rents, vehicles, gear and the calendar's holiday rules come from **data tables**. The defaults are **Modern US**: approximate figures reflecting roughly 2025–26, recalled from public sources and rounded, not live data. Prices are national averages; each state's cost-of-living multiplier scales them when something is priced. Included are about 340 priced items in 19 categories, 56 occupations with pay and typical schedules, the 50 states and DC with tax rates, rents, electricity and minimum wage, finance parameters (credit cards, deposit accounts, loans, investing, payroll deductions, federal tax), 19 vehicle classes, 90 gear items, 34 containers, 5 kits, and federal holidays, common observances and school-year and work-shift patterns.

You can edit your world's copy of any table in **Data tables**. The list shows each table's pack, how many rows it has and whether the figures are *approximate* or *authored*; new tables come from packs, so there is no New. A table's page has **About this table** (its schema, revision and source), then its **Rows** as a grid, 25 to a page, with **Search rows** and the first column kept in view as you scroll sideways; a row with a problem shows how many. Tap a row's name to open it as a sheet with a field per column (money in the world's currency), where your changes go into the table as you type; **Delete row** asks first, and **Done** closes it. **New row** asks for the row's name and takes its id from it (or asks for the id, in a table whose rows have no name). **Save table** puts the table in the draft, saying how many problems it still has, and **Revert** sits beside it; saving makes a new revision of the table. Each world revision pins the table revisions it uses, so a running chat never sees a table change until you [Upgrade](#upgrade). Swapping in another region's table isn't in the editor yet.

### Templates

In Build, tap **New world** and pick a card under **Start from** (**Blank**, **Fantasy town**, **Mystery manor** or **Modern slice-of-life**). The **Name** field takes the template's name until you type your own; then tap **Create world**. A template is a normal world with its own cast, schedules, quests, rules, weather and belongings, plus two dozen or so lore entries on its places, people and history (kept in the world, where the engine's helpers read it; the brief and the prose outline draw on its public entries for the narrator); change anything you like. Its puzzles show in **Play → Story → Quests** once you have come across them: the study safe when you step into the study, the vestry tiles when you find the vestry floor, the township truck when the shop takes the job.

- **Fantasy town** is *Brindlemere*, a river-ford market town on the eve of harvest, on a made-up calendar of thirteen moons. It starts on the **Story** preset and counts money in bits, marks and crowns.
- **Mystery manor** is *Harrowmere Hall*, a storm-bound fenland country house in October 1934, in pre-decimal money. It starts on the **Grounded** preset and has clues to find and a locked safe to open.
- **Modern slice-of-life** is *Port Alder*, a Lake Michigan harbor town in September 2026, in US dollars. It runs on the **Simulation** preset: payroll, rent, a garage, a boat and a shop to look after, a small index-fund account to invest in (Invest and Sell investments, on the Simulation preset), a school term for the landlady's youngest granddaughter, and a neighbor away on a weekend trip.

### Problems, Check world and Playtest

**Problems** runs the full lint on the draft and reports every problem in plain language: each row has a badge for how bad it is (**Error**, **Warning** or **Info**), the message, and where it is by name ("Entities › Rosie Alvarez"); its path and code show only with **Show ids** on. Filter by **All**, **Errors**, **Warnings** or **Info**, tap **Open** to jump to the thing (an item, or the part of the world it is in: a tier problem or the tier new people join opens **Tiers**), and **Check again** after a fix. **Check world** plays the world forward in the background and shows a transcript of each day as the author's view: every event, worded with names (no ids or bookkeeping, up to 40 lines), including people the player hasn't met and secrets, so you can check off-screen routines. Dropped changes, failures and leaks go in the notes. **Playtest** plays through the world in the background, without touching any chat: pick the number of turns (1 to 50), optionally give it a script, and tap **Run playtest** for a transcript and an **Issues found** list; its transcript shows each turn as a player would see it. A scripted line can say an action in its own words, with filler words in between ("walk down to the coffee shop"); when several actions fit, the one whose details the line fills is used. An action's words count only when you are the one doing it, in the first person ("I", "we", "let's") or as a command; a line that negates it ("I don't want to walk to the market"), says it of someone else ("Danny says he'll walk over to the marina"), muses about it ("I wonder whether to buy a boat", "I might", "thinking about") or uses it in an idiom ("I pay attention") triggers nothing. Playtest and Check world make no model calls: nothing reads typed text, judges an approach or decides an NPC's close call, so checks roll and close calls go by the numbers. Like looking in GM mode, they leave nothing recorded.

### Import and export

Each world's **More for** *name* button on the Worlds page holds **Export** and **Delete**, and **Import a file** on the **New world** page brings one in. Worlds export in the published **`lre-world-v1`** format and import from the same; the world's own lore travels inside the file, and your World Books stay separate. A world is one document, so it can be copied, shared and read by other tools: its JSON Schema is `schemas/lre-world-v1.schema.json`, and [STUDIO.md](STUDIO.md) is the contract for tools that author worlds outside Lumiverse. It carries its own **currency** (a code, symbol, minor unit, number of decimals and display format), which is why a fantasy town can count in *bits* and the mystery manor in pence (*12d*); every amount in the drawer and the chips is written with it. Importing never changes your settings.

**Imported worlds list any macros in their lore for your review.** After an import, a **Review before playing** card appears above the list of worlds if the file's lore, section templates or step templates contain macros, control characters or `<user>`, `<char>` or `<bot>` shorthands (all things Lumiverse's macro engine acts on): "“*name*” came from a file. Its lore and templates contain macros that Lumiverse runs when it builds a prompt; open each one and check it before you play." Each row names the macro, control character or shorthand and where it is, and **Open** takes you to that lore entry, section or step; **Done reviewing** puts the card away (it is not kept, so open the Lore list and look for double braces if you come back later). The toast says how many items there are to review. Nothing is changed for you: macros in lore you write yourself are kept as written, just as in any World Book.

**Worlds from LumiRPEngine Studio.** [LumiRPEngine Studio](https://github.com/TheLiquorPriest/LumiRPEngineStudio), a separate extension, builds worlds with an assistant, from scratch or from a chat you're already in and its lore. When you install a world there, LumiRPEngine opens **Build** on a **From LumiRPEngine Studio** card: what it is (a new world, or the next revision of one of yours), how many people, places, quests and lore entries it holds, its problems and its macros. Nothing changes until you tap **Install**; **Not now** puts the card away, and **Import from LumiRPEngine Studio** on the **New world** page brings it back. A new world comes in as an import does, with its macros listed for review. A revision lands on its own world, and that world's chats stay where they are until you Upgrade them in **GM → This chat → Pins and upgrade**; it waits while you have an unsaved draft of that world in Build. If the Studio made it for the chat you have open, and that chat has no world yet, you're asked whether to attach it, as Play's **Attach** asks. The Studio may read your worlds, and what your world chats show you (place, time, cash, who's here and the open quests), so its assistant can refine a world you're playing; no other extension can.

If that world changed in Build since the Studio took it, the card says so and **Install** becomes **Install anyway**: the Studio's version becomes the world's head without what you committed since. That revision stays, and so do the chats on it; to keep both, take the world into the Studio again.

### Revisions and Upgrade

You edit a world as a draft: the world bar in **Build** says **Uncommitted** while the draft has changes, with its error and warning counts, and **Commit** makes it a new revision. While errors remain, **Commit** says how many block it, and tapping it asks you to fix them first. **Discard changes**, under the bar's **Draft actions** button, throws away every uncommitted change once you confirm with **Discard**. When you **commit**, the draft becomes a new revision; revisions that a chat depends on are never deleted automatically. A chat stays on the revision it was started on. Once you commit a newer revision of its world, **GM → This chat → Pins and upgrade** shows the world, the chat's revision and its pinned generators and tables, and offers **Upgrade to the latest revision**. (An imported world is a new world, and an extension update changes no stored revision, so neither brings an Upgrade.)

A world has one draft, shared by every window and device you use. When something else changes the draft while Build has it open (another window, or an outside agent working through the `lumirpengine` CLI or its MCP server), Build reloads it and tells you: *This world's draft changed in another window, or an outside agent changed it; Build reloaded it.* Changes are made one at a time, so none is lost. A draft begun before the world got a newer revision can't be committed over it: discard it and start again from the latest revision.

If you were editing an item when it changed elsewhere, Build keeps your edits, even text you are still typing in a field: the world bar says *This item changed elsewhere while you were editing it.* and offers **Load theirs** (the other change, in the form) and **Keep mine**. Saving over the other change asks you first (**Save mine**). A discard or a commit of a draft that changed while Build was asking you is refused, and Build reloads the draft so you can look again. Build also reads the draft again when it reconnects or you come back to the page, in case it missed a change.

Each world keeps a **history** of how each of its revisions became the latest: created, committed in Build, committed by an outside agent (with the note it gave), imported, or installed from LumiRPEngine Studio. Worlds made before this was kept start their history at their next commit. The `lumirpengine` CLI and MCP server show the history, and they can put an earlier revision back into the draft for you to commit again.

#### Upgrade

Upgrade moves a chat to the newer revision from the next turn forward. It is recorded like any other change, so it works with swipes, branches and Re-narrate. Detail that has already been shown stays as it was; the new tables and generators apply to anything not yet shown. Nothing ever changes silently.

**Upgrade to the latest revision** first shows what the revision brings into the chat, entity by entity: people and things that are new, ones the revision no longer has, and each change to the rest. That covers fields such as appointments, home or employer, plus name, tier, classes, relations, an account's opening balance, and **Placement**: where something is and the map cell it lies on, taken or kept as one, so a thing a new map lays in a cell, or moves, goes there. Something you picked up or put away counts as moved in play, and anything still in someone's hands or inside a container stays there: the Upgrade never lays it on the map. The rest of the world's definitions (places, routes, rules, actions, checks and the like) aren't listed: they come with the revision, as in a new chat. Each change shows the chat's value, the revision's, and why it is set the way it is:

- **Unchanged in play:** nothing in the chat has touched it, so it's set to **Take**.
- **Changed in play:** a turn, a fix or a GM edit changed it, so it's set to **Keep**. That holds even if it later changed back.

Flip any change with its **Take**/**Keep** toggle. **Take all unchanged** resets the untouched ones to Take; **Review changed in play** lists only the touched ones. Someone the revision no longer has stays in the chat unless you pick **Remove**. **Upgrade with these picks** applies it.

A change that would give a secret away (someone or somewhere your character doesn't know, a quest kept from you) reads **A hidden change** until you tap **Show spoilers**, as GM mode waits behind its own gate. This gate is the preview's own: it starts closed each time, whatever the GM gate is doing. Its toggle works either way, and its pick applies like any other.

A quest sitting at a stage the revision removed goes back to its first stage, with a Journal note. If an earlier Upgrade moved the chat without bringing these changes in, Pins and upgrade says so (**Not everything came in**) and offers the Upgrade even at the latest revision.

## Your agent's MCP server

Your own agent (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor…) reaches your worlds through the `lumirpengine` MCP server ([MCP.md](MCP.md)). It follows LumiRPEngine: when you update LumiRPEngine, a server on the same machine as Lumiverse loads the new build's tools and guide by itself, with no reinstall.

**Settings → MCP server** (under *Your agent*) shows the setup that follows LumiRPEngine, with this extension's own path, under each runtime that runs it, each with **Copy**: first `bun <path>/dist/lumirpengine.js mcp`, for Bun 1.4 or newer, which Lumiverse itself runs on, so it is always on Lumiverse's machine; then `node <path>/dist/lumirpengine.js mcp`, for Node 22 or newer. Give one to your agent's harness as the command for its lumirpengine server. On a machine with only Bun, use the Bun one: installing with npm, or running with npx, needs Node. Below it is each server that has connected from your account, with the harness that runs it, its machine, its builds and whether it is connected now (Claude Code's and Codex's servers on one machine are two lines; a restarted one keeps its line): **Update now** asks one that hasn't reloaded yet to do it, and **Forget** drops one you no longer use. A server running as more than one process (two Claude Code windows on one machine) says how many, *2 processes*, and its line is the one furthest behind: it says *reloaded* only once all of them have.

**Lumiverse can run the server too.** In Lumiverse's Settings → MCP Servers, add a Stdio server with the command `bun` (or `node`) and the arguments `<path>/dist/lumirpengine.js, mcp` (the same path): Lumiverse runs only `node`, `bun`, `deno` and `python` there, not `lumirpengine`. Sign in once first, as the user Lumiverse runs as. It is listed like any other server, as *lumirpengine for lumiverse*, and follows each update. Lumiverse, though, keeps the tool list it read when it connected, so once the server has reloaded, its line offers **Reconnect in Lumiverse**. Pressing it asks the server to finish any tool call in progress and exit; Lumiverse starts it again about a second later, with the same command, and reads the new tools. The line waits for it to come back (Settings → Timeouts → **MCP server reconnect**, 1 min) and then reads updated; if it isn't back in time, the line says so, and you can **Disconnect** it and **Connect** it again in Lumiverse's Settings → MCP Servers. LumiRPEngine never adds or changes a Lumiverse MCP server. A server set up before this button existed doesn't know how to exit when asked, so its line asks you to reconnect it by hand that one time.

To have the line check Lumiverse's own tool list rather than take the server's word for it, switch on LumiRPEngine's **Mcp Servers** permission in Lumiverse's Extensions tab (only an owner or admin can). With it, LumiRPEngine reads which of your Lumiverse MCP servers hold LumiRPEngine's tools, and nothing more of them: only those servers' names show on the line. Without it, **Reconnect in Lumiverse** still works, and the line says Lumiverse's own list can't be checked.

**After an update** a toast says so, once, and LumiRPEngine's tab in the drawer opens on **LumiRPEngine updated**, with a line for each server:
- *reloaded this build's tools*: nothing to do;
- a server Lumiverse runs that *reloaded this build's tools, but Lumiverse's MCP client keeps the list it got when it connected*: press **Reconnect in Lumiverse** (above);
- *hasn't reloaded yet*: it does within a minute, or press **Update now**. Right after the update no server has checked in with the restarted LumiRPEngine yet, so the line says so: a running one takes the new tools at its next check, and a stopped one when it next starts;
- *can't follow*: why, and what to do, such as installing again on another machine (**Copy the install**) or using the setup above (**Copy the setup for Bun 1.4 or newer**, or **for Node 22 or newer**). A server never loads tools other users of the machine could change: when Lumiverse's extensions folder, or the extension's files in it, can be written by anyone but their owner, the line names the path, and the fix is to make it writable by its owner only (on Linux and macOS; Windows isn't checked);
- *from before MCP servers followed LumiRPEngine*: update it once (`npm i -g github:TheLiquorPriest/LumiRPEngine`), or switch it to the setup above.

The notice stays until you press **Dismiss**, and comes back with the next update. If LumiRPEngine's tab was open when it updated, Lumiverse switches the drawer to another tab while it reloads the extension (on a phone, the RPEngine button can end up just past the edge of the tab strip): open RPEngine again to see the notice.

## Troubleshooting

**A reply came back empty, and the turn says "degraded" with a Retry chip.**
The model returned nothing, which reasoning models do when their thinking uses up the token limit. The engine won't regenerate on its own. Raise **max tokens** to **1,500 or more** in your preset or connection settings, then tap **Retry**. The world checker warns you when a preset's max tokens are below 1,500 with a reasoning model, and **Settings → Caveats** shows the warning too.

**The helper steps fail, or return nothing.**
The same limit applies to the helper calls (reading typed text, reading replies, minds). A reasoning model needs several thousand tokens, because its thinking counts toward the limit; every step starts at 8,192. Set the step's **Max tokens** in **Settings → Steps and connections** if a step's replies still come back cut off; it warns you below 4,096.

**Settings → Caveats says it's still checking.**
Caveats checks your preset against the open chat with a test prompt build, which takes a while on a long chat. The page and its row on the Settings list say **Checking your preset…** until it answers; if the build is still running after 20 seconds, the page says so and the next visit picks up the answer, and if the build fails, the page says the preset's warnings aren't known. Settings itself never waits for it.

**Settings → Caveats says a step's connection or model is gone.**
Caveats checks the helper model's connection and every switched-on step's own connection and model before your preset: a connection you deleted in Lumiverse, or a model its connection no longer lists, would make that step's call fail and stop the turn. Pick another in **Settings → Steps and connections** (or **Helper model**). When Settings saves a value other than the one you gave (out of range, say), the page says what it kept.

**The AI keeps writing a JSON block, or the replies look wrong.**
Some presets contain a **JSON-ledger block**: a block that makes the narrator append a JSON object (gold, inventory) to its replies. It can confuse reasoning models, and it competes with the engine's own record of money and belongings. The world checker warns you if the active preset has one, and **Settings → Caveats** shows the warning too. Use a preset without that block for world chats.

**A section turns up outside my preset's blocks.**
Your preset places some of the world's sections with `{{outlet::lre_…}}` but not all of them, so the ones it leaves out go in where the world places them. **Settings → Caveats** names the missing outlets; add them to the block where the others are.

**A section says "not delivered".**
The test prompt build didn't find it in the prompt. Check where the world's book and your preset place it (a position your preset doesn't assemble never reaches the prompt), then open **GM → This chat → Context** ([The Context inspector](#the-context-inspector)) again, which runs the check again.

**The chat is paused.**
You edited an earlier message of yours. Choose **Keep** or **Discard** (see [Editing an earlier message of yours](#editing-an-earlier-message-of-yours)). Until you do, new turns are written without simulation.

**The world doesn't match what the reply says.**
You edited the reply, or continued it in a way the engine couldn't read. Tap **Re-read edit** on that reply (or **Retry read** for a stopped Continue).

**A warning says a saved file was damaged.**
A saved file the engine couldn't read, or couldn't make sense of, is kept next to the original as `<name>.corrupt-<time>` (a second damaged copy of the same file gets its own time), and the engine carries on: settings fall back to their defaults, and the chat's journal, extraction learning, chips, plans, Outcome notes or inspector history, your jobs or the call meter start fresh. A damaged journal is also reported in the Context inspector's warnings, with where the file is kept. The records saved on your messages still apply; edits, pauses and discards that only the journal held may be gone. A single record on a message that has the wrong shape (from an imported or forked chat, or another extension writing to the same message) is skipped instead: the message reads as if the engine had not simulated it, and the inspector's warnings name the message and swipe. Nothing is deleted.

**Something shows "pending".**
You opened detail or made a change while a reply was streaming. It lands right after the reply finishes.

**A Timelapse stopped early.**
Something interrupted it, and the chip says what: **Timelapse stopped: an invitation** (or a bill, a declined payment, an incident, a quest event, a world rule, someone arriving, an overdue loan, a need, a weather alert), with a **Continue** button that carries on to the original target. The Montage and the Journal say the same. A **Continue timelapse** chip means it hit the safety cap, and a Timelapse that couldn't run at all says why in the turn's changes ("Timelapse not run: …").

**My own lore entries are missing.**
LumiRPEngine doesn't touch your World Info for now, so your entries follow Lumiverse's own rules. Check that the book is attached to the chat, the entry is enabled and its keywords match, and Lumiverse's World Info budget leaves room for it.

**I imported a world and I'm not sure what's in it.**
A world file is someone else's content, and its lore and templates may contain Lumiverse macros (double braces such as `{{…}}`), which Lumiverse runs whenever it builds a prompt, in a World Info entry or here. LumiRPEngine keeps your own authored text exactly as written, macros included, and never lets a value it inserts (a name, a price, what a model said) form a macro. Besides double braces, Lumiverse's macro engine also acts on control characters and on the `<user>`, `<char>` and `<bot>` shorthands, so the import lists those too. After an import it lists what it found: a **Review before playing** card appears above the world list, with each item as "Review this macro before playing: …", the place it sits in (`lore.<id>.content`, `sections.<id>.template`, `steps.<id>.template`) and an **Open** button for it. Open each, delete or rewrite what you don't want, and tap **Done reviewing** before you attach the world to a chat. The card is shown only right after the import. The step templates' own placeholders (`{{pov}}`, `{{facts}}` and the like) appear in the list too; they belong to the engine and are harmless. Section templates may use only `{{lines}}`; the world checker reports any other macro, control character or shorthand in one as an error.

**A step never seems to run, or the call meter shows fewer calls than I expected.**
A step runs only when one of its signals fires and its phase's budget still has calls. Check the step's **Enabled** switch and its **Signals** (under **Advanced: signals and lore**) in **Settings → Steps and connections**, and the budgets under **Advanced: budgets and the engine budget** in **Settings → Model use** (**Calls** 0 means no model at all in that phase). Lean has NPC choices switched off, so it never asks a model about them.

**The prompt is too long, or the chat history disappeared from it.**
The engine's fixed overhead plus your context is over the connection's limit. Pick a smaller preset (Lean), lower the budgets in **Settings → Model use**, or use a connection with a larger context.

**The map closed the drawer.**
On a phone the map goes fullscreen, so opening it closes the drawer first. Reopen the drawer when you're done. If the drawer can't be closed, Play opens on **Places** instead, where the map is.

**A step failed and the engine did something simpler.**
That's the fallback: a failed decision call goes to the helper, then to the engine alone, and a step that passes its deadline uses its deterministic fallback. The turn still finishes. Check the call meter and your connections.
