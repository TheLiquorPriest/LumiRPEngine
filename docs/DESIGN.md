# LumiRPEngine: design

LumiRPEngine is an extension for [Lumiverse](https://github.com/prolix-oc/Lumiverse) that keeps a real, persistent world underneath a roleplay chat: places, people, belongings, money, vehicles, schedules, quests and the calendar. The engine settles what rules should settle (prices, travel times, dice odds, who is where at 3 a.m.), and the AI model spends its effort on writing. The model proposes; the engine decides.

This document is the design behind it: what the engine is made of, the rules it follows, why, and where it stops. I wrote it as a plan before I built the extension and revised it while I built and measured, so this version describes the product as it ships. Where the build departed from the original plan, §20 says so. For how to use the extension, read the [README](../README.md), the [user guide](USER-GUIDE.md) and the [GM guide](GM-GUIDE.md); for the module contract, read [ARCHITECTURE](ARCHITECTURE.md). Authors will want the [formula language](EXPRESSIONS.md), the [reference](REFERENCE.md) generated from the registry and the [recipes](RECIPES.md); a studio, the [studio contract](STUDIO.md).

**Product**
- Name: **LumiRPEngine**. Repository: <https://github.com/TheLiquorPriest/LumiRPEngine>. Manifest identifier: `lumi_rp_engine`.
- Requires Lumiverse's `staging` branch from 16 October 2026 (commit `9cc9f5d`) or later.

A few terms, in plain words:

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **World** | An authored JSON document (`lre-world-v1`): the kinds of things that exist, the entities, places, rules and schedules. It is shared by every chat that uses it. |
| **Run** | One chat's own state in a world. |
| **Ledger** | The record of what happened in each reply, kept with the reply, plus a durable shadow copy (the journal). |
| **Record, event, marker** | A *record* holds the events of one reply. An *event* is one concrete change (an account posting, a move). A *marker* is a message-level intent such as a realism or time-control change or a world upgrade. |
| **Fold** | Replaying the world, records and markers in order to get the current state. Everything the player sees is a fold. |
| **Rebased view** | How a later record folds when an earlier swipe or message changed underneath it. The stored record is never rewritten. |
| **Pack** | A domain module (money, gear, vehicles, and so on) with a pure engine part and a UI part. |
| **Level, tier, band** | How deeply a domain is simulated (0–4); a world's own rank of importance for an entity (its **tier**); and the fixed **detail band** a tier maps to, whose caps limit its levels (`principal` Leads, `supporting` Supporting, `background` Background). |
| **Slot** | A lore entry the engine owns and fills each turn (Outcome, Scene, People…). |
| **Chip** | A small notice or action offered in the chat: what changed, an undo, a confirmation. |
| **Step** | One unit of the turn pipeline: an engine step, a decision step or a prompted step (§5.2). |
| **Helper, Decision connection** | The helper is the ordinary model connection used for small JSON-answer calls. The Decision connection is the one used through Lumiverse's Decisions API for probability questions. |

## 1. Goal and constraints

**Goal.** Persistent world simulation for Lumiverse with game-engine features, as a Spindle extension:
- **World state:** a schema builder; entities and relationships; deterministic actions; locations; inventories; ownership.
- **Rules and system:** character sheets.
- **NPCs:** full NPC simulation, on and off screen.
- **Weather state.**
- **Quest engine:** gating, puzzles, secrets and event triggers.
- **Play:** deterministic actions during play; location grids and tracking.
- **Context:** the engine controls what reaches the AI. For now that is its own sections: World Info lore handling is switched off and the narrator gets no lore section. The engine's helpers read the world's lore through its own search (§5.4), and what `brief` and the prose `outline` write from its public entries reaches the narrator in the Outline slot (§6).
- **Model calls:** targeted simulation calls (regular or decision-type), and outline steps fed into regular generation.
- **A mobile-friendly UI.**
- **Realism:** granular finance, vehicles, gear and NPC lives on a **sliding scale of granularity**, with as much as possible deterministic and taken off the main model (§9).

**Constraints**
- Minimally invasive.
- As simple as possible without compromising any goal.
- Target Lumiverse's `staging` branch from 16 October 2026 (commit `9cc9f5d`) or later.
- **One complete first deliverable (D16).**

**Out of scope**
- **Background timers.** Simulation is turn-driven.

| D3 | **Users can scale from lean to whatever ceiling they want** (model use, §5.3; realism, §9.1). |

A type flips to `auto` at ≥ 0.90 acceptance over at least 30 confirm/undo chip outcomes on that connection, with a notification chip and undo, and flips back to `confirm` when its rolling undo rate rises past 0.10. ## 3. Shape: few concepts, strict module boundaries

### Engine concepts
Six concepts plus one pipeline.

| Concept | Role |
|---|---|
| **Entity graph** | Kinds: typed fields, derived formulas, bands, `visibility`. Plus typed relations. |
| **Effects → Events** | The only way state changes. Effects are validated proposals. **Events are concrete:** assignment events carry final values (location, status, owner, flags, equipped), and accumulator events carry concrete deltas or postings (money, fuel, odometer, quantities, regard, needs). Balances are derived. |
| **Rules** | `on <event or tick> when <predicate> do <effects>`; `once`/`cooldown`; a cascade cap. Storylet beats are rules with an outline-hint effect. |
| **Actions** | Parameters, requirements, an optional check, effects per outcome, a duration, and optionally the activity they start with its span (§9.2). One check mechanism: a dice expression plus outcome bands, with exact odds. |
| **Tracks** | One state machine for quests, secret ladders, puzzles and NPC agendas. |
| **Schedules** | Calendar recurrence plus slot tables of fixed values or weighted distributions, optionally conditioned on the previous value. They cover shifts, timetables, routines, weather, bills, paydays and price paths. |
| **Pipeline steps** | Engine steps, decision steps, prompted steps (§5.2). |

**Cross-cutting metadata, not concepts:** realism level, which gates processes, rendering and extraction; and visibility.

### Feature mapping

| Feature | Expressed as |
|---|---|
| Schema builder | Kinds (single inheritance); fields of type number, text, bool, enum, ref, list, dice, money, date or duration; `default`, `min`/`max`, `bands`, `derived`, `locked`, bounds (§11), `visibility`. Relation types with cardinality, symmetry and fields. |
| Entities and relationships | Built-in relations: `in` (containment), `owns`, `uses` (a time-bounded **use grant**: "assigned to", loans; a loan ends at the time the player says, "until 7 tonight" or "for 3 hours", else after its days, and ends early when the thing is back with its owner or changes owner), `worn_by`/`equipped`, `knows`, `member_of`, `regard` (multi-axis), `route`. |
| Locations, grids, tracking | Places (hierarchy, per-place time zone), routes with travel modes, optional grids (terrain, blocked cells, zones), positions; A* with caps. |
| Inventories and ownership | Gear pack (§9.3). |
| Character sheets | Fields, derived formulas, bands as words, layouts; the check mechanism. |
| Deterministic actions | Built-in move, go-to, drive, take, drop, give, lend, wear, use, buy, pay, transfer, refuel, service, wait/rest, sleep, **let time pass / timelapse** (a duration or *until*; §9.2), examine; plus author actions. |
| Weather | A climate schedule per region. |
| NPC simulation | §8 plus the Cast life and Relationships packs. |
| Quests, gating, puzzles, secrets, triggers | Tracks plus rules (§10). |
| Context control | The engine's sections (§6); World Info lore handling is off for now. |
| Decision and regular model calls | Decision steps and prompted steps; prompted steps can propose effects. |
| Outline steps | Deterministic `beats`, plus any number of prompted outline steps. |
| Granular realism | §9. |
| Time progression (D29) | Time controls (ticks per scene type, time scale, clock pause) recorded as `time` markers; Timelapse with an interrupt policy and unit cap; Speed through; the Montage slot (§9.2). |

### Modules
- **Registries:** systems and packs register event types, effect handlers, services, query functions, renderers, actions, panels, editors, data tables and materializers.
- **Pack split:** each pack is a **pure `core`** (engine, services, data) plus a **`ui`**. A boundary lint enforces no cross-pack internals and no core edits. The studio API may import only pure modules.
- **Layout:**
  - `src/engine/`: the pure engine;
  - `src/packs/<pack>/{core,ui}/`: twelve packs (the engine's own plus calendar, space, gear, vehicles, economy, weather, relationships, castlife, sheets, story and combat);
  - `src/backend/`: the host adapter (the only `spindle` importer), ledger storage, runs, pipeline, deciders, hooks, worlds, builder, push, child bridge;
  - `src/worker/`: the child lane entry, engine only;
  - `src/frontend/`: Preact;
  - `src/shared/`: types and contracts both sides use;
  - `src/studio-api.ts`: the studio seam (§15).
- `lumiverse-spindle-types` is pinned to `0.6.49`, the host's version (0.6.50 adds only `ctx.client`). One local augmentation module covers what the published types get wrong or omit: `ctx.ui.registerDomDecorator({mount, render, …}) → () => void` (the published top-level `registerDomDecorator` does not exist, and `instanceKey` filters anchors, so decorators omit it), `geometry.createResizeController(el, opts) → () => void`, and float and dock `persistGeometry`.
- The backend registers exactly **one** World Info interceptor and **one** context handler; a second registration replaces the first.
- **`userId`:** event payloads carry none. The host passes it as the handler's second argument, and the context-handler and interceptor contexts carry it. Operator-scoped (locally imported) installs must pass it on every host call. Every per-user cache is keyed by it.
- **Permissions.** The extension requests nine: `generation` (follow each reply, run the interceptor, make helper calls), `context_handler` (plan each turn before the reply), `chats` (read and update a chat's metadata, where a world chat records the world it plays and lists the world's lore book; find the open chat), `chat_mutation` (read messages and swipes, save the world's record on each reply, add palette, chip and map actions as messages), `world_books` (create the world's lore book and its slot entries, attach it, read the books chosen for Import from lore or lore indexing, and compare indexed lore with its book), `presets` (read the active preset to check where sections land and to lint it), `regex_scripts` (keep each world chat's script that hides JSON blocks, §6.7), `decisions` (the Decisions API) and `ui_panels` (the pill, the map and the palette sheet, which are float widgets; the drawer tab needs no permission). It deliberately does **not** request `characters`: character cards stay out of its reach (§4.2).
- **Safe keys.** Entity, place, track, kind and field ids, marker keys, editor keys and table keys are validated. Every lookup by a data id resolves own keys only, so `__proto__`, `constructor` and `toString` name nothing.

## 4. Data and persistence

### 4.1 World document and storage (D2)
- **Format:** a world is one JSON document, **`lre-world-v1`**, with a published JSON Schema (`schemas/lre-world-v1.schema.json`, its maps referring to the map schema by `$id`) and per-pack format versions.
- **Contents:** schema, entities, places, routes, grids, maps, rules, actions, checks, calendar, schedules, tracks, lore and its lookup table, classifications, enabled packs and their data tables, **pinned generator and data-table versions** (§9.2), realism defaults, tiers, the spawn cap (§9.4), the currency (code, symbol, minor unit, decimals, display format; Modern US by default), section templates, world-authored step templates, suppressed sources.
- **Taxonomy:**
  - **Classifications** are world-defined facets (`classifications`: label, one or several values per entity, order, description, `shown`), each a tree of values with optional parents. Entities hold values per facet in `class`, and free-form `tags`. Both are definitional: read live from the world for the entities it defines, so an Upgrade reclassifies a running chat, and kept in state for spawned ones, for those an Upgrade's `keep` pick held on to, for those a revision no longer defines (§4.3, `migrate`) and for those a GM edit classified (§7). Held in state, they win over the world's definition. A facet is hidden from the player unless `shown`, so its values fail closed; tags never reach Play.
  - **Groups** are entities of kind `group` or a kind extending it (the Relationships pack adds faction, family, household and company). `member_of` carries `role`, `rank` and `since`; membership is relations in state, so play can change it, and it follows member groups up to eight deep.
  - **Relation integrity:** a relation type names the kinds at each end, a cardinality, symmetry and an `inverse`. The `link` and `unlink` effects, and a spawn's relations, keep symmetric and inverse pairs two-sided in one atomic group and refuse a link that breaks the kinds or cardinality; a chat's initial state completes authored pairs written on one side, deterministically (sources in id order). Raw link events that packs write keep their own bookkeeping, so recorded ledgers fold as before.
  - **Selectors** (`{ids, kind, tier, class, memberOf, tag}`) pick entities: every constraint given must hold and a list matches any of its values; kind follows inheritance and a class value matches the values nested under it. A removed entity matches none (§7). A schedule's owner may be an entity, a group (its members that aren't groups), a kind or a selector, with that precedence when several set the same field of the same entity; equal-precedence overlaps are linted. An action param's `of` selector narrows its candidates. Formulas `kind_is`, `in_class`, `has_tag`, `member_of`, `tier_of` and `band_of` test one constraint each.
- **Maps (`lre-map-v1`):** the source of truth for a place's inside, one JSON document per map in `maps`, with a published JSON Schema (`src/shared/lre-map-v1.schema.json`).
  - **Two kinds.** A grid map draws an interior: `layout` rows of characters, each standing for what the `legend` says (`#` wall, `.` floor, `+` door and a space for nothing unless it says otherwise; a terrain, blocked or not; a `{place}`'s area; a `{zone}`; an `{item, place?}`, a thing lying on that floor square, in the legend place's area or else the room most of its neighbours belong to). An optional `cellMeters` sizes a square for walking times (80 m when absent). A node map draws a town or a region: `places`, with an optional `at` in its `size`, and `links`.
  - **Compiled when read.** `migrateWorld` compiles every map whenever a document is read. The map's places become children of its scope, with `cell` on a grid map (its `at`, else the middle of its area) and `layoutPos` on a node map (`cell` stays a position on the parent's grid). A grid map's grid (terrain, blocked cells, zones, and each place's area as a zone named by the place) goes to the scope. Links become routes, one per map and ordered pair, their modes merged. Each compiled item is marked with its map (`map`); compiling clears what an earlier compile made first, so the items stored in a revision or an export always match their maps, and a removed map takes what it made.
  - **Authored items win.** A map's place whose id the world already has, a grid id already taken, or a scope that already has a grid compiles without that part, and lint says so. Build shows compiled items read-only with **Edit in map**, and the draft refuses writes to them.
  - **Travel** follows routes: a map's links, or with none the free walking between a place's children. Without the grid system a grid map's walls and doors are drawn, not walked. With it, a floor plan without links gets walking routes from its doors: one per pair of rooms that a walk joins without crossing a third room's area, both ways, its minutes from the walk's cost at `cellMeters` a square; a room no door reaches is a warning. Links make their scope spread out (its places join only by routes), so a map links every place inside its scope or none; links that leave some out are a warning.
  - **Map settings** (`mapSettings`, optional): `display`, `knowledge` (the default; a place known of but never visited is drawn dashed) or `fog` (places never visited and, with the grid system, cells never seen lie under fog); and `grid: {movement, sightRadius}` (off and 3 by default). The player can override the display per chat (`lre.map`, stored in `runs/<chatId>/prefs.json`, never in the ledger). The engine builds the map for the display in force, the chat's pick or else the world's (`ViewPrefs`), so what it hides never reaches the frontend: in fog, a cell the POV hasn't seen goes out blank (no terrain, wall, area or thing), and `seen` lists the cells shown. Build's world settings write them and drop them when they equal the defaults, so the starters stay as they were.
  - **The grid system** (`grid.movement`): Space is at least 3 (§11), and the POV has a cell on the grid it stands on (its place's own grid, else the parent's grid when the place is drawn on it). Arriving puts it on the place's cell, else on a door at the scope's edge, else the middle. The `step` action and **Walk here** on the map walk it with `findPath` around blocked cells and through doors, at `gridMinutes`; stepping into a room's area makes that room its place. The POV never steps onto, or walks through, the area of a place it can't see unless it is already inside it, so a secret room is neither shown nor found on foot. Every position change is one `space.pos` event (`{entity, at: {scope, x, y} | null, from}`), so sight and triggers never see half a move; without the grid system, arrivals and steps write `set x` and `set y` as before. Things drawn in a legend start in their cells, and taking or dropping one moves it by `space.pos` too. `reach(a, b)`: `a` carries `b`, or both are on one grid within a square of each other, else in the same place; take, wear, use and taking a piece require it. `on_grid(thing)`: it lies in a cell. Lint checks the settings and the zone and cell triggers.
  - **Lint:** the schema; the id and the scope; ragged rows and the size; unknown legend characters, and legend places not on the map; places outside the grid or the frame; unknown places and maps (link ends, `opens`); id collisions; a scope that already has a grid; partial links.
- **Storage:**
  - `userStorage/worlds/<worldId>/index.json`: name, head, lore book id.
  - `rev/<revision>.json`: immutable revisions, named by their content hash (`hashJson`, a MurmurHash3 x86 128-bit digest written as 32 hex digits; not cryptographic).
  - Writes are atomic (temp file, then `storage.move`). Pinned revisions are never garbage-collected automatically.
  - Edits in Build go to a **draft**; **Commit** makes a new revision and **Discard** throws the draft away.
  - Revisions are cached per user, world and revision (an LRU of 128, never replaced in place). A read or export needs a revision the world's index lists. An open chat pins only the revisions its ledger references (its run's, the targets of its migrate markers on the chain or in held writes, and the revisions its records were planned under), loading each before a fold can need it, so evicting a cache entry never breaks a fold and a world's long commit history stays out of memory.
- **User-owned settings** (`userStorage/settings.json`): model-use preset, per-step provider, connection and model, budgets, realism and time controls (§9.1, §9.2). Realism and time **seed new chats only**, filling in what the world's own defaults leave out (or replacing them, with "Use my settings instead of a world's defaults for new chats" on); a running chat changes through markers. Imports never change them. A settings file is conformed field by field to its schema when read (types, enums, ranges; open maps keep safe keys only; a bad value falls back to its default).

### 4.2 Engine-managed lore book (D11)
- **Created per world:** "LumiRPEngine · <World>".
- **Slot entries** (Outcome, Montage, Scene, People, Stance, Money, Gear, Vehicles, Quest, Outline…):
  - stored `disabled:true, key:[]`;
  - **placed by the section's Position** (Build → Sections, `backend/book.ts` `entryPlacement`): `chat_depth` at that depth and role (the interceptor's placement repeats it each turn, with its direction, §6.4); `outlet` outlet-only (host position 8) with `extensions.outlet_name`, so the section lands at `{{outlet::name}}` and nowhere else; `native` before the character, where the host's World Info editor may move it. The entry records the Position it was placed for (`lumi_rp_engine.placed`), since the host doesn't read its outlet back; when the section's Position changes, the next ensure (commit, import, Upgrade, attach) moves the existing entry, outlet included. Until then a move in the host's editor stands, and a native entry an earlier build made, with no Position recorded, keeps its place. The book follows the world's head revision, so a chat still on an older revision has its sections placed as the head places them, except for its own chat-depth placement;
  - **delivery** is checked between generations rather than computed: when the inspector opens, a dry-run assembly of the chat's next prompt is searched for each section's `[Label]` heading from the latest generation, and each is marked delivered (`verified`) or "not delivered" (`unverified`). The search finds whether a section reached the prompt, not where it landed. Outcome, Montage and Outline hold only what a real turn planned, and a dry run plans nothing, so the check leaves them "unchecked" instead of reading them as not delivered; a clipped section stays unchecked too.
- **World lore** (`WorldDoc.lore`, `LoreEntryDef`): title, content, keys (keywords), links (entity and place ids), `aliases` (other names its subject goes by), `topics` (labels), `source`, group, priority, `visibility`, and `secret` with a `gate` and an `audience`. It is kept in the world document and versioned with it, and not synced into the book. The engine's helpers read it (§5.4); the narrator gets no lore section, though `brief` and the prose `outline` read public entries and what they write reaches the Outline slot. Lore entries an earlier build synced into the book are deleted (§6.4). `migrateWorld` fills nothing in an entry: every field after `links` is optional.
  - **Sources:** `{kind: "authored"}`, which is also what an entry without one counts as; `{kind: "book", bookId, entryId, hash}`, indexed from a World Info book, where `hash` is `bookTextHash` of the book entry's text at the time, so Build's drift check (`lre.lore drift`, `backend/lore.ts`) reads the book and offers its new text when the hash differs, or says the entry is gone; `{kind: "chat", chatId, messageId}`, saved from a chat; `{kind: "ai", status: "pending" | "accepted"}`, written by an indexing job from pasted text and accepted when the author applies it.
  - **Who reads it:** `visibility` is `public` (absent) or `known_by`, the two values the published schema takes: `known_by` is read only once the POV knows every person and place the entry links (one without links is linted `no_links`; any other value hides it, linted `unknown_visibility`). AI-written entries still `pending` are read by the GM only.
  - **Secrets:** a secret entry may hold the secret itself, the author's call case by case. `secret` is `true` or `false`; any other value (`"true"`, `"false"`, 1) reads as secret, so a malformed entry stays hidden (lint warning `secret_type`). It needs a gate (lint error `no_gate`). `audience` lists the helpers (`StepPurpose`) that may read it before the gate holds, none by default. The steps whose output reaches the narrator or the player (`PROSE_STEPS`: `brief`, `outline`, `choices`, `extract`) never read it, even when listed (lint warning `prose_audience`). A public entry that repeats a track's truth is a lint error (`secret_truth`).
- **The lore lookup table** (`WorldDoc.loreIndex`: `{terms, topics}`, a world part) is the curated routing for the engine's own lore searches, written by the author or an indexing job. `terms` maps a lowercase term or alias to entry ids; `topics` maps a topic id to its label and entries, in order. Entry aliases, keys, titles and topics feed Build's suggestions; the table is what a lookup reads. Documents written before it get an empty table. Lint (`engine/lore-lint.ts`): entry ids that don't exist (`missing_ref`), blank terms and terms that lead nowhere (`empty_term`), terms not in the form lookups match (`term_case`), a term that is another entry's alias (`alias_elsewhere`), a topic without a label.
- **Curation:** Build's Lore list (search across titles, aliases, topics, keywords and text), the entry form and the lookup-table editor; the indexing jobs (§9.7); and Save to lore in Play → Journal (`lre.lore save`): the latest reply, or an excerpt, becomes a draft entry whose `chat` source names the latest message holding the excerpt word for word, else the latest reply.
- **Retrieval** (§5.4): the engine's helpers read this model through its own search (`engine/lore.ts`), with visibility enforced there. Narrator delivery of curated lore is pinned for later.
- **User entries** are never linked, adopted, voted on or edited. Linking and adopting were planned and are retired with lore handling; `WorldDoc.suppressed` stays in the format and is not read.
- **Secret truth** lives only in the world document (a track's truth, or a secret lore entry) and reaches a slot through `mutated.content` at reveal; it never lives in a World Book.
- **Attachment:** the book is appended to the chat's `chat_world_book_ids` (read-merge through `runtimeState.write`).

### 4.3 Run (per chat; D6)
- **Chat metadata:** `lumi_rp_engine = {v, worldId, revision, runSeed, startedAt}`; `revision` is the world revision the chat was attached to, which its starting `migrate` marker repeats.
- **Ledger** in `spindle_metadata.lumi_rp_engine` on assistant messages: `{v, swipes:{[index]:Rec}, markers:[…]}`.
- **Ledger principles.** Four rules govern this section and §5; everything below applies them.
  - **P1. Path tags.** Every `post` entry and every marker records `at`, the parent path it was made under (the path hash, below, of everything before its message). An entry is **live** when `at` equals the current parent path, or when `at` equals its record's `path` (it was made with its record on-path, so it travels with the record into the record's rebased views). Only live entries fold and count toward the record and path hashes. Any other entry is **inert**: it changes nothing, and the Keep report lists it as "made on another branch". New `post` entries and markers go only to the head assistant message; that includes markers made during a regenerate of an earlier message, whose `at` therefore chains the new swipe. Switching back is therefore exact: whatever was made on the other branch is inert on this one. `keep[parentPath]` follows the same rule. Starting markers are the one untagged kind, since no branch made them (below).
  - **P2. One in-flight window per turn.** A turn is in flight from `GENERATION_STARTED` until its record commits, or until it closes as `degraded`, `stopped` or with its text accepted unread (§5.1 steps 5–6; Impersonate and Dry Run close at their end, with no record). Writes the user causes inside a window (materialization, ✎ fixes, chip confirms, markers, Keep, Discard, conversions and Re-read) are held in the journal and the UI shows "pending". Each is journaled, taking its `seq` and `at`, once every window that was open when it was made has closed. One that no longer validates then (the turn spent the money a correction took, or removed what it changes) is kept in the journal with its reason and shown as a notice, worded for the player, until dismissed; it is never dropped silently. A turn's post-step events are validated against exactly its **fold prefix**: the parent fold, the message's live markers with `seq ≤ markersApplied`, `pre`, then the earlier post-step events. Nothing made after the turn started can enter that prefix, so re-folding equals what was validated.
  - **P3. Re-narrate and Re-run.** A Re-narrate swipe copies the source's seed, `pre` events and `markersApplied`, plus the source's live ✎ fixes and materializations from `post`, keeping their `seq`s and their undos and taking the new swipe's parent path as `at`. Each copied entry is re-validated against the new swipe's own fold prefix (parent, live markers ≤ `markersApplied`, `pre`, then the copied entries and the message's later live markers by `seq`). An entry that depends on the source's post-step results (an entity they spawned, an event they recorded) fails and is dropped with a Journal note. The new narration renders from that same prefix, and its post-step events are stored as one `post` entry of kind `narr`, which takes its `seq` at commit, like `cont`, and the record's `path` as `at`, so it travels with the record like `events`. The source's confirmed chips and `cont` entries belong to its prose and are not copied. When the source cannot be reproduced (no planned source record, a pause, or a copied `pre` event that no longer validates), Re-narrate falls back to a Re-run, because P3 cannot hold without the source's prefix. A **Re-run** (a swipe or regenerate, or that fallback) carries the source's live ✎ fixes and GM edits the same way, with their `seq`s and undos and the new record's `path` as `at`, but at commit, after the new turn's events: each is re-validated event by event after the new `events` and the message's later live markers, by `seq`, so the GM's corrections stay in force over the new outcome and one the new turn makes impossible (an item its reply already lost) is dropped with the same note. The source is the nearest earlier swipe holding a record, the one swiped away from; a turn committed under a pause carries nothing. A Re-read edit (below) re-validates the entries it keeps the same way. A copy keeps its source's `seq`, so L9 reads "a `seq` never names two different entries".
  - **P4. The turn's own user messages.** The `pending` entry pins the hash of each user message the turn answers. The only change accepted to them is the host's macro re-render: a `MESSAGE_EDITED` for one of them that arrives after this generation's reply is saved and before its `GENERATION_ENDED` (normal generations only). The reply's save is its `MESSAGE_SENT` when the host creates it at the end, or its `MESSAGE_EDITED` when the host staged it empty before `GENERATION_STARTED`, which is what I observed on the reference host. I did not observe the re-render itself there, though the code path exists; the fake host generates it in both variants (§14), and a live run re-checks it. The commit's `path` uses that re-rendered hash, or the pinned hash otherwise. Any other change to them is a content edit (below).
- **`Rec`** = `{v, defHash, seq, swipeHash, path, readElsewhere, seed, pre, events[], post[], markersApplied, keep{}, status, undone[], intents{}, accepted, diag}`:
  - **`seq`:** taken from the chat-wide counter (shared with `post` entries and markers) when the record is made. A tombstone matches the stored record, `seq` included, so a new record identical to a discarded one is never taken for it.
  - **`swipeHash`:** hash and length of the swipe content the engine last read (simulated, or re-read).
  - **`path`:** the parent path the turn was planned on (path hash below). When it differs from the current parent path, the record folds as a **rebased view** (below). A **bare record** (no turn: it holds only an `accepted` identity or player entries on a message the engine did not simulate) has `path` `""`: it folds as a plain message, and its entries are live only where they were made, never travelling with it.
  - **`readElsewhere`:** set on a record read on another chain because the one it was planned on was gone when it committed (below, "During a generation"). It keeps its `path`, so the markers and entries made there travel with it, but it folds as a rebased view everywhere, on that path too, so Undo, Re-read edit and Retry read are refused on it, with a reason that names a Re-run as the way to change the reply (switching back can't help).
  - **`events`:** the turn's events, in order. The first **`pre`** of them were planned before the reply (resolution, simulation, timelapse, in-turn materialization); the rest come from the post steps (extraction with any `time.advance` and its catch-up, off-screen agents, tracks and rules). Materialization that happens during the turn is recorded **ahead of** the action's effects. A Re-narrate swipe holds only its copied `pre` here (P3). An event may carry a `group`: contiguous events with the same group are one unit (a purchase and its payment), which plans and reads keep or skip whole.
  - **`post`:** additions to this swipe after its turn, on the head record: player-facing materialization, ✎ fixes, confirmed chips, **Continue segments (kind `cont`, §5.1 step 5)** and a Re-narrate's post-step events (kind `narr`, P3). Each carries `at` (P1) and a chat-wide sequence number (`seq`), taken when it is journaled (P2); `cont` and `narr` entries take theirs when they commit.
  - **`markersApplied`:** the highest marker `seq` that was applied when this swipe was planned.
  - **`keep`:** the user's Keep-report conversions, keyed by the parent path they apply to (`{[parentPath]: {convert:[event refs]}}`). Absent unless the user converts an event.
  - **`accepted`:** `{len, hash}` of swipe content accepted without being read: an assistant edit (below), or a Continue that was not read (non-head, on a message without a record, stopped, or with a re-rendered prefix; §5.1 steps 5–6). Verification accepts content that matches `swipeHash` or `accepted`. Neither `swipeHash` nor the record hash covers it, so accepting text never moves a path. While it is set, the message offers **Re-read edit**.
  - `status`: `ok | degraded | stopped`. `conflict` is derived from a journal pause entry (below) and never written to records.
  - `intents`: always empty. The reading of a turn's text (`interpret`'s answer and any judgement) is kept in memory by the backend's turn bridge, keyed by the text, and a palette or chip action is recorded on its user message.
  - `diag`: a key into the `userStorage` diagnostics.
- **Markers** are message-level, **pure intents with no values**, each with `at` (P1) and a chat-wide sequence number (`seq`, shared with `post` entries). They attach to the head assistant message:
  - `migrate{to, merge?}`: world Upgrade; `merge` holds the owner's picks for the definition merge (below);
  - `resolve{domain, scope, to}`: realism change;
  - `time{control, scope, to}`: time-control change (tick per scene type, time scale, clock pause, interrupt policy, Speed through, Montage granularity, unit cap; §9.2, D29). Earlier builds also wrote an `npc_calls` starting marker (NPC model calls per timelapse, which nothing read): a stored one stays valid and sets nothing, and a request for it is refused;
  - `adjudicate{to}`: the adjudication policy, whole (§11); its body is read as the world's would be, so a named preset is its own values.

  A swipe planned after a marker is planned with it already applied; a Re-narrate is not planned and copies its source's `markersApplied` (P3). State-dependent output (such as detail for an entity that newly needs it) comes only from lazy materialization (§9.2), never from the marker itself.

  **The Upgrade merge.** A `migrate` without `merge` only switches the revision, as it always has; the state then notes which revision its entity definitions still come from. With `merge`, the fold also brings that revision's definition changes into the chat, as a deterministic step of the marker. It reads the revision the definitions come from, the new revision, the **touched record** and the picks, so the values come from the revisions and a swipe, branch or Re-narrate after the Upgrade folds it afresh.
  - **The touched record** (`state.packs.engine.touched`, names only) lists what applied events have written since the chat started: fields, relations by type and target, tier, agent flag, level overrides (a `resolve` marker for one entity counts), classes and tags (a GM edit's `engine.class`), life states (`engine.life`), entities play created and account balances. It is written inside event apply: every event type that writes entities' definitions declares `touches`, and the registry wraps them all. Both folds therefore keep it alike and an old chat rebuilds it on replay. "Never changed" comes from the ledger, so a value that changed and changed back still counts. A materialization's opening posting doesn't count. A value the merge takes is forgotten again. A coarse record's check leaves it out: what the stored record applies is what touched.
  - **Defaults and picks.** Each part is taken when nothing touched it and kept when something did. Its own pick, else one for its whole entity, overrides that. The picks (`take`/`keep` per field, aspect and relation, `take`/`keep`/`remove` per entity) are checked like the rest of the body, and one malformed pick makes the marker invalid.
  - **Coverage:**
    - an entity new to the chat is created as a first state creates it, its symmetric and inverse relations two-sided, an agent caught up to now, a thing a map lays in a cell lying there;
    - an entity gone from the revision stays, holding in state the classes, tags and visibility it was read with, or with `remove` leaves the world unlinked as a GM's Remove does;
    - kind, name, tier, agent flag, level overrides, classes and tags, and each field follow the rule above;
    - where an entity is, its place and cell (`place`, `x`, `y`), is one part, read as a chat starting on each revision holds it: a map that newly lays a thing already in the chat in a cell, or moves its cell, moves it there. Play moving any of the three, or putting the thing into something or taking it out (`in`), keeps all of them, and they are taken only together, so a cell never lands in another place than its own. A thing held inside something once the relations are merged keeps its holder: a taken placement may clear its place and cell but never gives it one, so a lantern in the POV's hands never lies on the map;
    - relations are merged by type and target from the relations a chat starting on each revision holds, and both sides of a symmetric or inverse pair change together or not at all;
    - a taken opening balance of an account whose holder's money is open posts the difference against the holder's outside account (`economy.reopen`), and a balance that moved in play counts as touched;
    - a track at a stage the revision no longer has goes back to its start, with a Journal note when the track is public;
    - a revision that turns the grid system on puts the POV on a cell of the grid it stands on (its own cell there, else where its place is entered) and it sees from there, as in a new chat.
  - **Preview.** The marker preview lists every change, each with its default pick and why (changed in play, or unchanged); a thing's place and cell are one Placement row. It words the same plan the fold applies.

  **Starting markers.** The first planned record that holds events carries the chat's starting state as markers: `migrate{to}` naming the world revision (and so its pins, §9.2), `resolve` for every domain's level and the band caps (§9.1), `time` for every time control (§9.2) and `adjudicate` for the adjudication policy (§11). They come from the world's own realism and time defaults, with the user's settings filling in what the world leaves out, or from the user's settings with "Use my settings" on; the policy is the world ruleset's, the user's where the world sets none, or the user's with "Use my check rules" on. Any Play changes made before that commit seed only these markers too, and later settings changes never reach a running chat. They are written once per chat, carry no `at` and are live on every path, so a switch, a Discard or a deletion never removes or re-seeds them. A chat started before the policy existed has no `adjudicate` marker and plays by its world revision's policy, else plain rolling. A Continue before that commit is accepted unread (§5.1 step 5). A reply read without a plan (the host gave up on the context handler) holds events but no starting markers, and the next planned turn writes them; a journal rebuilt from message metadata (a branch, an import, a lost journal) likewise counts the chat as started only once a message carries them.

  Starting markers are journaled as the marker check gives them (a world's half-minute tick as the minute it plays). An earlier build journaled them as the world writes them and hashed the chain with those, but flushed them to metadata clamped. A journal built from that metadata (a branch, an import or a rebuilt journal, by either build) held them clamped, and every record after them folded as a rebased view. So each time a chat opens, its starting markers are checked: where one is stored clamped but the chat's own marker, as the world writes it, differs from it and clamps to it, the written form is put back if that makes the first later stored path (a record's `path` or `keep` entry, a post entry's or marker's `at`) match the chain. The journal then holds them as written and writes them back to metadata. A chat this build started hashed its chain with the clamped markers, so its stored paths match as they stand and nothing changes.
- **Fold order per message** (its active swipe; live entries only, P1):
  1. this message's markers with `seq ≤ Rec.markersApplied`;
  2. `Rec.events`;
  3. `post` entries (including `cont` and `narr`) and this message's later markers, interleaved by `seq`.

  A message without a record, or with a bare one (§5.1 step 5), folds only its markers and a bare record's live `post` entries, by `seq`. Earlier messages fold completely before later ones. A Continue always follows its reply, so a chip confirm, ✎ fix, materialization or marker journaled before it started was already in effect when its suffix was generated; its `seq`, taken at commit, folds it after them. Writes made while it streamed are held by its window (P2) and fold after it.
- **Path hash:** chained over each earlier message's content hash (for an assistant message, its record's `swipeHash`, so `accepted` is excluded), record hash (`events`, `markersApplied` and the live `post` entries with their `seq`s, including `cont` and `narr`, plus the record's `keep` entry for the current parent path; not `accepted`) **and its live markers' hashes with `seq`**. No ids, `index_in_chat` or `defHash`. A rebased view therefore chains as itself, and a new turn on a rebased path is planned on, and chains through, the view. A record's `path` is computed at commit over the chain its turn was planned on, with the turn's user messages hashed per P4.
- **Seeds:**
  - **Action seed:** a **Re-run** draws a fresh random seed, recorded in `Rec`. A **Re-narrate** copies the source's seed (P3). Worlds in *same-roll* mode use `hash(runSeed, parent path, action)`.
  - **Interpretation** is cached per user text, so a Re-run re-samples the same probabilities with the new seed.
  - **Process seed:** `hash(runSeed, process, entity, slotIndex)`.
  - **Materialization seed:** `hash(runSeed, entity, domain)`.
- **Durability:**
  - records **and markers** are appended to the shadow journal, re-verified against the chain snapshot (below), and re-asserted through the queue. A re-assert writes a record's latest journal state, never a rebased view, and honors tombstones;
  - the journal also holds the per-chat `seq` counter, the chain snapshot, pause entries, tombstones and the writes held by open windows (P2);
  - the journal is stamped with its chat run (world id and run seed); one that belongs to another run is replaced by a fresh one. A damaged journal (unreadable, or the wrong shape down to a single entry: a pending turn, held write, pause, tombstone, chain entry, message or window; a message's ledger counts down to a record with a missing or wrong-typed field (undone and its refs, swipeHash, seq, path, keep, a post entry or an event), a marker that fails the marker check, or a swipe key that isn't a swipe index) does not make the chat unopenable: it is moved aside as `<file>.corrupt-<epoch ms>` (the next free millisecond if that name is taken, so a later set-aside never overwrites an earlier one), a warning is logged, the context inspector's warnings tell the player where the damaged file is kept, and a fresh journal opens; the records saved on messages still fold, and only what the journal alone held (edits, pauses, discards) may be gone. The settings, extraction-learning, chip and plan files are treated the same way (a damaged settings file means the defaults are used), and a chip entry with a wrong-typed field is dropped rather than shown. The extraction-learning file keeps only well-formed connection and type entries, so a wrong-typed one cannot break counting. A damaged snapshot file is ignored, since snapshots are only a shortcut. The journal is written only when it changed;
  - a record on a message that has the wrong shape (an imported or forked chat, another writer) is skipped: the message folds as if it had none, a warning is logged, and the context inspector's warnings name the message and swipe; if the journal still holds the record, it is written back;
  - on `MESSAGE_DELETED` of a message that carries markers, its live markers move from the journal onto the **nearest surviving assistant message before it**, keeping their `seq` and taking that message's current parent path as `at` (starting markers stay untagged); its inert markers are dropped with it. The move is journaled. That is the new latest message when the latest was deleted. If none exists, they go onto the earliest surviving assistant message; their `seq`s are below its `markersApplied`, so they fold ahead of its events.
- **Writes:** one queue per message, doing read-merge-write. Metadata is compared and written as it reads back, so a part the reader drops never looks clobbered; a re-assert that doesn't stick is tried at most 3 times in a row for the same ledger (a new ledger state, or a re-assert that sticks, starts the count again), so no other writer can keep the write and its edit event going round.
- **Coexistence warning:** the host replaces the whole `spindle_metadata` bag. Another writer can drop our data, and we can drop theirs. **G2 is required** for safe coexistence (§16).
- **Fold:** follows the order above, with the shadow overlay for every journaled entry not yet flushed (commits, `post` entries, markers). The cache holds each folded step under a key chained from the step before it (`keyOf`): the message's id and active swipe, digests of its text, its stored record (and whether that is tombstoned) and its markers, and whether the chain is paused there. Every 200th step, and each fold's last, keeps its state; the rest keep only their outputs. A fold also uses **persisted fold snapshots**: `runs/<chat>/snapshots.json` holds the 6 newest folded states, one at every 200th message of the chain (user messages count), each keyed by the build id, the start revision and the chain's path hash there, and none spans a pause. A cold fold starts from the furthest one on its chain, and caches the steps it rebuilt from it, so the next fold of that chain starts from the cache. The prefix walk memoizes digests of message text, stored records and markers.
- **Swipe identity:** records keyed by index and verified by `swipeHash` or `accepted`. The engine shifts them on `MESSAGE_SWIPED` `deleted` (its `swipeId`): the host shifts `swipes` but not `spindle_metadata`. Realigned or orphaned with a chip. Stored chips, shown by swipe, shift the same way: the deleted swipe's go, with the Outcome notes tied to them, and the later swipes' move down, their ids with them (a turn's chip names its swipe in its id), so none shows, or applies, on the swipe that takes their index.
- **Swipe switches, edits and Keep:**
  - **Chain snapshot.** The journal keeps the last-seen chain (message id, active swipe index, content length and hash). Every `MESSAGE_*` event, chat open and restart diff the chain against it, so classification never depends on event names (`MESSAGE_SWIPED` also reports `added` and `updated`). For an earlier message:
    - an **active-swipe change** (navigate, add, or delete of the active swipe; routine) or the **removal of a message** makes later records **rebased views**, with no pause (D4). The newly active swipe is verified on every switch: if it has a record and its content matches neither `swipeHash` nor `accepted` (for example after a swipe `PUT` made while it was inactive), the new content is accepted as an assistant edit (below);
    - a **content change** at the same swipe index (`MESSAGE_EDITED`, or a swipe `PUT`, which emits only `MESSAGE_SWIPED` `updated`) is a **content edit** on a user message and an **assistant edit** on an assistant message. Two host writes are neither: the macro re-render of the turn's own user messages (P4), and a Continue's own save, which is settled at the Continue's end (§5.1 steps 5–6);
    - metadata-only writes change nothing.
  - **Assistant edits keep the world.** Editing an assistant reply (a typo, a name, a trimmed line) is routine. An assistant edit, on the head or an earlier message, keeps the message's record and events and accepts the new content as the record's `accepted` identity; a message without a record gets a bare record holding `swipeHash` (its content before the edit, from the chain snapshot) and `accepted`. Nothing pauses, no path moves and the fold is unchanged (L38).
    - A one-tap **Re-read edit** chip re-extracts the message's post-step events from the new text with the `extract` step: they replace the events after `pre`, `swipeHash` takes the new content and `accepted` is cleared. Keeping is the default. A Re-read replaces the whole earlier read: its Continue suffix reads (the new text holds them), the proposals confirmed from its chips and its stored chips go; fixes and materialized detail stay, re-validated after the new read in fold order (one the new read makes impossible is dropped with a Journal note, as P3 drops a copy, so nothing kept fails on its own path). A change already confirmed from the old read's chip is undone with it, and the new read offers it again, once, if the new text still narrates it, so confirming the new chip charges once. The new read's chips offer whatever the new text still narrates.
    - Re-read is a user write (held in a window, P2). On an earlier message it changes the record hash, so later records become rebased views through the machinery below.
    - A read writes path-bound events, so Re-read edit and Retry read run only where the record folds as stored: on its own path, or on a bare record, which the read anchors at the current parent path (it becomes that message's turn record). While the record is a rebased view, the chip asks to switch back first. A read the ledger would refuse (the chat is paused, the record is a rebased view, or a Retry read is not on the head) is refused before its model call and leaves the chips alone.
    - A read runs once: it holds the per-(user, chat) chip lock and re-checks inside it that the read is still due, so a double tap reads once (one extraction, one confirm chip, one change).
  - **Rebased view.** A record whose `path` differs from the current parent path, or one read elsewhere (`readElsewhere`), folds as a computed, deterministic view. Its events and live `post` entries are **re-validated** against the current parent fold with the Keep report's defaults: an entry that fails (an item never bought; a decline made under another balance) is dropped, with a derived Journal note, unless `keep` converts it for this parent path. Accumulator deltas re-apply naturally. A group of events is kept or dropped whole, and a conversion of any member converts the whole group. Packs may add `guard`s to any event type, including the core `post` event, composed after the type's own check; the economy pack guards funds and limits from Money level 2, so a rebased cash payment cannot fold below zero.
    - **Nothing is written to later records.** New `post` entries and markers go only to the head, tagged with the parent path they were made under (P1), so whatever is made while the head is a view is inert on every other path, and switching back restores the original exactly, at any time.
    - A non-blocking **Keep-report chip** lists the dropped entries. A dropped group (a purchase and its linked changes) is one row with one Convert: its payment's line leads, with "(N linked changes)", and two groups that read the same stay two rows; lines outside a group still merge when identical. In the Journal a dropped group is likewise one note, at its first member's place, led by the same line as its Keep row, with "(N linked changes)" when it has more than one member. Each row has **convert to manual adjustment**, there is a one-tap **Discard**, and the inert entries appear as "made on another branch". A conversion is the only stored choice: a small `keep[parentPath]` overlay on the affected record.
    - The change chip and the Journal list what the fold applied: an event that was undone (Not an action?, an Undo) or dropped on a rebased view no longer reads as made, a Keep conversion still does, a `post` entry made on another branch does not, and a paused record lists nothing.
    - A new turn on a rebased path chains through the view. After switching back, that turn's record is itself a rebased view.
    - **Re-convergence:** a view whose state before it equals the state its record folded from on its own path with nothing failing, and which holds only the entries the record has there, folds as stored, without re-validation. The cache is keyed by what the record folds (its events, `markersApplied`, `post` entries, undos, `keep` and `path`) and its message's markers, not per path: the swipes of a message share their parent path. What no fold reads, such as an assistant edit's `accepted`, leaves the key as it was. A marker or `post` entry made while on the view's branch is live only there and can make a stored one fail, so a view holding one re-validates everything.
  - **A content edit** (an edit to an earlier user message, whose action text changed) puts later records into a derived `conflict` state (one journal pause entry, not a write per record) and pauses on Keep/Discard (L39). **Keep** clears the pause; the later records then fold as rebased views over the edited chain, with the same chip. Nothing after an unresolved pause folds, so a turn sent while paused gets no plan and commits `degraded` with no events; a pause that arrives while a reply streams leaves that reply unread (`degraded`, its plan kept).
  - **Discard** drops the later records: they leave the journal and are **tombstoned** by message id, `swipeHash` and the hash of the stored record, so re-assert and restart recovery never restore them, even after swipe indexes shift; their messages become plain (no record).
  - **During a generation,** an earlier swipe change or user-message edit, including one to the turn's own user messages (P4), leaves the new record off-path at commit (§5.1 step 5). An assistant edit moves no path, so it does not. Its reply is still read, and a Re-run's fixes carried, against the chain the turn was planned on (rebuilt with the swipes the plan saw, whose indexes follow a swipe deletion), so on that path it folds exactly as validated; on the chain in force it is a rebased view. When that chain is gone (a message or swipe the plan saw was deleted, or a swipe's text replaced), the reply is read on the chain in force and the record is marked `readElsewhere`: it keeps the planned path, so an Upgrade or another marker made there still applies to it, but it is a rebased view everywhere, even where the planned path comes back (the deleted swipe's text added again); a turn that reads and carries nothing holds only what was validated where it was planned, and stays on its path there. A reply whose own swipe is deleted while it streams is not read (its post steps make no model call) and commits nothing.
  - **Size:** per record, `seq` and `pre` (one integer each) and `keep` (absent unless the user converts); `at` (one path hash) on every `post` entry and marker; `accepted` is one `{len, hash}`. Per chat, the journal holds the `seq` counter, the chain snapshot, pause entries, tombstones and held writes. Switches and user-message edits write nothing per record; an assistant edit writes its record's `accepted` (or a bare record).
- **Record size:**
  - compact encoding; per-turn targets of about 2 KB (Grounded) and 6 KB (Simulation); hard cap 32 KB;
  - **overflow** first coarsens into period-close events, then writes continuation chunks in the same namespace; **never truncate**;
  - materialization is capped per write;
  - **timelapse and catch-up** record derivable events as per-period aggregates and period closes, never raw. They stay concrete deltas, so the fold is unchanged (L21, L29). A period close moves everyone once, which would lose or invent what the POV saw on the way (`space.seen`, §7), so a coarse record ends with one `engine.observed` event carrying the difference. A dense synthetic 600-agent-day segment produced 57k events when measured: 2.0 MB raw and 155 KB even after coarsening. Time was never the binding limit (38 ms per segment on a child process); the record size and the 5,000-event unit cap (§9.2) are.

## 5. Turn pipeline

### 5.1 Hooks and lifecycle

1. **`GENERATION_STARTED`.**
   - Journal a `pending` entry `{generationId, kind, messageId, swipeId}` from the event's `targetMessageId` and `targetSwipeId`. The event gives the target swipe for every type; for Continue its target message is null (G1), so the head swipe's content is captured instead (length and hash). A swipe or regenerate the player asked to Re-narrate (the swipe default, or the **Swipe as…** chip) names its source: the nearest earlier swipe of the target that holds a live record.
   - Open the turn's in-flight window (P2, §4.3).

2. **Context handler.** Non-required. One registration serves every user, so its timeout is the slowest before-the-reply wall clock among the users whose settings are loaded, plus 15 s for the plan, within the host's 120 s; before any user's settings are read it is the full 120 s, and it is registered again when a user's settings change it (the host replaces a registration), only when the value changes. The host replaces it inside the handler list its chain walks, which would skip the next extension's handler or run this one twice, so while a generation is under way (from its start to its end or stop) or a call of the handler is in flight, the change waits for the generation's end or the next settings read. The settings keep that wall clock at 105 s or less. The steps stop 15 s before the timeout, counted from the handler's start, so a plan lands even when the chat's queue ate into the wait.
   - **One queue per chat.** The handler runs on the chat's queue, after whatever is ahead of it (a previous turn's post steps included), and plans the newest unplanned `pending` entry of its generation's type. The interceptor reads that entry back from the journal (step 3), so the two calls need no matching.
   - **Not a world chat, Impersonate, Continue:** no plan. A Continue's target is known only at `GENERATION_ENDED` (G1); before the reply the narrator gets the head's sections.
   - **Dry Run:** no model calls and no writes; nothing is stored as a plan.
   - **Re-narrate** (P3, §4.3): no model steps and no new plan before the reply. The turn copies its source's seed, `pre` events and `markersApplied`, re-validates the source's live ✎ fixes and materializations against the new swipe's fold prefix, and its narration renders from that prefix, with the source plan's Outcome, Montage and Outline lines (from the turn bridge, or `plans.json`). Its post steps run at `GENERATION_ENDED` and commit as a `narr` entry. When the source can't be reproduced (its record is gone, or a `pre` event no longer validates), the turn is planned as a Re-run with a fresh seed but still runs no model steps: the plan reads only what the turn bridge keeps for the text, and there is no brief or prose outline. With no source at all (no earlier swipe holds a live record) it is a Re-run in full.
   - **Otherwise,** in this order:
     1. **Steps before the plan** (§5.2, §5.3), on the before-the-reply budget: the turn's intent (a palette or chip action recorded on its user message, else `interpret` when its signals fire; a typed duration over 24 hours becomes a Timelapse offer rather than the turn's action, while a typed *until*, a part of the day or the next shift or commitment, runs however far off it is), side by side with `npc.choose` for the agents with the POV in the tie zone or on an invitation, while a call stays free for the judge; then `puzzle.answer` when an Answer needs judging, `judge` when the player described their own action and its check is one the adjudication policy would roll with the player's words counting, or leaves to the story (§11), and any agent choice still waiting. The turn bridge keeps their answers in memory by the turn's text, and the plan reads them there. A Re-run of the same text, while they are kept, reuses the intent, a puzzle answer's probability and the judgement without asking again, but asks `npc.choose` afresh and plans with its new decisions. A step that can't start in time uses its fallback (§5.3).
     2. **The plan** (deterministic): fold to the parent with the target message's live markers applied (the target can be any assistant message: an explicit `message_id` on regenerate or swipe names an earlier one, that swipe is a turn on that message, and later records become rebased views, §4.3), and before the chat's first planned record its starting markers; then resolve the action with any judgement, a fight's round around it, the turn's **elapsed time** (§9.2), and simulation **in process** (D9): calendar processes, the day planner, LOD0, schedules, tracks and rules, and catch-up for up to four off-screen agents, bounded by deterministic units (days, agents, events), with any remainder left on the deterministic **backlog** (a timelapse runs whole inside its unit cap instead); then beats and the turn's chips. The plan goes on the `pending` entry with the chain it was folded on, the pinned hashes of the turn's user messages (P4), its seed and its events, and the turn bridge keeps its chips and section lines by seed (`plans.json` keeps the lines too), so a Re-run's plan never replaces another swipe's.
     3. **Steps after the plan:** `brief` and the prose `outline`, side by side on what is left of the budget. Their text goes to the Outline slot.
     4. No message writes.
   - **When the host gives up** before the plan is made, no plan is stored: the narrator gets the head's sections, and the turn commits `degraded` with no planned events (its reply is still read). The plan itself is never cut short by the clock, since simulation is bounded only by deterministic units (§9.2).

3. **World Info interceptor.** Registered at a **late priority**; it makes no model call (the host fixes 10 s).
   - **Not a world chat:** return `void`.
   - It renders the newest planned `pending` turn: that turn's fold prefix with its planned events, and the plan's Outcome, Montage and Outline lines (from the turn bridge, or from `plans.json` after a restart). With no planned turn (a Continue, a dry run, or a turn the host gave up planning) it renders the head's fold, with no plan lines. The declines the last reply left for the next turn lead the Outcome slot (§9.5).
   - Fill the slots within the section budget (§6.2, §6.3) and vote on the world's own book (§6.4). Entries of other books are left to native World Info. A planned turn's run goes into the inspector history (§6.5).
   - It writes nothing to the ledger or the journal, so an interceptor the host lets finish after its generation stopped changes no world state. It does write outside them: a planned turn's inspector record, with a push of the new view to the page, and, when the world's book is missing slot entries or still holds lore an earlier build synced into it, a repair of the book for the next turn.

4. **Narrator generation.**

5. **`GENERATION_ENDED`** (whenever content was saved).
   - **The saved reply** is `GENERATION_ENDED.content`, which equals `message.swipes[targetSwipeId]`. A normal turn saves with `MESSAGE_EDITED` (or `MESSAGE_SENT`, P4). Regenerate, swipe and both Edit-and-Send modes save with `MESSAGE_SWIPED` `updated`, whose `message.content` follows the *displayed* swipe when the user navigated mid-stream.
   - **An empty reply** (content `""` with no error; the measured model sometimes returned only reasoning) runs no post steps and commits the pre-reply events as `degraded`, with a **Retry** chip; the engine never regenerates on its own (D32).
   - **Post steps:**
     - extraction, including a bounded `time.advance` for narrated time beyond what this swipe has already applied, with that advance's catch-up, all or nothing (§9.2);
     - off-screen agents, in process (D9);
     - tracks and rules.
   - Validate with the bounds (§11), against the turn's fold prefix (P2, §4.3).
   - **Commit:** compute `path` over the chain stored with the `pending` entry plus the turn's user messages hashed per P4; compute `swipeHash`; write the record through the queue; clear the shadow `pending` entry; close the window (P2). The host keeps swipe navigation enabled during a generation. If an earlier message changed meanwhile, the new record is off-path: a swipe change makes it a rebased view (re-validated, with a Keep-report chip), and a user-message edit, including one to the turn's own user messages (P4), puts it under the pause; an assistant edit leaves it on-path (§4.3).
   - **Continue** (head target): `GENERATION_ENDED.content` holds the whole swipe, re-rendered. A head without a record (a greeting before the first turn) is accepted unread, like a non-head target. Otherwise extraction reads **only the suffix after the captured content**, and only if the content still starts with it. The host re-renders the whole combined swipe, and literal-brace shielding is keyed by the full content's hash, so a prefix with literal braces can change; then the Continue is accepted unread, as for a non-head target. The suffix is validated against the head's fold, and its events become a **`post` entry of kind `cont`** whose `seq` is assigned when the Continue commits. Everything journaled before the Continue started therefore folds before it, as it was in effect when the suffix was generated; writes made while it streamed are held by its window and fold after it (P2). `swipeHash` is updated; the head's own `path` chains only earlier messages and is unchanged. Effects already in the record are never re-read.
   - **Non-head guard.** `GENERATION_ENDED.messageId` names the continued message, and an explicit `message_id` can name any assistant message. If it is not the head assistant message, extraction is skipped and **no events are appended to any record**, since a non-head append would change every later path hash. This is an assistant edit (§4.3): the new content becomes the record's `accepted` identity (a bare record for a message without one), later paths are unchanged, and the message offers Re-read edit. Re-verification of a message edited during a Continue waits for its `GENERATION_ENDED` or `GENERATION_STOPPED` (step 6), because the save emits `MESSAGE_EDITED` first.
   - **Status:** `ok` when the turn was planned and its reply isn't empty; otherwise `degraded`. A turn without a plan still has its reply read, unless a pause sits before it. A pause that arrives while the reply streams (an earlier user message edited) also makes the record `degraded`: its plan stands, since it was made on its path, but the reply goes unread.
   - Unresolved confirm chips from the previous reply expire at this send; nothing is applied.
   - **Swipe index:** from the `GENERATION_STARTED` note, for every type. Both Edit-and-Send modes stage a blank swipe; in branch mode it is in the branch chat, where every event of the turn lands.

6. **`GENERATION_STOPPED`.**
   - The host lets the stopped generation's interceptor run to completion; it writes nothing to the ledger or the journal (step 3).
   - If content was saved (other than by a Continue), commit the pre-reply events as `stopped` (Retry read / Discard); otherwise drop the plan. Either way the window closes (P2).
   - **Retry read of a stopped reply.** The reply was saved unread, so a Retry read reads everything saved by then, a Continue after it included, and replaces what was read meanwhile (that Continue's suffix read, the proposals confirmed from its chips, and that read's chips). It then marks the reply read, so no change or charge lands twice and no Retry read is left; a Re-read edit of a stopped reply marks it read too.
   - **A stopped Continue.** The host saves `original + postfix + partial` onto the continued swipe without a macro re-render, then emits `GENERATION_STOPPED` with only the partial suffix and no `messageId`. Any message may also be edited meanwhile, and `updateMessage` emits `MESSAGE_EDITED` for every write, metadata-only ones included. The target is therefore found by content: among the messages whose `MESSAGE_EDITED` arrived during the Continue, the one whose swipe `targetSwipeId` (from `GENERATION_STARTED`) now ends with the partial suffix; if several do, the one whose content before the Continue (`swipeHash`, `accepted` or the chain snapshot) is a prefix of it. Every other edit made during the Continue is classified normally (§4.3). The new content becomes the target's `accepted` identity (a bare record if it has none), as for any assistant edit. On a head whose record holds events the chip is **Retry read**, which extracts only the partial suffix into a `cont` entry that takes its `seq` then; `swipeHash` then covers the text and `accepted` is cleared. Elsewhere it is Re-read edit.

7. **Restart recovery.** An assistant message whose reply was saved but that has no record is committed from its shadow `pending` entry as `degraded`, unread (*Not fully simulated*); that closes any window left open, and its held writes are then journaled (P2). A `pending` entry whose staged reply is gone (the host removes it at restart) is dropped: no record is made, its window closes and its held writes are journaled. Tombstoned (discarded) records are never restored; tombstones match by message id, `swipeHash` and stored-record hash (§4.3).

8. **Swipe options.**
   - **Re-run** (the default): a fresh seed (the same roll where the world's `seedMode` is `same_roll`). The turn's text keeps the reading kept for it (`interpret`'s answer, a puzzle answer's probability and any judgement) while the turn bridge holds it, so the dice change; `npc.choose` is asked again, and so are the brief, the outline and the post steps. The source's ✎ fixes and GM edits are carried after the new turn's events (P3).
   - **Re-narrate:** P3 (§4.3): the same seed, `pre` events and `markersApplied`, the source's ✎ fixes and materializations re-validated against the new swipe's fold prefix, narration rendered from that prefix, and new post steps committed as a `narr` entry.

9. **Activation check** on `WORLD_INFO_ACTIVATED`: the latest inspector record reads Activation confirmed once every entry the interceptor forced activated. It confirms activation, not delivery.

10. **Queued writes.** UI edits, between-turn materialization (`post`), confirmations, markers, Keep and Discard are journaled at once outside a window and held inside one (P2); each takes its `seq` and `at` when it is journaled. Journaled writes, late commits and re-asserts are flushed per message through its queue once no generation is in flight.

### 5.2 Steps
- **Phases:**
  - `pre`: context handler.
  - `post`: `GENERATION_ENDED`.
- **Engine steps:** `time`, resolve, simulate, `plan.day`, `beats`.
- **Typed-input classifier** (deterministic, no model). It fires when the typed text matches the world's action lexicon (risky verbs, transfer and travel verbs), names a known entity as a target, or carries a structural cue (quantities, money, destinations).
- **Decision steps** (routing per D10: `interpret` on the helper; `npc.choose` on the Decision connection; on a decision error, the helper, then engine-only; no blind retry):
  - Built-in purposes: `interpret`, `npc.choose`, `contradiction`, **`puzzle.answer`** (an authored opt-in per puzzle) and **`judge`** (the player's described approach, rated 0 to the policy's top with a one-line reason, and under narrative authority each outcome's probability, the exact odds given as guidance; §11). On the Decision route `judge` asks a score question and a choice question, so it has no reason.
  - The first option is the safe default.
  - Answers are probabilities, sampled with a seeded RNG. A helper yes/no without a probability is no answer.
  - On the Decision route, `interpret` asks a choice question over the available actions (and "none"); when the chosen action takes parameters, a helper call fills them (a second call), because the Decisions API answers questions, not parameters. With no call left for it, the action goes unread, as when the call fails (§12), rather than playing as one that names nothing.
  - `interpret` returns the input's actions in order, at most five (`ACTION_QUEUE_LIMIT`, §11): the first as its action, and the further ones it reads as engine actions as its queue, each with its params and its words as the player would type them ("I walk to Bluebird Coffee and buy a latte" → the walk, then "I buy a latte"); what follows them that no catalog action reads stays in words (`next`), and so do actions past the bound or after one it can't read. Time passing is never queued: it is `then`'s, and a timelapse given as the last action reads as `then`. A queued action is read as the first is (money in minor units, the player's way of paying, its ref params checked). One carrying an unrevealed secret is dropped on its own and what follows it is left in words; one that names nothing that fits ends the queue with its own decline chip ("Pay: couldn't"), and nothing after it is offered. What the turn doesn't play, the queued actions left when the queue stops and then `next`, is offered on one chip that sends it as the next message, so it plays as the next turn with its own prices, time and checks. Whether a piece is an action is the read's call, not the typed-input classifier's word list ("grab some milk", "get a latte" and "pick up groceries" are offered whatever its lexicon holds), so a piece is kept off the chip only as junk: a placeholder for nothing ("none", "N/A"); the message or its first action said again, compared as words without the player's "I", whose chip would play it twice; or words that only let time pass (a time word or cue, or the timelapse's phrases, with no other action's verb, phrase, amount or target: time after the actions is `then`'s). A piece is at most 120 characters (a longer one is dropped, never cut). One carrying an unrevealed secret (§7), or naming someone or something the POV can't see or what leads to a secret lore entry (its title, an alias, a key, a topic or a lookup term), is dropped on its own, and the actions still play. Nothing that follows an action that names nothing that fits is offered, neither the rest nor its `then`, and time the message lets pass after its actions isn't offered while some of them are left. On the Decision route only the helper call that fills a chosen action's params can read further actions, and it is shown only the chosen action, so it queues only more of that action (a second Buy) and leaves the rest in words for the chip: the Decisions API answers one choice, and choosing each further action there would cost a choice and a params call apiece. A choice with no params, or no call left to fill them, has none.
  - `interpret`'s ref params must name the right real thing: an entity of the param's kind, or a place where it takes places. A read that doesn't (a name the story made up, as in "I pay Rena" with no Rena in the world) plays as plain roleplay, and its reply gets a decline chip, "Pay: couldn't", saying why: what the param named, as the player typed it ("Rena isn't anyone in this world"), a real thing by its name when it is of another kind ("Bluebird Coffee isn't a person"), or that it has none. It repeats no name the player didn't type, none the POV can't see, none that leads to a secret lore entry (its title, an alias, a key, a topic or a lookup term), none carrying an unrevealed secret, and nothing too long for a name ("it names no one you know"). As for a refused change (§11), the next Outcome slot is told the action didn't happen, and *Remove it* or *Accept* rewords that note. Both are about the player's message, not the reply, so a Continue, Re-read edit or Retry read of the reply keeps them.
- **Prompted steps:**
  - World step templates use the engine placeholders only: `{{world}}`, `{{pov}}`, `{{place}}`, `{{clock}}`, `{{scene}}`, `{{facts}}`, `{{present}}`, `{{outcome}}`, `{{beats}}`, `{{brief}}`, `{{user}}`, `{{reply}}` and `{{recent}}`. The prompted steps fill them all: `brief`, `outline`, `choices`, `extract` and every `effects` template. An `interpret` template on the helper route gets only `{{world}}` and `{{pov}}`, and the decision steps' templates (`npc.choose`, `puzzle.answer`, `judge`, a `decision` contradiction template, and `interpret` on the Decision route) are sent as written, with no placeholder filled. `{{brief}}` is the turn's brief once its step has run: an outline template naming it waits for the brief, the steps after the reply read the plan's (while the backend still holds the plan, as for `{{outcome}}`), and in the brief step itself it is empty. `{{recent}}` is the Journal's latest five lines before the turn, as the player sees them there (only what the POV could see, worded with labels); a ✎ fix or GM edit the player may see is a line of its own, so it takes one of the five. Host macros in a template are not evaluated, because helper calls are quiet generations that skip prompt assembly; the import lint lists any macro a template carries. Every placeholder is player-visible content.
  - Output `text` or `effects` (bounded like extraction; money amounts in an `effects` output are plain amounts in the world's currency as written, 8 for $8, and the engine converts them to minor units as it does for extraction). Built-in purposes: `brief`, `outline`, `extract`, `choices`.
- **Where each output goes:** `brief` leads the Outline slot as "Brief: …"; a prose `outline` adds Outline lines after the engine's beats; `contradiction` becomes a chip (Re-narrate / It's fine) plus a correction line in the next Outcome slot; `choices` become chips that send the suggestion as the player's message, and so does what the turn didn't play of `interpret`'s read (the queue's leftover actions and `next`); `effects` templates produce proposals that go through the extraction modes and bounds, after the reply only. Each brief, beat and suggestion is leak-checked whole, before any cut, and withheld when it carries an unrevealed secret anywhere in it; only then is a brief cut to 600 characters and a beat to 300, while a suggestion longer than 120 is dropped rather than cut. The narrator sees prose steps through the slots it already reads, and corrections and suggestions stay under the player's control.
- **Outline:** `beats` always renders the deterministic outline; prompted outline steps stack on top.
- **Signals:** `typed_input`, `classifier`, `reply`, `scene_change`, `slot_crossed`, `day_start`, `beat`, `agents_present`, `present`, `offscreen`, `tie_zone`, `invitation`, `timelapse`, `conflict`, `every_n:<k>`, `always`, `never`. A world step template for the retired `select` purpose is dropped when its world loads.
- **Secrecy batching rule:** questions that carry secrets never share a call with prose-producing steps.

### 5.3 Model-use presets and budgets (D3, D13, D25)

| Preset | Pre-reply (context handler) | Post-reply | Model steps a turn: most | Engine budget (sections) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Lean** (floor) | `interpret` when the classifier fires; `puzzle.answer` when an Answer needs judging | `extract` | narrator + up to 2 | 300 tokens |
| **Standard** (default) | `interpret` when the classifier fires; `judge` when the adjudication policy needs it; `npc.choose` on `tie_zone`/`invitation`; `puzzle.answer` when an Answer needs judging | `extract`; off-screen `npc.choose` on `tie_zone` | narrator + up to 4 | 600 tokens |
| **Full** | `interpret` on every typed message (a palette or chip action, or a reading kept for a Re-run, needs no call), `npc.choose` for all present agents, `judge` when the policy needs it, `puzzle.answer` when an Answer needs judging; then `brief` and the prose `outline` | `extract`, off-screen `npc.choose`, `contradiction` | narrator + up to 8 | 1000 tokens |
| **Custom** | Any step, signal, provider or model | Same | Depends on the steps enabled; the call meter shows it | User budgets |

**Measured calls per turn** (the test suite's busiest case: one typed turn that fires every signal, with the classifier, an open tie for an agent with the POV and one elsewhere, and a reply; the narrator is not counted):

| Preset | Calls | Which |
|---|---|---|
| Lean | 2 | `interpret`, `extract` |
| Standard | 4 | `interpret`, `npc.choose` ×2 (with the POV before the plan, off-screen after the reply), `extract` |
| Full | 7 | Standard + `brief`, prose `outline`, `contradiction` (an eighth call judges a puzzle answer or the player's approach when one needs it; an Answer has no check, so never both) |
| Custom, every step on `always` | 8 | Full + `choices` (`puzzle.answer` runs only when an Answer needs judging) |

The most a turn can run is what the preset's steps and phase budgets allow: each step takes one call from its phase's budget when it starts. Full's 8 is the 7 of the busiest tested turn plus a judged puzzle answer or approach. On Standard the judge's call is held ahead of `npc.choose`'s, so a judged turn whose action `interpret` read spends both calls and leaves the choice of an agent with the POV to the numbers; when `interpret` spends none (a palette or chip action with words of the player's own, or a reading kept for a Re-run), both run. Lean never judges an approach, and `puzzle.answer` is on in every preset until turned off. No typical count is given: the earlier figures were measured with the retired lore-selection step and haven't been measured again. The call meter shows each turn's real count. A step can cost more than its one call: each lore lookup round takes one from its phase's lookup budget (§5.4), and a JSON repair, a Decision-to-helper fallback or `interpret`'s one retry after a transient failure adds a call that no budget is charged for.

**Budgets**
- Budgets are a **call count and a wall clock per phase for model steps**. Simulation is bounded only by deterministic units.
- Defaults, as calls and time before / after the reply: Lean 1 (20 s) / 1 (30 s); Standard 2 (30 s) / 2 (45 s); Full 8 (60 s) / 4 (60 s).
- **Lore lookups** have their own budget per phase (`PhaseBudget.lookup`: rounds, and the window from the phase's start in which one may begin), apart from the calls: Lean none; Standard 2 (15 s) / 1 (20 s); Full 3 (30 s) / 2 (30 s) (§5.4). The calls counted above leave them out; the call meter counts them.
- Before the reply, the turn's intent and the choices of agents with the POV run side by side (the choices only while a call stays free for the judge), then `puzzle.answer`, `judge` and any choice still waiting; after the plan, `brief` and `outline` side by side, except an outline template naming `{{brief}}`, which runs after the brief. After the reply, independent steps run side by side.
- When a phase's calls or time run out, each remaining step uses its fallback: utility-only NPC choice, beats-only outline, a check rolled as if unjudged (one the story would decide goes to the dice), or plain roleplay plus extraction. The post phase keeps the user's deadline, with no hidden floor: a budget of 0 ms means no post call.
- **Host caps:** the context handler waits at most 120 s (§5.1); World Info interceptor 10 s; decisions 30 s per call. Each Import or Refine model call has its own 120 s deadline besides cancel.
- **Reasoning models:** helper calls to a reasoning model need `max_tokens` of about 1,500 or more; at 160 they failed because reasoning counts toward the limit.
- The call meter shows each turn's calls, tokens, latency and lore lookups in the HUD, with averages and the 95th percentile in Settings. It is kept per user in `userStorage/call-meter.json` (the last 200 turns and the current one), so a restart keeps its numbers.
- **Lean** is the documented floor of D3 and does not meet "a model decides" on its own.

### 5.4 Lore for the engine's helpers

Every model step reads the world lore it needs (§4.2) through the engine's own search; native World Info activation plays no part.

- **The search** (`engine/lore.ts`, pure): `loreSearch({query?, terms?, links?, topics?, purpose, limit, tokenBudget})`, `loreGet(id)` and `loreList(topic)`. It reads the folded state and the world only and never calls a model.
  - **Ranking**, compared in order: exact hits on lookup-table terms, aliases, keywords and titles (in `terms`, or said in `query`); links to what the step is about; topics asked for or named in the query; the query's words in the entry (stop words dropped, each word counted at most 3 times); the author's priority. Ties go by id. Of a group's entries only the best is returned.
  - **Snippets** are `Title: text`, cut at a word to the token budget, with the caller's counter: the host tokenizer's count once it has counted that text for the user (fetched in the background, as for sections, §6.3), else the section estimate.
  - **Visibility is enforced inside it:** a secret entry whose gate doesn't hold reaches only the purposes in its audience, never a `PROSE_STEPS` purpose or an `effects` template; a gate that is missing or can't be evaluated never holds; once it holds, the entry reads as public. A `known_by` entry follows §7 for its links and fails closed. The GM (`purpose: "gm"`) reads everything.
- **Policy per step** (`StepConfig.lore`: `{policy, tokens, transport?}`): `none`, `prefetch`, `lookup` or `prefetch+lookup`. A settings file without one reads with its own preset's values (Custom fills in from Standard), and a step no preset configures prefetches 300 tokens. Presets: Lean prefetches 200 tokens and never looks up; Standard gives `interpret`, `extract`, `npc.choose` and `judge` both at 400; Full both at 600 for those and `contradiction`, and a 400-token prefetch to `brief` and `outline`.
- **Prefetch:** before a step's call, the index is searched with the step's own inputs: the player's message, the reply, the POV, its place and the places holding it, the people it can see there, the visible tracks' labels, stages and cues, a decision's agents and options, or a judged action's label, targets and place with the player's words. The hits go into the prompt as a "World lore" block, or into the state a Decision call reads, at no extra call. A world without lore changes no prompt.
- **Lookups** (JSON helper steps, through quiet generation): the model is offered the lore tools (`lore_search`, `lore_get`, `lore_list`; JSON mode is off while they are) or, on a connection seen refusing tools, the two-phase `{"need": [...]}` form. The host has no tool-role messages, so a round's results (each round within the step's lore tokens, at most 3 asks) go back in a follow-up user message and the call is made again, after the model's ask replayed with only the calls the round answered. A `lore_list` answer names at most 12 entries, each costing the round's tokens as a snippet does, and says how many more there are; a call to a tool that isn't a lore tool is refused, never run as a search. The instructions name up to 12 of the lookup table's topics, and only those the step can read an entry under, so a topic only secrets it can't read are filed under stays unnamed.
  - **Transport:** `auto` uses tools until a connection refuses them. A tool call that fails non-transiently is asked again in the need form on the round already held, and once that works the refusal is kept per connection, in memory and in `userStorage/lore-tools.json`. A step may pin `tools` or `need`.
  - **Budget:** each round is one call on the phase's lookup budget (§5.3), held before the model is offered it and released when it answers instead, so parallel steps never overdraw it; lookups therefore go to steps in priority order. A round starts only within the lookup window and while a third of the phase's time is left. A step that can't hold one gets no tools or need form and answers with its prefetch. Rounds are marked as lookups in the call meter.
- **Decision pre-pass:** the Decisions API takes no tools, so a step on the Decision connection whose policy looks up gets one bounded helper call on the default connection first, asking what lore the decision needs (tools or the need form), on a lookup round. It never answers the decision; its hits join the prefetch in the decision's state.
- **Traces:** each step records its searches, the entries it was given with their token cost, its lookups, the transport and why a lookup didn't happen, for the inspector (§6.5), within what the player may know (§7).
- **Secrecy:** §7.

## 6. Context control: the engine's sections

The engine no longer selects user lore for the narrator; its own helpers read world lore through the engine's search (§5.4), and the narrator gets no lore section, only what `brief` and the prose `outline` write after reading public entries. In a world chat the World Info interceptor puts the engine's sections into the prompt and votes only on the world's own book. Every other book's entries, and recursion, are left to native World Info.

### 6.1 Retired: lore selection
The interceptor no longer builds candidates from user books (with pins, D19), skips pass-through books, pre-ranks a hit set capped at 64, asks the `select` step with its per-candidate cache (D13), spends a lore budget, votes user entries off, suppresses adopted sources (D11), syncs world lore into the engine book, overrides recursion or warns about Council World Info. Native World Info decides user entries in world chats, Council's included. World lore stays in the world document (§4.2), where the helpers read it (§5.4).

### 6.2 The section budget
- The slots spend the **section budget** (§5.3) in rank order. **Outcome** (rank 0) is never evicted; a later slot that doesn't fit is clipped, left out for that turn and shown as clipped in the inspector.
- The inspector's budget-used figure is the section budget spent; only an Outcome that alone exceeds the budget can take it past the total.
- **Hint lines** (a renderer marked `hint`: Stance's cues, §6.6) follow what the POV doesn't know, so they ride last: once every section has its place, each section's hints go in if what is left of the budget covers them, else the section goes without them, and a section with hints alone isn't sent. Clipping, the sizes and the budget-used figure count a section's own lines only, so an off-screen change (a flag set, a lore gate opening) never shows in what the player sees.

### 6.3 Slots and rendering
- **Compact lines,** at most one per fact. A section costs the token count the host's tokenizer gives for its exact rendered text (header and bullets included), cached per user and text, since the host counts with each user's own main model. Until the host has counted that text for that user, or when it cannot within a second, an estimate stands in: 3.2 characters per token, plus 2 for the header, 3 for each line break and 1 for each number, which stays at or above the host tokenizers on the shipped worlds' sections. A count that is not a finite number from 0 to 4 times that padded estimate is ignored, so the estimate stands in and a bogus count cannot clip every section. Counts are fetched in the background, so the interceptor never waits on the host.
- **Dynamic values never form a macro.** World Info content is macro-evaluated by the host, so everything the engine inserts (names, prices, model output, proposals) is neutralized: every brace becomes its fullwidth look-alike (｛ ｝), the host's escape sentinels (the C0 control characters `\x00`–`\x02`, which its evaluator turns back into braces on a second pass) are dropped, and the `<user>`, `<char>` and `<bot>` shorthands are broken with fullwidth angle brackets. Section labels and templates are covered too, and a section template may hold only `{{lines}}`. Breaking only `{{` pairs is not enough: it leaves `{{{…}}}` and escaped braces runnable by the host's lexer, and backslash escaping keeps the text literal but delivers visible backslashes. **Authored lore keeps its macros**, as in native World Info, and a world import lists every macro, sentinel and shorthand in its lore and templates for the player to review before playing.
- **Sizing:** native context clipping sees the real size. Slots are fixed overhead and are never clipped by the host; when the fixed prompt exceeds the context budget, the host drops all chat history, including the latest user turn. Nothing checks the engine budget (§5.3) against the connection's context, so keeping it well inside that is the user's call.

### 6.4 The vote
For world chats:
- **`disabled`:** every engine-book entry the interceptor did not fill this turn, so an unused slot, or a lore copy an earlier build left in the book, cannot activate natively even if someone re-enabled it in the host's UI. The host keeps a `disabled` from any interceptor in the chain, so no other interceptor can revive it.
- **`enabled`** and **`forced`:** filled slots only.
- **`mutated.content`:** slot texts.
- **`mutated.placement`:** a section's own chat-depth position, when the world sets one (`backend/book.ts` `placementOf`). The host accepts only `{type:'chat_depth', role, depth, direction}`; an outlet or native section has none and goes where its entry is stored (§4.2).
- **Nothing else:** no entry of another book is disabled, enabled or forced, and `activationOverrides.disableRecursion` is not set. Recursion follows the user's World Info settings, so a slot's text, like any constant's, can trigger a user entry. Slots carry no unrevealed truth (§6.3, §7), so this changes prompt content, not secrecy.
- **Leftover lore:** a world lore entry an earlier build synced into the book is deleted the next time the book is ensured (commit, import, Upgrade, attach), or after a generation whose interceptor sees one; the hook itself reads nothing from the host.

### 6.5 Inspector and limits
- **Inspector:** per generation, the engine sections with their token cost, clipping and delivery, the section budget used, and the activation confirmation. A section left out over the section budget shows a "clipped" badge with "left out: over the section budget" and is never shown as delivered; a delivery check leaves it unchecked, since it was not in the prompt. It keeps the last 10 interceptor runs per chat, with the last delivery check by slot, in `userStorage/runs/<chat>/inspector.json`, read when the chat is first viewed, so the history and its badges survive a restart; inspector dry runs stay out of the chat history. Its warnings cover a detached book or missing slot entries (both repaired for the next turn), the D32 preset lint and journal notices.
- **Lore traces** (§5.4): each record also carries, per model step of its generation, the step's searches with what each matched, the entries it was given with their token cost and whether they were cut, its lookup rounds, the transport and why a lookup didn't happen; the reply's steps join the record after the reply, and the history keeps them. The Context panel shows them to the player, so they carry only what the player may know. A secret entry whose gate doesn't hold isn't in them at all: neither listed nor counted in what a search matched. An `npc.choose` trace shows only whose choice it is, by name ("Dev's choice"), never the options it searched with, which are private plans; for the same reason it is `private`: its model's lookups show as their kind alone, no search says how many entries it matched, and the entries it was given aren't listed, since their titles, number and token cost would all say something of those plans. A batch of choices with no one the POV may know has no trace. A step that may read a secret whose gate doesn't hold shows each search, read and list its model asked for as its kind and what it found, never its text. Any other search or title that carries a secret's text, or names someone the POV doesn't know or a place or thing it can't see (read as a `judge` reason is, `namesAny`: a person by any word of their name, however short in another script), is withheld, and so is a lookup the model asked for that names a secret entry (its id, title, an alias, a key, a lookup-table term or a topic it is filed under). Words the player or the reply said that turn are never withheld: that would only show which of them are secret. The history is stored as shown, and read back by the same rules: one kept before them (`v: 1`) shows its searches' kinds and counts without their text, one that lists a secret entry isn't shown, and an `npc.choose` trace reads as one kept now, uncounted and with no entries.
- **Other extensions' World Info interceptors:** they cannot switch on an engine-book entry the engine left off; what they do to user entries is theirs. The README lists this caveat.
- **If the backend stalls:** the slots stay disabled, so they are absent (fail-closed), and the inspector has no record for that generation; a turn the context handler couldn't plan in time commits `degraded`.

### 6.6 Stance
The **Stance** slot (`stance`, written by the Cast life pack) tells the narrator how the people with the POV stand toward it and what they are about, in one or two lines each. It sits beside People (chat depth 1) and takes the last default rank (9), so on a tight section budget it is the first left out, never displacing a section a world already relied on; a world that wants it sooner gives it a lower rank in its own sections. Its cue lines are hint lines (§6.2): they go in only from what the budget has left after every section, and the Context panel's size for Stance counts its people lines alone, since the cues change with what the POV doesn't know.
- **Who:** every person at the POV's place that the POV can see (`canSeeEntity`) and who acts. "Acts" is one predicate (`acts` in the pack), today someone not removed, so a person's life state can decide it in one place. Nobody else gets a line, and no line names anyone the POV can't see.
- **What they know of the POV:** whether they know it (a `knows` relation to it, or regard toward it: nobody regards a stranger) and, from Relationships 1 at them, their regard in the world's band words, never numbers: the overall band and, from Relationships 3, each axis whose band differs from it (`relationships.words`: "knows you; regard: warm (trust: cold)").
- **Intent:** their activity as People reads it (field visibility, an agenda's step as "busy"); for each public agenda they own, its current stage's cue, or the stage label Cast shows when it has none; and for an LOD1 agent from Cast life 3, the block of today's plan that their own recorded choice settled (`npc.choose`, or `castlife.decide`), the one under way or the next, named as Cast names the day (a block of an agenda that isn't public is "Busy").
- **Cues:** behaviour the author wrote for a secret, never the secret. The current stage's `cue` of every other track they own (secret ladders, quests, puzzles, and agendas that aren't public), while the track still keeps something from the POV: a stage truth not yet revealed, or the track itself, which the POV may not be told of (public, revealed, or `known_by` and known). And the entity's own `cues` (`EntityDef.cues`), each keyed to exactly one secret: a lore entry, while it is secret and its gate doesn't hold; a track, while it keeps something from the POV as above; or a story flag, until it is set. A cue keyed to none, to several or to one the world doesn't have never shows. Cues are read live from the world, so an Upgrade changes them in a running chat.
- **Hint risk:** a cue, an agenda's words or a decision's label that carries an unrevealed secret (`findLeaks` against `unrevealedSecrets`) or names anything the POV can't see (`namesHidden`: a hidden person by any word of their name, a hidden thing, place, track label or secret lore title by all its words) is dropped on its own, and the rest of the person's lines stay.
- **Narrator note:** the shipped template opens with a note: the narrator knows only what the POV knows, these lines say how people behave, and it never states what a cue only hints at. It is the section's template, so a world edits it like any other.
- **Not a fact:** the helpers' fact lines (`factLines`, read by `brief`, `outline`, `choices`, `contradiction` and the step placeholders) leave the section out. Its cues are hints to be played, and a prose helper given one could state it; the contradiction check would read a reply that plays a cue loosely as contradicting it.
- **Lint** (`castlife.cues`): an entity's cue with no text, standing for no secret, for more than one or for one the world doesn't have, or for one that hides nothing (lore that isn't secret, a public track with no truth), and cues on anything but a person; an entity's cue that holds a track's truth or a phrase of secret lore (an error), or names an entity, place, track or secret lore entry the player may not know (a warning: it is dropped while that stays hidden). A stage cue of a person's own track gets the name warning too, and names are read as the runtime reads them (`namesAny`), so a part of one counts. A stage cue can also show in the Quest panel and section, so one that says a secret entity's or place's name any other way than whole ("Vex" for Vex Morrow) is an error; truths and whole secret names there are `story.leaky_label`'s.

### 6.7 Hidden JSON blocks
- **Why:** a preset may ask every reply to end with a `<json>…</json>` block (a tracker, a ledger). In a world chat the names in it would trigger World Info entries and mislead the engine's own reads, and the player sees raw JSON.
- **The scripts:** four regex scripts the engine creates and owns (`backend/json-hiding.ts`, in the regex panel's LumiRPEngine folder). World Info's keyword and vector scans read a chat's messages as saved, before any prompt script runs (assembly activates World Info on the stored messages and applies prompt scripts to the assembled prompt afterwards; the dry run and the council read stored messages too), so the exclusion has to be saved with the reply, and everything else should read the reply without it. The frontend runs display scripts by sort order whatever their scope; the backend runs response and prompt scripts global first, then the chat's, each by sort order. All four match a block as the host reads one, `<json>` in any case and without attributes up to the first `</json>` outside a JSON string, so a wrapper never lands inside a block, and the block stays byte for byte as written, so `{{jsonBlock}}` and other readers still find it.
  - **LumiRPEngine · JSON blocks out of World Info**, the world chat's, runs on `response` (a Continue bakes only what it added) and again on an edit, last among the chat's scripts (sort order 100,000), after the global and character ones: it wraps each block in AI output as `<span wi-exclude><json>…</json></span>`, which the scans skip and which hides nothing. A lookbehind keeps a block from being wrapped twice, or inside an earlier build's wrapper.
  - **LumiRPEngine · JSON blocks as written (display)** and **(prompt)** are the user's: global, at sort order −100,000, so each runs before every other script on its target, and they take that span off a block, matching nothing else. A preset's display script that draws the block as a tracker, a prompt script that strips old blocks from the history (anchored on the block or not), markdown and the narrator all read the reply as the AI wrote it.
  - **LumiRPEngine · Hide JSON blocks**, the world chat's, runs on `display` only, at sort order 100,000, after the other display scripts, and wraps each block still raw in `<span hidden>`: a block a preset drew as a tracker isn't raw any more, and keeps its tracker.
- **Lifecycle:** a world chat's two scripts are created when a world is attached, and checked the first time the engine meets a world chat after it starts (the chat opened in the UI, or its generation), so a chat attached before this build, or one whose scripts were deleted in the regex panel, gets them back; the user's two unwraps are checked with the first world chat the engine meets in a run, before the chat's own. The single script an earlier build wrote (on both targets, wrapping in `<div hidden wi-exclude>`) is rewritten in place as the hiding, a script an earlier build wrote differently or sorted elsewhere is brought up to date, and extra copies are removed; whether a script is switched on stays the user's. **Settings → Preset → Hide JSON blocks in world chats** (on by default) removes every world chat's hiding when turned off, and when turned on brings back the open chat's at once and every other one's as the engine meets it; the exclusion and the unwraps stay, so replies show their blocks as the AI wrote them and World Info still skips them. Deleting a world removes its chats' scripts, since they aren't world chats any more (each script records its world), and they never come back: the engine writes a chat's scripts only while its world exists. The host doesn't delete a chat-scoped script with its chat, so when it reports a chat deleted (`CHAT_DELETED`, sent as `{ id }`) the engine removes that chat's scripts; a chat deleted while the engine wasn't running keeps them until its world is deleted. The unwraps stay through all of this, since the replies saved meanwhile keep the span. Chats that aren't world chats never get scripts of their own.
- **The engine's own reads** of a reply (`readableReply`), while hiding is on, leave out every block, with its wrapper, whether the display hides it or a preset draws it as a tracker: `extract`, the lore prefetch and what the player has seen said, `contradiction`, `choices` and `effects` templates (`{{reply}}`), the check for a Buy told again, and Save to lore of a whole reply. A Continue's read is matched against the whole message, so a block it split is left out whole. A reply an earlier build saved with `<div hidden wi-exclude>` around a block stays hidden in the display whatever the setting, so the block inside one is always left out, whitespace inside the wrapper or not; with hiding off, every other block is read as saved, span and all. The ledger keeps the reply as saved.

### 6.8 The preset's say
What the active preset says applies while it is active (`backend/preset-binding.ts`; for authors, `docs/PRESETS.md`). The preset is the one the host assembles with, as far as the engine can tell: the active Loom preset, which only the frontend can read (`loom.activePresetId`, sent with `lre.hello` and, when it changes, with `lre.preset active`), else the preset the generation's connection links (the context handler's `connectionId`), else the one the chat's last reply was assembled with. Presets and block states the host binds to a chat, persona, character or connection aren't visible to the engine. Its blocks are read through the presets API once per preset revision (`cache_revision`), in the context handler for a generation's phases and its interceptor run; a read without a pre phase takes the user's latest read, and a dry run reads it in the interceptor.
- **Placement:** each section has an outlet twin in the world's book (outlet-only, `outlet_name` `lre_<slot>`), and the narrator rules have one at `lre_narrator`. When an enabled block that assembles for every generation (no injection trigger) holds `{{outlet::lre_<slot>}}`, the interceptor fills the twin instead of the section's own entry and sets no placement, so the section lands at the preset's outlet for that generation; otherwise the world's placement applies (§4.2, §6.4). Nothing is written to the book per generation. Precedence: the preset's outlet, then the section's Position, then where the host's editor put a native entry.
- **A chat behind the head:** a chat on an older revision still places each section where its own revision says. When that is in the chat but the head moved the section's own entry to an outlet, the entry would land at both, so the section's twin is filled with the chat's placement instead.
- **Outlets the host won't assemble:** a block limited to some generation types or to characters with certain tags (`characterTagTrigger`), and any switched-on block after the first under a radio category, place nothing. A block the host leaves out for reasons the engine can't see (a preset profile's block states, prompt-activation regex scripts) is caught by the delivery check (§4.2): a section the test build doesn't find that the interceptor's run for it sent to the preset's outlet goes back to the world's placement while that preset revision stands.
- **Guidance:** a switched-off block named "RPEngine · <step>" (`interpret`, `extract`, `brief`, `outline`, `choices`, `npc.choose`, `judge`, `contradiction`, `puzzle.answer`) is appended to that helper's prompt, after the world's step template or the built-in prompt and its output contract, as style and rules that never change the contract; on the Decision route it rides in the decision's state as `rules`. The engine resolves no macro in it: its braces, control characters and shorthands are neutralized (§6.3). Lines starting with `//` are notes and are skipped, and the rest is cut at a word to 1,500 characters. A switched-on block of that name reaches the narrator in every chat, so the engine doesn't read it. The first block of a name counts.
- **Narrator rules:** a switched-off "RPEngine · narrator" block reaches the narrator in world chats only, as `[Narrator rules]` in the narrator entry (before the character) or its twin at `{{outlet::lre_narrator}}`, apart from the section budget.
- **Settings → Preset** shows the active preset, the `lre_` outlets its enabled blocks hold with the section each places, and each guidance block's state, and **Add RPEngine blocks to this preset** adds the missing ones, switched off and holding only `//` notes, under an "RPEngine" category (`lre.preset`). They are spliced into the preset's stored blocks, which are written back once, each exactly as stored, at the revision they were read at: the host's block API would re-normalize every block of the preset on each insert and drop what it doesn't know, a sealed block's protection among it.

## 7. Knowledge and visibility
- **Values:** `public`; `known_by`, seen by whoever knows the subject: a `knows` relation to it, or matching its `knownBy` selector (§4.1: the members of a group, people of a kind or tier); `secret` (GM only). Entities, fields, places and tracks take `knownBy` beside `known_by`. A place may also be `discoverable`: hidden until the POV has been there, has a `knows` link to it or lists it in `known_places`, and found for good after that; its `knownBy` counts as for `known_by`. A reader that doesn't understand a value (an unknown string, `discoverable` on anything but a place) hides the subject, and a `knownBy` with a constraint the engine doesn't read, a malformed list or no constraint at all grants nobody: every rule fails closed, and lint flags both.
- **The POV's own observations** (`space.seen`): every place it has been, with its first and last visit (a visit counts for the places around the one visited), and where and when it last saw each person it was with and could see. It is written inside the apply of the events that move someone (a `place` set, an `in` link or unlink, a spawn, a `space.pos`), so both folds keep it alike, no turn writes an event for it and an old chat rebuilds it on replay; the start place counts as the first visit. A POV the world doesn't place starts at `start.place`. From Space 3, the POV arriving at a place with a cell stands on that cell. With the grid system on, it also keeps the cells the POV has seen on each grid (`cells[scope] = {width, bits}`: a hex bitmask by cell index with trailing zeros trimmed, started again when the grid's width changes): every `space.pos` that moves the POV adds the cells within `sightRadius` squares (at most 32) that a line of sight reaches, a blocked cell stopping the line but itself seen. The fog display reads it.
- **People on the player layer.** Scene → Present, palette targets, the narrator's people lines and the people the helpers' prompts name as present (`brief`, `outline`, `choices` and `judge`) list only people the POV can see. Cast lists the people the POV knows (met: something it owns or holds, anyone it has a relation with either way, anyone where it is, or of its region) and anyone it can see where it stands. Someone here shows what they are doing and, for an LOD1 agent, their day and next commitment; someone away shows only where and when the POV last saw them, never where they are now or what they plan. Agendas show only when public, each track on its own, so a secret one never shows behind a public one; a block of an agent's day spent on an agenda that isn't public shows as "Busy", for "their own business", in the timeline and as the next commitment. An agenda's step as someone's activity ("agenda") doesn't record which agenda it is, so Cast, the Journal and the narrator's people lines and outline read it as "busy" unless every agenda the person has is public (`castlife.activity`). Every field follows its field visibility, wherever the player or the narrator reads it: a change to a field the POV can't see (a `set`, an `add`, or a pack event that writes it, as its type's `touches` declares) is in no chip, Journal line or Montage (`eventVisible`), and the narrator's people lines and outline say what someone is doing only when the POV may see their activity field.
- **Places on the player layer.** The map, the travel list, the palette, the place ✎, the narrator's facts (nearby exits included) and the Journal show only places the POV can see. A place inside one the POV can't see is as hidden as that place, even when it is public on its own: nothing names it or offers a way there. Being anywhere inside a `known_by` or `discoverable` place counts as being there, whatever lets the POV see the room it stands in, so walking from someone's flat into its kitchen keeps both named; a secret place is never seen, not even from inside. The map still draws the places the POV is inside, but one it can't see (a secret one, or a room of one) goes by a stand-in: "Somewhere", under an opaque id, in its scopes, breadcrumbs and path, never with its own id, name or scene type, and never as somewhere to travel to. Every other player surface names the POV's own place the same way (`povPlace`): the HUD pill, Scene, the sheet, the narrator's Place line and the facts the helpers read say "Somewhere", with no id, and the place ✎ neither names it nor offers it. Cast names the place of someone there with the POV the same way, and the Garage names a vehicle's place or home base the POV can't see "Somewhere" too, while the narrator's vehicles line says the vehicle is "somewhere". The scene ✎ offers the engine's own scene types (conversation, action, exploration and downtime) and those of the places the POV can see, and no other: not one only a hidden place has, nor one the world only gives a tick or only an action sets, since a tick says nothing of who may know the type. Its "Place default" option names no other type, and a pinned one isn't marked current. The scene in force (the Scene row in the HUD details and Play → Scene, and the time controls' "now") is named the same way: any other type, whether the POV stands in a place of that type or is pinned to it, reads as the engine's default scene ("conversation"). That is display only: the clock still reads the type in force and paces by its tick, a world's own included, and Settings → Time and the time controls still list every type the world ticks, as the author's configuration. On a grid, by knowledge, the area of a place the POV can't see goes out as nothing (`void`, walled), unless the POV is inside it; in fog, the cells of it the POV hasn't seen go out blank, exactly as any unseen cell, so fog never outlines a hidden room. A route is drawn only when the POV can see both its real ends, so a hidden end is never drawn as a way to the place around it. The narrator's Place line names a place around the POV's only when the POV can see it too, and a refused narrated move names a place the POV can't see as "somewhere".
- **Player-facing views** are filtered to what the POV knows. The GM view shows everything, secrets and removed entities included, and records nothing by being looked at (L34). It is built when asked (`lre.gm`, once its spoiler gate is open) from the head's state and is never part of a snapshot or delta, so its data never travels with the player's state. It holds every entity with its fields as stored (derived ones read now), relations, placement, levels and designed sheet; every place with its visibility; every track with all its stages and truths; the ✎ fixes and GM edits in force; the held writes that didn't apply, with their raw reasons; and everyone else's money: each person at their own Money level (a band at 1; cash and one account at 2; every account and their bills from 3), with their latest history lines and payday, bill and decline notes; accounts not opened yet show the opening balances they will start from. The player never sees another person's money unless the POV holds the account. A track still on a bare start stage is dormant and stays out of the quest log until it moves on, so a quest can wait unseen for its trigger.
- **GM edits** (`lre.edit`) change anything the GM view shows, on the head: a field (`set`, or `add` to a number, which stops at its bounds), a relation (`link` with its fields, `unlink`), where something is (`move` to a place, `put` into a container or take it out), money (`adjust`: a posting between the account and `economy.external:correction`, memo "Correction"), a track's stage, an entity's tier, agent flag and level overrides (`engine.entity`), its classes and tags (`classify`, as `engine.class`), `spawn`, `despawn` and `restore`, and `effect`: a pack effect registered for GM edits (`EffectDef.gm`), for what no entity field holds (an asset's price); any other effect is refused (`not_editable`). Each operation is checked as the effect it becomes (field types, enum values, min and max with a pool's evaluated max, relation kinds, fields and cardinality, the world's facets and their values); values are taken literally, never as formulas, and a value out of range is refused, not clamped. If one fails, the request is refused and nothing applies. A request is one `fix` post entry (its events grouped when there is more than one), so undo, Keep, swipes, Re-narrate and Re-run treat it like a ✎ fix (P2, P3); locked fields are writable here and only here. **Classify** sets the classes (any facet, hidden ones included, since the GM sees everything) and tags whole; a facet or value the world doesn't define is refused (`unknown_facet`, `unknown_class`, `single_class`, as a spawn's are), and so is one that reads as it already does. The chat holds both in the entity's state from then on, so selectors match them over the world's definition, and they count as play's for an Upgrade. The event keeps the classes it replaced (`was`), so its player line names only the facets players can see whose values changed, a cleared one as "none", the same on every replay, and never tags; an edit that changes no such facet has no player line, and its Corrected chip names nothing, as for any hidden edit. **Despawn** sets the entity's reserved `gone` field, unlinks its own relations (taken out of a container, it keeps the place it was at) and everyone's relations to it except containment, so what it holds stays inside and is gone with it. A gone entity drops out of every player surface and process: Scene, Cast, the palette, the narrator's people, visibility, schedules, agents, encounters and the money processes. It matches no selector and is no group's member (`selectorMatches`, `membersOf`), so rules, schedules, `knownBy` grants and a group's regard pass it by, and a removed group's schedules route none of its members; it fires no zone or cell trigger, and `step` refuses to move it. The GM view, Restore and Upgrade read state directly, so they still see it. **Restore** clears `gone`; an agent comes back caught up, without replaying the time it was away. The POV can't be removed. Kinds and places are the world's definitions and are edited in Build, not here.
- **Life state edits** (`life`, §8): an entity's life state, the people told it, or both; everyone with the entity learns a change anyway. The GM view shows each entity's state as it is and who knows it (everyone but those whose word is older, or only those who learned it).
- **Slots** carry POV-known and public facts plus cues; truth enters only at reveal.
- **Extraction** may propose `knows` **only for public facts**: a public entity the POV doesn't know yet, or a `known_by` or `discoverable` place it can't see yet ("you hear about the boathouse"), named by id or by its name, since the prompts never show a hidden place's id; never a secret one. Secret stages advance only by predicates. The extraction and `interpret` prompts list only the entities and places the POV may know (itself, those it has met, its own, or visible where it stands; every visible one in a world that does not place the POV), and a proposal naming anything else is dropped before any change or chip; a `knows` proposal's place is the one exception, left to its channel. A proposal that carries an unrevealed secret's truth is dropped before any chip.
- **NPC decision inputs** may carry that NPC's secrets. Outputs are authored actions with observable labels; lint flags leaky labels. Secret-bearing questions never share a call with prose steps.
- **Secret lore** is for the helpers in its audience only, until its gate holds, and never for a step whose output reaches the narrator or the player; the lore search enforces both (§4.2, §5.4). The leak check (`unrevealedSecrets`) lists, besides unrevealed truths, every sentence or clause of secret lore whose gate doesn't hold, where it has at least four words and 16 characters (`lorePhrases`), so chips, Journal lines, sections, Playtest and the prose prompts never carry one word for word; an `interpret` answer that does plays as roleplay (a second action that does is dropped alone, §5.2), and a `judge` reason that does isn't shown (the roll chip and the Journal give the rating alone), unless the player typed those words. Nor is a `judge` reason that names someone or something the POV can't see (a person, a place, a thing or a track) or a secret lore entry, whatever was typed (`namesAny`): a person counts by any word of their name ("Vex" names Vex Morrow), of two or more letters in ASCII and of any length in another script, and a name of single letters (Q, M) by that capital standing alone, A and I aside since they are words; anything else, a track's label and an entry's title included, counts only by all its words in order, words like "the" and "of" aside ("under the chapel" says Under the Chapel, "under" alone doesn't). Case and the accents people leave out don't matter: a name and the text are read as written and plain, without the accents of Latin, Greek and Cyrillic letters and with ø, æ, ß and the like spelled plain, so "Zoe" names Zoë and "Soren" Søren. A word in ASCII letters counts only whole ("dangerous" doesn't name a Dan) or with an English plural ending ("the Okafors", "two lanterns"); one with any other letter counts anywhere in the reason, since a script written without spaces (李雷在监视你) or an inflected form ("Вексом" for Векс, "Łukaszem" for Łukasz) has no word end to find. A paraphrase isn't caught, which is why secret-bearing calls stay apart from prose. The inspector's lore traces leave a secret entry out entirely, show a secret-reading step's lookups only as their kind and count, and withhold any other search that carries a secret, names someone or somewhere the POV doesn't know, or names a secret entry (§6.5).
- **Player-facing wording.** Chips, Journal lines and prompts never name an entity the player has not met or a secret. A dropped or failed event is worded for the player (its player line when the POV may see it, otherwise "A change no longer applies"), and the raw reason stays in the GM view. Validator reasons name things by label (entity, place or track name), not by id. A relation refused for its kinds or cardinality (on a decline chip, or in the Outcome) names only the people the player knows, by the Journal's rule (`playerKnows`: seen and met), and anyone else as "someone"; a GM edit refused the same way is told the raw reason (`Rejection.gm`). A pack's event describer is told whom a player's line is for (`describe`'s `viewer`), so it can leave out what that POV can't see: a way opening or closing to a place it can't see runs to "somewhere", while the GM's edit lines and Check world, worded with no viewer, name it. An NPC's invitation names where to meet only when the POV can see the place, and otherwise asks the POV to "meet up", in its chip, the Journal, the Montage, the Outcome and what the narrator hears; the GM's line names the place. An encounter between two NPCs names its place only in their own record, which no player line shows. Enum values and code-like words read as words ("needs service", "on shift"). A turn's Journal entry lists the same lines as its change chip: at most 8, and when more exist, money, quest-stage and signal lines (a payday, a statement, an invitation) are kept ahead of ordinary ones and routine lines (activities, other people's moves, needs) go first; the kept lines stay in order.
- **Acceptance:** truth text and judged paraphrases are absent from Breakdown, choices, the Journal and panels until reveal.

## 8. NPC simulation (level of detail)
- **LOD0 (every NPC):**
  - schedules: each sets one target field, such as `place` or `activity`, slot by slot, and where several set the same field the most specific owner wins;
  - event overrides until a given time;
  - seeded incidents per slot (process seeds).
- **LOD1 agents (flagged NPCs; on at every realism level, including Story):**
  - Agendas (tracks) and goals feed choices. At realism ≥3 the Cast life planner schedules whole days.
  - The engine enumerates valid actions, always including "do nothing".
  - The decision model is consulted only in the tie zone or for an invitation, and its choice is recorded in the turn's events.
  - **On screen:** intents come from public labels; the narrator reads them in the Stance section (§6.6).
  - **Off screen:** catch-up on every `slot_crossed` up to K agents per turn from a round-robin queue, in process (D9). It runs before the reply when the player's action advances time, otherwise after it.
  - **During a timelapse,** and in the catch-up for an applied `time.advance` (§9.2): every LOD1 agent steps through the interval inside the unit cap; tie-zone choices are utility-only by default (§9.2).
- **The player's character** follows its own routine (place and activity schedules) only when time runs on its own (a timelapse or catch-up). An ordinary turn leaves it where the player took it. A narrated move across a gap that no single-mode route spans is declined ("There's no way from … to …"), and a POV that is stranded, or whose routine's place cannot be reached, stays where it is.
- **Life states.** Every entity has one: the built-in `alive`, `dying` and `dead`, or one the world defines (`lifeStates`: a label, whether someone in it still acts, and how the narrator reads it, "lies dead"). An entity starts in its own `life` (alive when unset) and play changes it only through `engine.life`, which the `life` effect writes (`{entity, to?, tell?}`, for rules and actions), as do a GM edit, the `life` narration channel and combat's down rule (`ruleset.combat.down.life`: falling into it, alive again once back up from it). `engine.life` declares the state touched, so an Upgrade keeps it (§4.3).
  - **Stillness.** Someone whose state doesn't act follows no schedule and no day plan, takes no trip, meets no one, is asked no `npc.choose` (day-plan ties and fight turns alike), steps no more as an agent, on screen or off, and rolls no initiative and takes no turn in a fight. An agent who acts again comes back caught up, as a restored one does.
  - **The body stays.** Unlike Remove from the world, the entity keeps its place and relations: Scene lists it, the palette offers it, selectors match it, and the narrator reads it where it lies ("Sir Alaric lies dead.").
  - **Who knows** is the engine slice's `life`: per subject whose state changed in play, what everyone took it to be before and what each person who has learned of it since last learned. It is written inside event apply, so both folds keep it alike: a change teaches everyone in the subject's place; `engine.told` teaches the people a rule, a GM edit or a reply names; and every event that moves someone teaches whoever it leaves in a place with the subject, someone walking in or a body carried in. Cast, Scene, the narrator's people lines, the outline, the helpers' prompts and the Journal read the state the POV last learned, so a death it hasn't heard of shows nowhere and a revival it hasn't seen still reads as death, with no hint; the GM view reads the truth. A timelapse whose coarse record would teach differently keeps its full events.
  - **What the player reads of it.** A change records who saw it (`witnesses`: everyone in the subject's place as it resolves), and only they get its line, so a death learned of later is the telling's news and the Journal never dates it. Other people's place and activity changes, and their changes to any field a schedule sets, never read in the Journal, the change chip or a Montage: the Journal words past turns against what the POV knows now, so it can't tell which of someone's comings and goings the POV saw, and a known person who died unseen would simply stop appearing; Scene and People say who is here. People voices no regard for a body the POV knows of; Stance and `agents_present` read who acts from what the POV knows; `invited(host)` is false for a host the POV knows is dead, and nobody invites a POV that doesn't act. A reply can't move someone who doesn't act, nor anyone the POV knows only as a body, and what the POV knows decides how the move is refused. A body the POV knows of is declined in a plain chip, in what the POV takes it to be ("Sir Alaric lies dead; that move didn't happen"), even one since brought back where the POV didn't see, so the chip never hints at a revival; someone the POV takes to act who doesn't is dropped as a change that changed nothing, applied at once or offered for Apply, with no chip and no word to the next reply, and only the log says why, since a refusal no living person gets would tell of a death the POV hasn't heard of. Neither, nor a move no route spans, is worded as a declined payment.
  - **Starting knowledge.** An entity can name who knows the state it starts in (`lifeKnownBy`, entity ids); everyone else takes it for alive until they see it or are told, so list anyone who starts in the same place. Unset or empty, everyone knows.
  - **Time skips and Upgrades.** A timelapse reads who still acts at every slot, so someone who dies part-way stops there, their cursor at the last slot they lived, and a two-day skip ends as two one-day skips do; catch-up passes over anyone dead or removed. An Upgrade that brings an agent back to a state that acts (its own life state, or a world state that now acts) catches it up from then. A life change the player hasn't heard of goes to a hidden row of its own in the Upgrade preview, and hidden rows sort after every shown one.
- **Group chats:** character entities link to Lumiverse character ids.

## 9. Granularity and domain packs (D17, D18)

### 9.1 Two dials

**Depth per domain** (realism levels 0–4):

| Domain | 1 Narrative | 2 Simple | 3 Detailed | 4 Simulation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Money** | Wealth band | Cash plus one account (ledger) | Accounts, cards (with autopay), recurring income and bills | Interest/APR, statements, minimum payments, loans, investments, withholding |
| **Gear** | Notable items | Items, containers, cash as currency in a wallet | Worn/equipped slots, kits (template plus overrides), condition | Wear per use, consumables |
| **Vehicles** | "Runs fine" | Fuel %, location, owner | Specs, consumption, odometer, use grants, home base | Maintenance intervals, seeded breakdowns, registration/insurance, marine and off-road specifics |
| **Cast life** | Day-part routines; agendas as tracks | Commitments set the day | Day planner | Needs/drives, invitations, NPC↔NPC encounters, knowledge spread |
| **Relationships** | One band per pair | One number per pair | Multiple axes plus appraisal rules | Decay, history, group regard |
| **Space & travel** | Named places | Places plus routes and travel times | Grids, positions | Terrain multipliers, mode-specific routes |
| **Calendar & time** | Day parts | Clock and dates | Recurrence, time zones | Holidays, terms, exceptions |
| **Weather** | Season mood | Climate states | Regional climates | Microclimate effects on travel and rules |
| **Sheets & checks** | Bands as words | Stats plus one check | Skills, derived fields | Full sheet |

Level 0 turns a domain off. Story systems (tracks) do not depend on realism. Time controls and Timelapse (§9.2) need Calendar ≥1; at level 1, targets round to day parts.

**Tiers and bands.** A world names its own tiers (`WorldDoc.tiers`: label, band, order, an optional agent default and description), and each maps to one of three fixed **detail bands**: `principal` (shown as Leads), `supporting` (Supporting) and `background` (Background). Caps are per band and domain. A world written before tiers existed gets three tiers whose ids are the band ids, so its entities, settings, `resolve` markers and stored ledgers read unchanged.
- **Effective level** = the per-entity override if set, otherwise min(domain level, the cap of the band its tier maps to). An override can never exceed the domain level. The tier is read through the world each time, so a tier's band follows the revision in force. An entity's tier that the world doesn't define keeps its band when it is a band id (one spawned before the world renamed its tiers), else runs at the domain level; lint flags unknown tiers.
- **The POV** is `world.pov` and is never capped, whatever its tier. A legacy `pov` tier on the POV is moved to the lead tier (the lowest-order Leads tier) when the revision is read; revisions are immutable, so this happens on every read.
- **New entities:** a spawn that names no tier of the world gets the world's spawn tier (`spawnTier`, by default its lowest-order Background tier), and an entity without its own agent flag takes its tier's agent default (never the POV).
- **Realism presets** (`Custom` allows any combination):

| Preset | Money | Gear | Vehicles | Cast life | Relationships | Space | Calendar | Weather | Sheets | Band caps (Leads / Supporting / Background) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Story** (default, D22) | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 1 | Domain levels for all |
| **Grounded** | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 2 | 2 | none / none / 1 |
| **Simulation** | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 4 / 3 / 2 |

- **New chats start from the world.** The starting markers (§4.3) carry the world's own realism (its levels and band caps, a band it caps being its whole say for that band) and time controls; the user's settings fill in what the world doesn't set (the time scale, and any domain, scene type, interrupt class or activity the world leaves out). With "Use my settings instead of a world's defaults for new chats" on (off by default), the user's realism and time settings replace the world's instead, and a world cap the settings don't keep is lifted. Later changes are new markers. A level is a whole number from 0 to 4; anything else reads as 0.

### 9.2 One representation, gated by level
- **Same data at every level.** Levels gate which processes run, what panels and slots render, and what extraction may propose. They never change the data shape.
  - **Money:** one double-entry ledger whenever an entity has numeric money (≥2).
    - Level 1 renders a band from net worth.
    - Entities without a ledger keep a band field.
    - Changes made at level 1 post as balanced adjustments against the **external economy** counterparty.
  - **Kits** are a template plus sparse per-piece overrides. Taking a piece creates one override item, and completeness is derived.
  - **Fuel and odometer** are tracked as numbers from level 2.
  - **Decay and needs** are computed closed-form at read time.
- **Promote and demote.**
  - Promotion switches processes on. An entity that lacks representation gets it through lazy materialization.
  - Demotion switches processes off and narrows rendering. The data stays live (nothing goes dormant), so re-promotion never restores stale detail.
  - The demotion preview lists predicates whose inputs will freeze.
- **Level contract per pack:**
  - the fields that are live at each level;
  - **level-independent query functions** for predicates, the planner and renderers (`wealth(x)`, `can_afford(x, amount)`, `has_item(x, kind)`, `fuel_ok(v, route)`, `regard_band(a, b)`);
  - **cross-pack services** with defined behavior at every level. For example, `economy.charge` is a no-op at 0, a band check plus adjustment at 1, and a posting from 2. Similar services cover `gear.transfer`, `vehicle.consume` and `relationship.appraise`.
  - **Mixed-level parties.** Every party posts at **its own** effective level. A counterparty with no numeric money (level ≤1, or Money off) is represented in the ledger by the **external economy** account, tagged with the counterparty's id, and its band moves by its level-1 rule. Money is therefore always conserved in the ledger, whatever the mix of levels.
  - Cash is a currency stack in a wallet container when Gear is 2 or higher, otherwise a plain cash account.
- **Lazy materialization:**
  - **Materializers take the entity's current representation as input** (band, notable items, current aggregates, known facts) and sample **within** it. A level-1 NPC moved from "poor" to "rich" materializes a balance inside the "rich" band, never a value drawn from the seed alone.
  - **What counts as "show":** a **player-facing observation or an in-fiction action** (opening a trunk, emptying a wallet, auditing an account). Showing a pack's Play panel is an observation of the POV: switching to the tab sends `lre.view.open` for that panel with no entity, which materializes the POV in the domain of the panel's pack. The inspector, audits, playtests and Dry Run render **labelled previews from the seed** and record nothing; the GM view shows what is recorded and, for what isn't yet, its representation (an unopened account's opening balance), and records nothing either. One more thing needs a person's money: the recurring money processes (Money 3+) open the authored accounts of the people they act for, posting their opening balances (the same event a view records; it draws no randomness) in the first slot holding 09:00, so their bills draw on the money the author gave them.
  - **Until it is materialized, an entity shows its representation.** For money that is each authored account's `opening` amount, exactly what the materialization posts: the POV's Money panel and the HUD cash show it (net worth and band included), as the GM view does for everyone else, so a new chat never shows zero and nothing changes when the accounts open. A ✎ cash fix materializes the holder's money first and measures its correction from there, recording both in the fix.
  - **Where it is recorded:** materialization during a turn goes in that swipe's `events`, ahead of the action's effects. Between turns it goes in the head record's `post`. **Inside a turn's in-flight window it is deferred** (P2, §4.3): the panel keeps showing the representation (or "pending", for detail that has none, such as a container's contents) and the detail materializes on the head once the window closes, so nothing shown is ever re-sampled.
  - It is capped per write and recorded with `generatorVersion` and `tablesRevision`. Detail materializes once per entity and domain: a materialization of detail already recorded fails its check, so a carried, kept or held entry holding one leaves it out and keeps the rest, re-checked without it (a ✎ cash fix whose accounts a Re-run's reply already opened still sets the cash), and an opening balance never posts twice. An entry left with nothing to apply is dropped or refused with its note.
  - **Pinned versions.** Each world revision pins the generator versions and data-table revisions it uses: tables are copied into the revision (§4.1), and every pinned generator version stays in the bundle. A running chat keeps its revision's pins across extension updates. New tables or generators reach it only through **Upgrade** (a `migrate` marker), never silently. First-shown detail stays recorded in `events`/`post`.
  - Detail whose source materialization entry is missing from the current fold (a branch cut before it, a Discard, a drop or an inert entry in a rebased view, a Re-narrate drop) materializes again from the same seed and pins when it is next shown, but from the current representation, so it may differ.
- **Time (D29).** Deterministic by default; no time control or timelapse needs a model call.
  - **Every turn has an elapsed time:** a timelapse segment or the action's duration, otherwise the scene's default tick × the time scale; zero while the clock is paused. The Scene slot states it ("This turn took N min." or "No time passes this turn.").
  - **Time controls.** A new chat takes the world's scene-type ticks, interrupt policies, Speed-through policies, montage and unit cap as starting `time` markers (§4.3), with Settings → Time filling in what the world leaves out (always the time scale), or replacing the world's with "Use my settings" on; Play → Calendar changes the running chat. A mid-chat change to any time control is a `time` marker (§4.3): a pure intent with `seq`, journaled and folded like a realism change.
    - **Default tick per scene type**, editable: conversation 2 min, action 1 min, exploration 10 min, downtime 30 min; worlds may add types and set their defaults. A tick is 0 to 1,440 minutes. Settings and time markers, the starting markers included, round it to the nearest whole minute, so a tick under half a minute plays as 0 and its turns take no time; a world's own tick for a type no marker sets (with "Use my settings" on, a type the user's settings don't list) is read as written. The time scale multiplies the tick after that, so a turn's elapsed time can be a fraction of a minute ("This turn took 0.5 min."). The scene type is the POV's `scene` field, set by actions, rules or a ✎ fix; while it is empty, the place's (or an ancestor's) default applies.
    - **Time scale:** a global multiplier on default ticks only (0.25×–10×, default 1×). Action durations, travel times and timelapses stay exact.
    - **Clock paused** for the current scene: zero elapsed time (ticks and action durations) until resumed or the scene changes. Timelapse asks to resume first. Pausing again after a scene change restarted the clock holds it in the new scene: a pause marker drops the old scene's pin, and the next plan pins the scene it finds. The HUD, the Timelapse sheet and the Scene section read the pause as the plan does (`clockPaused`). A time skip the reply narrates still applies (§9.4).
    - The interrupt policy, Speed through, Montage granularity and the timelapse unit cap (below).
    - **Hard maxima** hold wherever a value is read, so a world document, an imported ledger or an intent can never ask for an unbounded run: a tick is at most one day; the time scale stays within 0.25×–10×; a timelapse reaches at most 366 days from now; a Speed-through threshold is between 1 minute and 7 days (one range, `SPEED_THRESHOLD_MINUTES`: Settings keeps a stored threshold inside it, rounded to the minute, the time controls' stepper offers exactly it, and markers and the engine clamp to it).
  - **Timelapse** (palette sheet, Calendar panel or chip; §11):
    - **Targets:** a duration (minutes, hours, days, weeks), or *until*: next morning or another day part, the POV's next shift or commitment, arrival (the active trip ends), a date and time, a track reaching a stage, or "until something happens" (the first `stop` interrupt). "The next <day part>" or "tomorrow", whether in the parameters or in the player's text, means the following occurrence when the coming one is under three hours away, so "sleep until the next morning" at 07:14 sleeps the night.
    - **It is an action:** a user message with `intent:{timelapse, target, overrides}` and `triggerGeneration`. Per-run policy overrides travel in the intent.
    - **Simulation:** the context handler steps the interval in process (D9), slot by slot with process seeds (so it equals N one-day steps), inside the unit cap. The POV follows its own commitments and routine like an LOD1 agent, so pay, bills, commutes and fuel post as they fall due.
    - **Interrupt policy,** per class `stop` | `note` | `ignore`: invitations, bills, declines, incidents, quest triggers, rule triggers (rules may be tagged `interrupt`), NPC arrivals at the POV's place, overdue use grants, needs thresholds (Cast life 4) and weather alerts. Defaults: `stop` for invitations, declines, incidents, quest triggers and arrivals; `note` for bills and paydays, rule triggers and overdue use grants; `ignore` for needs thresholds and weather alerts. After each slot step, in slot order, the first `stop` event the POV can see (`signalVisible`) ends the timelapse at that slot's end; `note` events go to the Montage. A signal the POV can't see never stops one, as it never stops an action queue (§11): the early stop, its chip and its Outcome line would give it away. The same state and policy give the same stop. A turn that stopped early says so ("Timelapse stopped: <reason>") and offers to continue.
    - **Unit cap** per segment (deterministic; it can be lowered but never raised): 600 agent-days (LOD1 agents plus the POV, × days) and 5,000 events. A longer request ends its segment at the last whole slot inside the cap, **always at least one slot**, and shows a **Continue timelapse** chip with the time left. Settings and lint reject a cap smaller than one slot for the world's LOD1 agents plus the POV. Seeds do not depend on segmentation, so segments compose exactly when nothing else changes state between them (a timelapse turn proposes no `time.advance`; below).
    - **NPC choices:** tie-zone choices use utility only; a timelapse makes no model call.
    - **A timelapse that can't run** (the clock is paused, the target has passed, lies more than 366 days ahead or doesn't exist, there is no upcoming shift, commitment or active trip, or Calendar is off) simulates nothing; the turn records only a note saying why ("Timelapse not run: …"). The simulation is bounded by the unit cap, never by the wall clock; a context handler the host gives up on leaves the turn unplanned, and it commits `degraded` (§5.1).
    - **Recorded** as the turn's `events` plus a summary event `{from, to, target, stoppedBy, segment}`. Derivable events go in as per-period aggregates and period closes (§4.3, record size). Re-run reproduces the interval (process seeds, utility choices); Re-narrate copies it.
    - **Not a model channel:** an explicit timelapse is user input and is not bounded by the `time.advance` cap. Model-proposed advances stay capped (§9.4).
  - **Montage slot** (engine book; filled on timelapse turns; ranked after Outcome on the section budget):
    - key events, filtered to what the POV knows (§7);
    - aggregates: money in and out per account at the POV's Money level, distance and travel, regard changes that cross a band, notable needs;
    - granularity: key events only (default), per day or per week; coarsened to fit the budget;
    - default line: "Narrate the interval from the Montage; don't add events." The Journal keeps the full list.
  - **Speed through,** per activity type, when the activity's remaining span exceeds its threshold. New user settings give the four listed activities (sleep, work shift, travel, waiting) `offer` past 30 min; a settings file saved earlier keeps the policies it has and isn't refilled. A world may set its own for any activity. An activity without a policy (in a chat started before it had one, a world's own activity the world leaves unset, or one the user's saved settings don't list) passes within the turn; its controls say so until a mode or threshold is picked, which gives it a policy. Actions declare the activity they start and its span (until 07:00, the shift's end, arrival).
    - `offer` (default): the turn elapses only the action's own duration, and a chip offers a Timelapse to the activity's end.
    - `auto`: the turn runs that timelapse itself, with the interrupt policy and a Montage.
    - Activities first seen by extraction always get the offer chip, since the engine never starts a generation on its own.
    - Shorter spans elapse within the turn.
  - **Typed time.** The action catalog always includes "Let time pass" (minutes; until a day part, shift or commitment; or at a stated day and time). Its label tells `interpret` the day and time as the HUD shows them, in the POV's place's time zone ("it is now Mon 2026-09-14 06:45"), so "skip ahead to Friday at 6 PM" can be read as `at: "2026-09-18 18:00"`, and `then` may carry an `at` too. The engine reads it with the world's calendar, as local time at the POV's place, into an `at` target. Only `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM` (or with `T`) naming a day the calendar has, at a time from 00:00 to 23:59, is a stated time; anything else is none, and the other parameters are read instead. A stated time that has passed, or lies beyond the 366-day timelapse reach, is refused like any target the timelapse can't reach: the turn runs none and records the note ("Timelapse not run: that time has passed"). A duration up to 24 hours runs as the turn's own timelapse; a longer one is offered as a Timelapse chip and never run unasked, so a misread number can't skip days. An *until* or a stated time isn't capped: "until my next shift" runs as the turn's timelapse however far off the shift is, inside the unit cap. A span typed after other actions ("…then wait until morning") is offered, not run, once they have all played; a stated time is offered as "Timelapse to Fri 18:00?".
  - **Extraction** may propose a bounded `time.advance` only for narrated time **beyond** what the swipe has already applied: it is given the clock from its fold prefix (P2, §4.3) and the time already applied for this swipe (the turn's elapsed time plus any earlier advance, `cont` or `narr` reading). It proposes none on timelapse or Speed-through turns.
    - **Under the cap** (240 minutes a proposal, §9.4) the advance applies together with its **catch-up**: the interval runs whole, like a timelapse segment (every LOD1 agent and the POV's routine, inside the unit cap, every interrupt class at `note`), so it equals a timelapse to that time. Under `auto` it is computed at once in the post pass and stored with it; under `confirm` the post pass checks it the same way and offers a confirm chip, and Apply runs it from the head's clock and stores it in the confirm entry, paused clock or not, as `auto` does. It is all or nothing: if the interval exceeds the unit cap or the catch-up cannot finish within the post-pass bound, the advance is not applied and becomes the Timelapse chip below. **During a fight** (`fightHoldsClock`) none applies in either mode and none is offered: the post pass declines it with a decline chip, and the next reply is told so; Apply on an earlier chip is refused while one is under way (§11).
    - **Over the cap,** up to the 366-day timelapse reach, or when its catch-up cannot apply whole, it becomes a **Timelapse** action chip to that time, with every interrupt class at `note` (§9.4). A skip beyond 366 days is dropped, with no chip.
  - Recurring processes step per slot with process seeds. Closed form is used only where it is exact.
  - **Property:** skipping N days equals N one-day steps and any segmentation of the same timelapse.

| `extract` → `life` | Someone died, is dying, collapsed or came round, named by a person the POV may know and a life state by id or label. Everything else (balances, prices, interest, payroll, fuel, travel, time ticks, timelapse intervals and interrupts, weather, schedules, planner days) is engine-only.

**Extraction quality was measured on a deliberately weak model** (DeepSeek V4 Flash), and the numbers are a floor, not the design target (D31). The first measurement, on 38 labelled narration fixtures, gave precision 0.41 with the base prompt and 0.74 with strict rules, against a 0.90 target. Two corrections followed:
- **strict prompt rules:** `time_advance` only for an explicit jump or stated duration; `knows` only for public facts the POV newly learns; `regard` only when the prose states a change; `give` only for a handover between two characters; never a duplicate;
- **deterministic dedupe in validation:** a purchase subsumes the seller's handover and its payment transfer; a cash `give` (only when the whole item is money) is a transfer with method "cash"; a payer must be an account.

I then re-measured with the engine's own `extract` step as shipped (strict rules plus dedupe; the same 38 fixtures and scorer; 38 calls): overall precision **0.68** (30 of 44), recall 0.71, and no forbidden proposals. Per type: `time_advance` 1.0 (6/6), `condition` 1.0 (1/1), `regard` 1.0 (4/4), `transfer` 1.0 (2/2), `spawn` 1.0 (1/1), `give` 0.71 (5/7), `location` 0.67 (2/3), `purchase` 0.56 (5/9), `knows` 0.44 (4/9), `quantity` 0 (0/2). The samples are too small to move a cold-start mode, so D4's defaults stand; learning on the player's own connection (§11) is the gate.

### 9.5 Narrator contract
- **Facts are authoritative.** The sections hold the established facts, and each line that reports what happened says how to tell it: an Outcome line ends "Narrate this result." (or "Narrate the attempt failing.", and the like), and a timelapse's line ends "Narrate the interval from the Montage; don't add events."
- **Narrated purchases:**
  - `extract` returns the item, its category, the payer (the person who pays, never an account or a card) and, when the prose says so, how they paid, in the prose's words;
  - the engine prices it in this order: a price the player set on a chip (*Set price*, *Edit amount*), charged as set; the catalog row that names the item (by id, or `matchCatalog`: whole words with simple plurals, the item's main noun among the row's head nouns, then the row covering most of the item's words, the purchase's category, what the place sells); the stated price; the band of the purchase's category, or of a category the item names. A price the player states in a Buy is charged in full (and declines when it cannot be paid); a price the narration alone gives is capped at the economy pack's `unpricedCap` (minor units; by default `derivedUnpricedCap`, the highest `price_cents` band maximum across the world's `economy.catalog` and `economy.categories`, else 500 major units; worlds saved with the old `unpricedCapCents` are migrated once). Build edits it in the economy pack's entry, a **Narrated-price cap** field (the economy ui's `config` fields) that shows the derived default and the world's currency; a cap that isn't a positive whole number is the lint warning `economy.unpriced_cap`, and the engine then rounds a positive fraction and reads anything else as unset. The buy's `economy.txn` records the narrated amount and whether it was capped or clamped, and every purchase chip shows the amount charged with "(priced as …)" and "narrated X, capped at Y" or "clamped to Y";
  - one purchase per item: the extract prompt asks for two things bought as two purchases, and a narrated purchase naming several things one of which is the turn's own Buy (`splitAlongBuy`) drops the Buy's part and prices the rest on its own, without the bundle's price ("fish and chips", which holds no part of the Buy, stays whole);
  - a narrated purchase of the turn's own Buy (`boughtAlong`) is dropped only when it is the Buy told again: the same main noun, one name's words holding the other's, any stated price the Buy's (±25% of the charge or the typed price, or inside its row's band) and no "another" beside it in the prose. "Another", "a second", "one more", "for <person>", a different quantity or a qualifier that rules the Buy's out ("green" for "red", "large" for "small") make it a new purchase; anything else of the same noun is unsure and always asks with an *Also bought …?* confirm chip, whatever the purchase mode. So does another noun whose catalog row (as `economy.buy` prices it where the player character is) is a sibling of a Buy's row, in the same group of the price table (Drip coffee and Latte are both Coffee), unless "another", "for <person>" or a different quantity makes it a new purchase: the reply's "coffee" after a typed latte may be the latte in other words, so it asks rather than charging a second drink;
  - a narrated purchase nothing prices gets a decline chip, *Bought X: no price*, with *Set price* (the shared money input), *Pick item* (catalog rows close to it, then what the place sells, from `economy.item_choices`) and *Dismiss*; an automatic one keeps *Remove it* and *Accept* beside them;
  - a Buy may say how it is paid, and the extract step passes the prose's words the same way: "credit card" pays from a card, "debit card" or "checking" from checking, "cash" from the wallet, "savings" from savings. A kind of account the payer doesn't have falls back: a card to checking, anything else to the usual order. When the extractor names an account as the payer, its holder pays from that account; when it names a thing someone owns (a debit card in the wallet), its owner pays the way the name says (a debit card from checking, a credit card on the card, a wallet in cash). A method the proposal states wins over all of these;
  - for a typed Buy, when `interpret` leaves the way of paying out, the player's own words fill it ("credit card", "debit card", "cash" or "savings"), but only when said as a payment: after "with", "on", "using", "by", "from" or "in" ("pay with my credit card", "in cash", "from savings"), and not as a thing named after it ("a credit card holder"). A negated mention ("not with cash", "instead of …", "rather than …") is skipped and the last mention counts, so "not with cash, with my debit card" pays by debit card, "I buy a credit card holder for $12" doesn't charge the card, and a long sentence that says "pay with my credit card" charges the card, not checking. A method `interpret` set stands;
  - money the model states as written (8 for $8) is converted to minor units once, before the channels read it, in `extract` and in a world's own `effects` steps (their prompts ask for plain amounts in the world's currency), and a narrated transfer's chip shows the formatted amount;
  - a payment's named source pays only when it is the payer's own cash, checking, savings or card account, at every money level; a loan or a brokerage account named as the source falls back to the usual choice. A narrated transfer's sum is one rounded number in minor units (its cents when given, else the converted amount), and its chip states that sum;
  - a narrated give of money becomes a transfer of that amount with method "cash", converted once before dedupe. A give is money only when the whole item is: an optional article, an amount with the currency's symbol or code or a money word, or a cash word, then optionally "bill", "note" or "in cash" ("$20", "a twenty", "a $20 bill", "20 dollars in cash"). A priced thing ("my $300 jacket", "a $20 gift card") stays a thing. A Gear give never makes money into a thing: "cash", "$20", "a twenty" and "20 dollars" are refused as items ("2 old daggers" in pence is an item, since a letter currency symbol counts only beside the number);
  - each side of a narrated transfer is a person or a person's own cash, checking, savings or card account. An account stands for its holder, paying from it (`from`) or depositing into it (`to`), and two sides of one holder move money between two of their accounts, so money is conserved. A thing, a loan or brokerage account, or an account no person holds is refused with a *Didn't apply* chip, and nothing moves. The extract prompt states the contract: transfers are between people (entity ids) or their own cash, checking, savings or card accounts, never things, with method "cash" when bills or coins change hands in person ("hands me $20", "slips her a twenty") and no method for a bank transfer, an app payment or a card;
  - cash handed over (a transfer with method "cash") lands in the payee's carried cash, else checking, and comes from the payer's carried cash when it covers, else the usual source, so "Danny hands me $20 for gas" adds $20 to the wallet. A transfer's other stated method picks the payer's account the way a purchase's does, so *Pay another way* on a declined transfer tries each account;
  - a narrated transfer that tells again a movement of money the turn's own actions made (`movedAlong`): a withdrawal, a deposit, a move between accounts or a payment, as a posting among the turn's pre-reply events. It is dropped when both sides name that posting's and its amount is the purchase rule's near: within ±25% of what left the one side or reached the other (a machine's fee makes them differ), or no amount stated. A side names a posting's side as an account it drew on (`from`) or paid into (`to`), a party holding one, or a party whose outside account it moved. "I take $60 out of the machine" told as checking paying the wallet $60 moves the money once; a transfer of another amount, or to another account, keeps its chip;
  - in **lenient** worlds a narrated price inside the band is accepted, and one outside it is clamped with a chip. A capped or clamped purchase's chip has *Edit amount*: on a confirm chip it applies the purchase at the amount typed; on an automatic one's Undo chip `economy.reprice` posts the difference between the same two accounts.
  - **A decline after the prose** shows a chip: *Pay another way* (retries savings, then card, then checking, then cash), *Remove it* or *Accept*. By default the item stays unowned and the next Outcome slot says so ("the payment was declined; they don't have it"). Remove and Accept only reword that note. Notes go to the next turn and its swipes, and are dropped once a later message has committed and another follows. They are kept in `userStorage/runs/<chat>/outcome-notes.json`, so a restart before the next turn still tells it.

### 9.6 Scale and storage
- **Record targets and overflow:** §4.3.
- **Materialization** records only player-facing views and actions.
- **Previews** are never stored.
- **Turn-critical work** (simulation, catch-up and timelapse segments) runs in the backend process, inside the context handler, bounded by the deterministic unit caps (§9.2). A default-cap segment took 38 ms on a child process and 2.5 ms in process, far inside the host's 15 s watchdog, so a second process and a per-chat registry copy would have bought nothing (D9).
- **Background lane** (one child process, `dist/worker/background-lane.js`): Check world, Playtest, imports, Build with AI validation. It sends heartbeats within the host watchdog (15 s) and never blocks a turn.
- **Jobs** are kept per user in `userStorage/jobs.json`: kind, world, input, status, progress, message and result. A request for a kind the runner doesn't have, or with an input that isn't an object, is refused. The input is kept as the job runs it: only the fields its kind reads, each cut to what the job can use (Refine's request at 4,000 characters, a map's prose at 4,000, 50 playtest turns of 2,000, pasted text a part past what an index job reads), while an id longer than 200 characters or more than 64 books for an import is refused. An import keeps its checked proposals after every chunk. A job stored as running that the process isn't running (the extension stopped while it ran) reads as **interrupted**: its proposals so far can be reviewed and applied, and **Run it again** starts it with the same input. Running jobs are always kept; of the ended ones, the newest 8, and **Dismiss** removes one. Build lists a world's kept jobs when a tool opens.
- **G9** (follow-up) if chat-open payloads grow.

### 9.7 Bootstrapping from existing lore books
- **Import from lore:**
  1. Pick books.
  2. Extraction on the background lane turns them into per-pack proposals. Proposals cover every pack's kinds (the prompt lists the kinds and fields per pack), are checked against the world's kinds, field types and references, deduplicated deterministically, and grouped by pack. An import reads at most 72 entries per pass; the rest is a second pass. A helper reply that is empty, not JSON, not in the proposals shape, lists no readable proposal or (for lore with text) is an empty list is asked once more with a reminder of the shape, inside the same deadline (a call that timed out or was cancelled is not asked again); Refine keeps an empty answer. Retries spend only the calls no chunk needs, within the import's 12 calls, and Refine shares the retry. Import and Refine calls get room for a full answer (6,000 tokens), each call's outcome and time go to the user's diagnostics, and a reply cut off at its token limit is logged. When proposals are dropped, the job says why.
  3. Review: accept or reject each item. Show value names each source by its entry and book ("Lighthouse · B2 Chat Lore"). Edit accepted items afterwards in the editors.
  4. Entities are created; sources are back-linked (`EntityDef.sources` and `PlaceDef.sources` hold `bookId/entryId`), never edited. Refine changes merge onto the existing entity, and a change that leaves out the entity's kind keeps the kind it has.
  5. Gaps are filled from data tables and marked "Assumed from a table" (`EntityDef.assumed`; `FieldDef.table` marks table-backed text fields). A new entity whose kind the lore leaves out takes the world's single base kind (`thing`: the one root kind that other kinds extend), is marked "Assumed kind" and starts unticked in the review whatever its confidence; `assumed` holds `kind` until a change gives the entity another kind. With no single base kind the proposal is dropped and the job says why. A kind named in a proposal or a kind field is looked up by its own key, so a name like `constructor` is an unknown kind. An uncited proposal stays unlinked rather than guessing an entry.
- **Indexing World Info books and text into lore** (§4.2; `backend/lore-index.ts`): `index_book` (one book) and `index_text` (pasted text) ask the helper for lore entries (title, aliases, topics, links, extra lookup terms) in batches bounded like Import's: 6 book entries or about 6,000 characters of text per call, 12 calls per run, the same one retry for a reply with nothing usable. They read the draft, so a book's entries already in the lore are skipped before any call and a second run picks up the rest. A book proposal must cite an entry of the book (the most confident proposal per entry wins) and keeps the entry's own text, keys and `bookTextHash`; a text proposal keeps the helper's words (at most 4,000 characters) and an `ai` source, pending. A title the lore already has is dropped, a key never replaces an entry (a taken one gets a number), and links to anything but the world's entities and places are dropped with a note. Each proposal carries its lookup additions (`JobProposal.index`: its title, aliases and extra terms; its topics by label). Applying puts the entries, an `ai` source accepted, and merges the additions into the lookup table in one more write.
- **Data tables:** 17 editable **Modern US** tables (approximate current prices, pay, taxes and rents), built from raw sources by `bun run tables` into pinned tables whose revision is `hashJson({schema, columns, rows})`. Each world revision pins the revisions it uses (§9.2). The table editor edits a table; replacing it with another region's is not built.

## 10. Tracks and rules (story systems)
- **Quest track:** stages, objectives, effects, next-stage predicates, gate, fail, rewards, visibility.
- **Secret track:** cue, reveal stages through to the truth. Stages update `knows`, and a stage's truth reaches a section only once revealed. **Advance by predicates only.**
- **Puzzle track:** a solution predicate, or an answer check (exact or normalized, then `puzzle.answer` only where the author enabled it). Attempts and a hint ladder.
- **Agenda track:** steps feed planner priorities.
- **Rules:** triggers on events, ticks, place entry or times, and with the grid system on, someone stepping into a grid's zone or onto one of its cells (`{zone, in?, who?}`, `{cell: [x, y], in, who?}`; each place's area is a zone named by the place). `who` is a selector, the POV when absent; the rule fires when a `space.pos` puts someone inside who wasn't before, so staying inside never fires it again and a replay fires it the same. Cascade cap of 8. Storylet beats.
- **Gates are predicates.** A model decides one only through an authored `puzzle.answer` threshold.

## 11. Actions, checks and bounds
**Inputs**
1. **Action palette:** filtered actions, exact odds, catalog prices. A candidate is offered only if it passes the action's requirements about the candidate alone (they read only the candidate and `self`, and may call on the candidate, like `invited(host)` or `carries(self, item)`), so Pay card lists only your cards and loans, Lend and Transfer only your own things, and Accept invitation only the hosts who invited you. Requirements that read anything else (`at(to, here)`) stay availability.
2. **Map tap:** a place's travel options, or with the grid system a cell on the grid the POV stands on (**Walk here**, the `step` action).
3. **Typed text** through the classifier and `interpret`, its actions played as a queue (below); a "Not an action?" chip. Lend takes days or a said end ("until 7 tonight", "for 3 hours"), at least one; the palette always sends days.
4. **Optional narrator tools** (`inline_available`, off by default).
5. **Timelapse and Pause/Resume clock** through the palette's Timelapse sheet and the Calendar panel. No command-palette commands are registered.

**Execution.**
- Palette, action-chip and map actions append a user message (text plus `intent`) with `triggerGeneration`.
- Resolution runs requirements, then the check, then effects, then the duration.
- The Outcome slot says the result is decided, worded for how the adjudication policy decided it (below).
- An unmet requirement reads as a plain decline ("Not possible here and now: you need to have it with you"; "You're already there."), never as its expression.
- **The action queue.** A typed message's actions (`interpret`'s first and queued ones, §5.2) play in order in one turn, each against the state the one before it left: its requirements, check and effects, its event rules and a fight's turns around it, then its own time, so durations add up and each action's events land in order among the record's pre-reply events; the scene tick stands in only when no action ran. The first draws from the turn's seed as a single action always has, and each queued one from its own fork of it, so a Re-run, which reads the same cached queue, re-samples them all, and a Re-narrate copies them. The queue stops after an action that fails (a requirement, its params, a refused effect such as a declined payment), after a check (resolved, so the player sees how it fell before going on), or at an interruption: the POV no longer acting (dying, dead, or a world's state that doesn't act), a fight starting or under way (a fight's turn is one act), an activity that Speed through offers or runs, a change of place or scene (`sceneKey`) the action's own effects didn't make, or a signal the POV can see whose interrupt class is at `stop` (one it can't see never stops it: stopping would give it away). Off-screen catch-up runs once, after the queue. The plan records how many actions were left and why; the Outcome slot gives every played action's result in order and, when the queue stopped, says the rest of the message hasn't happened. What's left goes on the chip of §5.2. The judge rates the queue's check: the first action with one, weighed in the state the turn starts from, and not judged when that state doesn't offer it yet; `puzzle.answer` reads only the first action.

**Ruleset primitives.** Generic and ruleset-agnostic; rulesets are data over them (`engine/checks`, `engine/ruleset`, `engine/ruleset-lint`).
- **One check mechanism, still exact.** Everything a check reads (modifier, advantage, DC, the opponent's side) is evaluated from the state before the roll and fixed into one notation and one set of bands, which both the palette's odds and the roll read. Bands may test the total, the **natural** die (the first dice term's single kept die: `d20`, `2d20kh1`), matched before the rest, and the **margin** over the DC; odds count the joint law of the natural die and the total, which is exact because the natural die and the other terms are independent. A DC with no bands reads `failure`/`success`. **Advantage** (−1, 0, 1) changes the dice, never the modifier: one more natural die, keeping the higher or lower, cancelling a die at a time; lint refuses it, and natural bands, on dice with no natural die. An **opposed** roll folds into one notation (`1d20-1d20`, modifier A − B, DC 0, so ties go to the roller); a passive score is the DC. Outcome effects read `roll.natural`, `roll.total`, `roll.modifier`, `roll.dc`, `roll.margin` and `roll.outcome` as variables.
- **Anyone rolls.** The core `check` effect has any actor roll, from rules and effects; the roll forks the effect's generator by `actor:<id>`, so it never moves the POV's rolls, and an `engine.check` event records its final values (hidden from the player unless the POV rolled).
- **`roll()` only in effect arguments.** A `=` formula in an effect's arguments evaluates with a generator forked from the effect's own, and the event stores the result, so folds stay deterministic. Modifiers, requirements, derived fields, rule conditions and sheet formulas have no generator: there `roll()` is a lint error and reads as null, so shown odds and reads never change on their own.
- **Pools** are number fields with `pool: {max, min?, recover?}`. `set` and `add` clamp to the evaluated max, an unset pool is full, and `recover` restores by an amount, the pool's `recover`, or to the max. Being fields, they keep undo, Keep, rebase and the reference fold.
- **Progression** (`ruleset.progression`): after each effect (and a ✎ fix), every entity whose XP field it changed rises to the level its XP has reached, one level at a time; each level sets the level field and runs `onLevel` with `level` and `from`, and the POV gets a Journal line. The level-ups are events recorded with the effect, so replays never level up twice. `award` adds XP. `table(id, key, column)` and `table_step(id, value, column)` read pinned data tables anywhere, since tables are pinned per revision.
- **The sheet** (`ruleset.sheet`) lays out Play → You for the POV; `buildEntitySheet` builds it for any entity, so a GM view can show anyone's. Build's **Sheet Designer** lays it out (sections, and rows picked from the POV kind's fields, pools and progressions, or a labelled formula), and its preview is `lre.world sheet`: `previewSheet` on the draft with the unsaved ruleset, so it reads as a new chat would. The backend reads that ruleset as outside data first (`sheetRulesetProblem`): sections, rows and progressions in their shapes, at most 32 sections of 64 rows, 200 thresholds a progression and 1,024 characters a formula, or the request is refused as a bad request. Progressions get a form of their own, with `onLevel` in the rules' effect list. **Level floors** (`ruleset.minLevels`) wrap every effective level the engine reads (`rulesetFloor`), so ruleset math such as stat modifiers works under any realism preset; the chat view carries them (`controls.realism.floors`), so Settings → Realism → This chat and Build's realism defaults name them and never offer a level below one, and a realism marker's preview reads its from and to through them, so a stored level below a floor a later Upgrade or profile added previews as the level the chat plays at.
- **The grid system's floor.** With `mapSettings.grid.movement` on, Space is at least 3 for everyone through the same wrap (`rulesetFloor`), so cells, steps and sight always run; `controls.realism.floors` carries it like a ruleset's.
- **Combat** (`ruleset.combat`, the combat pack) is data over these primitives: an initiative check, an action economy (budgets per turn or round, off-turn ones for reactions), the HP pool, a down rule with its condition (through `sheets.condition`), sides (`hostile`, `ally`), an end rule, when a fight peters out (`stalemate`, in rounds), the POV's turn end (`spent` or `manual`), tactics (`threat`, `lowHp`) and how others' HP shows (wound bands or exact). With wound bands, nothing the player or the narrator reads gives someone else's HP as a number: the Encounter card shows the band, a change chip and the Journal word an HP change as the band it leaves them in ("Goblin: badly hurt") or which way it went ("Goblin takes damage"), through the pack's `combat.hp_line`, and the Outcome says "Goblin takes damage (badly hurt)."; the GM view and Check world keep the numbers. Combat actions are actions with `combat {cost, start?, role?}`, offered only where the ruleset has combat (`offersAction`), so a world without it plans exactly as before. The engine's resolve asks `combat.spend` what one costs (refused out of turn or when spent; spent before the roll, so a miss uses it up), and `planTurn` opens `combat.round` around the POV's action: everyone else's turns still due, then the POV's act (`combat.act`), who fell (`combat.down`, re-checked against the down rule when it folds), whether it's over (`combat.end`, re-checked), the POV's turn end, and everyone else's turns in initiative order until the POV's comes round (one round at most). Their picks are deterministic tactics, or a recorded `npc.choose` decision (`combat.decision`, asked before the plan through `combat.choices`) while still legal; their rolls fork the plan's generator by round and participant. All of it is the turn's events, so swipes, Keep and Re-run behave as for any turn, and the Outcome slot gets the round as the POV sees it (`EngineTurnPlan.combat`). The Encounter card (`combat.encounter`, null between fights) and the palette's Combat tab name no one the POV can't see; the GM view's `encounter` shows everything.
  A combat action's checks roll plainly, under Crunchy, whatever the adjudication policy (`policyFor`, in `resolveAction`, `actionOdds` and the judge step), while other checks during a fight follow the policy: the same attack comes round every turn, so a retry rule would repeat a miss and `marked` timing would let unmarked attacks pass. `combat.adjudicate: "policy"` hands the POV's own combat actions to the policy like any other check; everyone else's always roll plainly, as the `check` effect's NPC checks do, since no player chose them and no judge reads them, and their odds in the Combat tab and the engine's tactics read the same. A fight takes no clock time, so the retry record keeps the fight and its round (`combat.round_key`) and under `changed` a later round is a change; `once` still waits for a new scene, which the ruleset lint warns of.
  A fight is fought where it started: once the POV isn't there it ends (`combat.end` `left`, no winner, re-checked when it folds), so no one fights on unseen, and the player's card and the fight's actions in the Combat tab close as soon as the POV is elsewhere (the GM's card stays until it ends), while Fight is offered again at once: a start counts a fight the POV has left as none (`fightOffers`, `combat.spend`), since the next turn ends it before its action runs. The fold keeps a calm at each new round: a round is calm when no one attacked in it (an act whose action has the attack role or that is aimed at someone on the other side, hit or miss) and everyone's HP is as it was, and `stalemate` calm rounds in a row (3 by default; 0 never) end the fight (`stalemate`, re-checked when it folds), so holding a stance against a foe who keeps swinging never ends one and an author's own end rule gets its turn; a hurt fighter guards only while a foe acted against it this round or the last, so no fight stalls. Its rounds take no clock time: a turn whose action comes inside a fight already under way, and that is still under way after the fight's part of the turn, advances no clock (no scene tick, no action duration), while the turn whose action starts one keeps that action's time (a twenty-minute stalk that ends in an ambush takes its twenty minutes); while one is under way after the turn nothing speeds through, a timelapse or a narrated time skip is refused (`fightHoldsClock`), and everyone in it is held out of schedules, day plans and trips (`combat.engaged`): a trip that would start, or end, while they fight waits for the fight to be over, and one that ended then takes them home only from its place; either way it lets go of them once it is over, so a later visit to its place is only a visit. Other actions stay open and simply take no time. The turns still due and those after the POV's share one round at most. Any combat action that spends something is an act (`combat.act`), so a stance is logged and narrated, and each act, fall and end is one event group with the rule events it fires, so a rebased view keeps or drops it whole. The pack defines the symmetric `hostile` relation type the default sides rule reads. Combat options read their check as every palette option does (`checkView`), so an attack the policy settles without a roll shows the verdict, and lint warns on `adjudicate: "policy"` with narrative authority or a retry rule. The card's down badge reads the fight, at any Sheets level.
- **Profiles** (`shared/profiles`): ready-made rulesets as world fragments in the shapes above, applied once and then the author's to edit. **Modern** (six abilities with `floor((score − 10) / 2)` modifiers, Defense from Dexterity, a level bonus and equipment, twelve modern skills with ranks, Fortitude, Reflex and Will, Action Points that add 1d6 to a skill and refill each level, HP, Reputation, levels on the triangular XP scale), **Classic** (base attack and saves on good, average and poor progressions read from a level table, skills with ranks, Armor Class, HP from a hit die), **Fifth** (a proficiency bonus by level, skill proficiency 0–2, two proficient saves, Dodge as disadvantage on attacks against the dodger, a hit-die pool spent on a short rest and half regained on a long one, advantage for a well-told approach) and **Custom** (one stat, one check, an HP pool with a formula max, a plain attack, a progression at level 1 only). Each brings fields, checks (a skill attempt, a save, an attack against the defense with natural 1 and 20 bands, initiative), actions, data tables, a progression, a sheet, a Sheets 3 floor, an adjudication policy and combat rules. A stance (Total defense, Dodge) spends a budget of its own besides the action, and the defense reads that budget spent as holding the stance until the defender's next turn refills it, so nothing outlives the fight. The names are neutral and the content is mechanics only, original implementations of common tabletop mechanics.
  `applyProfile` puts the fields on the POV's kind (a pack's kind gets a world copy that keeps every pack field, since a world kind replaces a pack kind whole) and sorts every field, check, action, table and ruleset part into new, the same, or a conflict, which is kept unless the author takes the profile's; people read the new defaults, and a stored value changes only when a taken field can't hold it (clamped to its bounds, else cleared). Applying the same profile twice changes nothing. Build previews the plan on the draft with the packs' kinds and applies it as one editor write (`lre.editor profile`), so lint runs as for any edit; `ruleset.profile` records the last profile applied.
- **Table params.** A text param with `table` names a row of that data table (Use a skill's `skill`, Make a save's `save`): the palette offers one option per row with its odds, the resolver takes the row's id or its label and refuses anything else, lint checks the table exists, and `interpret` is told the rows' ids.

**Adjudication policy** (`ruleset.adjudication`, `engine/adjudication`, `shared/adjudication`). How much a check settles against how the player describes their approach, and when a check happens at all, is data. A world without a policy plays by **Crunchy**, which is how checks always played: every check rolls and the roll settles it, and nothing new is recorded.
- **Presets:** `narrative` (Narrative-first), `balanced`, `crunchy` and `custom`. A named preset is always its own values; `custom` keeps them as edited, and a value it can't hold is clamped when read (lint names it).
- **Success:** a check's `pass` outcomes, else `success` and every band listed after it. Its chance is their exact probability, from the odds the palette shows. A check that names no success has none to grant, so it rolls whenever its action runs, whatever the timing (under narrative authority the story still decides, and the dice when the judge gives no odds), and its failures aren't remembered.
- **Order:** a refused retry, then too hard (`impossibleAt`: a chance at or below it fails), then a sure thing (`trivialAt`: at or above it passes), then the timing (`checks`: `every`; `risky`, only below `riskyAt`; `marked`, only for an action, a place (or one around the actor) or an in-progress track with `adjudicate: true`, a track counting only while the POV can see it, as the quest log would show it, so a secret track's progress never shows in whether the dice roll; `never`), then the authority.
- **Unrolled outcomes** are deterministic: a pass is `success` when the action or the check has it, else the check's `auto`, else its most likely passing band (the first on a tie); too hard is `failure`, else the most likely failing band; a refused retry takes the earlier failure's outcome. Outcome effects then see `roll.outcome` and a null total.
- **The player's approach** (`description`: `none`, `bonus`, `advantage`, `dc`, up to `max`): the `judge` step's rating (0 to `max`) adds to the modifier, grants advantage from `advantageAt`, or lowers the DC (a check without one takes it as modifier). It shifts only a check that rolls, after the verdict, so the palette's verdict never waits on a model. A rated roll's odds are recomputed exactly. The judge rates only words: a palette or chip tap with no text of its own (the message is the action's label) isn't judged. Before the reply, its call for the player's own action ranks above `npc.choose`: present agents' choices run beside `interpret` only while a call stays free for it, else they take what is left once it has run.
- **Authority:** `mechanics`, the roll settles it; `hybrid`, the roll settles it, and when the same roll `closeBy` points or fewer either way (the natural die unchanged) reads another outcome it is a close call, and the narrator is told a close success may come at a cost and a close failure leave partial progress; `narrative`, the story settles it: the judge's outcome odds are sampled with a fork of the turn's seed, over the check's band outcomes and the action's own. Without them (the judge off, out of budget or failing, and always for an NPC's check, which no judge reads) the dice decide, plainly and as the palette's odds showed, so a risky check never just succeeds unjudged; the roll chip says nothing judged the approach.
- **Retries** (`retry`: `free`, `changed`, `once`): a non-free policy records each attempt in the apply of its `engine.adjudged` event, in `state.packs.engine.tries` keyed by actor, action and parameters (its time, place, outcome, whether it failed, the check's dice, modifier and DC, and the fight and round it was made in, if any). A failed attempt stands, read without a roll, until the scene changes (the actor is elsewhere, or the POV, whose observations know when it last arrived, left and came back); under `changed` also once `retryAfter` minutes passed since the failed roll, a fight it was made in reached a later round (or ended), or the odds changed. Another action, stat, tool or target is another key. A refused retry records no attempt.
- **Recorded:** every check a non-crunchy policy decides writes `engine.adjudged` before the outcome's effects (rolled or not and why, the judge's rating and reason, a close call, the attempt), so the record shows how it was decided; replays fold it and never ask a model. The judgement itself rides on the turn's intent (`InterpretOutput.judgement`), like `puzzle.answer`'s probability, so a Re-run reads the same one with a fresh seed and a Re-narrate copies the planned events.
- **Where it comes from:** the chat's latest `adjudicate` marker (Settings → Checks → This chat; the starting one included), else the world revision's ruleset, else Crunchy. The palette shows the verdict (`No roll needed`, `Too hard to try`, `Retry needs a change` or `a new scene`, `The story decides (odds …)` with the odds bar as guidance); the roll chip says what was rolled, or "No roll:" and why, with the rating and a close call; the Outcome slot words the result for the authority. NPC checks rolled by the `check` effect always roll.

**One bounds mechanism (D4)**
- Every field has `{max_delta, mode: auto | confirm | never}`, with defaults per domain and per realism preset. **The most restrictive applies.**
- **Extraction defaults per proposal type (D4):** `auto` for `time_advance`, `location` and `condition`; `confirm` for `knows`, `give`, `purchase`, `transfer`, `regard`, `spawn` and `life`; `quantity` has no cold-start mode and starts at `confirm` ("From the field's bound"). Each mode is a setting, one tap to change (D31). These modes are only the **cold start**; promotion then **learns on the player's own connection** (D31), with no new surface and no extra model calls:
  - **Counting:** per extraction type and per connection (the one the `extract` step ran on), from the confirm and undo chips only. A proposal counts once: accepted when confirmed, or applied automatically and not undone by the next turn's commit; rejected when rejected or undone. An ignored confirm chip counts nothing. A read with no pre phase (Re-read edit, Retry read, Continue) counts against the user's real default connection.
  - **Promotion:** at ≥ 0.90 acceptance over at least 30 outcomes, both read from a rolling window of the latest 30, a `confirm` type flips to `auto` on that connection, with a notification chip and undo ("Purchases now apply automatically: undo").
  - **Demotion:** an `auto` type flips back to `confirm` when the window's undo rate rises past 0.10, with the same chip. Undo on either chip restores the previous mode and holds the type there for another 30 outcomes.
  - **Precedence:** a mode the player set in Settings pins the type (it is still counted, never flipped), then the mode learned for the turn's connection, then the cold-start default, then the field bound. Counters live in `userStorage/extraction-learning.json`, under the connection's own key only; nothing is recorded for an unsafe connection id.
- Locked fields are never offered to the model.
- Spawns are capped and deduplicated.
- **Engine outcome wins** over a contradicting extraction.

**Confirm chips.** Only a proposal that would validate right now gets an Apply button; a declined purchase gets a decline chip; the rest are logged and dropped, unless they were automatic changes (next paragraph). A reply that tells the turn's own action again offers no second chip. A narrated give of what the turn lent to the same person, or of something inside it (a lent truck's glovebox), is the loan itself; a narrated purchase of what the turn's own Buy already bought is that purchase (the item names match when one holds all of the other's words, and only when the player character or something they own pays; a sibling of it in the price table only asks, §9.5). A different thing given to that person, or bought, in the same turn still gets its chip. A confirmed chip is applied as a `post` event on the head record if confirmed before the next send. Otherwise it expires at the send, and nothing is applied.

**Changes the engine refuses.** An automatic change the engine refuses for any reason gets a decline chip and an Outcome note instead of being dropped silently: a payment that can't be covered keeps *Pay another way*, and anything else that can't apply (no amount, an unknown item, a transfer to a thing) reads "Didn't apply: …" with *Remove it* or *Accept* and "Nothing changed". The Outcome note quotes the change's chip label as written, so a name keeps its case ('"Danny Okafor → 114 Harbor Street" was narrated but didn't happen', not 'danny Okafor'). A typed action whose params name nothing in the world that fits gets the same chip and note, by the action's label (§5.2). A change that would not validate and was not automatic is still only logged. The people a narrated change names are materialized before it applies (a payer's accounts get their opening balances), and that detail is recorded with it, whether it applied automatically or from a confirmed or re-paid chip.

**Chip actions run once.** Stored chip actions run one at a time per (user, chat) behind a lock: inside it the chip is looked up again and acted on, and it is taken as soon as its write lands, before learning or other bookkeeping, so a double tap applies once and the second tap finds nothing. A swipe's chip acts only while that swipe is shown, as the view shows it, so a tap from a view of another swipe applies nothing. A failure in the learning that follows is logged and does not fail the tap. Adding a chip whose id already exists replaces it.

**Action chips** (Timelapse offers and Continue timelapse) start a turn like a palette action. They lapse at the next send.

**Undo.** Undo covers the latest reply; earlier changes go through Keep/Discard. An undo that later on-path events depend on is refused ("later events depend on it") rather than cascading, which keeps every on-path entry re-validatable (L7). "Not an action?" appears only on interpreted action resolutions, because a timelapse's events are clock and simulation output rather than undoable effects. It undoes the events of every action the turn played from the message, as the record holds them: a combat act's spend, outcome, the rule events it fires and its `combat.act` record are one event group (§11), and it takes all of them.

Each fix on the latest reply has a **Corrected** chip, worded for the player, with **Undo**. One made during a reply is held (P2); if it no longer validates when the reply finishes, a "… didn't apply after the reply" chip says so until dismissed, and the GM view keeps the raw reason. **Place:** the POV's `place`. **Weather** (Weather ≥2): the `weather` field of the region over the POV, picked from its climate's states (the `weather.fix` query). The climate process still steps the region, so a fix holds until the climate next changes the sky, or, under a scripted hold, until the hold ends. **Scene type:** the POV's `scene` field, picked from the engine's four scene types and those of the places the POV can see (§7), or "" to follow the place again; it wins over the place default until an action, rule or fix sets it. |
| **GM view** | Behind a spoiler gate; loaded with `lre.gm` when it opens and again as the chat moves on. **Entities:** search and a kind filter, with removed, spawned, agent and non-public entities marked; each opens a form typed by its field definitions (enum and reference pickers, bounds shown, 🔒 on locked fields, derived values shown but not edited, values the kind doesn't declare edited as JSON), its designed sheet, placement and balance, relations with one input per declared relation field, its tier, agent flag and levels, and its classification (Build's picker, one per facet, hidden facets included) and tags; Remove or Restore; Spawn. **Places** with their visibility, **Tracks** with a stage picker and every truth, then a tab per pack that adds one (`UiRegistry.gm`): **Money** shows anyone's money as their money hub at their own level (§25), with the GM's controls in place of the player's actions (money in and out, status, autopay, closing an account that holds and owes nothing, card states, holds, flows, jobs, prices, household members and every declared field, the ones no field holds as GM effects); and **Edits**: the corrections in force and the held writes that didn't apply. While a fight is under way, **Fight** shows everyone in it, seen or not, in initiative order, with HP exact and the round's acts. Each save is one `lre.edit` (§7) with its own Corrected chip. |
| **Chips** | What changed, elapsed time, rolls and odds (or, when the adjudication policy rolled nothing, why, with the judged approach and a close call; §11), prices and transactions, confirm chips (until the next send), declines, undo, Corrected (a ✎ fix or GM edit on the latest reply, with Undo), didn't apply after the reply (a held write that failed, with Dismiss), Retry read, Retry (an empty reply, D32), Not an action?, what a typed message's turn didn't play (Send, §5.2), Re-narrate/Re-run, overdue use grants, Timelapse offers, Continue timelapse, Keep report (one row per dropped group, convert per row, one-tap Discard, entries "made on another branch"), Re-read edit (an edited reply, or a Continue accepted unread), mode change (a type learned `auto` or went back to `confirm` on this connection, with undo; §11). Detail opened during a turn's in-flight window shows "pending" (§9.2). |
| **Palette** | Tabs by the action id's last part: Actions with targets and odds (moving money between one's own accounts among them), Buy (catalog), Pay, Transfer (only an action whose id's last part starts with `transfer`; none of the packs' does), Drive and Lend; in a world with combat, **Combat** (the actions that start a fight; during one, the combat actions and End turn under the round, whose turn it is and what the POV has left of each budget; first while it's the POV's turn); **Timelapse** (a bottom sheet at 390 px: duration chips, *until* targets, interrupt-policy overrides), Pause/Resume clock. A check the adjudication policy won't roll shows its verdict instead of the odds bar ("No roll needed", "Too hard to try", "Retry needs a change"); one the story decides keeps the bar as guidance (§11). An action that needs a second target (Drive's destination, Lend's borrower: at most 12, nearest first), a piece (Take from a kit lists each kit, then the pieces still in it with how many are left) or a number (Lend's days, default 1) asks for it as a second step; only final picks are judged. Before a chat's first turn the palette, like Settings → Realism → This chat, judges at the levels that turn will use (the starting markers: the world's defaults with the user's settings filling in, §9.1), so an option it offers is not declined when the turn runs. |

Touch targets are at least 40 px (including a number stepper of our own with named − and + buttons, a typed value committed on blur or Enter); icon-only buttons carry labels; tabs use a roving focus with arrow, Home and End keys; dialogs close on Escape and manage focus; text colors follow the host theme at a contrast of at least 4.5:1. The layout is proofed at 390 px and works down to 326 px; real devices are checked live before a release; English first. Drawer layouts keep critical values clear of the host's chat-head bubbles on the right edge at 390 px (D33).

**Money input.** Every amount the player types (the palette's Amount for Pay, Buy without offers and Transfer, the cash ✎, chip amounts) goes through one input and parser driven by `world.currency` (`src/shared/money.ts`, `src/frontend/money-input.tsx`): the currency's symbol or unit beside the box, its own number of decimals ("5" is 5 bits, 5d or $5.00; "5.5" is refused where the currency has none), thousands separators and the symbol accepted either side. Build stores money as whole minor units, and its money fields and money table columns name the world's minor unit ("(bits)", "(pennies)", "(cents)"). Playtest's typed amounts scale by the currency's decimals.

**Cash ✎.** Where the POV's cash is held in cash accounts (the economy panel's cash), the cash fix posts the difference between the POV's cash (carried cash first) and the outside `economy.external:correction` account, memo "Correction" (`economy.set_cash`), so the panel and the pill show the amount and the books balance; it stays a `post` entry of kind `fix`, so undo, swipes and P3 treat it like any fix. A world without cash accounts keeps setting the `cash` field.

**An action the helper couldn't read.** `interpret` asks once more on a transient failure (a reset or refused connection, a timeout, a 5xx); still failing, the text plays as plain roleplay and the turn gets a *Couldn't read your action* chip (`read_failed`) whose *Retry* is a Re-run of the turn, which reads the text afresh. On the Decision route, a chosen action with params and no call left to fill them goes unread the same way.

## 13. Authoring and validation
- **Schema-driven editors** for every pack. Four special widgets: **map/grid, schedule/timeline, track graph, expression**; and the **map editor**, a map's `lre-map-v1` JSON checked on every keystroke against the schema and the map lint (each problem marked on its line), with a preview drawn as Play's map draws it, and grid and node templates. The expression editor's functions are the registry's (`formulaFunctions`, sent with `lre.world get`), never a list kept in the frontend; an effect's arguments are edited one at a time, as a value or a `=` formula, and only those formulas run the editor in effect mode, which adds `roll(`.
- **Describe a map:** a background job like Refine. The author's prose about a place goes to one helper JSON call whose prompt carries the schema and a short example; the answer, with the requested id and place, is checked against the schema and the map lint on the draft, and errors get one repair call that lists them. The map comes back as one proposal with its preview, ticked when it has no errors; applied, it goes into the draft like any map. An answer that is no map fails the job and says why.
- **Lint** (live, plain language):
  - refs, types, cycles, unreachable stages;
  - oversized sections;
  - leaky labels, secret truth in public lore, secret lore without a gate or read by a prose step, cues that give their secret away, name what the player may not know or can never show (§6.6), and lookup-table terms and topics that name missing entries, lead nowhere or contradict an alias;
  - attachment, groups;
  - unbalanced postings;
  - double-booked commitments;
  - predicates on frozen fields after a demotion;
  - a timelapse unit cap smaller than one slot for the world's LOD1 agents plus the POV;
  - section templates that hold a macro (only `{{lines}}` is allowed), and, in an import review, every macro, escape sentinel and shorthand in lore and templates;
  - the adjudication policy: a preset that isn't one, values a custom policy can't hold (each named with the value it plays as), a sure thing that doesn't start above too hard to try, `risky` below 0, `marked` with nothing marked, `pass` or `auto` naming an outcome no band reads, and checks whose success the policy can't tell;
  - **presets (D32):** a dry run of the open (else last played) world chat flags max tokens below 1,500 with a reasoning model, and an enabled preset block that asks replies to carry JSON (such as the Default preset's "Caravan Ledger"). The preset is the one the host assembles with, found as §6.8 says. A dry run sees exactly the blocks that assemble.
- **Check world, Playtest, Import from lore, Build/Refine with AI:** all run on the background lane. Playtest drives typed turns without a model: it finds an action's lexicon phrase as said or with filler words inside it ("walk down to" says "walk to"), and when several actions' phrases are said, the one whose required details the line fills wins (so "I walk over to Alder Market" goes to the market, not a map step with no square), then an exact phrase, then the longest. It reads only the actions interpret is offered: a combat action only where the ruleset has combat, and as the fight's state allows (`combat.offers`: a start outside a fight, any other combat action in one the POV is in). A phrase counts only where the player does it, judged in its own clause: not after a negation ("I don't want to walk to"), not in a hypothetical ("might", "whether to", "thinking about", or an "if" earlier in the sentence), not inside an idiom ("pay attention"), and only with the player as the subject. The nearest subject before the phrase decides: first person ("I", "we", "let's") acts; another pronoun ("he'll"), or a person's name followed only by an auxiliary or "to" ("Danny will walk", "ask Danny to walk"), doesn't; with no subject word the line is a command, unless an auxiliary shows someone else's ("the clerk will walk"). It fills references from names said and reports each turn as the player would see it, rather than bookkeeping. Check world's transcript is the author's view of each day: every event, worded with names like Playtest's (no ids or bookkeeping, at most 40 lines), including people the player has not met and secrets, so off-screen routines and unmet people's events can be checked. Dropped events, failures and leaks stay in the notes.
- **Templates:** three starter worlds, each converted from a neutral template into `lre-world-v1` by `bun run templates`: **Brindlemere** (a fantasy town; Story preset, a custom 13-moon calendar), **Harrowmere Hall** (a 1934 mystery manor; Grounded preset) and **Port Alder** (a modern slice-of-life town; Simulation preset). They carry 24, 21 and 26 public lore entries. Each lints clean, runs eight days with its books balanced, and has scripted walkthrough tests: quests finish, secrets stay hidden until found, puzzles appear once found.
- **World import/export:** `lre-world-v1`, one document with the world's lore inside; World Books are not exported.
- **The authors' reference and recipes:** `bun run reference` writes `docs/REFERENCE.md` from what the engine and each pack register (kinds and fields, effects with the arguments they declare, formula functions, actions, events, proposals, panels, slots, tables and lint codes), and a test keeps it current; every recipe in `docs/RECIPES.md` is a world fragment a test loads, lints and plays.

## 14. Quality gates and acceptance (D15)

**Unit tests**
- determinism and fold order (markers up to `markersApplied` → `events` → `post`, including `cont`, and later markers by `seq`), including **Upgrade or chip confirm, then Continue**: the `cont` entry folds after them, exactly as validated;
- concrete deltas versus final values; Keep re-validation and report;
- **adjudication** (§11): a world without a policy rolls and records exactly as before; when a check rolls under each timing (marked actions, places around the actor and in-progress tracks included) and the palette's verdict is the turn's; the sure-thing and too-hard thresholds; each approach weight with a recorded judgement replayed to the same turn; narrative authority with the judge's odds and without them; hybrid close calls and their wording; each retry rule (time, odds, a new place and a later round of the fight, or its end, lift `changed`; only a new scene lifts `once`); and the chat's `adjudicate` marker over the world's and the user's policy, with the property suite writing `adjudicate` markers among its random writes;
- **rebased views:** re-validation with the Keep-report defaults writes nothing to later records; a `keep` conversion applies only under its parent path; **switch away, then make a materialization, ✎ fix, chip confirm, Continue, realism, time and Upgrade marker and a new turn on the switched path, then switch back: the fold equals the original exactly**, each of those entries is inert there and listed "made on another branch", and switching to that path again makes them live; a marker made during a regenerate of an earlier message is inert after navigating that message back to its old swipe; re-convergence skips re-validation once the state matches; a Discard is tombstoned, so re-assert and restart recovery never restore it, even after a lower swipe's deletion shifts indexes;
- Re-run independence (a 50% check across trials) and Re-narrate (P3), including **Upgrade, then Re-narrate**: the new swipe folds exactly as the source up to the reply, its narration renders from the same prefix, its post steps run once and its `narr` entry folds after the Upgrade; a copied entry that depends on the source's post-step results is dropped with a Journal note;
- **in-flight window (P2):** a chip confirm, ✎ fix, materialization, Keep or marker made while a turn streams or during its post pass shows "pending", takes its `seq` after the commit and folds after the turn's events (after the `cont` entry for a Continue); one made while two windows are open waits for both; post-step events validate against exactly the fold prefix, so re-folding equals validation; `seq` keeps increasing across restarts;
- skip N days equals N one-day steps and any segmentation of the same timelapse; an applied `time.advance` to T equals a timelapse to T with every interrupt class at `note`; an over-cap advance, or one whose catch-up exceeds the unit cap or the post-pass bound, applies nothing and becomes a Timelapse chip;
- timelapse interrupts: the same state and policy stop at the same slot; switching a class from `stop` to `note` leaves the events before the stop unchanged; unit-cap segments end on slot boundaries and always advance at least one slot;
- time controls as markers: a tick, scale or pause change folds by `seq`, and a swipe planned before it keeps its old tick; changing Settings → Time or the world's default ticks after the first record leaves a running chat unchanged; a Continue on the greeting before the first turn is accepted unread, the starting markers land on the first record that holds events, and a Discard of that record or a greeting-swipe switch leaves them in force;
- `time.advance` extraction: narrating the turn's own elapsed time ("after the three-hour drive", a Montage's "three days later") adds no time, a timelapse turn proposes none, and a Continue that refers back to time the reply already advanced adds none;
- `interpret` re-simulation: typed "I go to bed" re-simulates the night's duration, and with Sleep on `auto` runs the timelapse;
- Montage: no secret or POV-unknown events; aggregates equal the ledger totals for the interval;
- ledger balance invariants; promote/demote leaves aggregates and the trial balance intact; level contracts at mixed levels (a transfer between a level-4 party and a level-1 party keeps the trial balance);
- materialization determinism, including across an extension update with unchanged pins; new tables or generators arrive only through Upgrade; promotion from a band changed at level 1 lands inside that band; detail opened during a turn's in-flight window shows "pending" and materializes once, after the window closes;
- marker `seq` fold order; kit template plus overrides; planner determinism with recorded tie-zone choices; expression and A* caps;
- leak tests: no chip, Journal line or model prompt names an unmet or secret entity, even when the narration describes an unnamed figure who is such an entity.

**Fake-host tests**
- a stopped reply, or a generation that ends in error, commits `stopped`, unread, with a Retry read (none on a record read elsewhere, whose read is always refused);
- the Continue path: suffix-only extraction, no double application, the `cont` entry's `seq` at commit. A non-head Continue (explicit `message_id`) appends no events, records `accepted`, offers Re-read edit and leaves later paths unchanged. A Continue whose prefix holds literal braces is accepted unread, with no conflict. A stopped Continue, head or non-head, records `accepted` on the message found by content, even when another message is edited or another writer touches metadata during the Continue, and raises no conflict; Retry read on the head extracts the partial suffix once;
- an earlier-swipe switch makes later records rebased views without a pause; switching back restores the original at any time. An earlier user-message edit pauses. Editing an assistant reply, on the head or earlier, by edit or swipe `PUT`, keeps the world and offers Re-read edit; Re-read on an earlier message rebases later records. Deleting a middle message rebases. A regenerate with an explicit `message_id` on an earlier message is a turn there, and later records become views;
- an earlier swipe switch while a reply streams: the new record commits off-path as a rebased view with a Keep-report chip; an earlier user-message edit while it streams puts it under the pause, and an assistant edit does not; the host's macro re-render of the turn's own user message does not pause either;
- timelapse: zero model calls under Standard, the Custom call cap, nothing applied on a simulation timeout, Speed through `offer` versus `auto`;
- deleting a middle message keeps its markers' effect on later turns; in a world chat the interceptor sends nothing for user entries and no recursion override while the sections go in; an engine-book entry it didn't fill is voted off, and a lore copy an earlier build left there is removed; a world with a `select` step template loads without it;
- marker durability (frontend echo, deleting the latest message, restart); Discard tombstones under re-assert, restart and swipe-index shifts; the shadow overlay; Edit-and-Send; Dry Run purity (previews only); background-lane failure and backlog; per-preset call counts;
- after a restart: an interrupted job is reported and its proposals can still be reviewed and applied; a decline note reaches the next turn's Outcome slot; the inspector's history and delivery badges and the call meter are unchanged; a damaged file of any of these is set aside and a fresh one used;
- a double-tapped chip applies once.

**Ledger property suite (the ledger gate)**

The ledger is accepted by this executable suite, not by review on paper.
- **Reference fold:** a small, obviously correct implementation of §4.3, kept in its own module and sharing only the pack effect handlers and validators with the optimized fold. It has no cache, overlay shortcut, snapshot or re-convergence, and re-folds every message from the world revision on every query.
- **Fake host:** replays the host event orders and payloads recorded on the reference host (§18).
  - Every generation runs `GENERATION_STARTED` → context handler → interceptor → `WORLD_INFO_ACTIVATED` (only when a book is attached) → `GENERATION_IN_PROGRESS` → tokens → save → `GENERATION_ENDED` or `GENERATION_STOPPED`.
  - A normal turn stages its reply empty with `MESSAGE_SENT` before `GENERATION_STARTED` and saves it with `MESSAGE_EDITED`, or creates it with `MESSAGE_SENT` at the save (P4). Regenerate, swipe and both Edit-and-Send modes stage with `MESSAGE_SWIPED` `added` and save with `updated`. A Continue saves with `MESSAGE_EDITED`, twice when its prefix re-renders.
  - `GENERATION_STOPPED` is `{generationId, chatId, content}`, with the suffix only for a Continue. Its save comes first.
  - Swipe navigation stays live on any message while a reply streams. Branch creation is silent. Event payloads carry no `userId`; it arrives as the handler's second argument. A reply can be saved empty. A restart removes a staged reply. A swipe delete leaves `spindle_metadata` unshifted.
  - The host's macro re-render of the turn's user messages (P4), which the reference host did not show, is generated in both variants (present and absent).
- **Randomized operation sequences** (seeded generator; failures shrink to a minimal sequence). Operations:
  - send, regenerate and swipe (Re-run and Re-narrate);
  - Continue on the head, on a non-head message and with a stop;
  - Edit-and-Send in both modes;
  - earlier swipe navigate, add, delete and `PUT`;
  - middle and latest delete;
  - branch and import;
  - chip confirm, ✎ fix and materialization;
  - realism, time and Upgrade markers;
  - Keep, Discard and convert;
  - restart, and foreign or frontend clobbering;
  - any of these interleaved inside an in-flight turn, while it streams and during its post pass;
  - after the rest, from a stream of their own (so every sequence before them plays as it did): turns the engine plans with life changes (the `life` effect and a rule that tells of a death) and queued actions that stop at a check, a failure or an interruption, GM life edits, and the Re-runs, edits, swipes, Keep choices, Upgrades and in-flight turns that move them onto another view. The suite asserts that life changes, tellings and queues of each kind happen.
- **Oracle:** after every operation, the optimized fold equals the reference fold (state, Journal notes and Keep report), and the invariants the runner checks hold: L1–L3, L5–L9, L11, L13–L20, L31, L34 and L38–L46 (`test/support/ops.ts`). The rest are checked case by case: L4, L10 and L12 in the ledger scenarios and reference cases, L37 in the fake-host tests, and L21–L24, L26, L28, L29, L32, L33 and L36 in unit tests; L27 and L30 have no test of their own.
- **Volume:** a default run executes a fixed-seed sample of 200 sequences of up to 60 operations; `LRE_PROPERTY_RUNS`, `LRE_PROPERTY_OPS`, `LRE_PROPERTY_SEED`, `LRE_PROPERTY_BASE` and `LRE_PROPERTY_FROM` set the count, the longest sequence, one seed to replay, the seed base and the first seed index, so a long run can be split into shards. Every run also replays the fixed regression seed set (every seed that has ever failed, shrunk). The ledger gate passes only when both pass.
- **Named invariants.**

| Invariant | Property |
|---|---|
| **L1 Reference equality** | The optimized fold (cache, overlay, re-convergence) equals the reference fold. |
| **L2 Switch away and back** | Switch away from any earlier swipe, do anything there (materialization, ✎ fix, chip, Continue, markers, turns, conversions), switch back ⇒ the original fold, exactly. Everything made there is inert and listed "made on another branch". |
| **L3 No pause on a switch** | An upstream active-swipe change or message removal never pauses; only a user-message content edit does. |
| **L4 Classification by state** | Classification is a function of the chain-snapshot diff, never of event names: a user-message edit, an assistant edit or swipe `PUT` (on the active swipe, or on an inactive one once it becomes active), an explicit-`message_id` regenerate and a middle delete classify as content edit, assistant edit, turn and removal. |
| **L5 Off-path commit** | A turn whose chain changed while it was in flight never commits on-path: an upstream swipe change gives a rebased view, and a user-message edit, including one to its own user messages, gives the pause. An assistant edit leaves it on-path. Back on the chain it was planned on (its swipes as they stand after deletions), it folds on its path with nothing failing. |
| **L6 Host writes are not edits** | The host's macro re-render of the turn's own user messages and a Continue's own save never classify as content edits. |
| **L7 Re-fold equals validation** | Every committed event and `post` entry, re-folded, sees exactly the state it was validated against (its fold prefix, or the head's fold up to its `seq`). |
| **L8 One window per turn** | No write made inside a turn's window is journaled before the window closes; each then takes its `seq` and folds after the turn's events, and detail shown as "pending" is recorded once. |
| **L9 Persistent `seq`** | `seq` comes from the journal's counter, strictly increases across restarts and clobbering, and never names two different entries (a Re-narrate copy keeps its source's `seq`). |
| **L10 Fold order equals planning order** | A swipe folds as it was planned: live markers ≤ `markersApplied`, `events`, then `post` entries and later markers by `seq`. A marker made after a swipe never changes how that swipe's events fold. |
| **L11 Re-narrate** | A Re-narrate folds exactly as its source up to the reply; each copied entry passes re-validation against its own prefix or is a Journal drop; its `narr` entry folds after everything in effect when it was narrated. When the source cannot be reproduced, it is a Re-run. |
| **L12 Re-run** | A Re-run records a fresh seed and re-folds identically; across generated trials a 50% check passes within tolerance. |
| **L13 Continue reads only its suffix** | A Continue never re-reads or re-applies effects already in the record, and its `cont` entry folds after every entry journaled before it started. |
| **L14 Accepted Continue** | A non-head Continue, one on a message without a record, a stopped one and one whose prefix was re-rendered append no events to any record and move no later path; the target holds the text as `accepted`. |
| **L15 Stopped-Continue target** | A stopped Continue's text lands on the message found by content, whatever else was edited or written meanwhile; no record is orphaned and nothing pauses. |
| **L16 No silent drop** | Every stored entry the fold does not apply has a Journal note or a Keep-report line (dropped, inert, Re-narrate drop, tombstoned). |
| **L17 No resurrection** | After a Discard, no re-assert, restart, clobber recovery, swipe-index shift or later operation restores a discarded record. |
| **L18 Durability** | After foreign or frontend clobbering and a restart, records, markers and held writes are re-asserted and the fold is unchanged. |
| **L19 Deletion keeps live markers** | Deleting a middle or the latest message keeps its live markers' effect on later folds; its inert markers go with it, journaled. |
| **L20 Starting markers** | Each chat has exactly one set, on the first planned record that holds events, live on every path, each journaled as the marker check gives it (a world's half-minute tick as the minute it plays); a chat an earlier build started keeps them as that build journaled them, since its paths were hashed with those. Later Settings changes, switches, Discards and deletions never change it, and a copy gets them where its chat does. |
| **L21 Concrete deltas** | Events hold deltas, never final values, and a view re-applies them. |
| **L22 The ledger balances** | The trial balance is zero after every operation, at every level mix, including external-economy postings and payroll withholding. |
| **L23 Uniform representation** | Demote, change and promote leave aggregates and the trial balance intact and restore nothing stale. |
| **L24 Materialization** | A first promotion from a band lands inside the band. With unchanged pins, detail is identical across extension updates; new tables or generators arrive only through a `migrate` marker. |
| **L26 Elapsed time** | Every unpaused turn advances the clock by its elapsed time; a time-control change applies by `seq`, never to a swipe planned before it. |
| **L27 No double time** | A swipe's clock advance equals its elapsed time plus advances for narrated time beyond what it had already applied; timelapse and Speed-through turns propose none. |
| **L28 Advance equals timelapse** | An applied `time.advance` to T folds like a timelapse to T with every interrupt class at `note`; an advance whose catch-up cannot run whole applies nothing. |
| **L29 Segmentation** | Skipping N days = N one-day steps = any segmentation of the same timelapse; every segment advances at least one slot. |
| **L30 Interpret re-simulates** | After `interpret` changes the action, its duration or its activity, the recorded elapsed time and simulation equal those of a turn planned with that action directly. |
| **L31 Determinism** | The same operations and seeds give the same fold, whatever the wall clock, lane scheduling or chunking. |
| **L32 Bounds** | Every applied model-proposed change is within its field bound and channel (§9.4); prices come from the catalog or the category band. |
| **L33 Overflow** | A record past its size target coarsens or chunks, and round-trips without truncation. |
| **L34 Previews record nothing** | Dry Run, the GM view, the inspector, audits and playtests leave the ledger and journal unchanged. |
| **L36 Kits** | Kit completeness is derived from template plus overrides; taking a piece adds one override. |
| **L37 Context on the fake host** | The interceptor disables, enables and forces nothing outside the world's own book and sets no recursion override; inside it, only filled slots are enabled and forced, and every other entry is disabled. |
| **L38 Assistant edits keep the world** | Editing an assistant message, on the head or earlier, by edit or swipe `PUT`, active or inactive, never changes the fold unless Re-read edit is tapped; Re-read on an earlier message makes later records rebased views. |
| **L39 User edits pause** | Editing an earlier user message pauses later records until Keep or Discard. |
| **L40 No negative accounts** | No own account folds below zero on any path (a Keep conversion is the exception); a rebased view drops what it cannot cover, and its dropped notes and Keep lines agree. |
| **L41 Groups land whole** | Within a record, a group of events is dropped whole or kept whole, never in part. |
| **L42 Snapshots** | A cold fold that starts from the chat's stored snapshots equals the full fold. |
| **L43 A Re-run carries its fixes** | Every live ✎ fix or GM edit of the swipe a Re-run was made from is on the new record with its `seq`, events and undos, anchored at the record's path, or is a Journal drop; nothing else is copied. |
| **L44 An Upgrade keeps what play changed** | An Upgrade with picks never takes a revision's value over one play changed, unless the owner picked it. |

## 15. Reuse from Warp and the studio seam (D1, D23)

[Warp](https://github.com/japolino/warp) is japolino's earlier roleplay engine. I reused parts of it with his permission for this non-commercial project, and the `NOTICE` file lists every file.
- **Adapted code** (carried over and changed): `engine/expr.ts` (the safe formula language, with hard limits, a typed limit error, a shareable step budget and a finite-number result), `engine/dice.ts` (the dice notation and roller, with an injected generator, exact outcome distributions and bounded input) and `engine/decide.ts` (the decision question and answer shapes, with strict validation and a canonical option order).
- **Design only, with no Warp code retained:** the per-swipe turn record in message metadata and the path hash (the ledger, §4.3), a small deterministic fingerprint (`hash.ts`), revision-counted state pushes (`push.ts`), a fake host that drives the real backend in tests, a whole-loop simulator as a quality gate, and pan and zoom kept as pure functions (`map-math.ts`).
- **Written for the adaptation:** `engine/rng.ts`, a seeded generator (sfc32 seeded from the hash of a seed string) that replaces the generator inside Warp's dice module. MurmurHash3 (Austin Appleby) and sfc32 (Chris Doty-Humphrey) are public-domain algorithms.
- **Looked at and not used:** Warp's overlay layout, world, quests, companion-life and companions test files. A comparison found no shared code or structure.
- Warp's reasons for cutting scope are inferred from its commits; its design documents are not published.

**Studio seam (fixed now, so the studio needs no breaking change later)**
- **World format:** `lre-world-v1`, with its JSON Schema (`schemas/lre-world-v1.schema.json`) and the map's (`src/shared/lre-map-v1.schema.json`), both in the package `exports`, and per-pack versions.
- **`src/studio-api.ts`:** imports only pure pack `core` modules (lint-enforced) and is exported through the package `exports` map as `lumi-rp-engine/studio`. It provides load, validate, migrate, lint, a new world, the revision id, the export file, Playtest, Check world, the Sheet Designer's preview, the `lre-state-v1` payload, the schema reader, the Studio bridge's names and payloads, and the format, engine and pack versions. `docs/STUDIO.md` is its manual.
- **`lre-state-v1` window event payload:** `{engineVersion, worldFormat, packs:{id:version}, inbound}`, announced when the frontend loads, when the pack set changes and when a `lre-state-request-v1` event asks. `inbound` lists `file` and `rpc:lumi_rp_engine_studio.offer`.
- **Install from a studio:** an export file through Build's **Import an lre-world-v1 file…**, which makes a new world and lists its macros for review; or LumiRPEngine Studio's offer, through the Studio bridge.
- **The Studio bridge** (`shared/studio-bridge.ts`, `backend/studio.ts`; STUDIO.md "The Studio bridge"). A shared RPC read carries no request and no user, so it carries no commands: each side publishes endpoints per user and the other pulls.
  - **Engine → Studio, read-only.** Per user: the world list, each world's head and each revision, and the world chats' summaries. Channels name ids by their bytes in hex (a segment takes only `[a-z0-9_-]`; user and world ids are mixed case). Every endpoint answers `lumi_rp_engine_studio` only, and the summaries' read policy needs `chats`. A summary is built from the view last pushed to the chat, so it carries only what the player sees (place, clock, cash, the people present with their life state as the POV knows it, the open quests): the Studio is an authoring tool and reads the world document for the rest, but the summary never becomes a way around the player layer. A secret track the quest log masks goes with an opaque id (`hidden-1`, …), since its own id is the author's and may name the secret.
  - **Studio → engine, by offer.** The Studio publishes the user's pending offer (a document, `new` or `revision` of a world, the revision it was taken from, an optional chat to attach) and its frontend dispatches `lre-studio-offer-v1`; Build reads it, reviews it (counts, lint, macros, what blocks it) and installs it only on the player's **Install**, as the offer reviewed: a new world as an import makes one, or a new revision under the world's own id, whose chats move to it only through an Upgrade. A revision is refused while Build holds an unsaved draft of the world, which a later Commit would put back over it. When the world's head has moved since the revision was taken (its `baseRevision`, or none given), Build warns rather than blocks: revisions are immutable, so the head it passes over stays, with its chats, but installing takes the player's explicit **Install anyway** (`acceptMovedHead` on the request), which also catches a head that moves after the review. Install names back the hash of the offer reviewed and is refused when the offer has changed since. Installs run one at a time per user, and the offer is marked installed as soon as its world is written, so it installs once however often Install is pressed. The chat the offer names gets Play's Attach and its confirmation when it's open and plays no world.

## 20. Where the design yields to the host

**Principles an extension can't keep whole.** The engine can't be the authority over the chat, because an extension isn't the host, so three principles take a weaker form:
1. **Authority.** LumiRPEngine is a namespaced proposal layer with a versioned export, not the owner of the chat.
2. **Canonical plane.** The ledger rides on message metadata; the shadow journal compensates for the host's weaknesses there (§4.3).
3. **Branching.** Re-narrate versus Re-run is re-render versus re-execute. A chat branch is "branch world state".

**Choices and their reasons.** The sections above describe the engine as it is; each row here names the more obvious design, what the engine does instead, and why.

| The obvious design | What the engine does | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Turn-critical simulation in a child *turn lane* (D9) | In the backend process, inside the context handler, bounded by the same unit caps; the child lane stays for Playtest, Check world, imports and Build with AI (§9.6) | A segment took 38 ms on a child process and 2.5 ms in process |
| Re-narrate always copies its source (P3) | It falls back to a Re-run when the source cannot be reproduced (§4.3) | P3 cannot hold without the source's prefix |
| A `seq` is never reused (L9) | A Re-narrate copy keeps its source's `seq`; L9 reads "never names two different entries" | P3 and L9 conflicted as written |
| An undo cascades | An undo that later on-path events depend on is refused (§11) | Keeps L7 |
| An inspector history kept for diagnostics | The last 10 runs per chat with the delivery badges, stored with the chat (§6.5) | Bounded, and a restart keeps it |
| World document without a currency | Adds `currency`; Build drafts with Commit and Discard (§4.1) | Authoring needed them |
| Import: accept, edit or reject each proposal | Accept or reject; edit afterwards in the editors (§9.7) | Bounded like extraction |
| Data tables: worlds can replace them with another region's | The table editor edits a table; replacement is not built (§9.7) | Not on the critical path |
| World lore without a world-document field | `WorldDoc.lore` and an editor collection, kept in the world and read by the engine's helpers, not by the narrator (§4.2, §5.4) | The starter worlds carry lore |
| Models select lore in the World Info interceptor (D13), with pins (D19), adoption and suppression (D11) | Retired: lore handling is off, user books follow native World Info, and the interceptor delivers only the engine's sections (§6) | The engine's own lore search serves its helpers instead (§5.4) |
| Decline after the prose: other method / remove / accept | Pay another way retries savings → card → checking → cash; Remove and Accept reword the next Outcome note (§9.5) | "Accept" reads as accepting the decline |
| "Not an action?" undoes an interpreted Speed through | Only interpreted action resolutions get the chip (§11) | A timelapse's events are clock output |
| A confirm chip for every confirm-mode proposal | Only proposals that would validate now get Apply (§11) | Apply always failed for unpriced items |
| Prompted steps may use host macros with `commit:false` | Engine placeholders only (§5.2) | Helper calls skip prompt assembly |
| Where a step's output goes was unspecified | §5.2 | Corrections and suggestions stay under the player's control |
| Dynamic values never contain `{{` or `}}` | Every brace, escape sentinel and shorthand is neutralized (§6.3) | Breaking only `{{` pairs left runnable forms |
| A rebased view drops a failed event | Events may be grouped, and a group is kept or dropped whole; packs may add guards (§4.3) | A dropped payment left its history line and the bought item |
| `interpret` maps typed text to the world's actions | The catalog always includes "Let time pass"; a typed duration up to 24 hours runs as the turn's timelapse, and an *until* however far off it is (§9.2) | A cap on durations keeps a misread number from skipping days |
| Import fills gaps from tables "marked assumed" | `sources` and `assumed` fields, `FieldDef.table` (§9.7) | The plan named no fields |
| One option per palette target | A second step for two-target and numeric actions (§12) | Two-ref actions had no way to pick their second target |
| An in-memory fold cache meets the 50 ms gate | Persisted fold snapshots (§4.3) | A cold fold at 1k turns took 158 ms |
| The D32 preset lint reads the active preset | It reads a dry run of the open chat (§13) | Spindle has no read API for the active preset setting |
| Action tick of 30 seconds | 1 minute: settings and markers round ticks to whole minutes (§9.2) | |
| A command palette (D12) | Not built; the input bar's palette and the Calendar panel cover Timelapse and Pause (§11) | Not on the critical path |

**Known limits of the engine.** There is no dormant-quest field: a quest that waits for its trigger starts on a bare stage, which the quest log hides until it moves on. Rules fire at slot ticks, so an authored death at 23:05 lands at the next one (Harrowmere's at 06:00 on Saturday), and the body only then stops its routine.

## 22. Accepted residual risks

The ledger is accepted by an executable property suite (§14): a reference fold, a fake host that replays the host's recorded event orders, randomized operation sequences and named invariants. These are the risks the design accepts.

1. If the backend stalls, the world's sections are missing from that turn; a turn it couldn't plan in time commits `degraded`.
2. If two generations in one chat overlap, the interceptor renders the newest planned turn, which may be the other generation's.
3. The host-wide worker cost remains until G3.
4. Cross-extension metadata clobbering remains until G2.
5. Activation is confirmed, not delivery: the delivery check finds whether a section reached the prompt, not where it landed.
6. Retired with lore handling: the engine no longer judges user entries, so a card entry forced by another interceptor no longer matters to it.
7. A non-head Continue, a Continue on a message without a record, a stopped Continue and a Continue whose prefix the host re-rendered are accepted unread, like any assistant edit: the world stays as simulated until Re-read edit (Retry read for a stopped head Continue), so the text and the state can disagree until then.
8. Detail first opened during a turn's in-flight window (while it streams or during its post pass) shows "pending" until the window closes; writes made then land after the turn.
9. Before the reply, a Continue renders the head's state, since its target is known only at `GENERATION_ENDED` (until G1).
10. Entries made on another branch stay stored but inert; the Keep report lists them, and they apply again only when that branch is active. A Continue made on another branch leaves its text in the message, unsimulated on this one. Deleting a message drops its inert markers (journaled).
11. A foreign extension's metadata write on a reply that is still streaming reads as the reply's save, opening its P4 window early; a player edit to that turn's own user message after it would then be accepted as the host's re-render.
12. Extraction quality is a floor measured on a weak model (§9.4); only time, location and condition changes start `auto`. Learning on the player's own connection and the per-type pin in Settings are the safeguard.
13. Authored lore keeps its macros, as in native World Info; the import review lists them but does not block them.
14. Keys aren't linked to the vehicles they open, so handing over keys with a lend shows its own confirm chip; a future `opens` relation would fold it in. (A narrated give of the thing lent, or of something inside it such as a lent truck's glovebox, is folded into the loan and offers no chip.)

## 23. Outside agents

A user's own agent harness (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and the like) manages their worlds through the `lumirpengine` CLI and its MCP server (`docs/MCP.md`). Both live in this repository, and neither needs a change to Lumiverse.

**The way in.** The client goes in by the same doors a browser does:
1. It signs in once with the user's name and password and keeps only the session token.
2. It finds the engine's extension id in the extension list.
3. It takes a single-use WebSocket ticket and opens the socket as a widget runtime, so it never takes over the browser's frontend slot.
4. Over that socket it sends `lre.*` requests as `SPINDLE_BACKEND_MSG` frames and reads the `lre.result` and `lre.error` replies by `reqId`.

The host sends no acknowledgements, so the client sets its own timeouts. The token reaches the whole account, and sign-in is rate-limited, so the client reuses the token and doesn't sign in again for each command.

**The contract** (`AGENT_API` 1, `shared/protocol.ts`). `lre.hello` answers with `agentApi`. A client names itself there with `client: {name, version}`, and the user's diagnostics log that an outside client connected. Beyond that, the contract is these requests and one push:
- **The working copy's version.** Every `lre.editor` result carries `version`, the version of the world's working copy: the stored draft's or, with none stored, the count the index keeps (`draftVersion`). Each write counts one, and so does each change to the working copy that isn't a write: a discard or a commit records the count before the draft's file goes, and a head moved under no draft (a Studio install) counts one. So a version never names two states of one world, and a client holding one from before a commit or a discard can't write over what came since. A new draft starts one above the count. A stored draft at or below it was dropped (its file couldn't be removed) and reads as gone; one stored before versions existed reads as one above it, and an index without the count counts on from the stored draft's own. A write, a discard or a commit that sends `version` is refused with `conflict` when the working copy is at another, and changes nothing; one that sends none isn't checked.
- **`patch`** applies RFC 6902 operations (`shared/json-patch.ts`) to the whole draft, or to a copy of the head when no draft is stored. It applies all of them or none, and a failed `test` is a `conflict`.
- **`restore {revision}`** makes the draft a copy of one of the world's revisions. Its `base` stays the head, so a commit makes that content head again.
- **`lre.world commit`** takes a `note`, which is trimmed and cut to 200 characters, and a `version`, checked like an editor write's.
- **`templates`** lists the starters `create` takes, with a name and a summary.
- **`chats {worldId?, chatId?}`** lists the user's chats that play a world that still exists: all of them, one world's or one chat. Each comes with the revision it plays now, after any Upgrade, and whether that revision is `behind` the head; a chat whose ledger can't be read (its world lost a revision it needs) comes with `revision: null` and the `reason`. Each listed chat's ledger is opened to read that, and folded once it has upgraded, so a filter keeps the cost to the chats asked about. The host lists chats newest first by offset, so the list shifts while it is read: the pages overlap, and the front is read again until it shows nothing new. `upgrade` answers `pending` when the chat is mid-turn; its marker lands when the turn ends.
- **`lre.draft.changed {worldId, version, stored, base}`** goes to every session of the user after a write, discard, commit or restore of a world's draft, after a lore save, and after a Studio install moves the head.

**Concurrency.** Requests are handled concurrently, and two writers that both read the same draft would each write it back without the other's change. So every write of one world waits for the one before it, through one queue per (user, world) in `WorldStore`. That covers editor writes, discards, commits and restores. It also covers the writes that reach a world from outside the editor: lore saves (which pick a free id from the draft inside the queue), job proposals (which the client applies as editor writes), Studio installs, the lore book id kept in the index, and deletion.

A push goes out after the reply to the request that made the change, so the session that made it already holds the new version and has nothing to reload. **Build** names the version it holds on every write, on a discard (the version shown when it asked) and on a commit, and one refused as stale reloads the draft. A push newer than what Build holds reloads it once a burst of pushes stops (`QUIET_MS`), with one toast; an older push, or one from Build's own write, is ignored, and a load already under way finishes first, since it may have read the change already. Build also reloads after a commit, and when its page comes back (the socket reconnects, the browser is back online, or the page is shown again), since it may have missed a push. A reload never resets a form being edited: when the draft's value of what a form edits changed under its edits, Build's bar says *This item changed elsewhere* with **Load theirs** and **Keep mine**, and a save over that change asks first (*Save over the other change*).

**History.** `history.json`, beside the world's index, keeps one note for each time a revision became head: `{revision, at, source, note?}`, oldest first, where the source is `create`, `build`, `agent`, `import` or `studio`. `lre.world get` returns it as `history`; the index and `list` leave it out, so listing worlds stays small however long a world's history grows. A commit from a session that said hello with `client` is `agent`'s, and the dispatcher keeps those sessions in memory. Committing the head as it is adds nothing. A world made before history was kept starts its history at its next head.

**Failures.** A discard or a commit records the count before it removes the draft's file. When the count can't be written, nothing has changed and the request fails. When the file can't be removed, the request fails saying what was done (`… but its file couldn't be removed (…); it no longer reads as the draft`), and the file, now at or below the count, reads as dropped, so it never locks the world out of committing.

**Guards.** A patch is refused, as `bad_request` naming the operation and its path, when it:
- brings in more than 4 MiB of JSON (`PATCH_SIZE_CAP`): the values it adds and replaces and what it copies count, so copies that double the draft stop early;
- would nest the world deeper than 64 levels (`DOC_DEPTH_CAP`), counted where each value lands, moves included (`put` and `set` are held to the same cap for their item or part);
- leaves an item in a world collection under an id that isn't 1 to 200 characters long;
- carries more than 500 operations;
- names a prototype key (`__proto__`, `constructor`, `prototype`) in a path or anywhere in a value;
- changes `/id`, `/format`, `/engineVersion`, `/pins` or `/suppressed`;
- changes a place, grid or route a map draws, or marks anything but a map with `map`. This is the same rule as `put`, checked by comparing the draft before and after.

What a patch leaves must pass the shape checks the Studio seam's `validate` makes, or it is refused as `validation`. After that the document is migrated and its maps compile, as after any collection write. An item a patch or a `put` leaves in a shape the engine can't read at all (its lint, migration or map compile throws on it) is refused as `validation` too, naming the item, before anything is written.

Every editor write that changes a table (`put` or `remove` on `tables`, a `profile`, or a patch) gives that table its content's revision, `hashJson({schema, columns, rows})`, and pins the world to it in `pins.tables`. A removed table loses its pin. A revision the client sends is never used.

A commit is refused as `conflict` when a stored draft began on a head other than the current one ("the world changed since this draft began; reload it"), since committing it would put back what was made head since. Discarding the draft, or restoring a revision onto the head, clears the way.

## 24. Character minds

Simulated perspectives drive the characters, and the mechanics ground them. Each character the story is close to gets a **mind**: one model call made as that character, on a context scoped to what they know, blind to every other mind. It never writes narration or dialogue. It answers with how they read the moment, what they intend and how they come across, and the narrator depicts it. Concrete outcomes (money, moves, time) are still settled after the reply, from what was actually written (§5.1, §9.4). Every answer is recorded as an event (`minds.*`, the `minds` pack), so a fold, a Re-narrate and the background lane never call a model.

### 24.1 The fidelity ladder
- **Levels:** Live, Near, Far, Background. A character's level is recomputed every turn and at every time-skip beat, as a pure function of the state and the turn's texts (the player's latest message and the reply before it; none inside `advance` or at a beat).
- **Focus score:** the sum of the world's weights for each input that holds:
  - **Proximity:** in the POV's scene, else close by (under the same parent place).
  - **Engagement:** named or addressed in the latest message or reply. A name counts whole, or by a word of it that singles them out; honorifics never count.
  - **Their relationship to the POV:** regard either way, scaled by its size, and a `knows` relation either way.
  - **Story involvement:** they own a track under way; up to three tracks under way name them in their stages; their role facet values, a value nested under a weighted one counting as it (a villain under antagonist); and they are named by a world rule.
  - **Pending interactions with the POV:** an invitation, an appointment together, an open plan or commitment with the POV, up to three.
  - **Tier and recency:** the detail band of their tier, and being seen with the POV lately (all of it right after, nothing a day later).
- **Who decides:** the GM's pin in the chat decides first. Then the author's `never` selector (always Background), which wins over `always` (always Live). Then the score against the world's thresholds, each read as at least the next one down. Live is only for people at hand: someone neither in the POV's scene nor named this turn is Near at most, however high their score, so a Live slot never goes to someone across town while a person in the room has none. "Close by" scores but doesn't count as at hand: it means under the same parent place, which in a town is the whole town. The GM view shows each input's points, any pin and that cap.
- **Who thinks this turn** (the user's cadence, §24.6):
  - Live characters think every turn, best score first, up to the Live limit ("none" for no limit).
  - Near characters, and Live ones past the limit, think on their triggers: their first mind, a rise on the ladder, entering the POV's scene, being named, a number of turns, or a number of game minutes since their last mind.
  - Far characters think on their first mind, a rise on the ladder, or their period.
  - These refreshes share the refresh limit, the most pressing first. A GM's request for a fresh mind always runs.
  - Background characters follow routine only.

### 24.2 The world's minds config
`world.minds` (optional; a world without it plays with the defaults, `worldMindsOf`):
- **`secrecy`:** `brief` (the default) or `full` (§24.3).
- **`weights`:** points for each input of §24.1, from −100 to 200 (`roles` by classification value).
- **`thresholds`:** the lowest score for `live`, `near` and `far`.
- **`always` and `never`:** selectors, or null.
- **`memory`:** the defaults for §24.4: the witness lines shown, recall's recent and relevant turns and tokens, the notes shown and kept, the open commitments shown, salience's half-life in game days and fade floor, and consolidation (on or off, and its period in game days).

A value out of range or of the wrong kind plays as its default, and lint (`minds.config`) says so. The world JSON Schema describes the part (`$defs/minds`). Build writes it whole as the `minds` part.

### 24.3 What the narrator gets: the Minds section
- **The section:** a section of its own (slot `minds`, outlet `lre_minds`). Every world has it, even one that defines its own sections. It takes this round's minds for the people at the POV's place that the POV can see, plus anyone whose mind interrupted a time skip. A mind from an earlier round isn't the narrator's.
- **Its budget:** a token budget of its own (Settings → Minds), apart from the section budget, so minds never crowd out Scene or People. When the section is over its budget it is left out for that turn, as a clipped section is.
- **`full` secrecy:** each mind's monologue, intent, conditional reactions, mood and depiction go to the narrator. The section's note says to depict them, never to state a thought, and never to let one character act on another's mind. Under `full` the narrator may read a character's own secrets in their monologue; that is the author's choice.
- **`brief` secrecy:** only the depiction, written as behaviour anyone present could see. It is screened as Stance's cues are: an unrevealed secret (`findLeaks`) or a name the POV can't see (`namesHidden`) withholds it. The screen runs when the mind is recorded (the event is marked `withheld`, which only the GM view shows; the inspector reads the call as `recorded`), and again when the section is drawn. The monologue stays in the GM view.
- **Stance:** whoever has a line in the Minds section is left out of Stance (`minds.minded`). Stance still tells of everyone else with the POV.

### 24.4 Memory
Four layers, each tunable (the world's defaults, then the user's overrides):
- **Witness log:** who saw or was told each notable event. After each reply, `minds.presence` records who was in the POV's scene (everyone acting at its place, seen by the POV or not) and the turn's notable lines, which reach each of their logs as seen. A `memory` answer's `told` reaches each listener's log as told. Life-state knowledge keeps its own record (§8).
- **Recall by presence:** the chat turns a character was there for, built here from the presence records, since the host's memory can't filter by who was present. It reads the turns' messages through the host's chat (the player's message and the reply as displayed, hidden JSON blocks left out). It takes the latest `recall.recent`, plus the `recall.relevant` that share the most words with the moment, oldest first, within `recall.tokens`. A character never recalls a turn they weren't present for.
- **Model-written notes:** after each reply, the `memory` step runs once for each present character whose level the user chose (Standard: Live; Full: Live and Near). It is written from their perspective and based only on what was written: up to three notes with a salience from 1 to 5, the commitments the turn made and resolved, plans with times, what they told whom, and their summary when the turn changed it.
- **Commitments:** promises, debts, grudges, favours and plans with someone. They stay until kept, broken or released.
- **Salience and consolidation:**
  - A note's salience halves every half-life. One under the floor has faded and is no longer shown.
  - Notes past the number kept, and faded ones, are folded into the character's summary by the `consolidate` step, at most once per period per character and for at most two characters a turn.
  - The state keeps hard bounds whatever the settings say: 64 notes (the least salient go), 48 open and 16 closed commitments, 24 open and 16 closed plans, 48 witness lines and 400 presence records.

### 24.5 A mind's call
- **The context**, built only from what the character knows:
  - their entity's fields (never a Lumiverse character card: the extension doesn't request `characters`, §3);
  - their summary, notes, commitments, plans, witness log and last mind;
  - the scene as they perceive it: who they can see, and how they stand toward each;
  - the occasion: clock, day part, holidays, weather;
  - their own life: a plain money summary (`economy.summary`), the day's schedule, and their agendas, the truth of their own secrets included;
  - lore read as them: public entries, `known_by` entries by what they know, and of the secrets whose gate doesn't hold only their own (an entry that links them);
  - recall by presence, told to ignore narration of other people's private thoughts;
  - the moment: what the POV just said and did if they are with the POV; else a note that they aren't.
  - Wherever story text appears (recall, the moment, the memory step's turn), a line says that its "you" is the POV, never the character. Narration speaks to the POV in the second person, and a mind told "You are X" would otherwise take it as itself.
- **Never in it:** the GM view, another character's mind, or a turn they weren't present for.
- **The answer:** JSON `{monologue, depiction, intent, reactions[], plans[{what, kind, when{from, to}, where?, with?[], priority}], mood?, interrupt?{reason}}`.
  - Lengths are capped.
  - Plans resolve to place and people ids and game minutes, and one with no time, or wholly past, is dropped.
  - `interrupt` counts only at a time-skip beat.
  - An answer that isn't JSON gets one repair call. One that says nothing records no mind.
- **Prompts:** the steps are `mind`, `memory`, `skip` and `consolidate`. Each has built-in instructions (`MIND_PROMPT_DEFAULTS`).
  - **Precedence:** the world's step template for the purpose (the first by id), then the user's own prompt (Settings → Minds, with a reset to the default), then the built-in.
  - **Placeholders:** `{{character}}`, `{{world}}`, `{{pov}}`, `{{place}}` and `{{clock}}` are filled.
  - **Answer format:** the engine appends the purpose's answer format (`MIND_ANSWER_FORMATS`), which no template replaces, and then the preset's `RPEngine · <purpose>` block (§6.8).
  - **Lint:** a minds template's signals are ignored (`minds.step_signals`), and a second template for one purpose is never used (`minds.step_duplicate`).
- **Calls:** every mind is its own call, made in parallel on the pre phase's wall clock, apart from its call count. The inspector lists each call (§6.5) with no text: purpose, phase, level, outcome (`recorded` or `failed`), time, tokens, whose prompt it ran, and the character by name. A call for someone the POV can't see or hasn't met isn't listed at all, so the panel never shows that someone unseen is in the room or that an unmet person matters; a history kept by an earlier build, which named them "Someone", reads back without those calls.

### 24.6 Settings and cost
- **The user's minds settings** (`UserSettings.minds`, Settings → Minds): on or off; the Live limit; the Near triggers and their counts; the Far period; the refresh limit; the levels that get memory; the Minds section's budget; memory overrides; prompt overrides; and a time skip's caps (beats per skip, minds per beat). "No limit" is `"none"`.
- **Model use presets:** a preset sets all of these but the prompts and memory overrides.
  - **Lean:** minds off.
  - **Standard:** a Live limit of 3, two refreshes a turn, memory for Live.
  - **Full:** no limits, memory for Live and Near.
- **When minds run:** whenever they are on and the purpose's step card isn't switched off. An unsaved card (Lean saves none) runs on the helper with 2,000 max tokens, and Settings → Steps shows it so (`stepConfigOf`), so a change made on it keeps it on; a saved one sets the connection, the tokens and the lore. The `skip` step is the exception: unsaved, it is off, and every preset saves it off (§24.7).
- **Cost:** minds' calls aren't counted in §5.3's per-preset tables. A turn makes one call per mind that runs, and after the reply one `memory` call per present character at a chosen level and the occasional `consolidate`.

### 24.7 Time skips
A timelapse turn (§9.2: the palette, a chip or typed time) is a **time skip** while minds are on: before the reply, the skip finds its beats, lets the minds of the people each beat involves think there, and may stop where one of them would interrupt it (`backend/skip.ts`). The background lane and Re-narrate never run one, and minds off leaves a plain timelapse. Nor does one run while a fight holds the clock (§11): the plan refuses that timelapse, so no mind is asked for it. Typed time that states a day and time ends the skip exactly then: "I put in my shifts all week. Skip ahead to Friday at 6 PM." typed on Monday morning skips to Friday 18:00, with the week's beats on the way, unless something stops it first.
- **The state it starts from** is exactly the plan's: the fold before the turn's target, the turn's decisions and its minds round, and the turn's first steps (§5.1). So it reaches each beat in the state the plan will.
- **The beat scan** finds the following without a model, only up to where the skip would end on its own:
  - the POV's appointments;
  - the plans of Live and Near characters (as recorded: the GM's pin, else their latest mind's level) that involve the POV, where the planner lays them (§24.8);
  - from a scratch run of the skip: invitations to the POV, story moments (a track changing stage; a time rule, or one that interrupts or tells a beat), and the POV's money (paydays, bills, statements, declines, at their own instants).

  Beats at the same minute are one.
- **The `skip` step** is off unless the user turns it on. Its prompt is the world's template, else the user's own, else the built-in, and the answer format follows it. It is given the span, the beats found and what the people involved plan, and proposes at most five more beats, each a local time inside the span with a reason. Its reasons, like the scan's, are recorded in `minds.skip` for the GM only: no mind and no player surface is given them, since they may come of someone's private intent.
- **The caps:**
  - **Beats per skip** (`minds.skip.maxBeats`) keep the weightiest: plans with the POV, then invitations, its appointments, story moments, its money, and the step's last.
  - **Minds per beat** (`maxMindsPerBeat`) keep the people the beat names first, then whoever is with the POV at Live or Near by fidelity at that moment, best score first.

  `"none"` is no cap, and the before-the-reply clock still bounds it.
- **Running it:** one timelapse segment, run beat by beat under one unit cap (only its first slot runs whatever the cap). The cap is checked at a slot's start, as for one piece: a beat inside a slot the cap can't run is never reached, and when the events cap runs out inside such a slot, the run goes back to its start, so a cap stop is always on a slot boundary (§14).
  - At each beat, each of its people thinks in a call of their own, blind to the others, and the reason they are given names only the time.
  - Their `minds.mind` events apply at that moment, so their plans shape what follows.
  - Past the last beat the skip runs to its end, where whoever is with the POV thinks once more, within the Live limit, as the turn's own minds for the Minds section.
- **Stops:**
  - **A mind's `interrupt`** stops the skip at its beat. The summary records `stoppedBy: "mind"` and who. The stop chip reads "Timelapse stopped: <name>" (or "someone", for a character the POV doesn't know) and offers Continue to the original target. The Outcome line and the outline say who stepped in; the Minds section tells how.
  - **The before-the-reply clock** (Settings → Model use, at most 105 s) running out stops the skip at the next beat it reaches, the first one too when it ran out before it, so every Continue gets further; whatever that beat's minds finished stays, and no one is asked after it ran out. The summary names no stop, and the Continue timelapse chip offers the time left; the turn never fails.
  - Any other stop (a `stop`-class signal, the unit cap) is the timelapse's own (§9.2). A slot a beat falls inside is still stepped once, at its end, and its stop check reads every signal raised in it, before the beat and after, so a `stop`-class signal ends the skip exactly where the same span run whole ends, and its Continue's scan finds the beats after it.
- **Recording:** the skip's record is every beat it reached, each with the events decided there (none when its minds gave nothing: a failed call, or no one to ask), and where it stopped. The plan replays it: the same segment, cut at the same beats, stops where the run before the reply stopped, in the same state, and applies the same events. The turn's events are therefore an ordinary timelapse record (§4.3), coarsened, holding `minds.skip` (the beats, their times, reasons and sources) and each beat's minds. A re-fold or a Re-narrate reads them, and a Re-run asks again.

### 24.8 The planner
The Cast life planner (§8) lays each character's own plans into their days and lives them; plans already recorded drive it whether minds are on or off, on the background lane too.
- **Laying a plan.** A character's open plans (`minds.plans`, from a mind, the memory step or the GM) become blocks of their day:
  - **A tight window** (at most twice what its kind takes) is the plan's own time.
  - **A wider window** gets its kind's length (an errand 45 minutes, a meeting 90, a visit 120, work 240, rest 60, leisure 90, other 60). It goes at the first time in the window, within its kind's hours (errands 9 to 18, meetings 10 to 22, visits 10 to 21), that trips, appointments and, for a plan that doesn't take its time from routine, the routine leave free. The search covers the window's first 14 days.
- **Precedence:**
  - A plan takes its time from routine (work, school) when it is strong (priority 4 or 5), or, for a Live or Near character, ordinary (3) at a set time.
  - A Far character's routine bends only for strong plans, and a Background character follows routine only.
  - A plan that must happen (5) overrides appointments too.
  - Plans cut each other by priority, then age, and a trip overrides everything.

  Laying is a pure function of the plans and the authored commitments, never of the clock or of where anyone stands. A plan therefore lands at the same time however often, and from whatever timelapse segment, its day is planned.
- **Living it:**
  - A plan's block starts and ends at its own minute, between slot steps: an agent process's moments (`ProcessDef.instants`), applied among the schedule edges in a turn and a timelapse, for an agent stepped through the slot before. Catch-up steps by slot.
  - At the start the character goes to the plan's place (home for rest).
  - After the end they take up whatever follows, going home when that names no place.
- **Settling it:** at each slot step a `minds.plan` event marks a plan done once every block laid for it has passed. It marks one dropped once its window has passed with nothing laid: "no time for it" (routine, appointments or a trip left no room), or "routine only" for a Background character. Each records its own time, so a late catch-up settles it alike.
- **What the player sees:** a plan's block shows its kind ("Errand", "Meeting someone", "A visit", "Work", "Rest", "Time out", "Busy") and "their own plan", never the plan's words, which are the character's private intent. The GM view shows the plan whole (§24.9).
- **Occasions.** A day's occasion shapes its options:
  - **A day off** (the weekend, or a holiday that gives one) makes a slow morning (breakfast 7 to 11) and more time for leisure and friends.
  - **Holidays and observances** (Calendar 4) each bring an option of their own.
  - **Birthdays:** a person's own birthday and a close friend's (regard 30 and up) bring theirs. The `birthday` field is `MM-DD`, or `YYYY-MM-DD` with the year they were born. 29 February falls on the 28th in a common year, and lint flags a birthday that doesn't read as a date.
  - **Season and weather:** summer draws people outdoors, winter and bad weather keep them in.
- **Needs** (Cast life 4) follow the plan. An NPC's need is met at the end of the latest block that meets it, or now while they're in it, and the routine's times stand in before the day has met it. Options weigh needs as the day so far leaves them, so a plan through the lunch hour leaves them hungry for dinner.
- **Invitations** (Cast life 4) come from plans with the POV. One is asked at most 6 hours before the meeting, never before the meeting's day or before the plan was made, and only once (it is keyed by the plan). Its text never names a place the POV can't see. The old formula invitations run only while minds are off.
- **Stance** leaves out whoever the Minds section tells of (§6.6, §24.3).

### 24.9 The GM view
- **What it shows:** per character, their level, score and each input's points, the pins, whether a fresh mind is asked for, their latest mind (monologue, depiction, intent, reactions, mood, interrupt, and whether its depiction was withheld), their plans, notes (with their salience now, and whether faded), commitments, witness log and summary.
- **The controls,** each an `lre.edit` operation, so one `fix` entry with its Undo:
  - pin a level, or unpin (`mind_pin`);
  - ask for a fresh mind next turn (`mind_refresh`);
  - add, edit or delete a note, commitment or plan (`mind_memory`; a value names only the fields that change).
- **Player surfaces:** never any of it. Every minds event words nothing for a player's line, so no Journal note, chip, Cast or other panel tells a mind. The inspector's traces carry no text, list only people the POV can see and has met, and never say a depiction was kept from the narrator.

### 24.10 Determinism
- **Events:** every model answer is recorded as an event: `minds.round` and `minds.mind` before the plan; `minds.presence`, `minds.memory` and `minds.consolidated` with the reply's read. Each apply reads only its own data, the minds slice and the clock, never an entity's fields, so a coarsened record (L21) folds the same.
- **Swipes:** a Re-narrate copies its source's pre-reply events, so it reuses the pre phase's minds. A Re-run asks again. A Re-read edit writes the memory again from the edited text.
- **No model calls:** the background lane (Playtest, Check world) runs no model and records no mind. Plans already recorded still drive the planner (§24.8).
- **Replay:** a replay, a restart or the other fold gives the same minds state. The property suite generates minds turns, replies that record presence and memory, and GM minds controls, and checks them on every view.

## 25. Money foundation

The economy pack's core (`src/packs/economy/core/`) is one double-entry ledger with an outside-economy counterparty (`economy.external:<id>`), and everything money does is built on it. The work module (§26) and the holdings module (§27) register through the `FinanceRegistry` it hands them (`module.ts`; the list is `modules.ts`): fields on its kinds, flow sources, amount providers, net-worth sources, sale hooks, view sections and summary lines. No module registers a kind or a second ledger, and none adds a top-level world collection: money is entity kinds (account, card, flow, trust, business, job, holding, property, invoice), the person fields `pay_prefs`, `filing_status` and `local_tax`, the place fields `atm` and `branch`, and `packs.economy.config` (`products`, `features`, `assets`, and module keys such as `taxes`).

### 25.1 Parties, owners and roles

A party is a person, a business, a trust or a household. An account, a holding, a property or a business lists `owners: [{party, role, share?}]`. The role grants the rights: `owner` and `joint` spend, manage and count it; `authorized_user` spends; `custodian` and `trustee` manage it for a `beneficiary`, who counts it. Every "your own things" read in the engine goes through `heldBy`, so held accounts and cards count as a character's own. The first owner opens the account. A joint account opens once, by `economy.open`, whichever owner's money materializes first. Shares split what a shared account is worth among its owners.

### 25.2 Products and classes

`product` names a world product (`packs.economy.config.products`) or a class. Classes are cash, checking, business checking, savings, money market, CD, trust, escrow, credit card, charge card, line of credit, loan, mortgage, brokerage and retirement. A product gives terms (APR or APY, fees, grace days, minimum rule, limits, hold days, term); the finance table's class defaults fill whatever it leaves out. Rules read class traits (`CLASS_TRAITS`: asset, liability, pays, revolving, installment, locked, …), never a class name. A locked class (retirement, CD) can't be chosen to pay, moved from or drawn on by a flow; its own module may post from it.

### 25.3 Cards

A card is an entity on an account, in a holder's hands. It carries a network (debit, credit or charge), a state (active, frozen, lost or cancelled), daily purchase and cash limits, and optionally the gear item that is the physical card. Freezing and reporting a card lost are the holder's actions. A replacement is a new card on the same account; the old one is cancelled, and its gear card is replaced where gear runs. A purchase by card posts at once, pending until it settles a day later where holds run.

### 25.4 One statement cycle

Every credit product runs through one cycle (`cycle.ts`). Revolving accounts close a statement on their statement day: interest falls only on a balance carried past a grace period, and the first statement is always in grace, so an opening balance isn't charged at once. Interest follows the average daily balance where that feature runs, or the month's balance otherwise. Each statement also posts any annual fee and rewards, and sets the minimum due (a percentage plus interest, with a floor).

On the due day, autopay pays the minimum, the statement balance, the full balance or a fixed amount, capped at what's owed. If less than the minimum arrives, the account moves through the delinquency stages: late, 30, 60, 90, 120, collections and charged off. A late fee posts, never more than the minimum. At 60 days the penalty APR starts, at 90 the account freezes, and from 120 it goes to collections and then charged off. Each stage is a credit-report entry. Six on-time payments in a row lift the penalty APR.

Installment accounts bill an amortized payment on their due day. `months_left` counts down, the last payment takes everything left, and the loan is paid off at its term. Deposit accounts earn interest and pay any maintenance fee on the 1st.

### 25.5 Flows

Every scheduled movement of money is a flow: authored flow entities, and flows the foundation and its modules derive. The foundation's own are card and loan autopay, living costs and vehicle premiums. The modules' are payroll, owner's draws and invoices.

A flow has:
- a payer and a payee;
- an amount: fixed, from a formula, a table, a percentage, what's due, or an amount provider's;
- a schedule (`every`, `interval`, `days`, `weekdays`, `months`, `except`, an `at` time, `once`, start, end and count), with a roll to a business day;
- splits;
- a link to what it belongs to;
- a shortfall policy for when the payer can't cover it: skip, partial, defer (added to the next occurrence), retry (after a number of days, a set number of times), fallback (another account), or overdraft.

The money process (`economy.money`) runs every occurrence, statement and due check in a slot at its own instant, in time order. It works on a scratch state, so two payments due the same morning see each other. A payment to someone in the world lands in their deposit account; any other payee is an outside counterparty.

### 25.6 Journal and holds

Every posting writes a journal line on each account it touches: memo, kind, category, counterparty, card, flow, and status (pending or posted). The journal keeps the last 40 lines per account, monthly totals for 13 months, the last 6 statements and the notes. A hold makes part of a balance unavailable until its time: an authorization, or a deposit's hold days at a cash machine. Release is the GM's control. What can be spent is the balance, less holds, plus overdraft room.

### 25.7 Credit

Each person has a credit report: inquiries, accounts opened, on-time and late payments, collections, charge-offs, closures and payoffs. A score is computed monthly from payment history, utilisation, account age, new credit and mix (`credit.ts`), and the report keeps its history. Applying for credit makes a hard inquiry; the score then sets the APR tier and the limit, or a decline that says why. Below the level where credit runs, an application simply opens the account.

### 25.8 Payment preferences

A person's `pay_prefs` lists ways to pay, in order: cash, debit, credit, charge, checking, savings, or a named card or account. Each tier can set `min`, `max`, `categories` and `except`. A payment takes the first tier that fits and can cover it, then any account the payer can spend from. Cash needs a till, an in-person sale where the payer is. A method or account the player names ("with my card") goes before the preferences.

### 25.9 Cash, machines and branches

Where cash needs a machine (the `atm` feature, at the top level), a withdrawal takes one of two routes. At a branch of the account's own bank (a place's `branch`) it needs no card and has no limit. At a cash machine (a place's `atm`, found at the person's place or a place it is inside) it takes an active card on the account. The machine's per-withdrawal limit and the card's daily cash limit both apply. Another bank's machine charges its surcharge plus the account's out-of-network fee, and cash from a credit account is a cash advance, with its fee. A cash deposit is available at once at a branch; at a machine that takes deposits, the account's hold days apply. Below the top level, cash moves freely between the wallet and the accounts.

The machine rule holds only in a world that places at least one `atm` or `branch`. A world that places none, such as a migrated older world or one that keeps banking offstage, moves cash freely at every level; otherwise the top level would leave cash out of reach.

### 25.10 Overdraft

A deposit account's `overdraft` policy decides a payment the balance can't cover. `decline` refuses it. `fee` pays it up to `overdraft_limit` and charges the product's overdraft fee. `linked` draws the gap from `overdraft_link`. A flow that bounces may charge a fee for insufficient funds.

### 25.11 Households and views

A household is a party its members belong to. Its accounts, the joint accounts every member holds, and its shared flows (paid by it, or split among its members) make its view: who pays what share of the month. `FinanceView` is one party's money at their own level: overview, accounts, cards, flows, jobs, businesses, investments, properties, credit and household. The POV's view is `economy.money`; every party's, POV first, is the GM's `economy.people_money`; and any one party's is `economy.finance`. Player views show "someone" for any party the POV can't see.

### 25.12 Realism gating

The Money level sets a baseline.

| Level | What runs |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | A wealth band, following a notional net worth. |
| 2 | Cash plus one account. |
| 3 | Every account, cards, flows, payment preferences, households and living costs. |
| 4 | Statements, interest, fees, delinquency, credit, cash machines, holds, limits and overdrafts. |

Each feature in `FEATURE_DEFS` has a floor (the lowest level it can run at) and the level where it turns on by itself. A world's Custom toggles (`packs.economy.config.features`) switch a feature on anywhere from its floor up, or off. Flows take the payer's level when the payer is in the world, else the payee's. Demotion stops processes and narrows views; balances stay live, and the trial balance is 0 at every level mix.

### 25.13 Minds

`economy.summary` is a character's own money in plain second-person lines, for their mind: cash and the bank, what they owe and when it's due, what comes in and goes out in the next two weeks, the modules' lines (their pay, their business), and what's pressing. It also returns the band and roughly what they could spend today. It never says anything about anyone else's money.

### 25.14 Older worlds

`migrateWorld` reads older revisions through `migrateMoney` (`src/engine/money-migrate.ts`). It converts:
- an account's `owner` and `type` to its owners list and product, and `remaining_months` to `months_left`;
- an autopay sentence ("minimum from acct_x") to a mode and the account it pays from;
- a deposit account's `apr_pct` to its `apy_pct`;
- a party's `bills` to flow entities, dropping a lender's bill that the loan's own autopay already pays, and linking a car insurance bill to the owner's vehicle;
- a person's occupation and payroll fields to a job;
- an economy config that names no region to the POV's `region`, so sales tax at its places runs at the rate its people's income tax does;
- a brokerage's units of a symbol to a holding of that asset (`<account>:<symbol>`, the id the holdings module's trades add to), with the symbol added to the world's assets.

Only a world with the old shapes is migrated, so a migrated world has nothing left to migrate. The starter templates are written in the foundation's vocabulary (`data-src/templates/README.md`).

## 26. Work and business

The work module (`packs/economy/core/work.ts`, with `employment.ts`, `business.ts`, `books.ts` and `taxes.ts`) runs on the money foundation (§25). Every payment it makes is a flow or a posting the foundation checks and journals, and every number it keeps lives in the economy pack's slice, written by its own events inside the posting's unit (§4.3 groups). So both folds keep it alike, a rebased view that drops a payment drops its bookkeeping too, and nothing in it calls a model.

### 26.1 Jobs and pay

**A job** is a `job` entity: one worker's employment.
- **The employer** is either a business in the world, which pays from its operating account (§26.2), or an outside employer by name, which pays from the outside economy. An employer named by a place, as worlds wrote it before businesses were parties, is the business trading there.
- **The pay basis** is one of: `salary` (a year), `hourly`, `per_shift`, `commission`, `tips` or `stipend` (a period). A rate the job doesn't set comes from its occupation row:
  - salary: the typical earnings;
  - hourly: the median wage, or for a tipped occupation the tipped cash wage, with the rest of the median as tips by the hour;
  - per shift: eight hours at the median.

**The work a job counts.** A job counts only the days from its `start` to its `end` (the last day worked), and inside its `season` (`MM-DD` to `MM-DD`, which may wrap the new year) when it has one. Hourly and per-shift pay count hours, from one of three sources (`hours`):
- **`scheduled`:** the worker's commitments at the job's workplace, as castlife gives them (`castlife.commitments`). That covers their routine there, after every cut a trip or a plan of their own makes. A shift is a day with work in it.
- **`attended`:** the player's own timesheet (`economy.timesheet`). When a slot completes, each work block of the player's that ended in it counts if they turned up: they are at the workplace, or arrived there or left it after the block began. Blocks from before the chat began count as attended.
- **`contract`:** the job's hours a week over the days it counts, thinned to its weeks a year unless a season already says which weeks.

With no source set, a worker who has a routine at the job is paid for it: the player as attended, anyone else as scheduled. A worker with no routine there is paid the contract hours.

**The pay schedule** (`pay_every`):
- `weekly` and `biweekly`: anchored per job, counted from its `pay_anchor`, else the first payday weekday (`pay_weekday`, Friday by default) on or after its start;
- `semimonthly`: on the chosen days (`pay_days`, the 15th and the last day by default);
- `monthly`: on any one day (the last by default);
- `custom`: a flow schedule, flattened (`pay_unit`, `pay_interval`, `pay_weekdays`, `pay_days`, `pay_months`, `pay_except`);
- `per_job`: each evening at 21:00, for that day's work.

A payday that falls on a weekend or a day-off holiday rolls to the business day before it (`pay_roll`: `before`, `after` or `none`).

**A paycheck** pays for the work since the last one, up to the start of the day it is paid; a gig's runs up to its payout.
- **Salary and a stipend** pay each scheduled period the paycheck closes, each prorated by the days the job counted in it. A seasonal salary divides over the periods its season reaches.
- **Commission** is the job's percentage of the sales credited to it since the last paycheck: sales at the business while the worker is at the workplace, and walk-in takings on days they worked, split evenly among those who qualify.
- **Tips** are the job's tips an hour times its hours.
- **Deductions** are fixed amounts or percentages of gross, never more than is left. A pre-tax deduction comes off income-tax wages; a retirement contribution is still FICA wages, a health premium isn't. Each goes to an account of the worker's, or to an outside payee. A business employer adds its match (`match_pct`) to the worker's account.
- **Withholding** follows §26.3, never taking more than the deductions leave.
- **The net** is routed across the worker's accounts (`splits`): fixed amounts first, then percentages of the rest; what's left goes to their deposit account.

The paycheck is one posting: the gross, plus the match and, at the top level, the employer's share of FICA, out of the employer's account; the net, deductions and taxes to where they go. Its `economy.payslip` rides with it. The payslip moves the job's record on (what it has paid through; the timesheet and the commission sales start again), adds to the worker's year of pay, and books the cost to a business employer. The payday note goes with it too: a signal for the player, a private note for anyone else (§9.3).

**Payroll as flows.** Each job derives one flow (`pay:<job>`, source `job`) from the employer to the worker, on the job's cycle, from its anchor to the first payday after its last day.
- It runs at its payer's level: a business's own, or for an outside employer, the worker's (§25).
- Its shortfall is `skip`. Each paycheck covers everything since the last one paid, so a payday the employer can't cover is paid with the next.
- A paycheck with nothing earned (a period on leave, out of season, or with no shifts) posts nothing but still moves the record on, so that time is never paid later.

**Hire, fire and raise** are actions for the business's owner and GM edits:
- **Hire** (`job_hire`) spawns a job. It starts today unless given a date, takes what it doesn't set from the occupation, and joins the business's own pay cycle, so its payroll runs on the same day.
- **End a job** (`job_end`, also the worker's own way to quit) sets its last day to today. The next payday pays through it.
- **Raise** (`job_raise`) sets the rate the next paycheck pays.

A GM spawns a job and sets its fields, or sets its `status`, `end` or `rate`. Payroll reads only the fields, so an edit changes payroll exactly as the action would.

**The old payroll.** The person fields (`hourly_cents`, `pay_frequency`, `deposit_to` and the rest) and the 09:00 payroll process are gone, and the world migration turns those fields into jobs (§25). An old chat's payroll left only plain postings, history lines and signals, so it still folds as recorded. The first paycheck after an Upgrade covers only from the day after the last scheduled payday, so it never pays a period twice.

### 26.2 Businesses

A business is a party (§25) with owners and shares, its own accounts and its books. **Its operating account** is where takings land and payroll and payouts come from: its `operating_account` when that is one of its own, else its best deposit account.

**Sales.** A purchase at a place a business trades at (its `places`, else where it stands; the most closely enclosing one wins) pays that business, through the foundation's sale hook:
- the price goes to the operating account, with the sales tax the business collects on top (§26.3);
- the tax is owed until it is remitted;
- the cost of the goods, the share its `margin_pct` doesn't keep, is owed to its suppliers;
- its commission staff at the workplace are credited.

A business doesn't sell to itself. One with no accounts is paid as any party without them is (§25).

**Walk-in takings.** A business with `daily_sales` takes that much, give or take its `sales_spread_pct`, from customers outside the world. The takings come at closing (17:00) on each of its `trading_days` (Monday to Friday by default), and never on a day-off holiday. Each day's amount is a triangular draw from the occurrence's own seeded generator (§25), and its customers pay sales tax at the business's level. These are its sales when no one in the world shops there.

**Payables** are settled monthly by derived flows: the suppliers on the 10th, the sales tax collected on the 20th, both rolled to the next business day. The sales tax goes to its region's authority (`tax:sales:<region>`). Each flow pays the whole balance owed, so one the business can't cover is paid in full with the next.

**Invoices** are `invoice` entities.
- **Issuing one** (`invoice_issue`) names the customer (someone in the world, or an outside customer by name), the amount, what it's for, and the days to pay: the business's `invoice_days`, else 30.
- **Payment is a flow** from the customer, paid as a bill (from an account, never wallet cash). It falls due on the due date and again every week after, until the invoice is paid in full. An outside customer pays on the due date; one in the world pays once their money covers it.
- **Late.** An invoice still unpaid when its due day ends goes `late` and takes the business's `late_fee_pct` of the amount, and the fee again every 30 days it stays unpaid. The customer and the business's owners are told.
- **Paying it now** (`invoice_pay`) is the customer's own action. **Voiding it** (`invoice_void`) is the owner's.

**Expenses** are flows the owners set up from the business's accounts: rent, utilities, loan payments. They reach the books through the journal.

**Owners are paid** in two ways:
- **Draws.** An owner or joint owner takes a draw (`owner_draw`) from the operating account. A corporation has none: it pays its owners by salary or dividends.
- **Distributions** (`distribute_profit`) share out an amount among the owners by their share of its worth. With no amount given, the business pays its `distribution_pct` of the profit since its last distribution. A distribution policy (`distribution_pct` with `distribution_every`: month, quarter or year) does the same as a flow on the 10th after each period closes.

Each owner's part goes where the holdings module routes their stake (`economy.stake_route`, else their deposit account). A reinvested part stays in the business. Every part is recorded on the stake (`economy.stake_paid`, §27).

**The books** are kept by calendar month, the last 25 of them:
- **Revenue** is sales, invoices paid to it, and other money in from the journal (less the takings of sales already booked).
- **Expenses** are the cost of goods, payroll with the employer's costs, invoices it paid, business income tax, and every other outflow from its accounts by journal category.

Owner draws, distributions, transfers between its own accounts and the sales tax it passes through count as neither. Its receivables are its unpaid invoices. Its payables are the invoices billed to it, the sales tax it holds and what it owes its suppliers. The holdings module values a stake from them (`economy.business_books`, `economy.operating_account`).

**On the dashboards**, a party sees itself when it is a business, and each business it holds a role on:
- its books for the year and for each of the last 12 months;
- its last 20 sales;
- its invoices;
- its staff, with their pay;
- what it owes for taxes and to its suppliers, with when each is next paid.

A party's own late invoices, owed or owed to it, are alerts. On the player's surfaces, anyone the player can't see is named only as "a customer", "an employee" or "an owner" (§7). A character's money summary for their mind says how a business of theirs is doing this month, and what they owe on an invoice.

### 26.3 Taxes

**Withholding** (feature `withholding`, from Money 3):
- **Flat below the top level:** the occupation's federal-plus-FICA rate (`withholding_rate_pct`) and the region's state rate (with its local rate for someone who pays it, `local_tax`), on gross pay less pre-tax deductions.
- **Brackets at the top level** (feature `tax_brackets`, Money 4). The period's taxable pay is annualized, the standard deduction for the worker's `filing_status` comes off, and the rest goes through the federal brackets of the finance tables and back to one period. Social Security is 6.2 % up to the year's wage base (counting the year's FICA wages so far). Medicare is 1.45 %, with the additional 0.9 % past $200,000. The state and local rates follow.

A contractor (`contractor`, by default the occupation's `self_employed`) has nothing withheld. A student working for their own school pays no FICA.

**Sales tax** (feature `sales_tax`) applies only at the top Money level (4), or under Custom with its toggle on. It runs at the payer's level: below it, a price is as listed, with no tax line. A business below it neither collects nor remits: a taxed customer's tax goes straight to the authority, and its walk-in takings, taxed at its own level, carry none.
- **The rate** is a place's own `sales_tax_pct`, else the combined rate of its tax region: a `tax_region` on it or an enclosing place, else the economy config's `region`, else the national row.
- **No tax** falls on a category exempt there (the economy config's `taxes.exempt`, or a place's `tax_exempt`), nor on what a business that doesn't collect (`collects_sales_tax: false`) sells.
- **Where it goes.** A business holds what it collects until it remits it monthly. On a purchase nobody in the world sells, the tax goes straight to the authority.

**Income tax nobody withholds** is paid as estimated tax, by derived flows on the federal due dates (15 April, June and September, and 15 January), rolled to the next business day. Each payment covers its period: January to March, April and May, June to August, and September to December.
- **A person's payment** taxes two kinds of income:
  - their contract pay in the period, from Money 3;
  - their share of the period's profit from each pass-through business they own (a sole proprietorship, a partnership, an LLC or an S corporation), at the business's `business_tax` level.
  
  Below the top level the tax is the occupation's `estimated_tax_set_aside_pct` (15 % without one) plus the state rate. At the top level the income is stacked on the year's wages through the brackets, with self-employment tax (15.3 % of 92.35 %, half of it deductible) on contract pay and on profit from any structure but an S corporation, plus the state rate.
- **A C corporation** pays the corporate rate (21 %, or the economy config's `taxes.corporatePct`) plus the state rate on the period's profit, from its own account, and books it as its income tax.
- **A missed payment** is made with the next: each one settles, as well as its own period, any period of the year before that no payment settled.

The holdings module stacks capital gains on the same income through two queries: `economy.ordinary_income` (a person's wages, contract pay and pass-through profit for a year, and their taxable income) and `economy.income_tax` (the brackets).

### 26.4 Records

**The economy slice** keeps:
- `payroll`: per job, what its last paycheck paid through, the paycheck itself, and the timesheet and commission sales since;
- `ytd`: per person and year, the last two years of gross pay, FICA wages, withholding, pre-tax deductions and contract pay by month;
- `books`: per business and month;
- `payables`: per business, the sales tax and supplier balances;
- `sales`: per business, its last 20 sales;
- `estimated`: per payer and period, what estimated tax has paid;
- `distributed`: per business, when it last distributed.

**The events** are `economy.payslip`, `economy.timesheet`, `economy.commission`, `economy.sale`, `economy.remit`, `economy.invoice_paid`, `economy.payout` and `economy.estimated_tax`. In a timelapse or catch-up record, a job's timesheet entries for one pay period merge into one (minutes add, days join), and so do its commission credits; nothing else merges, so a coarse record folds exactly as the full one. Invoice states, late fees and jobs hired or ended are plain field sets and spawns, so a GM edit of them is the same change.

## 27. Holdings and property

Everything a party owns besides money in accounts: positions in securities, funds, bonds, crypto and options, real estate, and stakes in businesses. It is the economy's holdings module (`packs/economy/core/{assets,holdings,income,options,gains,records,property,stakes,pricing,wealth}.ts`), registered through the money foundation's module interface (§25.12) by `registerAssets`. Its kinds, `holding` and `property`, are the foundation's; their behaviour is here. Everything it records is a ledger event, so a fold never draws, and Re-run, swipes and Upgrade see the same prices, payouts and gains.

### 27.1 Assets
- **What an asset is.** An entry of the world's `packs.economy.config.assets` (an `AssetDef`: a label and a class, with optional symbol, price, recipe, drift, volatility, yield, payments a year, correlation, bond terms and option terms); an option contract listed in play (`economy.mint`, when a player buys a strike and expiry no world lists); or, in an older chat, a finance-table recipe its brokerage account named as a symbol, read as a fund at 100 a unit.
- **Classes:** security, fund, bond, option, property, business stake, crypto, other.
- **Price models come from the finance tables.** Each class follows a recipe row of the table's `investing` section: equities and funds the stock index (`broad_us_equity_index`), bonds the aggregate bond row, crypto its own row, property the home-price appreciation row. The table's `investing.asset_classes` says how each class differs from its recipe (a single stock swings about 1.75 times as much as the index), how closely it moves with its market, how often it pays and whether it trades every day. An asset's own drift, volatility and yield override all of it; a table without the class rows (an older world's) falls back to the recipe rows and the module's last-resort defaults.
- **Total return.** The table's returns are total returns, so what an asset pays out comes off its price's drift: a fund yielding 1.3 % drifts at 11 − 1.3 % and pays the 1.3 % out. Nothing is counted twice, and options see the same yield.
- **Lint** (`economy.assets`): an asset needs a class; an option an underlying the world lists, a strike, an expiry and a right; a bond with a face value a maturity date; a recipe the table doesn't list is a warning; a price is above nothing.

### 27.2 Prices
- **The day's marks.** At the market's close (16:00 local) each trading day, `economy.markets` records an `economy.quote` per asset that moves: a seeded geometric Brownian step for priced assets, whose shock mixes one draw for its market (equities, bonds, crypto: the market's bad day is everyone's) with its own by its correlation. Crypto trades every day; everything else on business days (the foundation's business-day rule). Marks are rounded to a ten-thousandth of a minor unit, so they are the same on every platform.
- **Bonds.** A single bond (a face value, a coupon and a maturity) is priced from its market yield, its coupons still to come and the time left: the price with its accrued coupon, so it drops by a coupon when one is paid and pulls to its face as maturity nears. Its yield is what wanders: a mean-reverting step toward the aggregate bond row's yield. A bond's authored price sets the yield it starts at. A bond fund (a bond without a face) is priced like any fund on the aggregate bond row.
- **Options** are priced from their underlying, never marked: Black-Scholes-Merton with the underlying's price, volatility and yield, the three-month bill rate from the table's macro row and the time to expiry, times the contract's units (100). At and after expiry an option is worth what exercising it pays.
- **Property** values move once a month (§27.9).
- **Older chats.** An `economy.price` event an older chat recorded (a symbol's price in major units) still folds and still prices that symbol until its first new mark.
- **The GM** sets an asset's price with the `economy.set_price` effect (a GM edit's `effect` op): per unit for securities, funds, crypto and other assets; for a bond, the price is turned into the yield it implies. An option's price comes from its underlying, and a property's or a stake's value isn't a price, so those are refused with the reason.
- The market moves while anyone holding a position, or the POV, follows investments (the `investments` feature, §25).

### 27.3 Holdings and lots
- **A holding** is a position in one asset: in an investment account (brokerage or retirement, whose cash is ordinary guarded cash) or held directly by its `owners`. Its `lots` are `{qty, cost, at}`, a lot for each purchase, oldest first; its `route` says where its payouts go (§27.6).
- **Its owners** are its account's when it sits in one, so a joint brokerage account's holdings are both holders', by their shares; a custodian trades a child's account while the child owns it.
- **Older chats.** Before holdings were rebuilt, buying and selling added to the brokerage account's own `units`. The world migration moves the authored units into a holding of the account's symbol, so what an older chat's account still carries is what that chat traded: it counts as one more lot of that holding (at 100 a unit) until the next trade folds it in.
- **Lint** (`economy.holdings`): its asset is listed, its account is an account, a holding outside an account has owners, lots are a list, a route names an account.

### 27.4 Trading
- **`trade`** (`economy.trade`): buy or sell, a holding or an asset, an account to hold a new position in (absent: held directly), units or an amount (an amount buys what it covers; a sale with neither sells everything), the account a purchase pays from (absent: the investment account's own cash, else the buyer's deposit account) and the account a sale's proceeds land in (absent: the holding's account, else the seller's deposit account). Proceeds go where the player or the holding's account says, never to the first checking account found.
- **One unit:** the posting with the asset's market (`economy.external:market:<asset>`), the new holding when there was none, a minted contract, and the `economy.lots` change. A rebased view that can't afford the purchase, or doesn't hold the units, drops all of it.
- **Options** are bought by giving a right, a strike and an expiry with the underlying as the asset (the `options` feature); contracts trade whole. A contract past its expiry, or a bond past its maturity, doesn't trade.
- **Retirement money stays in its account:** a sale's proceeds and payouts land in it, and only its own account moves them out.
- Property and stakes change hands by their own actions (§27.9, §27.10), not by trading.
- **Who may:** spend rights on the account, or a spending role on a directly held holding's owners.

### 27.5 Gains
- **Realised in the fold.** The `economy.lots` reducer takes a sale's units first in, first out, whole lots oldest first, then part of the next with its share of the cost (the parts of a lot always add back to its cost). The proceeds against the cost of what was taken is the gain, short term for lots held under a year and long term for the rest, recorded for each owner by their share of its worth in their calendar year. Because the reducer does the arithmetic, a rebased view realises the gain on the lots that branch holds.
- **Unrealised** gains are what the views show: each lot's value now less its cost.
- **Records** (`economy.holdings` in the economy slice): gains by party and year, short and long apart, with the proceeds and basis; the latest 24 sales; income by party, year and kind; the basis of what's held outside lots (§27.9, §27.10).

### 27.6 Distributions
- **Payouts are flows.** Each holding whose asset pays derives a flow (`income:<holding>`, source `holdings`) that the foundation's flow engine runs with its schedule and journal. Dividends fall mid-month in evenly spaced months ending in December (quarterly: March, June, September, December); a bond fund pays monthly; a single bond pays its coupon on its maturity's day and month and every period back from it, its last on the maturity date.
- **The amount is worked out when it falls due:** the units held then times a payment's worth (the price times the yield over the payments a year, or the bond's coupon on its face).
- **Routing.** A payout lands where the holding's route says: reinvested (more units at that day's price, the payout as their cost, a new lot), an account the owners named, else the holding's account, else each owner's deposit account by share. A retirement account keeps what its holdings pay.
- **One line each.** A payout's posting is all a turn's lines and the owner's notes get of it (the flow's `quiet`, §25): payouts are routine and many, and a "received" note each would crowd paydays and statements out of a turn's eight lines. A reinvested payout touches none of the owner's accounts, so its memo carries the amount.
- **Income** goes on each owner's year (`economy.income`), which the investment view and the yearly tax read.
- **Rent** is the lease flows' (§27.9). **Business profits** are paid by the work module (§26) by share; where each owner's share lands is routed here (`economy.stake_route`: an account, or `reinvest`, kept in the business), and each payout is recorded with it (`economy.stake_paid`): income for the owner's year, and for a share kept in the business, more basis in their stake.
- **`route_distributions`** (`economy.route`, which the GM can run too): a holding's payouts, a property's rent (its `rent_to`) or a business stake's profits go to an account of the owner's, are reinvested (holdings and stakes only), or (an empty route) go back to the default.

### 27.7 Options and maturities
- **Exercise** (`exercise_option`, `economy.exercise`) before expiry: a call pays the strike for the underlying's units (100 a contract), which land in a holding of the underlying in the same account, made if there is none; a put delivers units the holder has there for the strike. What the contracts cost goes into the basis of the units a call brings, or comes off a put's proceeds, so the gain shows where a tax return would put it. A cash-settled contract pays what it is worth over the strike instead. The `economy.exercise` reducer does the lot arithmetic on the lots the branch holds.
- **Expiry,** at the market's close on the expiry date (`economy.settlements`, after the day's marks): a contract in the money is exercised and settled in cash at its intrinsic value, as a broker exercises and sells for the holder; one out of the money expires worthless, its cost a realised loss. Each owner gets a note.
- **Maturity:** a bond held to maturity is redeemed at its face at the close of its maturity date, its gain or loss realised.

### 27.8 Investment tax
- **At the top level** (the `capital_gains` feature), each party with gains or investment income recorded (people and trusts; a business's are its books') pays a yearly flow on 15 April (rolled to a business day) for the calendar year before (`investment_tax:<party>`, shortfall `defer`).
- **What it works out,** from the finance table's federal section and the work module's ordinary income and brackets (`economy.ordinary_income`, `economy.income_tax`): short gains and interest taxed at the ordinary brackets on top of the party's other income; long gains and dividends at the long-term rates (0, 15 and 20 %, by the thresholds for their filing status), stacked above it; the net investment income tax over its threshold; and the region's rate on the investment income.
- **Losses** of one term come off gains of the other; a net loss comes off ordinary income up to the table's limit (short losses first), and the rest carries forward, its terms kept. Selling one's home after two years excludes the table's amount of the gain.
- A loss that comes off ordinary income can make the year's figure a refund, which the flow pays into the party's deposit account.
- **The investment view** shows the year's gains so far, its income and the tax estimated on them, due the following 15 April.

### 27.9 Real estate
- **A `property`** is tied to a place (`place`), has `owners` with shares and a value (`value`, live: the monthly step moves it and a GM edit sets it). Its fields: its appreciation and swings, purchase price and date, mortgage, tax rate, insurance, upkeep, region, units to let, market rent, letting, lease term, vacancy, where its rent goes, an asking price.
- **Value:** at midnight on the 1st of each month, a geometric Brownian step on the home-price model (the property's own appreciation and swings, else the finance table's), sharing one housing shock across properties by its correlation. It runs while its owners follow property (the `property` feature, from Money 3).
- **The mortgage** is a foundation loan account (product `mortgage`) whose `secured_by` names the property; the property's `mortgage` names it back. Equity is the value less what the loans it secures owe. Lint warns when the two don't name each other.
- **Costs are flows**, derived per property and worked out when they fall due from its value: property tax at its own rate, else its region's effective rate (the regions table's `property_tax_effective_pct`), else the table's typical, in installments (twice a year by default); insurance a month, its own premium or the table's typical scaled to its value within the table's range; upkeep a month, a share of its value a year (1 % by default). Its owners pay them by their shares. Upkeep is an average, not a bill anyone sends, so like living costs it posts with no "paid" note.
- **Leases are rent flows** from tenant to owner, with a term: a `flow` entity of kind `rent` linked to the property, authored or started by its landlord (`lease_start`: a tenant, the rent, the term from today and the rent day), ended by the landlord or the tenant (`lease_end`). Rent lands in the property's `rent_to`, else its owners' deposit accounts by share. Routing the rent (`route_distributions`) re-points every lease not yet over that pays the owners, so the tenants already paying follow the route as later ones do.
- **Vacancy.** A unit with no lease in force is vacant. A property let to outside tenants (`managed`) lets each unit no lease covers, one lease after another: each runs its term (12 months by default) at the market rent grown at the property's appreciation to the month it starts, and the unit stands empty between tenants for a few months drawn from the property's and unit's ids (about `vacancy_months` on average), so the same on every fold and every machine. The first leases began some months before the letting did, each unit by its own offset, so they don't all end at once.
- **Buying** (`property_buy`): a property for sale (an asking price) or one nobody owns, at the asking price or more. The down payment comes from the buyer; the rest is a new mortgage the property secures (`openAccount`, product `mortgage`). The loans the property secured are paid off from the price and the rest goes to the sellers by share, each realising their gain.
- **Selling** (`property_sell`): one's share, to a buyer in the world (who pays as they choose) or outside. The seller's loans against it are paid off from the proceeds, the rest lands where the seller says. The buyer takes the seller's share; leases on a property its owner sold whole move their rent to the buyer, or end when it went outside the world. Selling one's home after two years excludes the table's amount of the gain (§27.8).
- **Lint** (`economy.property`): its place is a place, its owners are in the world, its mortgage is an account it secures, a managed property has a market rent, its rent goes to an account, a lease's end is a date.

### 27.10 Business stakes
- **A stake** is a party's share (the business's `owners`) of the business's value: its `value` when its author or the GM set one, else its book value (its net worth with its accounts, holdings, property and stakes, plus what its customers owe it less what it owes, from the work module's books) and its last twelve months' profit times its `valuation_multiple` (3 by default), never below nothing. A business that owns itself through others counts nothing the second time round.
- **Selling part of a stake** (`stake_sell`): the buyer pays, the shares move between them with everyone else's unchanged, and the seller realises the gain against what they put in (a stake play didn't record cost nothing: a founder's, held since before the story). A buyer outside the world can only take a whole business from its only owner.

### 27.11 Net worth, dashboards and summaries
- **Net worth** (§25) adds each party's share of every holding at market, every property's value and every business stake. The mortgage is already one of the party's accounts, so nothing is counted twice. An account that hasn't opened yet counts at the authored opening it will post, as the money views show it, so a party's worth at a chat's start is what it is once their accounts open.
- **The investment view** (`FinanceView.investments`): the holdings the party sees, with units, price, value and, where the books show detail, cost, gain and lots with their terms; the year's realised gains, income and estimated tax; the assets they could buy and the accounts that can hold them. **The property view** lists the properties they hold a share of, and those for sale: value, equity, the mortgage, every lease and vacant unit, and the cost flows. Alerts: contracts expiring within a week, bonds maturing within a month.
- **Routes in force.** A holding that pays, a property its viewer owns and a business they hold a share of each carry where its payouts go now (`route`: an account of the viewer's, reinvested, or the usual place); another owner's account shows only as that, unnamed. The hub's pickers open on it.
- **A mind's money summary** (`economy.summary`) gets plain sentences: what they have invested, the properties they own and what's left on the mortgage, the rent they collect, the businesses they own.
- **Secrecy.** Prices, mints, lots, income, disposals and routes are bookkeeping, never a player line; another party's trades and holdings show only in the GM view. What the POV sees of its own is the postings' lines and the notes its expiries and maturities send.
